# Ecosystem Conflict and Managed Development Foundation

Status: CANON-APPROVED FOUNDATION
Date: 2026-09-03

## Core rule

Ouros wild populations are not static encounter tables.

Population abundance, composition, strength, behavior and habitat pressure change over world time because of real Trainer interactions and abstract ecosystem processes.

## Wild strength

Repeated conflict can make surviving wild populations become more combat-capable over time.

Relevant world causes include actual resolved Trainer-versus-wild battles, abstract wild-versus-wild conflict exposure, predator/prey conflict exposure, survival and selective pressure, capture/removal patterns, migration and demographic turnover.

The population must not be represented by one fixed `area_level`.

Exact progression arithmetic and mapping into PTU Level, XP, Stats or Moves remain governed by audited rules contracts and are not defined by this foundation.

## Off-screen wild conflict

Wild Pokémon can fight, compete, displace and prey upon other wild Pokémon when no player is present.

Those off-screen processes are resolved algorithmically as ecosystem/world state. They are not simulated as invisible AutoPTU battles.

The abstract resolver may change population pressure, abundance, distribution, behavior, resource use or other approved ecological state. It must not invent a tactical transcript, Move use, exact damage, Status, initiative or other PTU battle facts.

When a concrete battle is instantiated as a real encounter, AutoPTU remains battle authority.

## Capture and predation

Successful capture removes a real member from the wild population.

Successful ecological predation can remove real prey membership through an authoritative demographic transition.

Neither process receives an automatic replacement spawn.

Predation can also change prey behavior and habitat use without a consumption event.

## Managed beginner-development areas

Areas intended for beginner development can have high encounter and battle volume.

They are not safe because ecology is frozen or because wild Pokémon have a magical hard Level ceiling.

Their risk envelope is maintained by active institutions and world processes that monitor conditions and prevent high-risk outlier Pokémon from remaining unmanaged in high-use beginner areas.

An exceptional wild Pokémon may still enter or emerge in such a zone. That is a real world event. Authorities may monitor, warn, restrict access, deter, contain, relocate or otherwise respond according to later canonized procedures.

If an individual is relocated, it remains a real member of the world and must transfer to an appropriate destination rather than disappear.

## World feedback

Ecosystem outcomes must be able to leave persistent, observable consequences in the world.

These can include changed wild behavior and activity, changed predator/prey spatial use, changed resource availability, vegetation browsing or recovery, changes in sapling/tree regeneration, trails and disturbed ground, feeding or nesting evidence, changed local population composition, NPC observations/reports/warnings, institutional responses and longer-term habitat presentation changes.

NPC knowledge remains evidence-based. Ordinary NPCs do not receive hidden simulator truth merely because the server calculated it.

## Authority boundary

Ouros owns ecosystem state and off-screen ecological resolution.

AutoPTU owns real tactical battle legality and outcomes.

Minecraft/Cobblemon projects the resulting world, entities and environmental evidence but does not manufacture ecological or PTU truth.

## Open implementation details

This foundation intentionally does not fix numerical conflict weights, exact simulation cadence, strength bands, PTU Level/XP mapping, predator/prey relationships, managed-area boundaries, institutional jurisdiction, risk thresholds, relocation destinations or vegetation response rates.

Those require source-backed species data, audited PTU/Caelo/Kairos rules and explicit future design work.
