## Why this pass exists

The existing anomalous-spaces layer intentionally refuses to infer time travel from unusual clocks, altered duration, portals or strange geography. That remains the correct default.

This pass researches the narrower question: if Ouros later authors a real temporal phenomenon, what structures keep the persistent world coherent without retroactive deletion, duplicated Pokémon/items, omniscient NPCs or a Minecraft-side rules engine?

## Source 1 — Pokémon Tabletop campaign seed: The Road to Tomorrow

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-the-road-to-tomorrow/

The PTU community site presents a Holon Institute campaign seed where one possible explanation for unusual Pokémon is a time rift. The proposed rift can pull extinct Pokémon from the past and alternate possibilities from parallel timelines. The seed also suggests escalation over time and leaves the cause open: Celebi, Dialga or something else.

Reusable structural lessons:

- temporal anomalies can begin as research problems rather than final-boss reveals;
- extinct species, altered traits and alternate possibilities should be evidence, not automatic explanation;
- escalation can occur through repeated observations and increasing instability;
- the cause can remain unknown for a long time;
- alternate versions of familiar actors are possible story material, but should never be generated by default;
- discoveries should affect science, institutions and society rather than remain isolated dungeon flavor.

Copyright boundary:

Do not import Holon Island, its Delta Species presentation, NPCs, exact mystery, or proposed Legendary cause into Ouros. Reuse only the high-level pattern of escalating temporal evidence and competing hypotheses.

## Source 2 — Pokémon animation: A Timeless Encounter!

Official episode page:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/22/episode-33-a-timeless-encounter

Public episode summaries describe a bounded Celebi-caused past visit involving Ash and Torracat, followed by a return to their own present.

Reusable structural lesson:

A temporal story does not need a fully editable timeline. A contained visit can preserve traveler identity, give the traveler information or relationships from another historical frame, and then return them to the current world.

Ouros translation:

- crossing record with departure and arrival times;
- same persistent actor/Pokémon IDs through the trip;
- explicit return state;
- knowledge gained during the visit stored with provenance;
- no automatic rewrite of unrelated present-day records.

Copyright boundary:

Do not copy the episode's characters, encounter sequence or dialogue. Celebi itself requires separate canon and PTU/Caelo approval before direct use.

## Source 3 — Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Time/Darkness

Reference:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Mystery_Dungeon:_Explorers_of_Time_and_Explorers_of_Darkness

This game uses future-origin characters, disrupted time, visions of past/future, and consequences tied to changing history. It also runs major story material across different temporal contexts.

Reusable structural lessons:

- a traveler can know facts that are not known in the destination time;
- future information should be scoped to the source temporal context;
- changing a causal event can threaten or alter a later world state;
- parallel action in different temporal contexts can make causality legible;
- visions are information events and should not be treated as identical to physical travel.

Ouros translation:

Keep `future observation`, `future claim`, `temporal crossing`, and `validated change to historical state` as separate objects.

Copyright boundary:

Do not import Time Gears, Temporal Tower, characters, plot turns or the game's erase-from-history logic as Ouros rules.

## Source 4 — Narrative World Model

Paper:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05577

Saifullah et al. propose a narratology-grounded typed temporal-state graph for long-form narrative memory. The motivating questions include who knows a secret, when they learned it, whether one event preceded another and how relationships changed.

Reusable architecture lesson:

Time-scoped facts and actor knowledge should be first-class graph state. A flat list of facts is insufficient once the same entity can be observed at different points in its history.

Ouros translation:

- facts need effective-time or state-version references;
- actor knowledge needs `learned_at` and provenance;
- historical records can be correct for their time while obsolete now;
- alternate temporal frames need explicit graph identity;
- a future claim carried into the present remains a claim until the current world reaches or validates it.

## Source 5 — Reconstructing Persistent Worlds from Narratives

Paper:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04037

Chen argues for reconstructing and maintaining persistent entities, locations, semantic relationships and evolving world state before creating interactive realizations.

Reusable architecture lesson:

Temporal content should operate on explicit persistent-world versions rather than regenerating the world from prose. A timeline change must therefore produce a validated state transition or branch, not a free-form rewrite.

Ouros translation:

- persistent entity identity survives temporal presentation changes unless canon explicitly creates a distinct branch entity;
- a historical state is a version of world facts, not a second unmanaged copy of the game;
- temporal interventions should be represented as diffs against an authoritative state version;
- Minecraft instantiation follows the validated state; it does not decide causality itself.

## Public fan-work scan

Public Pokémon fanfiction and roleplay communities contain many time-loop, timeslip and future-self stories. These are useful mainly as negative/structural evidence rather than direct inspiration.

Recurring design risks observed across loop fiction:

- repeating the same scenes without meaningful retained information becomes tedious;
- unclear reset boundaries make consequences feel arbitrary;
- repeated access to unique rewards creates duplication problems;
- protagonists can become effectively omniscient if all loop knowledge is treated as perfect current truth;
- emotional or relationship state is often rewritten without clear provenance.

Ouros response:

If a loop ever becomes canon, define exactly which state domain resets and which state survives. Do not model a loop as `reload whole server`.

No fan-work prose, characters or distinctive plots are copied into the proposals in this pass.

## PTU/Caelo rules boundary

The supplied PTU/Caelo corpus remains the governing mechanical source set for Ouros. During this runtime, the primary uploaded PTU/Caelo PDFs were not reliably retrievable for an exact time-travel rules extraction.

Therefore this pass does not claim any PTU/Caelo mechanic for:

- Celebi time travel;
- Dialga time manipulation;
- Future Sight or delayed attacks;
- temporal portals;
- reversing turns;
- rewinding HP/status state;
- repeating a battle round;
- changing initiative through time manipulation;
- preserving or erasing memories after a timeline change;
- time-loop item duplication;
- aging or de-aging.

Any such mechanic stays unresolved until the authoritative source text and implementation are inspected.

## Design conclusions for Pass 54

Ouros should default to one append-only canonical history.

Real time travel is opt-in canon, not a generic anomaly effect.

When temporal content is enabled, use explicit temporal contexts and state versions rather than destructive retroactive edits.

Recommended default causality policy for generated content:

1. observation of another time does not alter current truth;
2. information carried across time becomes actor knowledge with provenance;
3. physical crossing preserves persistent entity identity;
4. an intervention creates a proposed causal delta;
5. the delta must pass canon/rules/feasibility review;
6. if the project allows divergent history, create a branch/version instead of deleting the source history;
7. public memory and records update only through plausible information flow;
8. unique rewards remain idempotent across loops/branches unless canon explicitly creates a distinct object.
