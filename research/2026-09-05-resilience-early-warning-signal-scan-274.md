## Question

How can Ouros expose possible loss of ecological resilience before a state shift without turning noisy observations into deterministic collapse prophecy?

## New public sources

1. Scheffer et al., “Early-warning signals for critical transitions,” Nature 461 (2009), https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08227. Reusable structure: critical slowing down can produce statistical patterns such as rising lag-1 autocorrelation and variance before some transitions. Limitation: these are warnings, not guaranteed forecasts.
2. “Early warning signs for tipping points in systems with non-Gaussian alpha-stable noise,” Scientific Reports (2025), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-88659-0. Reusable caution: classical variance/autocorrelation indicators can become spurious under some noise structures; inference must be model- and context-aware.
3. Boettiger and Hastings, “Limits to the detection of early warning signals of population collapse,” Nature Precedings (2012), https://www.nature.com/articles/npre.2012.6857.1.pdf. Reusable caution: warning metrics trade true-positive and false-positive performance and cannot be treated as a binary oracle.
4. Pokémon Living World project page, https://www.pokeliving.com/. Reusable high-level pattern only: a persistent world can communicate that ecological consequences continue while the player is absent. Ouros does not import its NPCs, plots, AI claims, or mechanics.

## Transformed Ouros lesson

An early-warning observation should be represented as a scoped evidence object with method, response variable, baseline, semantic windows, comparison quality, uncertainty, and false-positive controls.

Rising variance, autocorrelation, slower recovery, spatial patchiness, or repeated anomalies may increase concern. None is sufficient alone to assert a threshold, future regime transition, population collapse, or species-wide rule.

## Proposed use in Marea/Sendero

A field investigation may notice that site-use timing takes longer to return toward its reference after small disturbances. Repeated matched windows can support `RESILIENCE_LOSS_SIGNAL_SUPPORTED_NARROWLY`. The player receives a warning that the site may be becoming less resilient, not a scripted prediction that it will collapse.

A second site with similar variability but different noise structure acts as a false-positive control. This allows the game to teach uncertainty through play rather than exposition.
