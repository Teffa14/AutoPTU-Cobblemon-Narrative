# Controlled comparison, detectability and causal attribution scan — Pass 271

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE. No canon changes.

## Question

Pass 270 can preserve spatial, temporal and resource-partition patterns without calling them avoidance or competition. The next gap is evidentiary: what must be observed before Ouros may strengthen a claim that a disturbance or intervention caused a behavioral shift?

## New public sources

### Imperfect detection and repeat surveys

USGS, Halstead, Kleeman & Rose, “Time-to-detection occupancy modeling: An efficient method for analyzing the occurrence of amphibians and reptiles.” https://www.usgs.gov/publications/time-detection-occupancy-modeling-efficient-method-analyzing-occurrence-amphibians-and

Reusable lesson: nondetection is not absence. Ecological survey designs commonly need repeated visits, multiple observers, or another explicit detection model because animals can be present but missed.

USGS, Riddle et al., “Effects of prior detections on estimates of detection probability, abundance, and occupancy.” https://www.usgs.gov/publications/effects-prior-detections-estimates-detection-probability-abundance-and-occupancy

Reusable lesson: a prior detection can change the probability of a later detection. Repeated observations by the same informed observer are therefore not automatically independent corroboration.

USGS, Royle, Nichols & Kery, “Modelling occurrence and abundance of species when detection is imperfect.” https://www.usgs.gov/publications/modelling-occurrence-and-abundance-species-when-detection-imperfect

Reusable lesson: raw observed counts can be biased estimates of underlying abundance when detectability varies. Ouros must preserve the observation process separately from population truth.

### Controlled comparison and intervention

Comte et al. 2023, Journal of Applied Ecology, “A Before-After Control-Impact experiment reveals that culling reduces the impacts of invasive deer on endangered peatlands.” https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2664.14498

Reusable lesson: before/after change is interpreted against untreated comparison areas. Effects can differ by place and by response variable; one intervention can reduce some impacts while others persist or move in a different direction.

Fisher 2019, Methods in Ecology and Evolution, “epower: An r package for power analysis of Before-After-Control-Impact (BACI) designs.” https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13287

Reusable lesson: spatial and temporal ecological variability matters. Replicated comparison locations and repeated pre-impact observations improve the ability to distinguish treatment-associated change from background variation.

## Transformation into Ouros

The new design should not duplicate the generic investigation ledger in `investigation-inference-hypothesis-revision-continuity-extension.md` or the observation/evidence pipeline in `observation-evidence-npc-knowledge-contract.md`.

Instead, it supplies an ecology-specific comparison protocol that those systems can reference.

A valid comparison record must preserve:

- the ecological response variable or Pass-270 axis being tested;
- focal and comparator sites/windows;
- before and after windows when an intervention or disturbance is involved;
- observation method and detectability context;
- observer/source lineage;
- relevant environmental covariates and known confounders;
- exact exposure/intervention provenance;
- uncertainty and causal disposition.

Repeated detections can strengthen a descriptive pattern while still failing to establish cause. A before/after difference can strengthen an intervention hypothesis while still failing if comparator validity or detectability changed.
