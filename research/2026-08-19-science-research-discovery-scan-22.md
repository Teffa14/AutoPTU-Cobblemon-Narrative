# Science, Research Institutions & Discovery — Pass 22

Status: research/provenance only. Nothing in this file is Ouros canon.

Date: 2026-08-19

## Why this pass

Ouros already models observation events, knowledge records, field reports, investigations, archaeology, medical cases, breeding institutions, ecological change, public memory and communication networks. What was still missing was a dedicated layer for scientific work itself: how a question becomes a research program, how observations become evidence, how evidence is replicated or challenged, how laboratories and field teams divide work, how discoveries are published or withheld, and how research changes the world without automatically becoming canon truth.

The goal of this pass is to treat science as a playable institutional process rather than as a dialogue box that gives the player a Pokédex reward.

## Source observations

### Pokémon Legends: Arceus — Survey Corps as a research loop

Sources:
- https://legends.arceus.pokemon.com/en-us/story/
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokemon-video-games/pokemon-legends-arceus/

Useful structures:

- Jubilife Village acts as a research base with multiple corps and services rather than one universal professor NPC.
- The Survey Corps specifically studies how Pokémon live.
- Assignments and requests begin at the hub, fieldwork happens in multiple regions, and the player returns to prepare and report before the next excursion.
- Research progress can come from repeated study of a species rather than one encounter.
- Greater research standing can unlock access to wider survey areas.

Reusable design lesson:

A research career can alternate between headquarters, field collection, analysis and reporting. Progress should come from accumulating trustworthy knowledge, not merely from defeating Pokémon.

### New Pokémon Snap — observation quality and rare behavior

Source:
- https://newpokemonsnap.pokemon.com/en-us/create-photodex/

Useful structures:

- Professor Mirror evaluates field observations after expeditions.
- The Photodex records different behavior categories rather than treating one image as complete species knowledge.
- Repeated expeditions reveal less common behavior.
- L.E.N.S. functions as a named research institution with an ecological specialization and assistants.

Reusable design lesson:

Research targets can ask for different conditions, contexts or behaviors. A dataset can be incomplete even when the species is already known. This supports research jobs such as observing feeding, migration, nesting, weather response or interspecies interaction without forcing capture or combat.

### Pokémon Legends: Z-A — research responding to urban change

Source:
- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/story-world/characters/mable

Useful structures:

- The Pokémon Research Lab reacts to a real change in the city: a sudden increase in Pokémon numbers during redevelopment.
- The acting director needs player assistance because the research workload exceeds institutional capacity.
- Research tasks are embedded in a living urban problem rather than a disconnected encyclopedia checklist.

Reusable design lesson:

Research opportunities should often originate from anomalies in existing world state: population changes, new behavior, infrastructure projects, altered routes, environmental disturbance or unexplained patterns.

### Professor Willow and Meltan — distributed field research

Source:
- https://pokemonletsgo.pokemon.com/en-ca/new-pokemon/

Useful structures:

- Willow specializes in fieldwork and habitat/distribution research.
- Reports from many players contribute to a larger investigation.
- Research can include collaboration between professors in different regions.
- An unusual observation first appears as a report, then becomes a sustained investigation.

Reusable design lesson:

Multiplayer research should permit distributed contributions. Ten weakly sourced reports should not necessarily equal one strong result; the system should preserve provenance, duplication and contradictory data.

### Official Pokémon ecology reference — Pokécology

Source:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/preorder-the-upcoming-book-pokecology-an-illustrated-guide-to-pokemon-ecology

Useful structures:

- The official product frames Pokémon knowledge in terms of ecological relationships, similarities, differences and natural interactions.
- It explicitly emphasizes Pokémon living and interacting within environments rather than only battle statistics.

Reusable design lesson:

Ouros research should have room for ecological relationships, behavior, habitat and interaction networks. A research system should not collapse every question into species identification.

### Pokémon professor laboratories as persistent institutions

Sources:
- https://pokemonletsgo.pokemon.com/en-us/kanto-region/
- https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-us/people-galar-region/professor-assistant-sonia/

Useful structures:

- Professor Oak's laboratory is a stable place that gives the Pokédex a social and institutional context.
- Sonia is a researcher and assistant rather than merely a battle mentor.
- Research careers can involve assistants, mentors and succession rather than one immortal professor archetype.

Reusable design lesson:

Research institutions need staff roles, specialties, mentorship and continuity. A laboratory should be capable of surviving staff turnover and developing apprentices into independent investigators.

## Scientific discovery and game-design research

### Inquiry-based games

Source:
- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00607/full

The reviewed examples describe inquiry play as identifying problems, collecting/evaluating data and drawing conclusions rather than simply choosing the correct answer from a menu.

Reusable principle:

Research gameplay should expose uncertainty and allow players to decide what evidence to collect. The game should not reveal the intended hypothesis before fieldwork unless the assignment already supplies one.

### Experimental Design Challenge

Source:
- https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/jmbe.v16i1.817

The activity emphasizes testable hypotheses, variables/controls, evidence-based interpretation and follow-up questions, with peer feedback between teams.

Reusable principle:

A research plan can have explicit assumptions, target variables and comparison groups. Review can improve or reject a method without declaring the researcher's entire career a failure.

### Citizen-science quality control

Source:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6763123/

The CrowdWater work shows that repeated crowd classifications can identify questionable observations and improve quality control.

Reusable principle:

Multiplayer research needs a distinction between raw contribution count and confidence. Independent replication, agreement, instrument quality and expert review can raise confidence; duplicated observations from one source should not be treated as independent evidence.

### Scientific discovery games and collaborative roles

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01653

The study reports that expert-role collaboration improved participants' experience of learning in a scientific discovery game.

Reusable principle:

A large Ouros investigation can support separate roles such as field observer, sample custodian, navigator, analyst, equipment specialist and reviewer. Those roles are narrative/operational until mapped to validated PTU mechanics.

## Patterns selected for Ouros design

1. A scientific claim is not world truth merely because a professor says it.
2. Observation, sample, dataset, hypothesis, analysis, conclusion and publication remain separate objects.
3. Research should originate from real knowledge gaps, anomalies, contradictions or institutional goals.
4. Repeated independent observations can strengthen confidence; duplicated or correlated observations should not falsely inflate it.
5. Negative or null results are valid outputs and can prevent wasted future work.
6. Field teams and laboratories can have different responsibilities and resource constraints.
7. Research institutions need specialties, staff, equipment, archives, funding/mandates and review processes.
8. Player research can create useful knowledge without requiring capture or combat.
9. A discovery may change route guidance, conservation policy, public information, services, faction behavior or future quests without changing PTU rules.
10. Scientific disagreement should usually concern evidence, method or interpretation rather than automatically creating a villain.
11. Research ethics and stewardship must constrain what data or specimens can be collected.
12. Multiplayer science should preserve authorship and provenance instead of merging every player's knowledge into omniscient truth.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

The supplied PTU/Caelo corpus remains authoritative for Skills, Features, equipment, Pokémon capabilities, capture, crafting, medicine, fossils and any mechanical research actions. Public PTU wiki/community pages are discovery aids only.

This pass does not define:
- Researcher/Scientist Feature effects;
- Pokémon Education or Technology Education DCs;
- specimen collection rules;
- sample yields;
- fossil restoration rules;
- research bonuses;
- Pokédex XP or progression;
- instrument modifiers;
- laboratory crafting;
- capture requirements;
- any Move, Ability, Item or Trainer Feature interaction.

The existing Caelo/PTU design boundary also remains: Skill Checks determine uncertain actions under governing rules; narrative research state cannot fabricate success, damage, capture, healing or progression.

## Copyright boundary

All external material is retained as attribution and high-level design reference. Ouros must not copy source characters, distinctive laboratories, custom Pokémon forms, exact missions, dialogue, plot twists, puzzle layouts, faction identities or authored prose.
