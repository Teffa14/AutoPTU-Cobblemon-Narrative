# Archives, Museums, Collections & Preservation Research — Pass 36

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon.
Date: 2026-08-19

## Scope

This pass fills a gap left by earlier work on public memory, archaeology, science, media, material provenance and language: institutions that deliberately preserve, catalogue, interpret, lend, display and restrict access to knowledge or physical collections.

The useful design problem is not simply "add museums." It is to distinguish an object's physical identity, provenance, custody, catalog record, interpretation, display status, public story and access policy.

## Sources and reusable structures

### 1. Nacrene Museum — collections can be wrong, active and institutionally entangled

Official Pokémon animation places the Nacrene Gym inside a museum and builds an investigation around an artifact that staff believed was a replica but was actually connected to a living Yamask. The museum is simultaneously a public exhibition venue, research/curation space and Gym institution.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/14/episode-14-a-night-in-the-nacrene-city-museum

Reusable structures:
- catalog identification can be wrong without the object itself being fake;
- exhibit preparation can expose hidden provenance problems;
- an institution can host multiple functions without collapsing them into one authority;
- a collection item may have an interested living Pokémon or community rather than being inert loot;
- correction of a catalog record can itself become meaningful world state.

Do not copy the Yamask plot, characters or exhibit.

### 2. Pewter Museum — museums can be progression-neutral knowledge hubs

The official Kanto guide describes Pewter Museum of Science as a city institution with Fossils on display. Modern Pokémon Fossil Museum material also explicitly frames exhibition design around comparison, curiosity and learning rather than passive object display.

Sources:
https://pokemonletsgo.pokemon.com/en-us/kanto-region/
https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/dig-into-the-pokemon-fossil-museum-exhibition-at-chicagos-field-museum

Reusable structures:
- exhibits can teach players how to compare evidence rather than state one answer;
- a museum can make discoveries legible to the public after a research arc;
- curatorial choices can connect fossil provenance, ecology, history and scientific uncertainty;
- visiting an exhibit can unlock conversation topics or research questions without granting mechanical progression.

### 3. Canalave Library — curated texts can preserve myth without making myth canon

Canalave Library preserves written Sinnoh myths and acts as a meeting point for researchers and protagonists. The important structural lesson is that an institution may preserve claims that are culturally central without those claims becoming automatically verified world truth.

Reference:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Canalave_Library

Reusable structures:
- library holdings can preserve versions of myths, translations and superseded scholarship;
- reading access can create knowledge state rather than direct quest completion;
- texts can be compared by edition, provenance, authorship and date;
- a library can be a safe research hub that points outward toward field investigation.

### 4. Nacrene Gym/library puzzle — knowledge spaces can be navigational puzzles

The Nacrene Gym uses books, memos and cross-references as a route through the institution. This shows that archival navigation can be gameplay when the challenge is following references rather than guessing an arbitrary password.

Reference:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nacrene_Gym

Reusable structures:
- finding aids, shelf references and catalog links can form recoverable puzzles;
- a puzzle should expose enough structure for players to understand why the next source matters;
- archival puzzle state should persist so solved cross-references stay solved;
- access to a document and interpretation of that document are separate steps.

### 5. Pokémon Reddit Roleplay museum quests — institution work can generate varied jobs

A public roleplay quest list uses museum work for exhibit setup and fossil transport rather than only archaeology or battles.

Source:
https://pokemon-reddit-roleplay.fandom.com/wiki/Kanto_Region_Quests

Reusable structures:
- collection institutions generate logistics, installation, conservation, transport, outreach and security jobs;
- museum quests can connect to infrastructure and media rather than always dungeon crawling;
- collection intake can produce custody/provenance state before public display.

Treat this only as community design evidence; do not copy exact quests or rewards.

### 6. Pokémon and the Great Library — knowledge institutions can be entire adventure spaces

This Eevee Expo fangame centers a short adventure on an ancient library and explicitly foregrounds books and interactive storytelling.

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/6193/

Reusable structures:
- a library can contain layered spaces, restricted stacks, damaged records and hidden cross-links;
- reading-heavy content needs indexing, summaries and recoverable navigation to avoid becoming text dumping;
- different players can pursue different research questions within the same location.

Do not copy its plot, characters, text or library.

### 7. Public RP archives — the archive itself can be living infrastructure

A public Pokémon RP forum explicitly separates active storyline events from an archive/timeline that records prior events and influences future updates.

Source:
https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/pokemon_roleplay/

Reusable structures:
- archived events can be an authoritative reference layer distinct from current rumors;
- old events should remain searchable by location, actor and consequence;
- future content can cite prior event IDs rather than regenerate history from memory;
- archival completeness can vary by institution.

### 8. PTU Mysterious Ruins seed — librarians can be participants, not vending machines

The official PTU campaign seed Oran Valley includes a town librarian becoming entangled in a supernatural ruin problem. The structural lesson is that knowledge workers can have their own exposure, risks and incomplete expertise.

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-mysterious-ruins/

Reusable structures:
- curators/librarians should have motives, specialties, blind spots and personal stakes;
- institutional expertise should reduce uncertainty without solving every mystery;
- archives can become threatened locations without requiring every threat to be a combat encounter.

### 9. Museum design research — curatorial authority can itself be questioned

Research on hybrid museum games identifies useful design tensions around visitor behavior, artifact roles and curatorial authority.

Source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12533

Reusable structures:
- an exhibit can present multiple interpretations instead of one omniscient caption;
- visitors can be asked to compare evidence or confront omissions;
- contested heritage is better represented through provenance and competing claims than through a universal morality meter.

This is a design reference, not a source for Ouros cultures or historical events.

## New design conclusions for Ouros

1. A collection object needs at least four separable layers: physical object, provenance/custody, catalog record and interpretation.
2. "On display" is a temporary institutional state, not the object's identity.
3. A replica can be legitimate, mislabeled, pedagogical, deceptive or mistaken; `replica` and `fraud` are not synonyms.
4. Libraries should store editions and claims, not inject their content directly into canonical truth.
5. An institution can restrict access for preservation, privacy, active investigation, cultural stewardship or safety; the generator must record the reason rather than invent generic secrecy.
6. Loans create temporary custody without changing ownership.
7. Deaccession, repatriation/return, destruction, restoration and loss require provenance-preserving events.
8. Exhibits can change public memory without changing historical fact.
9. Collection backlogs, damaged catalogs, missing provenance and obsolete classifications are strong quest generators because they connect fieldwork, cases, science and public history.
10. Minecraft should expose visible institutional state—closed gallery, new exhibit, returned object, renovation, restricted stacks—without attempting to simulate every shelf item.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

This pass does not define new PTU mechanics.

The governing PTU/Caelo source set remains authoritative for Skill Checks, Features, Pokémon capabilities, items, fossils, rewards, encounters and any mechanical interaction with artifacts. Earlier Caelo evidence already establishes distinct Social, Encounter, Job, Raid, Contest, Gym and Dojo activity containers; archive/museum content can feed those containers without replacing their rules.

Potential mechanics needing dedicated validation before executable use:
- General Education / Pokémon Education / Occult Education checks;
- Chronicler or other knowledge-related Features;
- item identification or restoration;
- fossil handling/revival;
- supernatural artifact effects;
- security or stealth checks;
- movement/hazard effects in damaged archives;
- Trainer Feature interrupts.

## Copyright / transformation guardrail

Do not transplant museum layouts, named exhibits, unique artifacts, library texts, dialogue, puzzles or characters from sources. Preserve only abstract structures such as catalog uncertainty, loans, exhibition lifecycle, archival cross-reference puzzles and provenance conflicts.
