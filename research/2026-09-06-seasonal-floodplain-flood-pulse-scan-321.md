## Public sources inspected

### U.S. National Park Service — Flood Plains, Theodore Roosevelt National Park
Source: https://www.nps.gov/thro/learn/nature/floodplains.htm

Source facts used at a high level:
- a river can gradually change course within a floodplain;
- periodic overbank flooding is part of the landscape rather than automatically an exceptional failure;
- periodic flooding contributes to habitat formation and supports different plant and animal use.

Reusable pattern for Ouros:
A floodplain can have several legitimate states through time. A route crossing that landscape may be useful during one phase and hydrologically connected during another. Ecological value and infrastructure value can coexist without one invalidating the other.

Excluded:
No Little Missouri geography, species list, park management policy, real-world frequency, hydrology value, or American institution is imported into Ouros.

### U.S. Geological Survey — High-Water Marks
Source: https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/high-water-marks

Source facts used at a high level:
- past floods can leave mud lines, seed lines, debris lines, erosion/scour marks, wash lines, and debris snags;
- different marks have different reliability;
- marks record a past maximum or flood extent, not necessarily current water level;
- investigators corroborate evidence rather than trusting every apparent line equally.

Reusable pattern for Ouros:
Environmental evidence should carry provenance and confidence. A mark on a pier can support the claim that water once reached a height while saying nothing by itself about whether the crossing is flooded now, how quickly water moved, why it flooded, or whether the route should remain closed.

Excluded:
No USGS measurement procedure, uncertainty value, flood model, gauge specification, or regulatory standard becomes an Ouros mechanic.

### U.S. Geological Survey — High-Water Marks and Flooding
Source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/high-water-marks-and-flooding

Source facts used at a high level:
USGS examples explicitly distinguish stronger and weaker candidate indicators. Physical marks must be interpreted in context because movement of branches or material can make some apparent marks unreliable.

Reusable pattern for Ouros:
Two field observers can report different conclusions from the same site without either being irrational. The investigation can reward cross-checking multiple traces, timing, substrate, fixed structures, and independent records.

## Transformed Ouros design lessons

1. Historical floodplain footprint, current inundation, route passability, tactical hazard, and ecological value must remain separate facts.
2. A high-water mark is evidence of a past state. It is not a current-state sensor.
3. Evidence needs a source, semantic time, observed feature, and confidence/qualification when relevant.
4. Floodplain scenes become more legible when players can compare fixed reference points: bridge piers, culvert mouths, route markers, gauge posts, old survey monuments, or protected high ground.
5. Conflicting reports can both be correct when they refer to different feature IDs or different times.
6. Flood pulses can create opportunities as well as hazards: temporary habitat connection, new sediment, stranded debris, changed access, reopened channels, or a route becoming briefly unusable.
7. Institutional decisions should be feature-scoped. Reopening one crossing does not reopen every path in the floodplain.
8. Pokémon ecology must be species-specific and canon-approved. A Water type is not automatically a flood expert.
9. Minecraft water presentation cannot create current, drowning, forced movement, LoS attenuation, damage, or PTU legality.
10. The reduced narrative version should work with scene-authored hydrologic states and ordinary route edges while full hydrodynamic tactics remain gated behind exact engine capabilities.

## Provenance model suggested for later implementation

For each observation preserve at minimum:
- feature_id;
- observation_id;
- semantic_time;
- observer/source;
- observation_type;
- directly observed value;
- confidence or qualification when the evidence type needs it;
- source record or physical-trace reference;
- later interpretation separately.

Suggested observation vocabulary is descriptive rather than mechanical: `HIGH_WATER_MARK_OBSERVED`, `FRESH_SILT_OBSERVED`, `DEBRIS_LINE_OBSERVED`, `CULVERT_BLOCKAGE_OBSERVED`, `SIDE_CHANNEL_ACTIVE_OBSERVED`, `GAUGE_RECORD_RECEIVED`, `CURRENT_INUNDATION_OBSERVED`, `CURRENT_INUNDATION_NOT_OBSERVED`, `PASSABILITY_UNRESOLVED`.

These labels are not PTU statuses and do not imply numeric modifiers.
