# Research scan 180 — mourning, memorials and legacy stewardship

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY
Date: 2026-09-01
Destination: Teffa14/AutoPTU-Cobblemon-Narrative
Canon impact: NONE. This file does not establish any Ouros death, funeral custom, afterlife rule, cemetery, religion, memorial institution or Ghost-type metaphysics.

## Research question

How can Ouros represent loss, remembrance, memorial places, legacy objects and institutional continuity without conflating mechanical fainting with death, private grief with public history, or Ghost-type presence with proof of a specific deceased spirit?

This pass was selected after inspecting the current narrative repository. Existing layers already cover care/recovery, public memory, archives, institutional succession, site aftermath, festivals, local knowledge, claims and communications. The missing seam is the transition from a verified permanent loss or historical death into custodial records, private remembrance, public memorialization, inherited responsibilities and later reinterpretation.

## Existing Ouros constraints found before research

1. Care/recovery already distinguishes mechanical health state, observed condition, diagnosis, treatment and narrative recovery. A memorial layer must not duplicate medical authority.
2. Public-memory systems already own what a community remembers and how accounts change. A memorial layer should contribute memorial records and practices to that system instead of becoming a second truth ledger.
3. Archives and collections already own provenance, custody and preservation of records/objects. Legacy objects should reference that custody model.
4. Institutional succession already separates a stable role from the person currently holding it. Loss of an office-holder may create a vacancy only through an authorized world-state transition, never from a Minecraft despawn.
5. Site aftermath already supports retained traces, repurposing and physical continuity. Memorial markers should project into that layer rather than owning blocks directly.
6. Marea canon establishes named residents and companion Pokémon but establishes no current death, cemetery, funeral custom or religion. Nothing in this pass changes that.

## Public source scan

### Pokémon Tower / Lavender Town

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lavender

High-level structures extracted:

- A memorial place can be part of ordinary town life rather than an isolated supernatural dungeon.
- People visit repeatedly to pay respects; remembrance has routine social presence.
- A location can contain graves, caretakers, visitors and wild Pokémon at the same time without those elements being equivalent kinds of evidence.
- A violent historical incident can become part of how a site is understood, but one famous story should not define every burial at the site.
- The player can encounter uncertainty about what is actually present; presentation and interpretation need separate authority.

Ouros transformation:

A memorial location should have custodians, opening/visitation behavior, records, upkeep and ordinary visitors. If supernatural claims exist, they remain claims unless canon and mechanics establish more.

Do not copy:

- Marowak/Cubone incident;
- Team Rocket plot;
- Silph Scope device;
- seven-floor tower structure;
- specific dialogue or ghost-identification sequence.

### House of Memories / memorial relocation after redevelopment

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/House_of_Memories

High-level structures extracted:

- Urban redevelopment can relocate a memorial institution while preserving its social function.
- Relocation creates custody questions: what moved, who recorded it, what stayed inaccessible, what changed in visitor practice.
- A quieter memorial can exist with no battles or wild encounters at all.

Ouros transformation:

If Ouros later renovates a district, moves a route marker, decommissions a facility or relocates remains/records, provenance must survive the move. A memorial place can produce compelling continuity without combat.

### Celestial Tower

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celestial_Tower

High-level structures extracted:

- A repeated ritual action can create player participation in remembrance without requiring exposition.
- Important public figures can be encountered during private remembrance, revealing that office/status does not erase personal history.
- A memorial journey can be adjacent to care and travel without becoming a reward gate that says grief is solved.

Ouros transformation:

Use simple repeatable acts—leaving a record, lighting a permitted lamp, adding a name to a ledger, walking a known route, ringing an approved signal—only if Ouros canon later establishes them. Participation records the act; it does not automatically change relationship values or emotional state.

Do not copy the bell, tower layout or named characters as Ouros content.

### Memorial Hill and regional visitation

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Memorial_Hill

## Project source cross-check

### Fainted is an engine/rules state, not proof of death

Read-only AutoPTU contains many rules and runtime branches that treat `fainted` as a battle state. For example, battle AI and phase logic exclude fainted combatants from ordinary actions; Moves such as Lunar Dance explicitly cause the user to Faint while interacting with HP/Injury rules.

Narrative rule derived from this evidence:

`FAINTED != DECEASED`

No quest, NPC dialogue, memorial object or Minecraft entity state may promote `fainted=true`, `hp=0`, entity unload or battle removal into a canonical death fact.

A permanent death, if Ouros permits it, requires a separate canon/world-state authority with explicit provenance.

## Reusable structures for Ouros

### 1. Private grief and public memorial are separate records

A person may privately remember someone who has no public monument.
A community may maintain a public monument whose meaning differs among residents.
A public ceremony does not reveal every private relationship.

### 2. Memorialization is a decision, not an automatic consequence

After a verified loss, possible responses include:

- private keepsake;
- archive record;
- marker at a worksite;
- grave or burial site if canon permits;
- community service named after someone;
- preserved equipment;
- scholarship/apprenticeship tradition;
- no public memorial at all.

The narrative generator must not choose among these without authored social context.

### 3. Custody of legacy objects matters

A deceased or retired actor may leave objects with different authority classes:

- personal keepsake;
- institutional equipment;
- Pokémon-held item with mechanical state;
- archive deposit;
- evidence in an unresolved case;
- loaned object;
- public memorial object;
- contested property.

Transfer requires provenance and an authorized custodian. Emotional significance does not equal legal/institutional ownership.

### 4. Companion Pokémon require their own continuity

If a Trainer dies or permanently leaves, a companion Pokémon does not become loot, wild by default, inherited automatically or mechanically reassigned.

Possible future states must be authored separately:

- remains with an institution by prior arrangement;
- chooses/accepts another caretaker if the setting supports that choice;
- returns to a family/social group;
- remains independently present;
- enters a care/sanctuary process;
- custody unresolved.

Exact ownership/capture rules require PTU/Caelo validation.

### 5. Offices survive people

A role may become vacant after verified permanent absence/death, but the institutional-role layer decides acting cover and succession. Memorialization cannot appoint the successor.

### 6. Historical names can diverge across records

Memorial inscriptions, archive records, oral accounts, nicknames and service ledgers may disagree. The discrepancy becomes an investigation surface without implying conspiracy.

### 7. Maintenance is narrative continuity

Memorials require cleaning, access, repairs, transcription, visitor guidance, archive correlation and sometimes relocation. This creates ordinary work that can persist across years.

### 8. Remembrance can change with new evidence

A public memorial may be revised after corrected records. Revision can add context, change wording, move an object or append a correction without erasing the history of the earlier interpretation.

## Anti-patterns rejected

- Killing an NPC because a Minecraft actor died, unloaded or despawned.
- Treating 0 HP or Fainted as death.
- Treating every Ghost-type as a dead person/Pokémon.
- Using grief as a reputation-point vending machine.
- Making a funeral scene automatically repair relationships.
- Giving the player ownership of a deceased Trainer's Pokémon.
- Treating a memorial inscription as perfect historical truth.
- Converting a private care record into public memorial text without consent/provenance.
- Turning every memorial location into a combat dungeon.
- Importing one franchise region's funeral custom as universal Pokémon-world law.

## Capability implications

Most remembrance gameplay can be non-tactical and currently avoids battle dependencies.

Mechanically rich memorial-site incidents can touch:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Every concrete encounter must name only the families it actually needs.
