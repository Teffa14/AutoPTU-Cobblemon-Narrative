# Pass 28 — Conservation, protected areas & stewardship research

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Nothing in this document is Ouros canon.

## Why this pass

The repository already has systems for ecological observation, wild collectives, settlement change, civic governance, care, crisis response, science, travel and public memory. What was missing was a dedicated model for stewardship: how people decide that a habitat, migration corridor, nesting site, cultural landscape or wild population needs active care; who is allowed to make decisions; how visitor access changes; how restoration is monitored; and how Pokémon encounters can emerge from management conflicts without assuming capture or combat is always the correct solution.

This pass therefore focuses on protected-area design, coexistence, habitat restoration, wildlife monitoring, access rules, ranger-like roles, biosecurity, relocation/release questions and community stewardship.

## Source 1 — Pokémon Legends: Z-A official Wild Zones

Source: Pokémon Legends: Z-A official gameplay pages.

- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-au/gameplay
- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/news/adventure

High-level pattern:

Lumiose City's redevelopment explicitly includes constructed Wild Zones intended to give wild Pokémon places to live while people and Pokémon share the city. This is a useful precedent for conservation content that exists inside a developed settlement rather than only in remote wilderness.

Reusable design lessons:

- protected or managed habitat can be embedded inside a city;
- conservation can compete with redevelopment, transport, commerce and public access;
- a managed habitat can still contain ordinary catching and battling if local policy permits it;
- the interesting story is not necessarily "humans versus nature" but how both fit into the same changing place;
- habitat quality can become a world-state variable independent of whether a Pokémon species is mechanically strong or rare.

Do not copy Lumiose, Quasartico, Wild Zone layouts or Z-A plot material into Ouros.

## Source 2 — Pokémon Ranger / Shadows of Almia

Sources:

- https://www.pokemon.co.jp/game/ds/ranger2008/
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-video-games/pokemon-ranger-shadows-of-almia

High-level pattern:

Pokémon Rangers are framed around keeping peace between humans and Pokémon, responding to incidents, working through regional institutions and temporarily receiving help from wild Pokémon rather than automatically owning them.

Reusable design lessons:

- ecological stewardship can be a profession and institutional mandate;
- a wild Pokémon helping during an incident does not have to become owned;
- response work can include rescue, investigation, access management and conflict reduction;
- regional ranger bases can create a distributed service network;
- stewardship careers can progress through trust, responsibility and field knowledge rather than only battle strength.

Do not import Capture Styler mechanics or Ranger organization canon into Ouros without explicit design approval.

## Source 3 — Poké Pelago official material

Sources:

- https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/power-up-at-poke-pelago
- https://www.pokemon.co.jp/ex/sun_moon/collect/161004_01.html

High-level pattern:

Poké Pelago distinguishes Pokémon already associated with the player from wild visitors. Wild Pokémon can visit a managed space and may eventually choose to join rather than being forced into an immediate battle/capture loop.

Reusable design lessons:

- managed spaces can attract visitors without converting every visitor into property;
- repeated contact can matter narratively before capture;
- habitat improvements can change who appears;
- coexistence spaces can connect agriculture, rest, exploration and Pokémon presence;
- "visited", "resident", "under care" and "owned" should remain separate states.

Do not import Pelago upgrade math, Poké Bean costs or joining formulas.

## Source 4 — Pokécharms marine-conservation RP

Source:

- https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/underwater-a-pokemon-scuba-diving-rp.13479/

High-level pattern:

A public Pokémon RP framed a protected marine area around reef ecology, research, rescue/rehabilitation, invasive-species concerns, restrictions on ordinary battling/catching and a seasonal ecological event.

Reusable design lessons:

- a protected area becomes more interesting when it contains different use zones and rules;
- visitor access, research, rescue and ordinary Trainer behavior can coexist under different conditions;
- conservation episodes can center on observation and habitat context rather than defeating an antagonist;
- seasonal biological events can create temporary access or behavior policies;
- restrictions become story-relevant when players understand what ecological state they are protecting.

Copyright boundary: retain only these abstract patterns. Do not copy its named location, staff, exact event or invented equipment.

## Source 5 — Pokécharms Ranger RP

Sources:

- https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/pokemon-ranger-almia-goes-old-school-discussion-thread.19702/
- https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/pokemon-ranger-almia-goes-old-school-rp-thread.19789/

High-level pattern:

The RP places rangers between wild Pokémon and human pressures such as lumbering, prospecting, trading, smuggling and poaching. Its discussion explicitly considers multiple ways to help wild Pokémon during emergencies instead of requiring a single scripted solution.

Reusable design lessons:

- stewardship conflicts often involve several legitimate or semi-legitimate land uses at once;
- ranger work can connect rescue, negotiation, fieldcraft and enforcement-like responsibilities without making every ranger a police officer;
- open problems should permit multiple solutions when world state supports them;
- ecological conflict becomes richer when wild Pokémon have their own behavior and do not exist only as objectives.

Do not import the RP's characters, historical setting or plot.

## Source 6 — Real-world Indigenous ranger and protected-area management

Sources:

- https://www.niaa.gov.au/our-work/environment-and-land/indigenous-rangers-program-irp
- https://www.niaa.gov.au/our-work/environment-and-land/indigenous-protected-areas-ipa
- https://parksaustralia.gov.au/about/about-us/
- https://www.niaa.gov.au/sites/default/files/reports/closing-the-gap-2018/dhimurru-aboriginal-corporations-rangers-working-both-ways.html

These are not Pokémon sources and must not be copied into fictional cultures. They are useful only as design evidence that conservation management can combine ecological monitoring, cultural heritage, fire/biosecurity work, visitor management, research partnerships, local employment, training and governance by people with long-standing relationships to the place.

Reusable design lessons:

- protected-area management is broader than patrols;
- local stewardship knowledge and scientific monitoring can coexist without one erasing the other;
- management authority and knowledge authority are different;
- conservation areas can support sustainable public use instead of being permanently sealed;
- visitor management, heritage protection, ecological monitoring, training and employment can all be part of the same institution;
- whole-landscape corridors matter because isolated reserves do not contain every ecological process.

Ouros must invent its own cultures, governance, terminology and institutions. Do not map real First Nations peoples or practices onto fictional Pokémon cultures as aesthetic material.

Source:

High-level pattern:

A dangerous wild landscape supports multiple player motives without forcing every participant into the same Gym-oriented arc.

Reusable design lessons:

## PTU / Caelo grounding

### PTU campaign guidance

The supplied PTU Core Rulebook emphasizes both sensible ecosystems and meaningful player choices. It also recommends weaving ordinary Trainer activities into larger stories instead of treating catching and Gyms as disconnected repetition. Conservation content fits that pattern when ecological facts create choices rather than serving as decorative exposition.

### Caelo encounter/access structure

The supplied Caelo Player's Guide distinguishes Social, Wild Encounter, PvP, Job, Raid, Contest, Gym and Dojo activities and explicitly allows locations to require particular items or levels for access. That provides a precedent for access requirements existing as world state. It does not automatically authorize Ouros to invent legal restrictions, permits or capture bans.

Caelo's Wild Pokémon encounters are mechanically defined as Risky battles and use explicit capture rules. Any conservation scenario involving capture must therefore call the actual governing capture mechanics rather than changing capture probability or Poké Ball legality in narrative code.

### PTU capabilities

Capabilities such as Naturewalk, Groundshaper, Pack Mon and other movement/sensory abilities can affect how a Pokémon interacts with an environment. Narrative stewardship must never grant those capabilities because a species "looks suited" to a habitat. Use the individual Pokémon's authoritative state.

## Design synthesis

The useful gap for Ouros is not "national parks" as a real-world copy. It is a generic stewardship layer that can represent:

- protected habitat;
- managed-use habitat;
- migration corridors;
- nesting/roost protection;
- restoration sites;
- community-managed areas;
- research reserves;
- urban coexistence zones;
- temporary closures;
- visitor rules;
- monitoring programs;
- release/relocation projects;
- biosecurity incidents;
- stewardship institutions and local custodianship claims.

The layer must keep ecological truth, management policy, public belief, visitor behavior and mechanical capture state separate.

## Important anti-patterns

Avoid:

- assuming every rare Pokémon needs a capture-ban plot;
- treating conservation workers as generic police;
- making local residents ignorant obstacles to outside scientists;
- calling a species invasive merely because it is new to the encounter table;
- using "protected" as a magic flag that prevents PTU actions without authored policy and implementation;
- making relocation automatically beneficial;
- turning rehabilitation into ownership;
- treating a Legendary sighting as justification for a boss encounter;
- simulating every wild individual permanently;
- adding hidden spawn bonuses that players can exploit without ecological cause.
