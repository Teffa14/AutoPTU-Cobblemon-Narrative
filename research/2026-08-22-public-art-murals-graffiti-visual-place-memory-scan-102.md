# Public Art, Murals, Graffiti & Visual Place-Memory Research — Pass 102

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon. External stories and communities are inspiration sources, not rules sources.

## Why this pass exists

Before researching, the current branch inventory and overlapping systems were reviewed. Ouros already has dedicated layers for material culture, fashion/visual presentation, architecture, urban public space, public memory, photography/visual evidence, museums/archives, media, civic governance, festivals, infiltration, wild territory and environmental markings. No existing layer owns persistent public artwork itself: a mural, painted wall, sculpture, community installation, unsanctioned mark, sanctioned street-art project, restoration, overpaint, attribution dispute or visually significant Pokémon-made mark can appear in those systems, but its own revision history and meaning are not preserved by one authority.

Pass 102 therefore focuses on visual marks attached to shared places and surfaces. It deliberately avoids replacing Fashion, Architecture, Museums, Public Space, Public Memory, Cases, Material Culture or Wild Collective state.

## New-source scan

### 1. Grafaiai — official Pokémon Pokédex

Source: Pokémon.com, Grafaiai Pokédex.

https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/grafaiai

Useful high-level pattern:

- individual Grafaiai repeatedly create distinctive patterns;
- the mark is tied to an individual rather than a generic species-wide symbol;
- the substance/color can vary with diet;
- marks occur on physical environmental surfaces.

Ouros adaptation:

A recurring visual mark can be evidence of an individual Pokémon's activity without becoming proof of ownership, authorship of every similar mark, a combat effect or a universal territorial rule. A `visual_mark_observation` can propose an individual match with confidence and corroboration, similar to photography/field-sign identity hypotheses.

Mechanical guardrail:

Grafaiai's poisonous saliva does not mean every painted surface applies Poisoned, Poison damage, a hazard zone or contact damage. Poison Touch is an Ability with its own rules; a visual mark remains overworld state unless exact PTU/Caelo mechanics and Java behavior authorize an effect.

### 2. Smeargle — official Pokémon Pokédex

Source: Pokémon.com, Smeargle Pokédex.

https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/smeargle

Useful high-level pattern:

- Smeargle produces a persistent individual pigment characteristic;
- adulthood is associated with marks left by other members of the species;
- the species supports visual identity, social marking and long-lived physical evidence.

Ouros adaptation:

Public or ecological marks can accumulate authorship history. A wall, signboard or rock face might contain authored human work, commissioned Pokémon-assisted work, old Pokémon markings and later repairs at the same time. These layers should remain distinct rather than being collapsed into one current texture.

Mechanical guardrail:

Smeargle's visual behavior does not imply free use of the Move Sketch, a painting Skill, a copying mechanic or the ability to reproduce another Move through overworld art. `Sketch` remains a move-specific mechanical question.

### 6. Graffiti as a palimpsest

Source: Mari Myllylä, “Graffiti as a Palimpsest,” Street Art and Urban Creativity, 2018.

https://www.journals.wisethorough.com/index.php/sauc/article/view/141

Reusable design lesson:

Marks in shared space can be layered over time. Interpretation depends on physical layers, viewer knowledge and social/historical context. A later mark does not erase the fact that an earlier mark existed.

Ouros adaptation:

A wall should support `visual_surface_revision` and `mark_layer` history. Overpainting, repair, weathering, removal and restoration are append-only events in Chronicle. A current Minecraft block texture is the projection of the latest revision, not the full historical record.

### 7. Public art, placemaking and urban renewal

Source: Tony Matthews & Sophie Gadaloff, “Public art for placemaking and urban renewal: Insights from three regional Australian cities,” Cities, 2022.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S026427512200186X

Reusable design lessons:

- public art can serve intrinsic cultural purposes and instrumental placemaking purposes;
- different actors may value the same work for community identity, tourism, renewal, memory or economic reasons;
- governance, maintenance and public participation matter;
- art is not a universal cure for weak urban design or community problems.

Ouros adaptation:

A commissioned mural may simultaneously be an artwork, landmark, tourism driver, grant project and contested public-space intervention. Funding does not define meaning; popular approval does not define artistic merit; increased visitors do not prove community benefit.

### 8. Murals as community narrative infrastructure

Source: Nursanty et al., “From Murals to Meaning: Community Identity and Urban Revitalization in Indonesian Kampungs,” 2025.

https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/1544/1/012014

Reusable design lesson:

Participatory murals can preserve intergenerational memory and support place attachment when residents actively shape their content and stewardship.

Ouros adaptation:

Community-authored work should retain contributor provenance, edition history and disagreements. The system should not flatten a collective work into `artist_id = one famous NPC` unless that is actually documented.

Cultural-use guardrail:

This source is used only to understand participatory public-art structures. Real communities, imagery, political histories and local traditions must not be reskinned into Ouros cultures.

### 9. Public art on main streets — impact and evaluation

Source: Journal of Place Management and Development, “Developing a framework for assessing the impact of public art for main streets,” published June 22, 2026.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1753833526000011

Reusable design lesson:

Stakeholders often need evidence about social, cultural and economic effects rather than assuming an installation worked because it was completed.

Ouros adaptation:

Public-art projects can have baseline, observation and review phases. A finished mural does not automatically increase tourism, reputation, cohesion or local revenue. Those consequences must arise from actual world-state changes and observations.

### 10. Environmental storytelling through visual marks

Source: Unity/Experimental Game Cultures level-design material, section on environmental storytelling.

https://wiki.experimentalgamecultures.uni-ak.ac.at/images/7/74/Ebook_Introduction-to-game-level-design_small.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

Visual marks can prompt players to infer prior events without an exposition NPC. The useful technique is to place evidence that supports questions, not to encode one guaranteed interpretation in scenery.

Ouros adaptation:

A faded mural, repaired sign, overwritten tag, unfinished painting or line of Pokémon marks can be a clue. The visual should point toward existing history, actor motives or unresolved evidence rather than fabricate lore at render time.

## Design synthesis

Pass 102 should add a visual-place-memory layer with these separations:

1. physical surface;
2. artwork/mark instance;
3. revision/layer history;
4. creator or creator hypothesis;
5. authorization/commission state;
6. material provenance;
7. depicted claim or subject;
8. interpretation by an actor;
9. public recognition;
10. conservation/restoration state;
11. current Minecraft projection;
12. mechanical PTU effect, normally none.

A depicted event is not evidence that the event occurred. A mural of a Legendary is not a Legendary sighting. A signature is not automatically authentic. A Grafaiai-like pattern is not proof of a specific Grafaiai until corroborated. A painted warning is not legal authority. A restored image is not the original physical layer.

## Reusable narrative patterns

### Layered-wall mystery

Several visually incompatible marks occupy the same wall. Each belongs to a different period. The investigation is about sequencing, context and provenance rather than identifying one “true picture.”

### Commission vs community meaning

An institution commissions a work for one reason while residents adopt it for another. Later tourism or media attention creates a third interpretation.

### Restoration as a choice

A damaged mural can be stabilized, repainted, partially exposed or documented and allowed to age. Each choice preserves different information and relationships.

### Pokémon mark as longitudinal evidence

A recurring distinctive pattern appears over years. Researchers can compare location, material and form to test whether the same individual or lineage of behavior is involved without forcing an identity conclusion.

### Public-art boom with second-order effects

A successful artwork changes visitor flow, business demand, noise, waste or neighborhood pressure. Those effects should hand off to Tourism, Public Space, Finance, Waste, Light and Demography rather than being represented by a single reputation bonus.

### Visual misinformation without forgery

An authentic old mural can still depict a myth, propaganda, misunderstanding or stylized memory. Authenticity and factual truth remain separate.

## PTU / Caelo / engine boundary notes

No public-art mechanic is assumed from Pokémon flavor.

The layer must not invent:

- painting or graffiti Skill DCs;
- `Sketch` behavior;
- poison exposure from Grafaiai markings;
- Invisibility removal from paint;
- wall cover/terrain because a mural exists;
- morale/reputation modifiers from viewing art;
- vandalism damage formulas;
- object HP for murals or sculptures;
- art-based Contest bonuses;
- Smeargle/Grafaiai production rates;
- crowd or tourism bonuses;
- supernatural effects from depicted symbols.

Mechanically rich encounters must list their exact permanent capability dependencies and provide a reduced version where possible.

## Copyright / transformation policy

No protected prose, dialogue, characters, plots, artwork, mural imagery or distinctive compositions from external sources are copied. Pokémon sources establish broad franchise precedents. Community/fangame sources contribute abstract structural lessons only. Original Ouros candidates must use new names, places, motives, visual subjects and consequences.
