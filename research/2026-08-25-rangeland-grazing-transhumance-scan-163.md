Official Pokédex material describes Gogoat as living in herds in mountainous places and notes a long history of assisting people with work. This supports authored histories involving pastoral work, but does not establish Mountable, carrying capacity, obedience, Pack Mon or a universal grazing role in PTU. Source: Pokémon Pokédex, Gogoat, https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/gogoat

Sources: Pokémon Pokédex, Wooloo, https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/wooloo ; Pokémon Sword/Shield official Wooloo page, https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-us/pokemon-galar-region/wooloo/

That supports cultural precedent for human–Pokémon mobility, but not a rules inference about PTU Mountable, willingness, passenger safety or terrain immunity. Source: Pokémon Pokédex, Skiddo, https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/skiddo

USDA NRCS Prescribed Grazing material treats grazing management as adaptive rather than binary. Monitoring can drive changes to intensity, frequency, timing and duration, with plant, soil, animal, wildlife and water objectives tracked separately. Deduction for Pass 79: preserve episode/review evidence instead of collapsing management to a single health score. Source: USDA NRCS FY2025 Prescribed Grazing scenario descriptions.

FAO material on transhumant systems describes regular seasonal movement between grazing areas, with timing influenced by forage, water, climate and local conditions. It documents intermediate camps, route variation and seasonal pasture differences. Deduction: an actual managed seasonal journey needs an event record distinct from both Pass 79's long-term `HERD_ROUTE_REVISION` and Pass 124's wild-migration episodes. Sources: FAO, Transhumant grazing systems in temperate Asia; FAO, Pastoralism in the new millennium.

FAO grassland guidance notes that waterpoints can alter which parts of a range are used and may concentrate use around permanent water. Deduction: waterpoint-use observations should inform distribution review without automatically proving degradation, causation or welfare state. Source: FAO, How to management grassland and pasture areas.

## Reusable narrative structures

A seasonal managed route can cross several authorities: a valid use relationship may exist while a bridge is closed, a waterpoint is unavailable, a wild migration overlaps the route or a festival occupies an old passage.

A visibly short pasture can have several histories: recent grazing, drought, mowing, wildfire, soil change, dormancy or incomplete observation. Preserve competing explanations.

Institutional success can reduce quests. After several seasons, better monitoring, alternate waterpoints and established reroutes can make routine shortfalls self-resolving.

A managed group can split, pause or refuse a route. Individual Pokémon retain agency and persistent identity; management never becomes ownership truth by implication.
