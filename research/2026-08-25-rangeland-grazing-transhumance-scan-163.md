### Rangeland and grazing systems

USDA NRCS Prescribed Grazing material treats grazing management as adaptive rather than binary. Monitoring can drive changes to intensity, frequency, timing and duration, with plant, soil, animal, wildlife and water objectives tracked separately. Design deduction: Ouros should store a management episode and its evidence rather than a single `pasture_health` score. Source: USDA NRCS FY2025 Prescribed Grazing scenario descriptions.

FAO material on transhumant systems describes regular seasonal movement between grazing areas, with timing influenced by forage, water, climate and other local conditions. It also documents intermediate camps, route variation, seasonal pasture differences and changes in movement timing. Design deduction: a managed seasonal route should be a versioned institutional/ecological process, not a copy of the Wild Migration system. Sources: FAO, Transhumant grazing systems in temperate Asia; FAO, Pastoralism in the new millennium.

FAO grassland guidance notes that waterpoints can alter which parts of a range are used and may concentrate use around permanent water. Design deduction: waterpoint placement may change distribution without proving degradation, causation or animal welfare state. Source: FAO, How to management grassland and pasture areas.

## Reusable narrative structures

A seasonal managed route can cross several authorities: a valid grazing-use relationship may exist while a bridge is closed, a waterpoint is unavailable, a wild migration corridor overlaps the route or a settlement schedules an event across an old passage.

A visible short pasture can have multiple histories: recent grazing, drought, mowing, wildfire, soil change, seasonal dormancy or incomplete observation. The mystery should preserve competing explanations.

Institutional success can reduce quests. After several seasons, a group may have better monitoring, alternate waterpoints and established reroutes so routine shortfalls are handled without player intervention.

A managed group can split, pause or refuse a route. Individual Pokémon retain agency and persistent identity; group management never becomes ownership truth by implication.
