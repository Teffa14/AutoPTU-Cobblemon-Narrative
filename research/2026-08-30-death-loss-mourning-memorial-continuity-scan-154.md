# Death, Loss, Mourning, and Memorial Continuity Research — Pass 154

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY
Canon effect: NONE
Date: 2026-08-30

## Scope

This pass investigates a gap left after the public-memory, care, archaeology, ritual/tradition, investigation, and campaign-continuity layers: how a persistent Pokémon world records confirmed death, uncertain loss, mourning, funerary or memorial practice, changes to burial/memorial sites, and long-term social consequences without allowing narrative shorthand to fabricate death or metaphysical truth.

The research deliberately separates:

- a tactical KO or fainting result;
- a severe injury or care case;
- disappearance or missing status;
- a report that someone died;
- authoritative confirmation of death;
- custody or disposition of remains when such state exists;
- memorialization;
- private grief;
- public memory;
- claims about spirits or afterlife;
- canonical supernatural facts.

No public source below is treated as PTU/Caelo rules authority.

## Public Pokémon sources

### Pokémon Tower and the House of Memories — Kanto

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lavender_Town
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/House_of_Memories

Observed structure:

Pokémon Tower functions as a burial place containing many graves. Visitors come to pay respects. Later generations repurpose the tower as a radio facility and relocate the graves to the House of Memories.

Reusable design lessons:

1. Memorial continuity can survive relocation of the physical site.
2. A location can change civic function while preserving obligations to earlier use.
3. Grave relocation should preserve provenance: old site, new site, relocation event, responsible institution, and records linking the two.
4. Public access can differ from actual custody. A memorial building can have inaccessible or private chambers.
5. A memorial place can remain socially important after its original architecture disappears.

Ouros application:

A settlement redevelopment project may move a cemetery, memorial wall, or Pokémon resting place. The Chronicle should retain the old coordinates, relocation record, individual memorial references, objections/support, and the new access policy. Minecraft can rebuild the site after Ouros decides the relocation; the physical move cannot erase historical association.

### Marowak at Pokémon Tower — reported death, identity, and extraordinary claims

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ghost_(Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower)

Observed structure:

The games distinguish ordinary Ghost-type encounters from a specific deceased Marowak whose identity matters to the story. A tool is required to correctly perceive what some encounters are.

Reusable design lesson:

Perception of an apparition, identification of a deceased individual, and metaphysical interpretation are separate claims. Ouros must not infer `DECEASED_ENTITY_PRESENT_AS_SPIRIT` from a Ghost-type spawn, a rumor, a memorial-site encounter, or a visual effect.

Ouros application:

Any future spirit/apparition content must carry explicit canon authority and provenance. A battle result around a cemetery cannot prove an afterlife claim.

### Mt. Pyre — mourning site plus other regional functions

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mt._Pyre
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_world

Observed structure:

Mt. Pyre contains graves and grieving visitors while also supporting regional legend stewardship, training, exploration, and antagonist activity.

Reusable design lessons:

1. A burial or mourning place can remain a multi-use location rather than a sealed narrative museum.
2. Stewardship roles can coexist with private grief.
3. Conflict can happen near a memorial without making the memorial itself a combat mechanic.
4. Site staff may know local history without receiving automatic supernatural authority.

Ouros application:

Memorial locations may host caretakers, visitors, researchers, route maintenance, archives, and unrelated ecological activity. BattleSpec should include only tactical participants.

### Lost Tower — loss tied to faction consequences

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lost_Tower
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_world

Observed structure:

A visitor mourns a Pokémon reportedly killed by Team Galactic. This connects individual loss to a larger antagonistic organization without requiring the grave itself to explain the whole faction conflict.

Reusable design lesson:

A death can create durable personal, factional, investigative, and public-memory consequences. Each consequence should cite the same underlying event while remaining independently authored.

Ouros application:

A confirmed loss may create a case lead, survivor testimony, changed route choice, organizational grievance, memorial practice, or political pressure. None of those consequences proves motive beyond available evidence.

### Celestial Tower and Alder — recurring grief outside the main plot climax

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celestial_Tower
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_world

Observed structure:

Celestial Tower is a burial ground where mourners can ring a bell in remembrance. Champion Alder can be found there remembering his first Pokémon.

Reusable design lessons:

1. Powerful recurring characters can carry old loss for years without every appearance revolving around it.
2. A memorial action can be meaningful without granting a mechanical buff.
3. Grief can influence philosophy, priorities, or dialogue while remaining separate from combat statistics.
4. A revisit years later can become a character-continuity scene rather than a quest objective.

Ouros application:

A mentor, rival, Gym figure, worker, researcher, or ordinary resident may revisit a memorial on anniversaries or personal dates when their schedule and knowledge make it plausible. The system records attendance and statements; it does not assign hidden grief percentages.

### Memorial Pillar and Tectonix — highly personal memorials

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Memorial_Pillar

Observed structure:

A single Trainer maintains a large memorial to an Onix named Tectonix. A favorite drink is left at the site, and the visitor receives thanks.

Reusable design lessons:

1. Memorial scale can reflect one relationship rather than public fame.
2. An offering can be a personal remembered preference rather than supernatural currency.
3. A small interaction can reveal history without requiring combat or a large quest.
4. Memorial objects can have provenance and maintenance history.

Ouros application:

Player-facing memorial interactions may involve cleaning, delivering flowers or food, repairing a marker, returning a personal effect, transcribing a faded name, or hearing a story. Any item reward must come from an actor or institution with a plausible reason; the memorial does not mechanically generate rewards.

### Hau'oli Cemetery — shared human/Pokémon mourning and survivor continuity

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hau%27oli_Cemetery
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_world

Observed structure:

The cemetery serves people and Pokémon. One vignette links a deceased husband, a surviving Machamp, a recalled Poké Ball, survivor guilt, and a transferred personal item.

Reusable design lessons:

1. Human and Pokémon losses can coexist within one memorial institution.
2. A surviving Pokémon can remain a full actor with changed behavior and relationships after a Trainer dies.
3. Custody of a surviving Pokémon, ownership, friendship, grief, and inherited possessions are separate questions.
4. A personal effect can carry memory without proving legal inheritance rules.

Ouros application:

A deceased Trainer's Pokémon must never be auto-transferred to the player, nearest relative, caretaker, institution, or finder. Future custody/ownership depends on explicit Ouros rules and authoritative state. The surviving Pokémon may have its own goals and knowledge.

### Memorial Hill and regional travel to gravesites

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_world

Observed structure:

Some memorial places draw visitors from outside the immediate settlement because of family or historical importance.

Reusable design lesson:

Memorial activity can create travel demand, lodging pressure, seasonal transport, maintenance work, accessibility needs, and route-specific stories without turning the site into a supernatural dungeon.

Ouros application:

The Travel layer can generate journeys whose purpose is remembrance. Ritual/Tradition may own repeated observances; this pass owns the underlying loss/memorial relationship and provenance.

### Old Cemetery — ordinary world integration

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Old_Cemetery

Observed structure:

A cemetery can exist as one geographic component of a larger explorable landscape rather than an isolated story level.

Reusable design lesson:

Memorial geography should persist during ordinary play. Players may pass nearby for unrelated reasons. Respectful environmental storytelling can exist without forcing a grief scene every visit.

## Design conclusions for Ouros

### Death requires authoritative fact provenance

A proposed canonical death record needs an authority source such as:

- an explicit approved PTU/Caelo mechanical result mapped through AutoPTU;
- an authored world event with canon authority;
- a verified medical/care determination produced by a future approved system;
- another explicit canon service whose contract includes death confirmation.

Narrative rumor, witness belief, memorial construction, disappearance, defeat, despawn, or a closed care case cannot create death.

### Missing and deceased must remain different

Long campaigns need durable ambiguity. A missing actor can later be found alive, confirmed deceased, remain unresolved, or be misidentified. Public memorialization may occur while status remains uncertain, but the Chronicle must preserve that uncertainty.

### Memorialization can precede certainty

Communities sometimes commemorate presumed losses. Ouros can support a `MEMORIALIZED_WHILE_STATUS_UNRESOLVED` state. That memorial is evidence of belief and social response, not proof of death.

### Grave, body/remains, marker, and memory are separate objects

A marker may be symbolic. Remains may be elsewhere. A grave can be relocated. A cenotaph-like memorial may contain no remains. A damaged marker does not mean remains were disturbed. Minecraft destruction of a marker cannot silently alter custody or death provenance.

### Survivor continuity matters

When an NPC or Pokémon dies, linked living actors continue to have schedules, goals, jobs, memberships, custody questions, routines, and relationships. Systems should update only facts that logically depend on the death.

Examples:

- a two-person workshop may become understaffed;
- an expedition seat becomes vacant;
- a recurring companion becomes unavailable permanently after confirmed death;
- a surviving Pokémon may need care or custody resolution;
- a club may hold a remembrance event;
- a public office may trigger its already-defined succession process;
- a transport route does not change unless an operator/resource dependency actually changed.

### Grief remains actor-specific

Do not generate one universal grief arc. Valid observed outcomes can include:

- visiting a memorial;
- avoiding a site;
- keeping routine;
- taking leave;
- continuing work;
- changing a goal;
- speaking publicly;
- refusing to speak publicly;
- caring for surviving Pokémon;
- donating possessions;
- disputing a memorial;
- participating in an investigation.

These are authored choices/events, not hidden emotional calculations.

### Memorial sites remain playable world locations

Useful gameplay around memorial continuity includes:

- route access and maintenance;
- relocation documentation;
- transcription or archive work;
- returning personal effects;
- restoring damaged markers;
- resolving duplicate or mistaken records;
- locating a missing grave register;
- escorting visitors through dangerous approaches using reduced contracts;
- investigating vandalism or theft;
- documenting ecological changes around an old cemetery;
- handling competing interpretations of a historical memorial.

Combat remains optional.

## Originality guardrails

Do not copy named Pokémon characters, dialogue, memorial architecture, exact quest sequences, or distinctive plots from the sources.

Allowed abstractions include:

- burial sites as persistent civic locations;
- personal memorials for Pokémon partners;
- relocation of graves after land-use change;
- recurring grief carried by powerful NPCs;
- surviving Pokémon with independent continuity;
- uncertain loss versus confirmed death;
- memorial practices with no automatic mechanical effect;
- conflict near memorial sites whose tactical result has narrow authority.
