# Waste, Sanitation, Recycling & Pollution Research — Pass 52

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon. Not a PTU rules source.

Date: 2026-08-20

## Research question

How can Ouros model waste, sanitation, recycling, wastewater, contamination, cleanup and waste-associated Pokémon as persistent world systems without duplicating the existing infrastructure, conservation, crisis, ecology, economy or care layers, and without inventing environmental combat mechanics?

## Repository gap identified

The existing Technology, Energy & Infrastructure layer already allows a `WASTE` utility network and models pumps, treatment-related assets, faults and maintenance. The Conservation layer already models restoration and water-quality objectives. Crisis, workplaces, food, care, material culture and interspecies ecology cover adjacent consequences.

No dedicated layer currently owns:

- physical waste streams and their provenance;
- collection and sanitation service state;
- treatment inputs, outputs and residues;
- wastewater and solid-waste handling as persistent civic systems;
- recycling/reuse lineage after disposal;
- contamination observations separated from causal claims;
- cleanup verification over time;
- urban scavenger/waste-associated Pokémon relationships;
- sanitation backlogs as a cause of ecological, service and social change.

Pass 52 therefore treats sanitation as the material-end-of-life and contamination bridge between those existing systems.

## Public Pokémon sources

### Garbodor — waste consumption does not equal harmless disposal

Official Pokédex material describes Garbodor consuming garbage and producing poisonous gas/liquid. The useful design lesson is negative as much as positive: a Pokémon associated with garbage cannot be treated as a magical zero-residue disposal machine.

Source: Pokémon official Pokédex, Garbodor.
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/garbodor

Reusable structure:

- some species may interact strongly with particular waste streams;
- processing can transform material into another output rather than erase it;
- output/residue may matter as much as the consumed input;
- species flavor never grants an unverified PTU sanitation capability.

### Grimer — Pokémon can become part of formal sanitation systems

Official Pokédex material distinguishes ordinary Grimer associated with polluted environments from Alolan Grimer that eagerly consume trash and are used at garbage-disposal facilities.

Source: Pokémon official Pokédex, Grimer.
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/grimer

Reusable structure:

- a sanitation institution may employ or accommodate particular Pokémon;
- local ecology can adapt around long-running waste services;
- the same species concept can behave differently by regional form/context;
- institutional use must not imply ownership, consent, efficiency, capacity or PTU mechanics unless those are authored/validated separately.

### Big Brother to the Rescue! — pollution can produce indirect ecological conflict

The official episode uses dirty water as a driver of Pokémon displacement and conflict. Investigation identifies a source condition and the solution includes habitat intervention rather than simply defeating the Pokémon involved.

Source: Pokémon official animation episode page.
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/25/episode-17-big-brother-to-the-rescue

Reusable structure:

contamination pressure → habitat loss/displacement → overlap between groups → apparent conflict → investigation → infrastructure/habitat response.

This is valuable for Ouros because the visible confrontation can be a downstream symptom rather than the actual problem.

### Here Comes the Trubbish Squad! — waste-associated species should remain characters

The official episode places a Trubbish associated with a junkyard inside a social dispute. Concerns about garbage and safety coexist with positive relationships and useful behavior.

Source: Pokémon official animation episode page.
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/14/episode-12-here-comes-the-trubbish-squad

Reusable structure:

- residents can have legitimate competing perceptions of the same Pokémon;
- a waste-associated species should not be coded automatically as villain, pest or sanitation tool;
- social conflict may be about acceptable coexistence rather than capture/removal.

### Healing the Healer! — investigation before miraculous cleanup

The official episode includes a polluted lake and a specific Legendary whose canonical abilities are involved in purification.

Source: Pokémon official animation episode page.
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/24/episode-5-healing-the-healer

Reusable structure:

- contamination can be investigated through ecological symptoms;
- restoration can become a regional story.

Hard guardrail:

Suicune's canonical purification role is exceptional. Ouros must not generalize Legendary behavior into a generic water-treatment mechanic, cleanup action or player ability.

### Gulpin It Down — urban food/waste pressure can attract aggregations

An official anime episode uses a large Gulpin presence affecting city food supplies and authorities attempting nonlethal relocation/containment.

Source: Pokémon official animation episode page.

Reusable structure:

- waste/food availability can change urban Pokémon presence;
- relocation and source management can be valid responses;
- an aggregation is not automatically an invasion or combat encounter.

## Reusable Ouros principles

### Waste transforms; it does not vanish

Every meaningful waste-processing step should be able to create an output stream:

input waste → sorting → reusable material + treatment residue + unrecoverable fraction.

Narrative systems should never silently delete a hazardous or unique material simply because a facility processed it.

### Visible cleanliness and environmental safety are different claims

A river can look clean while testing still indicates contamination. A transfer yard can look unpleasant while being safely contained. A bad smell can be evidence worth investigating without automatically proving toxicity.

### Sanitation is a service network

Collection, transfer, treatment, recycling and final containment can fail separately. A settlement does not have a single `cleanliness` score.

### Backlogs should have causes

Possible causal families:

- collection route interruption;
- staffing shortage;
- vehicle/asset failure;
- treatment-capacity reduction;
- event surge;
- storm damage;
- sorting contamination;
- downstream facility outage;
- access closure;
- unknown.

Sabotage remains only a hypothesis until supported by evidence.

### Cleanup needs verification

Removing visible debris may complete one cleanup phase while water, soil or habitat monitoring continues. Success can be provisional.

### Waste-associated Pokémon have agency

Trubbish, Garbodor, Grimer, Alolan Grimer, Muk and other species can interact with human waste systems, but their role must be based on authored species/ecology evidence and observed individual/collective behavior.

Do not infer:

- willingness to work for people;
- ownership;
- disease;
- hostility;
- pollution source;
- universal immunity;
- mechanical disposal capacity;
- ecological benefit/harm from species identity alone.
