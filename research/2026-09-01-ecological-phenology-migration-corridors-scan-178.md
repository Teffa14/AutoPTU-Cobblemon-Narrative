## Source 1 — New Pokémon Snap ecological survey and repeated-route observation

Sources:
- https://newpokemonsnap.pokemon.com/en-us/create-photodex/
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/New_Pok%C3%A9mon_Snap

New Pokémon Snap centers an ecological survey in which the same habitats are revisited repeatedly. Different times of day expose different species and behavior, and rarer behaviors become research targets rather than guaranteed encounters.

Reusable structures:
- one visit is a sample, not a complete habitat truth;
- repeated transects make change legible;
- day/night can alter what is observable without implying population replacement;
- rare behavior can be documented as an observation category rather than a combat reward;
- research progression can improve what questions the player knows to ask without magically revealing hidden state.

Ouros translation:
Mirador can own recurring transects whose value comes from comparable observations. The player records conditions, effort and what was seen; `not observed` must stay distinct from `absent`. Later passes can compare records to identify a likely temporal window.

## Source 2 — Annual Chinchou migration and community stewardship

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP212

The animated-series episode depicts an annual Chinchou migration through a mountain village. The community knows the migration as a recurring event, supports the movement physically and treats the passage as both ecological process and public tradition. The route includes a difficult crossing, and human stewardship is aimed at helping the Pokémon continue rather than capturing or defeating them.

Reusable structures:
- recurring wildlife movement can become civic calendar knowledge;
- migration can intersect infrastructure and require temporary operating changes;
- local tradition may preserve practical ecological knowledge;
- stewardship can be a meaningful objective without making the migrating group a faction;
- a route bottleneck can create urgency without requiring a battle.

Ouros translation:
A Marea migration window could temporarily change Sendero usage, ferry notices, school observation assignments or Field Office guidance. Any future tradition associated with it remains proposal-only until canon review.

## Source 3 — Beautifly migration and narrow weather-linked observation window

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/JN022

The episode frames Beautifly migration as a research target with a limited observation window tied to the arrival of a north wind. The useful structure is temporal anticipation: researchers know a phenomenon is likely, but exact timing still depends on an observable environmental condition.

Reusable structures:
- season and immediate weather trigger are different variables;
- a forecast can create readiness without guaranteeing the event;
- missing the window should produce incomplete evidence, not quest failure by default;
- preparation can include station staffing, route access, equipment readiness and observation-site choice.

Ouros translation:
Nerea and Ema can maintain predicted windows with confidence bands. A wind shift, temperature change or other condition can narrow the likely observation period only when supported by local evidence. Narrative generation must not invent exact thresholds or PTU weather effects.

## Source 4 — Lapras migration linked to season, water temperature and vocal tracking

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP257

A marine researcher studies Lapras travel routes and links them to season and water temperature, while vocalizations are used as a way to locate the herd. The reusable lesson is multi-signal inference: route, environmental measurements and acoustic evidence can be correlated without any one signal being sufficient by itself.

Reusable structures:
- migration research benefits from multiple evidence channels;
- repeated acoustic detections can support route estimates without showing exact population size;
- environmental correlation should remain a hypothesis until supported across multiple events;
- individual leadership changes and group route continuity are separate questions.

Ouros translation:
Mirador observations, ferry reports and Tideglass historical records can jointly support a migration-route claim. A strong correlation can improve confidence without becoming a universal species rule.

## Source 5 — Seasonal Hippopotas herd movement and separated individual

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rhonda_%28Sinnoh_Now%29

A documentary crew describes Hippopotas moving as a herd with seasonal changes in search of dry habitat, while one individual becomes separated from the group. The useful pattern is that group-scale movement and individual-scale welfare can intersect without collapsing into the same object.

Reusable structures:
- a population or herd route can persist even when one individual is missing;
- helping one Pokémon rejoin a moving group does not require claiming kinship, leadership or ownership;
- media/research attention can itself become disturbance;
- the destination may be inferred from previous movement rather than directly known.

Ouros translation:
A Marea observation arc can track a recurring group while a separate persistent Pokémon identity becomes relevant because it was injured, delayed or separated. The collective should not become tactically instantiated in full.

## Source 6 — Mass outbreaks as temporary aggregations in Legends: Arceus

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mass_outbreak

Legends: Arceus distinguishes temporary mass outbreaks and massive mass outbreaks from ordinary area populations. They appear and end dynamically, and multiple outbreak sites can exist during the same broad event.

Reusable structures:
- a visible concentration is not equivalent to a permanent abundance increase;
- simultaneous aggregations can have a shared environmental driver without forming one social group;
- temporary events need explicit start/end state;
- player-facing maps may expose that an aggregation exists without explaining its cause.

Ouros translation:
The ecology layer should support `TEMPORARY_AGGREGATION` and `OUTBREAK_CANDIDATE` observations without asserting invasion, overpopulation, aggression or breeding surge. Any cause belongs to world state/evidence, not spawn density alone.

## Source 7 — Wayfarer Island migration stopover

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP787

Wayfarer Island is described as a rest stop for migrating Pokémon, with multiple migrating species using the island and Butterfree departing after a temporary stop.

Reusable structures:
- stopovers are distinct ecological sites from origins and destinations;
- a location can matter because it provides rest/refuge during transit;
- different species can share a migration bottleneck without forming a mixed-species collective;
- local disturbance at a stopover can have consequences beyond the immediate site.

Ouros translation:
Later Ouros regions can contain stopover sites whose protection or disruption matters even when no Pokémon live there permanently. In Marea, the existing seasonal crossing can be tested as a candidate corridor/stopover interface without canonizing that role yet.

## Source 8 — Sawsbuck seasonal forms and herd structure

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sawsbuck_%28Pok%C3%A9mon%29

Sawsbuck visibly changes with season and lives in herds. The design lesson is not to import Sawsbuck into Marea. It is that Pokémon fiction already supports species whose appearance, behavior or visibility can be seasonally legible.

Reusable structures:
- seasonal state can be visually readable before a researcher knows its cause;
- form/appearance and migration are separate ecological dimensions;
- species-specific official biology must be checked before generalizing a seasonal pattern.

Ouros guardrail:
Do not assign seasonal forms, breeding seasons, migrations or herd leadership to a species unless official/PTU/Caelo evidence or repeated in-world observation supports it.

## Source 9 — Contemporary PTU campaign framed around social ecology

Sources:
- https://startplaying.games/adventure/cm9ydhbid00412xulbu7lzufh
- https://startplaying.games/adventure/cm4aek81u003322rvu243nv51

A current public PTU campaign explicitly uses the relationship between society and nature as a mystery/exploration frame while retaining battles, travel and character-focused play. Reusable structures:

- social and natural systems should affect each other through concrete world state.

Ouros transformation:

- wild population abundance, persistent collective identity, visible subgroup and tactical encounter participants are already separate narrative concepts;

- external Pokémon media provide narrative/ecological inspiration, not mechanical rules;

Therefore this pass does not canonize:

- breeding seasons;
- population sizes;
- route ownership;
- migration-related mechanical bonuses/penalties;
- capture-rate changes;
- encounter-table weights;
- special movement or hazard rules.

Those require located project source evidence and, when mechanical, implementation review.

## Cross-source design conclusions

1. `OBSERVED_COUNT != TRUE_POPULATION`.
2. `NOT_OBSERVED != ABSENT`.
3. `TEMPORARY_AGGREGATION != PERSISTENT_COLLECTIVE`.
4. `SEASONAL_CORRELATION != CAUSAL_TRIGGER`.
5. `MIGRATION_ROUTE != TERRITORY`.
6. A stopover can be ecologically important even when no group permanently occupies it.
7. Repeated transects are more informative than one spectacular encounter.
8. Observation effort, time and environmental conditions must be preserved with each record.
9. Human traffic, feeding, construction and research presence can change detectability as well as actual behavior; the system must distinguish the two where possible.
10. An expected event that does not occur is evidence worth recording, not proof of extinction or crisis.
11. A route window can affect settlement operations without becoming a combat encounter.
12. A migration should usually instantiate only the visible subgroup relevant to a scene.
13. Forecasts should expose uncertainty bands and revision history.
14. Public traditions can preserve ecological knowledge but remain claims until evidence supports the practical meaning.
15. Minecraft/Cobblemon spawn/despawn is presentation evidence only unless the server-owned ecology state explicitly binds it.
