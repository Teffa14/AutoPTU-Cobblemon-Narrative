# Spatiotemporal resource partitioning scan

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE. Pass 270. No species behavior, resource preference, avoidance relation, site rule, NPC interpretation or tactical rule becomes Ouros canon through this document.

Public research reviewed:

- Ferreiro-Arias et al. (2021), Ecology and Evolution, DOI 10.1002/ece3.8077. A Mediterranean mesocarnivore community showed coexistence distributed across temporal, trophic and spatial niche axes. High overlap on one axis could coexist with separation on another. Reusable lesson: Ouros must represent resource partitioning as multidimensional evidence rather than a single overlap/competition value.
- Seveque et al. (2020), Biological Reviews, DOI 10.1111/brv.12635. Review evidence shows human disturbance can alter spatial, temporal and trophic partitioning in heterogeneous directions. Reusable lesson: an activity shift cannot be interpreted independently of disturbance context.
- Seveque et al. (2022), Mammal Review, DOI 10.1111/mam.12265. Temporal avoidance between carnivores is not globally consistent and may depend on several ecological and meteorological factors simultaneously. Reusable lesson: non-overlapping activity windows are observations first, mechanism claims second.
- Temporal resource partitioning at carcasses (2022), Basic and Applied Ecology, DOI 10.1016/j.baae.2022.01.008. Different scavengers used one ephemeral resource at different times; temporal segregation and temporal coupling both occurred. Reusable lesson: asynchronous use can support coexistence, while synchronized use can also reflect facilitation. Timing alone does not identify the interaction sign.
- Kronfeld-Schor and Dayan (2003), Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics, DOI 10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.34.011802.132435. Temporal partitioning may facilitate coexistence, but experimentally supported competition-induced temporal shifts are comparatively uncommon and constrained by endogenous rhythms and physiology. Reusable lesson: Ouros must not infer competitive avoidance from different diel schedules without causal evidence.
- Chaudhary et al. (2025), Ecosphere, DOI 10.1002/ecs2.70125. Mammal coexistence can involve combinations of spatial and temporal separation, dietary differences and predator-prey tracking. Reusable lesson: absence of co-occurrence can be produced by several mechanisms and should remain decomposed by axis.

PROPOSED:

Represent a partition record as a set of axis-specific observations plus mechanism hypotheses. Allow the same pair to overlap strongly in space while separating in time, or overlap in time while using different resource fractions. Require explicit evidence before promoting avoidance, competition, facilitation or dominance.
