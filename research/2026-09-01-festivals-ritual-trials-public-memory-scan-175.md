# Research Scan 175 — Festivals, Ritual Trials and Public Memory

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-01
Scope: publicly accessible material not previously found in this repository under the source names/terms audited before this pass.

## Why this pass

Ouros already has public memory, communications, service dispatch, settlement institutions, battle institutions and event/quest taxonomies. The current canon also says Ivo Serrat can later bridge into food, hospitality and festival arcs. What is missing is a reusable structure for a recurring public event that can carry civic memory, ordinary work, play, ritual, contested interpretation and changing consequences without becoming a single scripted cutscene.

This scan therefore targets festival and ritual structures rather than adding another generic quest-board or information system.

## Sources

### Pokémon Tabletop United — Valoryn Region Campaign

Public event listing: Warwick Tabletop Games and Role-Playing Society, “Pokémon Tabletop United: Valoryn Region Campaign.”
URL: https://www.warwicktabletop.co.uk/events/809/
Accessed: 2026-09-01.

The listing presents a high-fantasy PTU region whose opening begins in a small town during a “Festival of First Bonds.” The relevant reusable pattern is not the setting, proper nouns or plot. It is the choice to make a public rite the campaign’s social entry point: newcomers, Pokémon companionship, institutions and regional values can all be visible before the central conflict has to dominate play.

Reusable lesson:
- a festival can introduce what a settlement believes about Trainer/Pokémon relationships through observable practice rather than exposition;
- participation can establish social context before major faction conflict begins;
- a public event can connect low-level ordinary activity to later regional stakes.

Do not copy Valoryn’s names, Titans, Guild Concord, rebellion premise or Festival of First Bonds.

### Pokémon Ranger: Guardian Signs — Rainbow Festival / Rasp Cavern

Public references:
- Bulbapedia, “Rainbow Grail.” https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rainbow_Grail
- Pokémon Wiki, “Rasp Cavern.” https://pokemon.fandom.com/wiki/Rasp_Cavern
- Pokémon Wiki, “Pokémon Ranger: Guardian Signs.” https://pokemon.fandom.com/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Ranger%3A_Guardian_Signs
Accessed: 2026-09-01.

The useful structure is a layered tradition. A village festival contains a public symbolic trial and a ceremonial object. Later story pressure reveals that the public object/trial is not identical to the older functional artifact/trial. The public tradition still has cultural meaning even though its literal assumptions are incomplete.

Reusable lessons:
- public ritual and historical mechanism can diverge without making the community foolish;
- a harmless ceremonial version can preserve memory of a more demanding historical practice;
- spaces can change function by calendar state: ordinary cave/route, festival trial, then deeper historical investigation;
- a puzzle can communicate tradition without needing combat;
- discovering that a ritual object is a replica can create an archive/investigation thread rather than merely a “gotcha.”

Do not copy the Rainbow Grail, Ho-Oh invocation, Oblivia hero myth, tile puzzle layout, characters, antagonist structure or legendary-bird plot.

### Pokémon Scarlet/Violet: The Teal Mask — Kitakami Mask Festival

Public references:
- Serebii, “The Kitakami Mask Festival.” https://www.serebii.net/scarletviolet/kitakamimaskfestival.shtml
- PokéJungle, “The Teal Mask Story Walkthrough.” https://pokejungle.net/pokemon-scarlet-violet-dlc/teal-mask-story-walkthrough/

Accessed: 2026-09-01.

The useful structures are social participation and contested memory. The player receives local clothing, attends stalls and a minigame, moves through a public celebration, then encounters evidence that complicates the community’s inherited story. Later, private family knowledge and public myth are not identical.

Reusable lessons:
- attire, food, games and routes can make participation feel cultural before plot revelation occurs;
- a festival provides believable crowd mixing between locals, visitors, vendors, children, workers and outsiders;
- public myth can be socially real while historically incomplete;
- private custodians may know corrections that have not reached public memory;
- revealing a corrected history creates relationship consequences because different people learn it at different times.

Do not copy masks as identity devices, Ogerpon, the Loyal Three, named characters, signboards, specific minigames, foreign-traveler history or story beats.

### Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers — guild expedition and sentry-duty structures

Public references:
- Bulbapedia, “Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Time and Explorers of Darkness.” https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Mystery_Dungeon%3A_Explorers_of_Darkness
- Pokémon Wiki, “Sentry Duty.” https://pokemon.fandom.com/wiki/Sentry_Duty
Accessed: 2026-09-01.

These are not festival sources directly. They are useful for participation design. Guild work turns an institution’s normal duties into playable routines, while expeditions split participants into groups with shared destination knowledge and different local experiences.

Reusable lessons:
- public-event participation can include ordinary operational jobs, not only contests and ceremonies;
- temporary duties can reveal an institution through work;
- group routes can create multiple perspectives on the same event;
- a festival should have setup, access control, supply, cleanup and recordkeeping roles that can become gameplay.

Do not copy Wigglytuff Guild, footprint identification, expedition locations, characters or story conflicts.

## Cross-source synthesis

A strong recurring Ouros public event can have six simultaneous layers:

1. ORDINARY OPERATIONS
Food preparation, deliveries, route safety, care station readiness, visitor handling, cleanup and recordkeeping.

2. PARTICIPATION SURFACE
Games, demonstrations, exhibitions, storytelling, food, local dress, guided walks or noncombat trials.

3. CULTURAL MEMORY
The event remembers a person, season, route, ecological change, settlement achievement or older practice.

4. HISTORICAL UNCERTAINTY
Not every story told at the event must be verified history. Claims keep provenance and can be corrected through existing archive/public-memory systems.

5. CALENDAR TRANSFORMATION
The same spaces can behave differently during setup, opening, peak attendance, closure and aftermath without requiring a new map.

6. CONSEQUENCE
The next edition can remember what happened: a damaged route changes the procession; a corrected archive caption changes a public story; a vendor withdraws; a new resident takes a duty; a popular exhibition becomes institutionalized.

## Ouros-specific transformed opportunities

### Public ritual as relationship graph

A recurring event should cause existing Marea residents to cross institutional boundaries naturally. Ivo can handle food logistics, Lia arrivals, Mina transport, Oren care readiness, Teo temporary infrastructure, Nerea public observation, Taro source displays, Mara coordination, Sela/Jace exhibitions and Loma residents supply or instruction.

This uses the canon network rather than creating one-off festival NPCs.

### Replica/reenactment without false authority

Ouros can use ceremonial replicas, reenacted route checks, copied logbooks or symbolic markers. These should be explicitly marked as ceremonial/pedagogical objects. A replica cannot become a canonical historical artifact simply because a player interacts with it.

### Tradition can survive correction

If Tideglass later shows that a familiar story is inaccurate, the annual practice can continue with revised interpretation. The system should preserve both editions in public-memory history.

### Festival puzzles can be noncombat first

Route-order puzzles, observation matching, provenance matching, cooperative carrying, ingredient identification, archive-source comparison and navigation tasks can provide meaningful play without relying on incomplete battle capability families.

## Risks to avoid

- every festival secretly hiding a Legendary;
- every old story being a lie;
- treating locals as ignorant so the player can “discover the truth” immediately;
- one global crowd state with omniscient knowledge;
- using battle victory as proof of historical or ecological causality;
- importing a copyrighted ritual, puzzle layout, character, dialogue or plot turn;
- making a once-per-year event functionally identical every year.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

The research supports narrative framing only. It does not establish PTU mechanical bonuses for ceremony, crowd morale, local dress, food service, ritual status, public reputation, companions, navigation or puzzle completion.

Any Trainer Feature, Skill, Move, Ability, Item, capture rule or combat effect used by a festival encounter must be validated against the project’s authoritative PTU/Caelo material and current engine evidence.

## Candidate architecture output

Recommended design follow-up: a recurring public-event lifecycle that composes existing calendar, communication, public-memory, relationship, service-dispatch, questline and battle-handoff systems instead of replacing them.
