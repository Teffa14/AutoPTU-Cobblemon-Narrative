# Ouros Narrative Research — Travel, Transport & Expedition Logistics — Pass 10

Status: Research only. Provenance and design evidence; not Ouros canon.

This pass follows the existing work on mission grammar, world agency, settlements, cases, material culture, archaeology and social systems. The gap examined here is movement between meaningful places: routes, public transport, Pokémon-assisted mobility, expedition planning, navigation, mobile bases, route disruptions, journey compression and the difference between routine transit and travel that deserves to become a playable scene.

The existing repository already references supply routes, blocked roads, expedition loadouts, travel access, pursuit paths and bridges. This pass does not duplicate those systems. It formalizes the connective tissue between them.

## 1. Galar Taxi — transport can be a public service rather than a personal traversal power

Primary source:
https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-us/pokemon-galar-region/corviknight/

Official Sword/Shield material describes Corviknight working for Galar Taxi to transport people between towns because of their flying ability and intelligence.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A player should not need to personally own every mobility solution. Travel can be provided by an institution, business, community or specialist service whose Pokémon, staff and infrastructure are part of the world.

Ouros translation:
A transport service can track:
- operator/institution;
- routes served;
- terminals or stops;
- staff and Pokémon assets;
- public access requirements;
- service state;
- dependencies;
- disruptions;
- passenger/cargo role;
- last confirmed operation.

The exact Corviknight/Galar Taxi implementation is not imported. The reusable principle is service-based mobility grounded in world actors.

## 2. Alola Pokémon Ride — mobility can be regional culture and shared infrastructure

Primary sources:
https://www.pokemon.co.jp/ex/sun_moon/world/160801_02.html
https://www.pokemon.co.jp/ex/usum/gamesystem/170922_04.html

Official Alola material describes Pokémon Ride as a regional practice allowing people to borrow Pokémon power to reach places humans cannot access alone. Ride Pokémon do not become ordinary party members simply because they provide transportation.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Traversal access can be cultural and institutional rather than equivalent to ownership.

Ouros translation:
Possible mobility relationships include:
- public ride service;
- community-maintained mount program;
- ranger/rescue mount pool;
- expedition-assigned Pokémon;
- ferry or caravan Pokémon;
- private partner Pokémon whose actual PTU capability is valid;
- mechanical vehicle or infrastructure where canon supports it.

This creates an important distinction:
`can physically traverse` is not always the same as `owns a Pokémon with that power`.

Exact Ride Gear, species assignments and mainline traversal rules are not imported.

## 3. Pokémon Ranger — field capability can alter route choice

Primary source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-video-games/pokemon-ranger-guardian-signs/

Guardian Signs explicitly uses Pokémon Field Moves to remove obstacles and enable further exploration.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A journey can contain alternate route solutions driven by actual Pokémon/Trainer state rather than generic keys.

Ouros translation:
A route segment may declare candidate traversal hooks, but each executable hook must be validated against the governed PTU/Caelo character or Pokémon state and AutoPTU/Cobblemon integration.

Examples at the narrative layer:
- cross water;
- reach elevated path;
- move through hazardous terrain;
- clear or bypass an obstacle;
- detect a safe route;
- access a hidden passage.

The narrative generator cannot infer a route power from species flavor or appearance.

## 4. Pokémon Horizons — a vehicle can become a mobile social hub

Primary sources:
https://horizons.pokemon.com/en-us/
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/horizons/1/the-treasure-after-the-storm
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/horizons/2/into-a-new-sky-the-brave-olivine
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/horizons/3/orla-grounded

Official Pokémon Horizons material presents the Brave Olivine as both transport and home base for a group with distinct roles. The official character material identifies communication/navigation, mechanical work, cooking and healing specialties among the crew. Episode summaries also show the vessel needing repairs and later receiving upgrades.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A long-distance transport can be a persistent location with residents, services, maintenance state and relationships rather than a loading-screen animation.

Ouros translation:
A mobile base may have:
- current location;
- route plan;
- crew roles;
- passenger roster;
- workshop/medical/storage functions;
- maintenance problems;
- upgrade history;
- cargo;
- communication state;
- docking/landing requirements;
- social scenes while traveling.

A mobile base should only exist in canon when Ouros deliberately authors the relevant technology/culture. This pass does not establish airships or any specific vehicle.

## 5. Travel can be compressed until a meaningful decision occurs

Design sources:
https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/17329/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl-part-4-navigation
https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/17320/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl-part-2-wilderness-travel
https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/36541/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl-addendum-describing-travel

These tabletop design articles separate travel procedure from travel narration and emphasize navigation choices, landmarks and interruptions that create actual decisions.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Persistent-world travel does not need a scene for every kilometer.

Routine movement should normally compress when:
- the route is known;
- current route state is safe enough;
- transport is operating normally;
- no active commitment or clock intersects the path;
- players are not deliberately exploring;
- no meaningful discovery is available.

Travel should expand into playable content when:
- a route choice matters;
- known state has changed;
- navigation is uncertain;
- a transport service fails;
- time pressure changes options;
- ecology produces an unusual event;
- another actor intercepts or requests help;
- a player chooses to investigate something en route;
- a meaningful social scene is available;
- the destination cannot be reached by the assumed mode.

Ouros should adapt this pacing principle without importing D&D hex procedures, movement rates or encounter tables.

## 6. Navigation knowledge should be separate from route truth

Derived from navigation design above and Ouros' existing actor-knowledge boundary.

The world may know that a bridge is gone while a player map still shows it intact.
A courier may know a seasonal bypass that another character does not.
A road may remain physically passable but be considered unsafe by one institution.

Reusable lesson:
Track route state and route knowledge separately.

Potential fields:
- actual connection state;
- last observed state per actor;
- known landmarks;
- known rest points;
- known hazards;
- alternate routes known;
- confidence/freshness;
- source of information.

This makes scouting, local guides, route reports and maps useful without turning navigation into arbitrary hidden punishment.

## 7. Fangame transport map concept — fast travel can preserve geography

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/9149/

A public fangame design discussion proposes tram lines connecting settlements, requiring transfers between lines rather than allowing arbitrary point-to-point teleportation.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Fast travel can preserve the topology of a region.

Ouros translation:
A transport network can remain legible when services follow authored connections. Opening a new stop or restoring a line then matters to world state.

This is a fan concept, so no names, maps or specific line structure are imported.

## 8. Public Pokémon journey fiction discussion — the journey itself can be the premise

Source:
https://forums.thousandroads.net/threads/where-to-set-a-traveling-caravan-type-story.1592/

Public discussion of a Pokémon caravan-type story highlights interest in journey-focused structures that are not simply badge progression.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Travel can support social and observational stories when the group itself is moving through the world for a purpose.

Potential Ouros forms:
- survey caravan;
- seasonal trade route;
- mobile clinic;
- traveling performance circuit;
- conservation migration watch;
- archaeology field season;
- rescue convoy;
- courier circuit;
- pilgrimage or cultural journey where canon supports it.

The specific fan-story concepts are not imported.

## 9. Public Pokémon RP — geography becomes meaningful when travel time and hazards are explicit

Source:
https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/the-mystery-of-rabua-bay.19596/

The public RP describes a bay with multiple islands, dangerous waters and a multi-day crossing scale, using travel geography to give different locations and risks a sense of distance.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Distance should matter selectively.

Travel scale can affect:
- which services are convenient;
- whether emergency response is plausible;
- supply-route reliability;
- which factions can project influence;
- how quickly information spreads;
- where a mobile base can reach;
- whether an expedition requires preparation.

Ouros does not need real-time simulation of every journey. Distance matters when it changes a choice, dependency or consequence.

## 10. PTU campaign evidence — exploration works best when the world feels pre-existing

Primary PTU source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/over-there-a-world-war-one-pokemon-campaign-a-retrospective/

The retrospective explicitly emphasizes exploration and a believable world that feels like it existed before the players and continues after they leave. Another PTU retrospective, Tales of Visiwa, places certified Explorers in a dangerous wilderness where entering the wilds is itself a meaningful social role.

Secondary PTU source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/tales-of-visiwa-a-retrospective/

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Travel should expose world state rather than generate disconnected road encounters.

A route can reveal:
- changing ecology;
- infrastructure condition;
- faction presence;
- abandoned or new settlements;
- public memory;
- supply traffic;
- seasonal use;
- signs of another ongoing arc.

This supports the existing dense-world preference: routes between familiar places can become richer over time instead of being replaced by endless new geography.

## 11. Transport failure can create stories without becoming a random punishment

Official Horizons examples show a mobile transport needing to land for repairs and later being upgraded. Public fangame premises also use disrupted trips or stranded travelers as inciting conditions, but those specific plots are not imported.

Reusable lesson:
Transport failure is valuable when it is causal, legible and produces alternatives.

Potential causes:
- weather;
- infrastructure damage;
- route closure;
- maintenance issue;
- ecological disturbance;
- staff absence;
- supply shortage;
- faction action;
- public event congestion;
- scheduled seasonal closure.

Possible responses:
- wait;
- repair;
- reroute;
- charter another service;
- travel under own power if legal;
- split cargo;
- establish temporary camp;
- turn the delay into social/research content.

Avoid random service failures whose only purpose is wasting player time.

## 12. Expedition roles should be operational, not new Trainer Classes

Derived from Horizons crew specialization, Ranger mission structure and the existing Ouros participation-lane model.

Potential expedition roles:
- navigator;
- scout;
- logistics lead;
- medical support;
- Pokémon handler/care lead;
- researcher;
- liaison/interpreter;
- mechanic/technician;
- security/battle lead;
- quartermaster;
- local guide;
- communications operator.

A character may fill multiple roles, and roles can change by expedition.

These labels organize responsibilities only. They grant no PTU Feature, Edge, Skill Rank or combat modifier.

## 13. Journey incidents need causal selection

A travel incident should be selected from state that intersects the route.

Candidate state sources:
- route hazard;
- active regional clock;
- faction front;
- ecological anomaly;
- weather;
- settlement request;
- damaged infrastructure;
- transport-service problem;
- known case/pursuit;
- public event traffic;
- personal relationship callback;
- player-selected exploration objective.

Avoid a universal random-encounter roll that must produce a fight on every journey.

A journey may be entirely uneventful, and that can be correct.

## 14. Multi-modal travel can create a regional graph

A single trip may combine:
- walking;
- public transport;
- personal Pokémon-assisted traversal;
- boat/ferry;
- caravan/convoy;
- authored mechanical vehicle;
- dungeon traversal;
- temporary expedition transport.

Each segment should know:
- endpoints;
- mode;
- current availability;
- access requirements;
- estimated narrative cost band;
- state dependencies;
- fallback routes.

Exact speeds, carrying capacity, ticket cost, mounting rules and travel time require mechanics/canon review.

## 15. Internal PTU / Caelo / AutoPTU grounding

The project repository establishes the supplied PTU/Caelo corpus and actual AutoPTU implementation as the mechanical authority. Existing project material already references movement capabilities, route access, environmental conditions and travel-dependent settlement systems.

The available AutoPTU `battle_state.py` material confirms that tactical movement is represented through explicit movement modes/capabilities such as sky, swim, burrow and Naturewalk-related handling. This is battle-engine evidence only; it does not authorize overworld travel rules.

Therefore Pass 10 does not define:
- overworld movement speed;
- mount eligibility;
- carrying limits;
- fatigue/exhaustion;
- vehicle statistics;
- Surf/Fly prerequisites;
- ferry costs;
- road travel times;
- navigation DCs;
- camping/rest bonuses;
- weather movement penalties;
- transport capacity.

Those require dedicated extraction from the governing PTU/Caelo sources plus current AutoPTU/Cobblemon support.

## 16. Design conclusions from this pass

1. Route truth and route knowledge should be separate.
2. Public transport lets mobility exist independently of Pokémon ownership.
3. Pokémon-assisted mobility can be cultural/institutional rather than a universal party ability.
4. Routine safe travel should compress automatically.
5. Travel becomes a scene when a meaningful choice, change, discovery or relationship intersects the route.
6. Transport services need staff, Pokémon/assets, dependencies and disruption state.
7. Mobile bases can combine travel, maintenance, services and social continuity.
8. Expedition roles organize multiplayer responsibilities without creating new mechanics.
9. Fast travel should preserve authored regional topology where practical.
10. Route disruptions need causal alternatives instead of arbitrary delay.
11. Travel incidents should emerge from route/world state, not mandatory combat rolls.
12. Distance should matter only where it changes plausible response, access, logistics or consequence.
13. Opening or closing a connection can alter settlement, economy, ecology and faction state simultaneously.
14. Overworld travel rules must not be inferred from AutoPTU tactical movement implementation.
