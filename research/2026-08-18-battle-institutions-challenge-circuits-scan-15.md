# Battle institutions, challenge circuits and competitive progression research — pass 15

Status: research and provenance only. Nothing in this file is Ouros canon.

## Why this pass exists

The repository already models public events, institutions, factions, arena state, alternative encounter objectives, rivalry memory, performance circuits, travel, recovery and long-term public reputation. It did not yet have a dedicated research layer for battle-centered institutions: Gyms, League qualification, rematches, Battle-Facility-like challenges, tournament careers, battle schools, challenge auditing, competitive titles and the social consequences of formal Trainer battles.

This pass studies those structures without inventing PTU battle formats, Badge rules, item restrictions, rewards, ranking math or custom combat mechanics.

## Source register

### Pokémon Tabletop RPG — Gym Design: Signature Elements

Source: https://pokemontabletop.com/gym-design-signature-elements/

The official PTU blog argues that memorable Gym Challenges are coherent packages rather than generic difficult battles. Building, arena, Trainer personality, signature Pokémon and mechanical theme should reinforce each other. It also frames a Gym Leader as a Trainer with a role in the community and recommends showing enough of a Leader's style beforehand that challengers can prepare.

Reusable structure:
- a Gym has an institutional identity beyond its Leader's type;
- the arena should express a battle philosophy rather than contain arbitrary hazards;
- challengers may learn about a Leader's style through public appearances, prior battles or the surrounding community;
- a signature Pokémon or recognizable tactical pattern can create continuity across rematches;
- preparation and scouting can be part of the narrative loop without giving the challenger perfect information.

Do not import the article's sample custom Moves, Boss Templates, Badge powers or encounter numbers into Ouros automatically.

### Pokémon Tabletop RPG — Gym Design: Unconventional Challenges

Source: https://pokemontabletop.com/gym-design-unconventional-challenges/

The PTU blog explores full-contact challenges, area control, defense, capture-oriented objectives, puzzle-like battle structures and surprise formats. Its strongest general design rules are that combat should remain central to a Gym challenge, win/loss conditions should be understandable, judging should avoid opaque subjectivity, and unusual formats should remain focused enough that players can reason about them.

Reusable structure:
- formal battle institutions may support multiple challenge formats;
- an alternative objective needs explicit success, failure and legal-action boundaries;
- unusual formats should be announced clearly enough to preserve legitimacy;
- a challenge can test movement, protection, area control or capture judgment rather than only total knockout;
- a Gym can combine dungeon-like traversal with a final formal battle.

Exact action economy, capture legality, healing/item policy, terrain effects and Trainer participation remain PTU/Caelo rules questions.

### Pokémon Tabletop RPG — campaign structure

Source: https://pokemontabletop.com/ and the supplied PTU Core Rulebook

The PTU campaign guidance treats Gym challenges as recurring Trainer activities that should be braided with personal and central plots. A League-based campaign can alternate periods of free Gym pursuit with periods of stronger plot pressure, and a Gym Leader can also function as an expert, gatekeeper or community actor inside a wider story.

Reusable structure:
- Gym progress should coexist with exploration, relationships and world arcs;
- a Gym need not be isolated from the town that contains it;
- qualification milestones can punctuate a campaign without becoming the only content;
- major tournament phases benefit from enough narrative space that unrelated crises do not constantly interrupt every match.

These facilities demonstrate how a recurring battle venue can gain identity from rules constraints, streak structure, opponent selection and a recurring master-level opponent. All videogame restrictions, rewards and streak thresholds are inspiration only.

### Pokémon World Tournament public documentation

Source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_World_Tournament

The World Tournament brings accomplished Trainers from different regions into a shared formal venue and distinguishes tournament categories rather than relying on an infinite streak.

Reusable structure:
- cross-regional invitational events can reactivate older NPCs and rivalries;
- a tournament category can communicate expected participant tier and identity;
- returning champions or Leaders can carry public history from earlier arcs;
- a shared venue can become a nexus for spectators, sponsors, media and side stories.

### Pokémon League across media

Source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_League

Across Pokémon media, League structures vary. Qualification can come from Badges or alternative routes; tournaments may include qualifiers, preliminary rounds and finals; Pokémon Adventures uses different League rules and institutional relationships from the games and animation.

Reusable structure:
- Ouros does not need to assume one universal League architecture before canon is defined;
- qualification routes, tournament format and institutional authority should be explicit data;
- public records must store which ruleset/version governed an event;
- different regions may recognize each other's credentials unevenly.

### PTU actual play — Pokémon World Tour: United

Source: https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/pokemon-world-tour-united/id1154176782

This long-running PTU actual play has operated since 2016 and continues into 2026. Public episode metadata shows the campaign reaching Indigo Plateau conflicts while also preserving holiday episodes, alternate-timeline material and long-running character continuity.

Reusable structure:
- a League destination can remain relevant across a very long campaign when it intersects with wider world events;
- competition venues can become story locations rather than isolated menus;
- long-running campaigns benefit from records that distinguish canonical events from explicitly non-canonical side material.

No episode plot or character material should be transplanted.

### Fanfiction discussion — tournament pacing

Source: https://boards.4chan.org/vp/ (public fan-work discussion indexed by search)

A public fanfiction discussion highlights a real pacing risk: Pokémon tournament arcs can become long because each match itself can consume substantial narrative space, and repeated matches may become plot dead zones.

Reusable structure:
- a tournament does not need to fully simulate or narrate every bracket match;
- NPC-vs-NPC rounds can be summarized into record state unless they create meaningful consequences;
- player matches should differ in opponent identity, stakes, arena, relationship or strategic question;
- tournament side stories should continue between rounds so the event remains a living place.

This source is treated as anecdotal design feedback only.

## Patterns worth adopting

### A battle institution has four identities

1. Institutional role: why it exists in the region.
2. Challenge philosophy: what skill or judgment it tests.
3. Public identity: how people talk about it and what history it carries.
4. Executable format: the rules contract AutoPTU must actually enforce.

The first three may exist narratively before the fourth is implemented.

### Challenge contracts should be explicit

Before an official challenge begins, the system should be able to display a reviewed contract containing fields such as:
- participants;
- team-size policy;
- Trainer participation policy;
- item policy;
- arena version;
- objective type;
- win/loss conditions;
- withdrawal policy;
- rematch policy;
- whether the result affects qualification, title, ranking or only exhibition history.

Every mechanical field requires PTU/Caelo + AutoPTU validation.

### Loss should create state, not erase progress

A formal loss can record:
- opponent observations;
- arena interactions that mattered;
- revealed tactical tendencies;
- public result;
- optional training hooks;
- rematch eligibility;
- rival reactions.

It should not automatically generate a mechanical buff or reveal the opponent's complete legal team.

### Rematches need information boundaries

The Leader may remember what the challenger publicly demonstrated. The challenger may remember what they legally observed. Neither side should gain omniscient access to hidden loadouts or future team choices.

A rematch can evolve because:
- the challenger prepared differently;
- the Leader chooses a different approved roster/profile;
- the arena is in a different reviewed state;
- the formal format is different;
- the battle serves a different institutional purpose.

### Tournament simulation should be selective

Fully resolve battles involving active players or battles whose exact mechanics matter to canon. Other bracket matches can be represented through validated result inputs, human-authored outcomes or a separate simulation policy. Never fabricate PTU dice results merely to fill a bracket.

### Gym Leaders can be teachers and civic actors

A Leader may plausibly:
- teach;
- mentor Gym Trainers;
- host exhibitions;
- advise on regional battle safety;
- participate in crisis response;
- serve on League committees;
- maintain a specialized facility;
- sponsor local clubs;
- appear in public events.

These roles create content for players who do not currently want a formal challenge.

### Competitive careers need multiple lanes

Candidate institutional lanes, pending canon:
- Gym/Badge circuit;
- open tournament circuit;
- Battle-Facility-like mastery challenges;
- club or academy competition;
- exhibition career;
- regional invitational events;
- ranked public ladder if Ouros chooses to implement one.

No lane should silently become mandatory for all Trainers.

## Copyright and attribution boundary

External campaigns, anime episodes, manga summaries, videogame facilities and fan discussions are inspiration and research evidence. Ouros may reuse abstract structures such as targeted rematch preparation, coherent Gym identity, tiered tournaments or persistent battle records. It must not copy source characters, dialogue, exact facility gimmicks, unique plots, custom Moves, badge powers or distinctive arena designs.
