# Ouros Narrative Research — Public Memory, Events & Legacy — Pass 05

Status: Research only. Provenance and design evidence; not Ouros canon.

This pass deliberately avoids repeating the existing work on mission grammar, dungeon state, ecology, faction fronts, evidence graphs, settlement growth and observation. It focuses on a missing layer: how communities remember players publicly, how recurring festivals and competitions create shared social time, how institutions accumulate history, and how later players inherit consequences from earlier campaigns.

## 1. PTU shared-universe retrospective — old campaigns can become world history

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/over-there-a-world-war-one-pokemon-campaign-a-retrospective/

The public PTU retrospective describes Over There! as a sequel to an earlier PTA campaign and part of a shared-universe experiment with another GM's future-set game. Earlier legendary encounters and prior campaign events were used to explain later world conditions.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Persistent-world memory becomes more powerful when completed player arcs can become history for later groups rather than only callbacks for the same character.

Ouros translation:
- distinguish immediate Chronicle facts from historical interpretation;
- allow later campaigns, seasons or player cohorts to inherit consequences without requiring them to replay old content;
- preserve links from historical summaries back to validated Chronicle events;
- allow institutions, settlements and factions to remember different aspects of the same past event;
- keep retired PCs under strict authorship boundaries: the system may record confirmed past actions but must not invent their later choices without approval.

## 2. Festival of Friendship RP — public event as social container plus bounded plot

Source:
https://forums.thousandroads.net/threads/pokemon-adventure-quest-festival-of-friendship-rp-signups-closed.1140/

The public campaign is organized around a celebration that gathers many characters into one location. Its structure gives broad time for free roleplay while limiting major plot-relevant scenes per in-world day. Investigation opportunities coexist with social interaction, and the event has an explicit day/night rhythm.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A festival can function as a temporary high-density narrative hub. It does not need to be a long chain of compulsory minigames. Public schedules can create predictable social windows while a smaller number of authored incidents, mysteries or competitions provide pressure.

Ouros translation:
A major event can expose:
- open social spaces;
- optional competitions;
- scheduled ceremonies;
- vendors and temporary services;
- visiting NPCs/factions;
- one or two major incidents per phase;
- investigation opportunities;
- player-specific invitations or obligations;
- closing consequences that persist after the event ends.

## 3. PokeMMO Sevii Islands Festival RP — regional tradition joins lore, competition and tourism

Source:
https://forums.pokemmo.com/index.php?/topic/103012-roleplay-1st-sevii-islands-festival/

The public RP frames a regional festival through local legends, historical memory, battles, catching competitions, tournaments and travel between multiple island locations. The event is used as a reason for outsiders to visit places that already have local identity.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Recurring public events can make regional history playable. A festival can connect traditions, local legends, commerce, tourism, competitions and place-specific stories without requiring every participant to care about the same central plot.

Ouros translation:
- events should attach to existing settlements and locations rather than float as generic menus;
- each recurring event should have a reason it exists in-world;
- different districts or connected locations can host different activity lanes;
- event traffic can temporarily alter NPC density, shops, wild-Pokémon disturbance and faction presence;
- the event may reinterpret old history, creating opportunities for investigations into what really happened.

## 4. PTU Gym design — institutions become memorable through cohesive identity

Sources:
https://pokemontabletop.com/gym-design-signature-elements/
https://pokemontabletop.com/gym-design-unconventional-challenges/

The official PTU blog stresses that memorable Gyms are more than stat checks. The building, arena, Leader personality, signature Pokémon and challenge style should form one coherent identity. The unconventional challenge guidance also emphasizes clear victory conditions, readable rules, limited complexity and alternatives to simple defeat-all combat.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Public institutions need a recognizable authored identity that survives individual encounters. Players should be able to remember a Gym, contest hall, guild, academy or tournament venue by how it behaves, what it teaches, who gathers there and how its spaces shape play.

Ouros translation:
Define an `institution_identity` separately from current leadership. Leadership may change while traditions, venue, public expectations, ceremonies or training philosophy persist and evolve.

## 5. Smogon World Cup history — recurring competition accumulates prestige and institutional memory

Source:
https://www.smogon.com/tournaments/worldcup/history

This is a real-world Pokémon community tournament rather than an in-fiction source. Its history shows how an event can become prestigious through repetition while formats, team counts and procedures change between editions.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A recurring tournament should remember prior editions. History itself becomes content: former champions, records, upsets, format controversies, rivalries, regional expectations and changed rules can make a new season meaningful before the first battle starts.

Ouros translation:
Track event editions rather than treating each tournament as a fresh copy.

Candidate historical fields:
- edition/year;
- host location;
- format version;
- participants;
- winners/finalists;
- notable public incidents;
- records;
- controversies;
- rule changes next edition;
- resulting rivalries;
- civic/faction impacts.

No Smogon competitive rules are imported into PTU.

## 6. Pokémon Supernova — school life, recurring events and relationships as parallel progression

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/8804/

The public fangame description combines academy life, relationships, events, exploration, side quests, factions and branching outcomes. Character affinity influences later dialogue and story direction while exploration areas retain their own hooks.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A recurring institution can support several rhythms at once: scheduled public activities, personal relationships, exploration, side quests and larger faction mysteries. Players do not need to suspend their personal arcs when an institution hosts a public event.

## 7. Pokémon Elite development thread — interpersonal state can include distance and strain

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/8866/

The public project description explicitly frames interpersonal simulation more broadly than dating, including friendship, rivalry, strained bonds and distance with branching consequences.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Public reputation and private relationship state must remain separate. A player may be celebrated by a settlement while distrusted by one resident, or publicly criticized while privately respected by an ally.

## 8. Public event storytelling can transform ordinary space temporarily

Sources:
https://forums.pokemmo.com/index.php?/topic/103012-roleplay-1st-sevii-islands-festival/
https://forums.thousandroads.net/threads/pokemon-adventure-quest-festival-of-friendship-rp-signups-closed.1140/

Both public examples use an event to concentrate characters, create temporary schedules and make familiar locations socially dense.

Reusable lesson for Minecraft/Cobblemon:
Ouros can express events physically through temporary stalls, banners, spectators, visiting trainers, altered NPC schedules, restricted streets, competition areas, special transport, changed encounter pressure and post-event cleanup.

The event should have setup, active and aftermath states rather than appearing/disappearing without causal transition.

## 9. Distinguish fact, public record and legend

Derived from the shared-universe PTU example and festival-history examples above.

A persistent world needs at least three historical layers:

1. Canonical event facts — validated Chronicle state.
2. Public record — what institutions or witnesses officially preserve.
3. Living memory — rumors, myths, commemorations, propaganda and family/community stories.

These layers can diverge without changing canonical truth.

This creates future investigation hooks such as:
- a monument credits the wrong person;
- a faction omits its role in a disaster;
- a local legend compresses several separate events into one hero story;
- descendants disagree over an old settlement decision;
- a tournament record is correct, but the public story about why someone withdrew is not.

## 10. Internal PTU / Caelo compatibility

The supplied PTU Core Rulebook already supports character-centric, central-plot and sandbox play and recommends meaningful player choices with self-contained satisfaction. Caelo already separates Social, Wild Encounter, PvP, Job, Raid, Contest, Gym and Dojo activities; Social space exists specifically to support character development.

Therefore public events should be orchestration layers that combine several existing activity types, not a replacement rules system.

Any contest scoring, battle format, capture competition, Skill Check, environmental modifier, reward or tournament rule still requires validation against the governing PTU/Caelo material and the current AutoPTU implementation.

## Design conclusions from this pass

1. Ouros needs public memory in addition to private relationship memory.
2. Historical truth, official record and living legend should be separate data.
3. Festivals and tournaments are temporary high-density world states with setup and aftermath.
4. Recurring events should track editions so history accumulates.
5. Public institutions need persistent identity beyond whoever currently leads them.
6. Witnesses should matter: fame, blame and controversy should spread from observed or reported actions, not global omniscience.
7. Retired player characters can become historical figures only through confirmed facts; future agency remains protected.
8. Public events should support multiple activity lanes instead of forcing every participant into one competition.
9. Event consequences should alter later settlement, faction, relationship or historical state.
10. Minecraft should visibly express event preparation, crowding and aftermath.
