# Settlement Abandonment, Return & Reoccupation Research — Pass 84

Status: external research and provenance only. Nothing in this file is automatically Ouros canon.

Date: 2026-08-27

## Research question

How can Ouros preserve a settlement after population loss, evacuation, institutional relocation or long decline so that the place remains usable as history, habitat, infrastructure and future story space rather than becoming a disposable ruin or instantly resetting when people return?

This pass intentionally avoids duplicating existing Ouros layers for crisis response, household relocation, settlement capabilities, public memory, conservation, maintenance or public works. The target gap is the persistent occupancy lifecycle of the settlement as a place.

## Repository review before external research

- `design/crisis-rescue-recovery-layer.md` owns SIGNAL through AFTERMATH for emergencies, including evacuation, shelter, service disruption and recovery projects.
- `design/residential-life-household-relocation-layer.md` owns household occupancy, individual relocation, former-home persistence and temporary displacement.

- `design/facility-maintenance-repair-inspection-extension.md` owns physical condition, fault, repair and reopening evidence for facilities.
- `design/conservation-protected-areas-stewardship-layer.md` owns ecological state, stewardship designations and habitat restoration.
- `design/public-memory-event-legacy-layer.md` owns public interpretation, institutional continuity and reconstruction memory.

The missing state is therefore not `settlement recovery` in general. It is the persistence of occupancy and use when a settlement is no longer normally inhabited, and the staged return or repurposing that may follow.

## Source 1 — Cinnabar Island and the relocated Cinnabar Gym

Sources:

- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blaine
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Seafoam_Islands
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/History_of_the_Pokemon_world

Observed structure:

- A volcanic eruption destroys most of an established settlement.
- One major institution, the Gym, continues functioning after relocation rather than disappearing with the original building.
- The replacement site begins as a minimal cave venue and later becomes a more developed version of the institution.
- Cinnabar itself is not represented as a clean binary of `city` versus `nothing`; later material retains a Pokémon Center near the devastated site while other functions remain absent.
- The original institutional identity continues to reference the lost place even while its physical operation has moved elsewhere.

Reusable Ouros lessons:

1. Settlement destruction does not require institutional erasure.
2. A service can continue through a temporary or alternate site while its home settlement remains unavailable.
3. A temporary location can accumulate its own history if recovery takes long enough.
4. One surviving/rebuilt service is enough to make a supposedly devastated settlement operationally non-empty.
5. Rebuilding intent and actual rebuilding state must remain separate.

Do not copy:

- Cinnabar, Blaine, Seafoam, the eruption, the Gym puzzle, Badge progression or source-specific characters.
- Any assumption that every destroyed settlement needs a Gym-in-exile analogue.

## Source 2 — Oblitus Town in Pokémon Rejuvenation

Source:

- https://rejuvenation.wiki.gg/wiki/Oblitus_Town

Observed structure:

- The town becomes mostly abandoned through long-term social/institutional decline rather than a single disaster.
- A very small permanent population remains while most buildings and former services are abandoned.
- The place still connects to active regional infrastructure and later story activity.
- Later leadership changes lead to partial restoration: old buildings are patched, new houses appear, vegetation/pathing changes and the town gains a visibly different state.
- Restoration is partial rather than a magical rewind to the old settlement.

Reusable Ouros lessons:

1. Population decline can be gradual, economic, institutional or demographic rather than crisis-driven.
2. `Mostly abandoned` can coexist with permanent residents, active households and useful local knowledge.
3. Return should be represented through concrete occupancy, service and physical changes rather than an abstract settlement-level score.
4. Restoration can create a new version of the place instead of reproducing its historic layout.
5. A small remnant population can become the bridge between old history and later reoccupation.

Transformation boundary:

The distinctive characters, Gym succession, exact geography and Rejuvenation plot remain source material only. Ouros may use the structural pattern of gradual decline plus partial return.

## Source 3 — Acrylic Town resident recruitment in the Pokémon Z fangame

Sources:

- https://pokemonzfangame.com/acrylic-town-residents-guide/
- https://pokemonzfangame.com/full-documentation/

Observed structure:

- A once-active settlement has lost most of its population.
- The player encounters people elsewhere who are looking for a new home or fresh start.
- New arrivals gradually make the town feel populated and restore functions.
- The same settlement therefore changes across many unrelated side activities rather than through one reconstruction cutscene.

Useful structure for Ouros:

A settlement return can be distributed across individual decisions, households, institutions and services. The player can create conditions that make return possible without personally deciding who must live there.

Important anti-pattern for Ouros:

The source often maps recruited residents directly to utility unlocks. Ouros should not treat people as collectible upgrade tokens. A returning or incoming actor needs their own motives, household constraints, work role, availability and agency. A settlement can gain a service because a resident actually operates it, but that outcome must flow through existing Staffing/Storefront/Residential state.

Do not copy:

- recruitable NPCs, prices, sidequest requirements, service lists, rewards or utility mechanics.

## Source 4 — Over There! A World War One Pokémon Campaign retrospective

Source:

- https://pokemontabletop.com/over-there-a-world-war-one-pokemon-campaign-a-retrospective/

Observed PTU campaign structure:

- The party receives an abandoned village as an operational base.
- Each in-game day alternates preparation/exploration with recurring nighttime pressure.
- The village therefore functions as persistent staging geography whose state matters across sessions.
- Information about the surrounding region accumulates while the group keeps returning to the same place.

Reusable Ouros lessons:

1. An abandoned settlement can have a second life before formal resettlement.
2. Temporary operational use should not automatically convert to ownership or permanent occupancy.
3. Repeated occupation can accumulate caches, repairs, defenses, routes, knowledge and wear.
4. The same place can shift from `abandoned settlement` to `field base` to another later use without changing location identity.

Important retrospective warning:

The source explicitly discusses the risk of a premise bait-and-switch. Ouros should preserve player-facing expectations when an abandoned place becomes a campaign hub; the setting can reveal deeper history without invalidating the activity players chose.

Do not copy:

- wartime premise, supernatural antagonists, nightly attack structure, characters or campaign plot.

## Source 5 — Late Starters episode 11, Hoothoot Havoc

Public actual-play episode page:

- https://open.spotify.com/episode/6FiWfIYpcJb0j64QZ8Krgi

Observed structure from the public episode description:

- The party chooses to stay in an abandoned village.
- The supposedly empty human settlement is already occupied in another sense by mysterious Pokémon activity.
- The location becomes a mystery/encounter space because its current ecology conflicts with the human assumption that abandonment means emptiness.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

A depopulated settlement should be eligible for ecological succession. Wild Pokémon may use buildings, gardens, drains, roofs, courtyards, roads or former service spaces. Later human return must respond to observed current ecology rather than treating the place as a blank asset waiting to be reclaimed.

Do not copy:

- species, mystery, characters, episode sequence or resolution.

## Source 6 — Inter-generational Kanto location change

Source:

- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto

Observed structure:

Across generations, the same regional geography changes function:

- a power plant becomes functional;
- a tower changes institutional use;
- graves are moved to a different memorial site;
- a cave can collapse and later be re-excavated;
- facilities close for renovations;
- leadership changes can remodel existing institutions.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Persistent-world continuity is stronger when a location ID survives functional change. `Former use`, `current use`, `closed`, `converted`, `restored`, `relocated service` and `reopened` should be historical states of one place where continuity supports that, not excuses to spawn replacement locations.

## Cross-source pattern 1 — Abandonment is not zero occupancy

A label such as `abandoned`, `evacuated`, `closed`, `ghost town` or `ruin` may hide several different facts:

- no permanent residents;
- a handful of permanent residents;
- seasonal residents;
- caretakers;
- field teams;
- temporary shelter use;
- service staff commuting in;
- wild Pokémon occupancy;
- institutional presence without households;
- visitors/tourism;
- technically inaccessible zones alongside active zones.

Ouros should store the underlying facts and treat the label as a public or descriptive claim.

## Cross-source pattern 2 — Return is a staged process

A useful return sequence can include:

1. access/survey;
2. hazard/condition review;
3. minimal service or caretaker presence;
4. provisional occupancy;
5. household return or new arrival;
6. selective service reopening;
7. infrastructure repair;
8. ecology/stewardship negotiation;
9. broader reoccupation;
10. stable new-use state.

Not every settlement should reach step 10. Some may remain partially occupied indefinitely.

## Cross-source pattern 3 — Institutions can outlive their buildings

An institution can preserve identity while operating elsewhere.

Useful state distinctions:

- home location;
- temporary operating location;
- service continuity state;
- displaced staff;
- archived assets;
- public identity;
- rebuilding proposal;
- return readiness;
- alternate-site history.

This should use the institution model already present in Public Memory and the relevant service layer rather than creating a new institutional authority system.

## Cross-source pattern 4 — Ecological succession creates legitimate new state

When humans leave, the space does not freeze.

Possible observable changes:

- vegetation enters roads or structures;
- wild Pokémon use former human spaces;
- water drainage changes;
- unused routes become habitat corridors;
- buildings become nesting/rest surfaces;
- former noise or light patterns disappear;
- damaged structures create shelter or barriers;
- human return creates new disturbance.

These are world/ecology facts. They do not automatically create a protected-area designation, capture restriction or tactical terrain effect.

## Cross-source pattern 5 — Restoration should not rewind history

Reopening a settlement should preserve:

- former residents and their moves;
- abandoned-period uses;
- repairs;
- demolition or lost structures;
- ecological changes;
- temporary institutional sites;
- public notices;
- memorial/history records;
- new residents;
- changed routes and services.

The target is a new stable state, not a pristine snapshot of the settlement before decline.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

This pass introduces no new combat rule.

Potential mechanically rich scenes around abandoned or returning settlements may involve:

- unstable terrain;
- weather exposure;
- narrow movement;
- retreat/evacuation;
- Push/Pull/knockback;
- Intercept;
- protected noncombatants;
- territorial wild Pokémon;
- destructible/protected structures;

Every such behavior must use governing PTU/Caelo rules plus current AutoPTU evidence. A visual ruined floor, smoke plume, vegetation patch or flooded cellar in Minecraft does not become tactical terrain, hazard damage or movement penalty by narrative declaration.

## Design directions for Pass 84

1. Preserve one settlement identity across inhabited, declining, evacuated, low-occupancy, provisional-return and stable-new-use phases.
2. Separate occupancy, physical condition, service capacity, access, ecology and public label.
3. Record remnant residents and caretakers instead of treating abandoned places as population zero.
4. Allow temporary operational use without inferring ownership or permanent settlement.
5. Model resident return through individual/household decisions owned by Residential state.
6. Let services reopen independently rather than flipping the whole settlement at once.
7. Preserve alternate institutional sites and displaced operations.
8. Treat new wild Pokémon use as current ecological evidence, not as empty-space decoration.
9. Require explicit ordinary-return/reopening handoffs after crisis or restoration.
10. Keep former states queryable for Chronicle, Public Memory and mystery play.
