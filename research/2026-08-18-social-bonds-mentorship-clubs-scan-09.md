# Social Bonds, Mentorship, Clubs & Learning — Research Scan 09

Status: research/provenance only. Nothing in this file is Ouros canon.

## Research question

How can Ouros make trainers, Pokémon partners, mentors, peers, clubs, schools and professional communities matter over months of play without reducing relationships to a single affection bar or forcing authored romance/social outcomes onto players?

## Sources reviewed

### Pokémon Scarlet / Violet — academy structure and parallel life paths
Source: Pokémon.com — Pokémon Scarlet and Pokémon Violet
https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokemon-video-games/pokemon-scarlet-and-pokemon-violet

The official game framing uses an academy as a social anchor while allowing several major paths to be pursued in an open order. The school is not only a tutorial location: it connects the player to recurring characters and gives a shared institutional context for otherwise different adventures. The Indigo Disk extends this with exchange-student status at Blueberry Academy.

Reusable structure:
- one institution can support several player identities rather than one class/job;
- a common social hub can connect otherwise independent adventure arcs;
- exchange or visiting status creates natural reasons to meet new peers and cultures;
- institutional belonging does not require linear progression.

### Blueberry Academy League Club and cooperative extracurriculars
Source: official Pokémon Scarlet/Violet DLC site
https://www.pokemon.co.jp/ex/sv_dlc/ja/features/230808_05/

The League Club is a shared space where players interact with peers, undertake extracurricular objectives, contribute resources to other clubs, unlock functions and invite special instructors. Some Blueberry Quests have group variants.

Reusable structure:
- clubs can act as persistent multiplayer/social containers;
- small repeatable activities can fund visible upgrades to a shared space;
- contributions can change who visits and what services become available;
- group assignments create cooperation that is not simply co-op combat;
- guest instructors allow mentorship networks to evolve over time.

Do not copy Blueberry Points, its club roster or specific quest content.

### Official friendship mechanics as a relationship precedent
Source: Pokémon Brilliant Diamond / Shining Pearl Trainer Guide
https://diamondpearl.pokemon.com/en-au/trainersguide/fundamentals/raising/

The official friendship system increases through repeated shared activity such as traveling, leveling, walking and participating in contests, while harmful or neglectful treatment can reduce it. The important design lesson is that relationship state is affected by accumulated behavior rather than one dialogue choice.

Reusable structure:
- persistent bonds should respond to a history of actions;
- several kinds of shared activity can contribute to a relationship;
- negative events can matter without permanently locking a relationship;
- visible behavioral feedback can communicate a bond without exposing a raw numeric score.

Do not import mainline friendship combat bonuses into PTU. Any mechanical trainer/Pokémon loyalty effects must come from the governing PTU/Caelo sources.

### PTU campaign retrospective — Wakino Privateers
Source: Pokémon Tabletop RPG official blog
https://pokemontabletop.com/wakino-privateers-a-game-of-love-extreme-power-levels-and-actually-not-that-much-piracy/

This long-running PTU campaign retrospective repeatedly describes PCs changing through friendships, rivalries, forgiveness, ideological conflict and long-term cooperation. It also describes a useful emergent technique: secondary NPC crewmembers were given trainer sheets and used for side missions when the main party was occupied. Those secondary actors produced consequential outcomes that forced the GM to revise later plans.

Reusable structure:
- relationships should allow direction changes rather than predetermined arcs;
- forgiveness, trust repair and ideological disagreement can be stateful long-term processes;
- supporting cast can temporarily become active agents instead of static quest dispensers;
- side teams can cause real world-state changes that the primary party later encounters;
- social bonds can survive disagreement without requiring binary ally/enemy states.

Do not copy campaign characters, jokes, plot events or relationships.

### PTU campaign retrospective — Tales of Visiwa
Source: Pokémon Tabletop RPG official blog
https://pokemontabletop.com/tales-of-visiwa-a-retrospective/

The retrospective shows PCs entering a campaign with distinct careers and personal motivations, then changing through discoveries, relationships and unexpected incidents. Some characters returned to study, became experts in new domains, changed careers or developed roles that were not their starting concepts.

Reusable structure:
- character expertise can evolve because of lived events;
- an NPC or PC can become a mentor after gaining experience rather than being born into the role;
- academic/professional identity can be a narrative arc rather than a static class label;
- learning can emerge from field experience and social connections.

### PTU campaign seeds — institutions built by players
Source: Pokémon Tabletop RPG official blog
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-the-road-to-tomorrow/

The Talent Scouts and Holon Institute seeds explicitly place players on the institution-building side of Pokémon society. Players may recruit Gym Leaders, teachers and researchers, decide which facilities to build, select new Trainers and shape institutional priorities.

Reusable structure:
- senior players can eventually become recruiters, mentors or founders;
- institutional growth can be an endgame/progression path;
- selecting who receives opportunities is itself a consequential story decision;
- schools, research groups and clubs can be created by players rather than existing only as authored scenery.

### Pokémon Elite — broad relationship state, time and clubs
Source: Eevee Expo
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/8866/

This in-development fangame describes relationships beyond dating: friendship, rivalry, strained bonds and distance. Events can be triggered by time, relationship state, participation or non-participation in activities. Clubs, instant messages, social links and student culture form a large part of the proposed experience.

Reusable structure:
- relationship type should not be inferred from a single scalar;
- absence and inconsistency can affect social state;
- messages and invitations can be lightweight social callbacks;
- clubs can create overlapping networks rather than isolated NPC quest lines;
- NPCs do not need to like the player for the relationship to remain narratively valuable.

Because this is a fan work, only these abstract systems are used. No characters, prose, academy details or plot are imported.

### Pokémon Supernova — school life, affinity and world exploration
Source: Eevee Expo
https://www.eeveeexpo.com/threads/8804/

The project describes student relationships affecting story direction, exploration programs, side quests and a campus connected to regional mysteries.

Reusable structure:
- social and exploration progression can feed one another;
- peers can become sources of optional field opportunities;
- relationships can change dialogue and access without becoming mandatory gates.

### Pokémon Blooming Beast — conversation knowledge unlocks
Source: Eevee Expo released games index / project description
https://www.eeveeexpo.com/released-games/

Its public description explains a conversation system in which new topics become available as the player learns more about people, regional history and relationships. Conversations can also affect willingness to support the protagonist.

Reusable structure:
- conversation options can be generated from known facts rather than generic dialogue trees;
- discovering a fact can unlock questions for multiple NPCs;
- different people can have different opinions about the same known entity;
- support can emerge from accumulated interaction rather than a single quest completion.

### Public Pokémon academy roleplay structures
Sources:
https://forums.serebii.net/threads/pokemon-regional-academy-ra-pg-14.467249/
https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/pokemon-academy-application.20589/

Public forum RPs have repeatedly used academy structures to combine battling, Pokémon bonding, breeding, research, contests and ordinary interpersonal RP. One especially useful community rule is explicit consent before defining a relationship with another player's character.

Reusable structure:
- educational institutions naturally support multiple Pokémon professions;
- mandatory/shared coursework can create cross-specialty interaction;
- electives create identity and subcommunities;
- player-to-player relationship state must respect explicit agency and consent.

The individual forum plots and characters are not reused.

### Distributed mentoring in fan communities
Sources:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01425
https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14111

Research on online fan communities describes distributed mentoring: useful feedback and learning can come from a network rather than a single designated expert. Relationship networks can have layers, with frequent reciprocal interactions more likely to contain substantive feedback.

Reusable structure for Ouros:
- a trainer's development can be supported by several partial mentors;
- peers can teach different skills or perspectives;
- mentorship can be reciprocal and change direction over time;
- a club/community can retain expertise even when one mentor leaves.

This is used as a general community-design reference, not as a Pokémon rule source.

## Cross-check against supplied PTU / Caelo material

The supplied PTU Core Rulebook already encourages GMs to create short dossiers around each PC's background, skills, interests and desired development, and recommends session tasks that connect different PCs' interests and Pokémon. This supports personalized social hooks but does not authorize automated changes to a PC's personality or goals.

The supplied Caelo Player's Guide explicitly treats Social threads as roleplay-oriented and does not cap them, to encourage character development. Caelo also defines a Rivalry framework with prerequisites, showing that recurring trainer relationships can have formal state; Ouros should not assume those exact mechanics until the intended Caelo carry-over is decided.

The supplied Caelo character-creation material makes concept, Skill Background, Features and basic descriptions explicit parts of a Trainer. Those are legitimate inputs for proposing learning opportunities and mentors, but generated content must not grant Edges, Features, Skill Ranks or other progression outside established rules.

## Design conclusions

1. Avoid a universal relationship score. Track several interpretable dimensions and concrete remembered events.
2. Relationship state should be evidence-based. The system may record observed actions and expressed statements, not hidden emotions as canonical facts unless authored.
3. PC-to-PC relationships require strict agency boundaries. The system can remember shared events but cannot declare friendship, romance, betrayal or reconciliation on behalf of players.
4. Clubs and institutions are strong content routers. They can combine social scenes, field work, training, research, contests and community projects without forcing all players into one main plot.
5. Mentorship should be a network. Different NPCs or players can teach different competencies and perspectives.
6. Learning arcs work best when tied to real tasks and outcomes rather than passive XP-only progression.
7. A mentor can retire, fail, disagree or be surpassed while the institution retains continuity.
8. Absence should be a valid social signal but never automatically treated as hostility.
9. Supporting NPC teams can perform bounded off-screen or side operations whose outcomes feed world state.
10. Conversation should be knowledge-aware: new facts create new topics, questions and disagreements.

## Copyright / provenance boundary

Only abstract mechanics, structural patterns and publicly described features are retained. No protected dialogue, unique character, scene prose, relationship plot or distinctive campaign sequence is copied into Ouros.

## Open research gaps

- PTU-specific rules for Pokémon Loyalty and any Caelo modifications need a dedicated mechanical extraction before implementation.
- Determine whether AutoPTU currently models trainer/Pokémon loyalty or persistent noncombat relationship data.
- Decide whether clubs are player-created, authored, or both.
- Determine how much social state should be shared across multiplayer parties versus private to one character.
- Define consent rules for PC-to-PC bonds, mentorship and romance before any automated social inference is enabled.
- Research Contests/performances as a future dedicated social-competition layer rather than folding them into this one.
