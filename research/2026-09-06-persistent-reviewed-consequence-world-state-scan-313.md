# Persistent reviewed-consequence world state scan — pass 313

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY / NOT CANON
Date: 2026-09-06

## Question

How can Ouros preserve a long-lived world consequence after the evidence behind a decision changes, while keeping the original decision, later review and selective repair separately inspectable across restart?

This pass deliberately searches for structures that support persistent world history, revisitable locations, long campaigns and layered exploration. It does not import protected characters, plots, dialogue, maps, Pokémon variants or homebrew rules.

## Repository check before research

The recursive repository inventory was inspected first. Passes 306–312 already cover evidence custody, custody assessment lineage, correction propagation, decision dependency, decision review and selective consequence repair. The missing implementation seam was atomic persistence of the three newest registries inside the global world checkpoint. Existing canon files were not edited.

Searches for `Reckless Rollers` and `Pokemon Odyssey Yggdrasil Talrega` returned no prior repository hits, so those sources are new to the recorded research corpus.

## Source: The Reckless Rollers — PTU actual play

Public source: https://rephonic.com/podcasts/the-reckless-rollers

The public listing describes a long-running actual-play campaign using Pokémon Tabletop United, with roughly 160 episodes and shorter episodes that sometimes shift perspective to Pokémon in the trainers' parties. Episode metadata also shows downtime and travel occurring between larger jobs.

Reusable structure for Ouros:

A durable campaign can move between major objectives, quiet travel, local jobs and changed perspectives without treating each session as a reset. Consequences can remain latent while attention moves elsewhere, then matter again when the party returns.

Transformation boundary:

Ouros does not copy the show's characters, championship structure, locations, dialogue or episode plots. The reusable lesson is only long-form continuity with variable scene scale.

## Source: Super Pokémon Online — PTU living-world community

Public source: https://www.reddit.com/r/PokemonTabletop/comments/1mkct0y/super_pok%C3%A9mon_online_ptu_living_world_rpg/

The campaign advertisement explicitly describes a PTU sandbox where the economy can shift from player actions, exploration can reveal new locations and reputation can persist. The play-by-post format also implies that different actors can advance at different real-world times.

Reusable structure for Ouros:

World state should outlive the scene that created it. A discovered route, changed price, damaged reputation or rescinded restriction should remain part of later play until another authored event changes it. Asynchronous actors should not require a global reset or omniscient synchronization.

Transformation boundary:

No Fiore setting material, guilds, NPCs, server procedures or homebrew mechanics are adopted. The source is evidence for a persistent-world campaign pattern only.

## Source: Pokémon Odyssey — fan-made dungeon RPG structure

Public source: https://romhaven.com/games/pokemon-odyssey/

The public overview describes a Pokémon ROM hack structured around repeated descent into a large labyrinth, sidequests, gathering points, naval exploration, shop upgrades and optional objectives. Progress is tied to learning the space and unlocking options rather than only moving from one badge checkpoint to the next.

Reusable structure for Ouros:

A location can accumulate operational history. Repeated visits can reveal different layers because access, services, knowledge and objectives have changed. A dungeon or service complex therefore does not need to be consumed by one visit; persistent state can make the same geometry support new narrative questions later.

Transformation boundary:

Ouros does not copy Talrega, the Yggdrasil Labyrinth, characters, floors, boss designs or story. The reusable element is iterative revisitation with persistent upgrades and changing objectives.

## Cross-source synthesis

These sources converge on a useful worldbuilding rule: continuity becomes more interesting when the world preserves both the current operational state and the history that produced it.

For Ouros that means a reopened gate should still have a traceable closure order; a corrected report should not erase the earlier report; a repaired consequence should survive restart; and NPCs who never received the correction may continue acting on older knowledge. The resulting disagreement is authored history, not simulation noise.
