# Pass 153 — Ritual, tradition, pilgrimage, and site-memory research scan

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY
Canon effect: NONE. This file does not approve religions, sacred sites, Legendary appearances, supernatural effects, or historical claims for Ouros.
Date: 2026-08-30

## Why this pass exists

The repository already has strong layers for travel, material culture, archaeology, archives, public memory, organizations, claims, investigation, science, festivals/events, and campaign continuity. What remained under-specified was living tradition itself: practices that communities repeat, reinterpret, transmit, interrupt, revive, dispute, or attach to routes and places over time.

This is useful for Ouros because a place can matter to people even when its oldest story cannot be proved. A route can remain culturally important after its physical course changes. A festival can preserve a misunderstood event. A community can venerate a Pokémon without proving that the Pokémon is present, divine, or mechanically empowered.

The design objective is therefore historical and social continuity, not a universal religion system.

## Repository fit and duplication check

The complete repository tree was inspected before writing. No dedicated ritual/tradition/pilgrimage continuity layer existed. Adjacent responsibilities remain authoritative:

- Travel/Expedition owns physical journey state, routes, arrivals, delays, and traversal consequences.
- Material Culture, heritage, archaeology, archives, and provenance layers own physical object identity, custody, dating evidence, and documentary lineage.
- Public-memory and testimony layers own attributed recollection and claims.
- Investigation owns hypotheses and inference about what historically occurred.
- World Agency owns what actors know, want, decide, and do.
- Organization Lineage owns organizations, branches, mergers, succession, and institutional continuity.
- Civic/governance/credential layers own lawful authority, access, office, and permission.
- Ecology/Science owns observed ecological or technical facts.
- Battle Institutions owns sanctioned battle meaning where applicable.

Pass 153 should own only tradition identity, variants, transmission, practice episodes, observance history, pilgrimage participation, community-attributed significance, reinterpretation, revival, dispute, and cessation.

## Public-source research

### 1. Sinnoh myths and Canalave Library — written preservation does not collapse myth into verified history

Bulbapedia's compilation of Sinnoh myths documents multiple in-world myths preserved through Canalave Library and other sources in the Sinnoh games. The useful abstraction is not the specific cosmology. It is that a region can preserve several old formulations, customary sayings, and textual witnesses whose existence is factual while their literal interpretation remains a separate question.

Reusable lessons:

- an archived text proves that a version was recorded, not that every proposition inside it is canonically true;
- similar passages in later/earlier traditions can support lineage or influence without proving direct descent;
- customary language can outlive the context that first produced it;
- multiple regional versions can coexist without forcing a single master text.

Source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sinnoh_myths

### 2. Kitakami and the Festival of Masks — tradition can encode a durable misunderstanding and still become socially real

The Teal Mask provides a particularly strong narrative pattern. Kitakami's public story about Ogerpon and the Loyal Three differs from the hidden family account of the historical incident. The public interpretation shaped memorials, a festival, and activities that persisted for generations. The festival itself also changed after the remembered event.

The reusable lesson is not to copy the plot or its characters. It is that four layers can diverge:

1. an event happened;
2. witnesses formed an interpretation;
3. that interpretation became a transmitted story;
4. practices and places accumulated around the story.

Later evidence can revise the historical interpretation without erasing the years in which the tradition was genuinely practiced.

Reusable lessons:

- a false or incomplete historical interpretation can produce authentic later social history;
- a monument can be younger than the story it represents;
- a festival can be older than one of its current explanations;
- family transmission and public transmission can preserve different versions;
- revealing new evidence should revise claims, not retroactively delete old practice episodes.

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/The_Teal_Mask
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Festival_of_Masks
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ogre_Oustin%27
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Loyalty_Plaza

### 3. Crown Tundra / Calyrex — extraordinary-Pokémon traditions can persist after collective certainty weakens

Crown Tundra material preserves stories of Calyrex as a historical benefactor associated with harvests, even though contemporary collective memory and belief are not equivalent to direct observation. The useful pattern is a community retaining names, stories, objects, celebrations, or habits after confidence in their original explanation has weakened.

Reusable lessons:

- remembered association with an extraordinary Pokémon does not prove current presence;
- loss of certainty can coexist with persistence of practice;
- material anchors, sayings, seasonal observances, and tourism may preserve different fragments of the same tradition;
- rediscovery should not automatically validate every inherited claim.

Source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Legendary_Pok%C3%A9mon#Calyrex,_Glastrier,_and_Spectrier

### 4. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — living heritage changes because communities keep recreating it

UNESCO's current Intangible Cultural Heritage materials describe living heritage as practices, knowledge, and traditions transmitted among communities and continually recreated in response to environment and history. The particularly useful design lesson is that continuity does not require frozen sameness. A practice can remain meaningfully continuous while changing form, route, participants, language, or interpretation.

Ouros should borrow only the continuity principle. It should not import UNESCO status, legal regimes, safeguarding bureaucracy, authenticity criteria, or real-world cultural classifications into canon.

Reusable lessons:

- transmission is an eventful process, not a static property;
- community participation matters more than an imagined unchanged original form;
- revival after interruption should be recorded as revival, not silently rewritten as uninterrupted continuity;
- different generations can legitimately reinterpret a practice;
- external labels should not overwrite local self-description.

Sources:
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/
- https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/52066-EN.pdf

### 5. PTU campaign precedent — sacred sites and legendary-scale belief work best as setting commitments, not assumptions smuggled in by the engine

The official Pokémon Tabletop community retrospective for Tales of Visiwa describes a long-running PTU campaign with abandoned shrines, forgotten gods, rival cults, supernatural assumptions, exploration, and faction conflict. Its value here is structural: PTU campaigns can deliberately choose a metaphysically rich setting, but that choice belongs to campaign/world canon rather than being inferred from a generic battle implementation.

A separate public PTU campaign listing for Valoryn similarly uses legends, ancient bonds, Titans, seals, guild control of travel, and a festival as authored setting premises. These are useful demonstrations that tradition and myth can organize regional exploration and faction arcs. They are not PTU rules authority.

Reusable lessons:

- metaphysical truth must be an explicit campaign decision;
- shrines can work as exploration anchors before their stories are resolved;
- multiple factions can interpret the same extraordinary phenomenon differently;
- cultural practice can provide hooks without guaranteeing a Legendary encounter;
- a festival can introduce regional values before the campaign escalates.

Sources:
- https://pokemontabletop.com/tales-of-visiwa-a-retrospective/
- https://www.warwicktabletop.co.uk/events/809/

### 6. PTU Sage material — source-specific mechanics must remain source-specific

Public mirrors of PTU material describe Sage as an Occult Education-linked class whose flavor can include guardianship of shrines or historical monuments and whose actual mechanics are explicit Features/Moves. This is a useful guardrail: narrative language around blessings or sacred sites cannot be generalized into a universal ritual bonus.

If Ouros uses any Sage Feature, Blessing, Move, Ability, or other PTU mechanic, it must be verified individually against the project's approved PTU/Caelo sources and engine contracts. Flavor alone cannot authorize an effect.

Source used as a discovery aid, not final rules authority: https://pturpg.wikidot.com/sage

## Design deductions for Ouros

### Living continuity needs an event history

A tradition should be able to change without being replaced every time. Useful events include:

- first attestation;
- adoption by a new community or branch;
- route change;
- practice-element addition/removal;
- translation or renaming;
- seasonal scheduling change;
- interruption;
- local cessation;
- revival;
- reinterpretation;
- dispute;
- institutional sponsorship beginning/ending;
- material-anchor replacement;
- tourism/commercial adaptation;
- discovery of older evidence;
- accepted ambiguity.

### Claims need attributed scopes

Examples:

- `THIS_ROUTE_IS_TRADITIONALLY_ASSOCIATED_WITH_X`
- `LOCAL_GROUP_A_SAYS_THE_SITE_MARKS_Y`
- `ARCHIVE_Z_ATTESTS_THIS_PRACTICE_BY_DATE_D`
- `CURRENT_PARTICIPANTS_INTERPRET_THE_RITE_AS_Q`

None of these means:

- X literally occurred;
- Y is metaphysically true;
- the route causes a supernatural effect;
- the practice is the oldest possible form;
- all residents agree.

### Extraordinary Pokémon require stricter separation

A Legendary/Mythical/rare Pokémon may be:

- named in a story;
- represented in art;
- invoked in a ritual;
- associated with a route or site;
- claimed to have appeared historically;
- reported in a modern sighting;
- directly observed in canon.

Those are seven different evidence states. They must never collapse into one boolean such as `legendary_present=true`.

## Anti-copying transformation notes

No Pokémon plot, character, shrine, festival, deity, dialogue, or distinctive ritual is imported into Ouros. The transformed patterns are:

- divergent tradition versions;
- public memory becoming practice;
- practices changing while remaining continuous;
- material anchors outliving explanations;
- mythic attribution staying separate from observed fact;
- campaign-authored metaphysics staying separate from PTU combat rules.
