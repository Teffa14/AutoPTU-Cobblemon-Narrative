# Layered snowpack, mountain-pass evidence, and winter-route research — Pass 323

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE — NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-07

## Research question

How can Ouros use winter mountain travel as persistent world content without turning visible snow, a past storm, a cleared road, or franchise flavor into hidden mechanical truth?

This pass deliberately avoids real-world avalanche prediction procedures. Public avalanche-science sources are used only for high-level narrative structure: snow can preserve layered history, hazardous conditions can be spatially uneven, old surface observations can become stale, and a route that looks used does not prove every connected slope safe.

Repository inspection before authoring found no existing snow/avalanche/snowpack/winter/cornice workstream in the indexed narrative tree. Recent passes already cover hydraulic infrastructure, acoustics, artificial light, olfaction, geomagnetic navigation, reclaimed extraction landscapes, post-fire mosaics, seasonal floodplains, and pollinator phenology. Pass 323 therefore opens a distinct winter-route evidence family rather than re-skinning those concepts.

## Public-source findings

### A buried layer can remain relevant after the visible weather event has passed

Avalanche.org describes persistent weak layers as long-lived weaknesses that can remain buried for weeks or months. Its persistent-slab material also emphasizes spatial variability and warns that previous tracks on a slope do not establish stability.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

`recent surface appearance != complete terrain history`

A world feature may contain older state that remains relevant after plowing, new snowfall, refreezing, traffic, or a calm weather interval. Narrative systems should therefore keep event history, current surface observation, authored feature state, and institutional interpretation separate.

Sources:
- Avalanche.org, Persistent Weak Layers: https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/snowpack/weak-layer/persistent-weak-layers/
- Avalanche.org, Persistent Slab: https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/avalanche/avalanche-problems/avalanche-problem-type/persistent-slab/
- Avalanche.org, Weak Layer: https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/snowpack/weak-layer/

### Wind can make nearby winter features differ materially

Avalanche.org describes wind slabs as deposits formed when wind moves snow from exposed areas into leeward or cross-loaded terrain. The important design abstraction is heterogeneity: two nearby route features can accumulate different conditions even during the same weather event.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Do not author a mountain zone as one global `SNOW_STATE`. Persistent features such as a ridge, gully, sheltered bench, road cut, bridge approach, and upper traverse should be able to retain different observations and route decisions.

Source:
- Avalanche.org, Wind Slab: https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/avalanche/avalanche-problems/avalanche-problem-type/wind-slab/

### Visible warning signs are observations, not an omniscient hazard meter

Avalanche.org documents collapses and cracking as warning signs of instability. NPS likewise notes that avalanche hazards may not be clearly visible and that winter trails can enter avalanche terrain.

For Ouros, an observed crack, collapse, old debris fan, drift, blocked marker, or recent maintenance record can become evidence with provenance. None should automatically expose the hidden full hazard model to every NPC.

Sources:
- Avalanche.org, Collapse / whumpf: https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/snowpack/snowpack-observations/signs-of-instability-red-flags/collapse-or-whumpf/
- National Park Service, Be Avalanche Aware, updated 2026-01-30: https://www.nps.gov/places/be-avalanche-aware.htm

### Snowpack is also infrastructure and ecology, not only danger

USGS describes mountain snowpack as seasonal water storage that later supplies lower-elevation landscapes. This makes a winter pass useful beyond a one-scene hazard: its snow history can influence spring water, route maintenance, ecological timing, work schedules, and later revisits.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

A winter route should leave consequences after the crossing. A deep drift cleared today can become a water-management, erosion, access, habitat, or scheduling issue later without requiring the original encounter to remain active.

Sources:
- USGS, Snow, Ice, and Permafrost: https://www.usgs.gov/science/science-explorer/climate/snow-ice-and-permafrost
- USGS, Aeolian dust deposition to Rocky Mountain snowpacks: https://www.usgs.gov/news/aeolian-dust-deposition-rocky-mountain-snowpacks

### Pokémon can belong to the environment without becoming rule shortcuts

The official Pokémon Pokédex describes Hisuian Avalugg moving along steep mountain paths through deep snow. The same official page identifies Avalugg and Bergmite as Iceberg Pokémon and exposes game Ability text, but that videogame behavior is not an AutoPTU contract.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Avalugg or Bergmite can be ecological candidates for an eventual approved mountain region. Their presence may help make a snowy corridor feel inhabited rather than designed only around human travel. Their Pokédex flavor cannot grant free snow traversal, hazard immunity, route-clearing power, forced movement, Ice Body implementation, or any PTU modifier unless project-authoritative rules and implementation evidence support it.

Source:
- Pokémon Pokédex, Avalugg: https://www.pokemon.com/br/pokedex/avalugg

### Pokémon Ranger shows an environmental encounter built around shelter geometry

Public documentation of Mt. Sorbet in Pokémon Ranger: Guardian Signs describes avalanche zones where stone pillars function as physical shelter from the moving hazard. The reusable pattern is environmental geometry that communicates a survival objective rather than a pure defeat-all-enemies objective.

Ouros transformation:

A mechanically rich winter encounter may eventually use authored refuge features, safe staging pockets, exposed crossings, and an objective such as reaching a shelter, moving a stranded actor, or holding a route until conditions change. Ouros must not copy Mt. Sorbet, Articuno, the Pokémon Pinchers, its map, scripted avalanche sequence, or puzzle solution.

Source:
- Bulbapedia, Mt. Sorbet: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mt._Sorbet

### PTU actual play demonstrates that an original region can sustain travel as part of a larger campaign

P.U.C.L. PTU is a public actual-play series using Pokémon Tabletop United in the original Belleza region. The useful precedent is modest: a custom PTU region can support an ensemble journey over time rather than treating each location as an isolated tactical map.

Ouros transformation:

A winter crossing can be a recurring piece of world infrastructure. Its condition can affect later NPC travel, commerce, rescue, rival schedules, deliveries, ecological observation, and return journeys without copying Belleza, its cast, dialogue, plots, or encounters.

Source:
- Podnews, P.U.C.L. PTU episode listing: https://podnews.net/podcast/i5kev/episodes

## Reusable Ouros model

For winter mountain features, preserve at least these distinct records when they matter:

`feature identity -> weather/event history -> authored current physical state -> bounded observation -> observer/receiver -> interpretation -> route/institution decision -> feature-scoped consequence -> later revisit evidence`

Optional snow-history evidence can be attached to a feature without becoming a combat status. Examples include an observed old debris boundary, a maintenance cut, a newly covered marker, a reported collapse sound, a drift distribution, a dated closure order, or a field team's note that a previous observation is stale.

## Design lessons

1. A cleared road and a safe upper slope are separate claims.
2. Evidence of prior traffic does not certify future or adjacent route safety.
3. One storm can create different local states across nearby features.
4. Old evidence remains historically true even after its operational meaning changes.
5. Route authorities should decide on explicit features or segments rather than applying a single mountain-wide safety flag.
6. A later reopening can coexist with monitoring, a separate closure, or an unresolved observation elsewhere.
7. The same pass can matter again during thaw, runoff, delayed delivery, seasonal migration, or institutional review.
8. Pokémon ecology should provide place identity and additional evidence only after species behavior is source-backed; it must never act as a universal danger detector.
