# Pass 320 research — fire mosaics, refugia, and post-fire recovery as persistent landscape state

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY / NOT CANON

Date: 2026-09-06

## Why this scan

A recursive repository inventory plus targeted searches found no prior Ouros workstream centered on wildfire, prescribed fire, burn mosaics, post-fire refugia, or recovery monitoring. Pass 319 opened layered industrial landscapes; Passes 315–318 covered acoustic, artificial-light, olfactory, and geomagnetic evidence. Pass 320 therefore opens a disturbance-and-recovery axis in which a single event changes a landscape unevenly and creates several valid temporal layers.

The design question is how Ouros can represent a burned landscape without reducing it to a uniform "destroyed biome", a binary quest flag, or a permanent combat hazard.

## External source scan

### Fire perimeters conceal heterogeneous local effects

Source: U.S. National Park Service, Yosemite National Park, Wildlife and Fire.
https://home.nps.gov/yose/naturescience/wildlife-fire.htm

Source: U.S. National Park Service, Glacier National Park, Fire Ecology.
https://www.nps.gov/glac/learn/nature/fire-ecology.htm

Reusable structure:
- Wildland fire commonly produces a patchwork of effects rather than one uniform condition.
- Burned, lightly affected, heavily affected, and untouched patches can exist near one another.
- Wildlife use changes with the spatial pattern and with recovery over time.

Ouros transformation:
- A world-level fire perimeter is evidence about event extent, not authoritative local terrain state.
- Track feature- or patch-level observations such as canopy loss, surviving cover, fresh regrowth, route obstruction, or refuge use separately.
- Two witnesses can report apparently conflicting conditions while both accurately describing different parts of the same disturbance footprint.

No real-world fire behavior equation, fuel model, safety threshold, or suppression procedure is imported.

### Disturbance creates succession and revisitable state

Source: U.S. National Park Service, Voyageurs National Park, Fire Ecology.
https://home.nps.gov/voya/learn/nature/fire-ecology.htm

Source: U.S. National Park Service, Bryce Canyon National Park, Fire Ecology.
https://www.nps.gov/brca/learn/nature/fire-ecology.htm

Reusable structure:
- Fire can create a mixed landscape containing vegetation at different ages and structural conditions.
- Recovery is temporal: early herbs, shrubs, seedlings, standing dead trees, and later canopy states create different habitat opportunities.
- A location that looks damaged immediately after an event can acquire ecological functions later without erasing the original loss.

Ouros transformation:
- Revisit the same map and allow evidence, routes, shelter, food availability, visibility, and stakeholder priorities to change over months or seasons.
- Preserve disturbance history so later regrowth does not overwrite the fact that a severe event occurred.
- Avoid labeling post-fire habitat as globally "better" or "worse"; evaluate specific species, features, and time windows.

### Burn severity and recovery require more than a perimeter map

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Rapid Assessment of Vegetation Condition after Wildfire thematic burn-severity mosaic, version 10.0, January 2025.
https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS%3A630caa07d34e3b967a8c9524

Source: USGS, Vegetation Recovery after Fire in the Klamath-Siskiyou Region.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2011/3140/

Reusable structure:
- Post-fire assessment distinguishes different severity classes and compares pre/post-event conditions.
- Recovery decisions can depend on soils, hydrology, wildlife, previous disturbance history, management actions, nearby human uses, and future risk.

Ouros transformation:
- A reconnaissance report should record what was actually observed and at what scale.
- Remote, patrol, resident, ranger, and Pokémon-derived observations can disagree because they sample different places or times.
- Restoration decisions can legitimately conflict because actors optimize different features: access, erosion control, habitat refugia, settlement safety, or monitoring.

No satellite index, severity formula, hectare threshold, or restoration prescription becomes an Ouros mechanic.

### South-American post-fire evidence supports refugia and repeated-disturbance questions

Source: USGS publication record, Burn severity controls on postfire Araucaria-Nothofagus regeneration in the Andean Cordillera.
https://www.usgs.gov/publications/burn-severity-controls-postfire-araucaria-nothofagus-regeneration-andean-cordillera

Reusable structure:
- Burn severity influences regeneration.
- Surviving patches and post-fire material legacies can matter for recovery.
- Repeated disturbance can remove refugia that previously supported resilience.

Ouros transformation:
- A surviving green patch inside a larger burn can become a meaningful exploration objective, monitoring site, temporary refuge, or faction priority.
- Protecting a refuge is not equivalent to declaring the whole burned area recovered.
- A later disturbance can target a different part of the same landscape and create consequences that only make sense if prior history is preserved.

This source is geographically relevant to southern South America as research inspiration, but Pass 320 does not assign Ouros a real-world geography, vegetation type, climate, or fire regime.

## Reusable Ouros design lessons

### 1. Event extent, local severity, and current usability are separate facts

A location can be inside a historical fire perimeter while a specific route remains passable. Another patch inside the same perimeter can be severely altered. A third can remain a refuge.

Use separate provenance for:
- event footprint;
- observed local condition;
- inferred severity;
- current route state;
- habitat use;
- management decision.

### 2. The useful mystery is often "why did this patch differ?"

Potential explanations can include topography, previous treatment, fuel continuity, a wet drainage, a road break, local weather, species activity, infrastructure, or incomplete observation. The answer can be multi-causal.

### 3. Recovery is playable world state

A first visit can focus on evacuation or closure. A later visit can focus on surveying. A third can involve reopening one route, protecting a refuge, or investigating unexpected regeneration. Historical state must remain queryable.

### 4. Green patches are not automatically safe and black patches are not automatically dead

Visual presentation should not become hidden ecology truth. A surviving patch can contain unstable access. A blackened patch can host early-succession activity. Observation and interpretation stay separate.

### 5. Faction conflict can arise from prioritization rather than villainy

Potential interests:
- route authority: reopen dependable access;
- fire/ecology steward: preserve refugia and monitor recovery;
- nearby residents: reduce recurrence risk and restore services;
- logistics operator: recover transport capacity;
- researcher: retain untreated comparison areas or monitoring continuity;
- trainer/rescue group: maintain emergency access;
- local Pokémon caretakers: protect currently occupied shelter or feeding patches.

Each can have legitimate evidence and incomplete knowledge.
