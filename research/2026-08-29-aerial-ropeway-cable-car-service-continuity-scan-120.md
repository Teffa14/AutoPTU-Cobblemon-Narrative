## Why this pass exists

A recursive inspection of the current narrative repository found broad Travel/Transport support, Transit Hub passenger continuity, Vertical Circulation for lifts inside or immediately attached to facilities, Weather, Facility Maintenance, Infrastructure Outage, Accessibility, Crisis/Rescue, Volcanic Monitoring, Slope Instability and mountain-operation layers. No dedicated aerial ropeway/cable-car/gondola continuity layer exists.

The gap is operational continuity for an authored exterior line connecting separately authored destinations: stations, line service, carrier trips, interruptions, operator restrictions, restart verification, alternate-route handoffs and the historical/social consequences of a line changing over time.

This pass does not establish that any specific Ouros region has a cable car, gondola, aerial tram or ropeway. Technology, geography, ownership and operating practice remain canon decisions.

## Existing Ouros boundaries retained

Travel owns journeys, connection graphs and route choice. Transit Hubs owns passenger-cohort and hub social continuity. Vertical Circulation owns lifts within or immediately attached to a facility. Facility Maintenance owns faults, work orders, repair and verification. Infrastructure Outage owns upstream dependency loss/restoration. Weather owns observations and forecasts. Crisis/Rescue owns rescue and evacuation operations. Accessibility owns actor-specific route viability and accommodation. AutoPTU owns tactical battle truth. Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics is presentation/playback only.

The new layer should preserve ropeway service state between those owners rather than duplicating them.

## Pokémon sources

### Hoenn Route 112 and Mt. Chimney cable car

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Route_112
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Chimney

Reusable structure: the cable car is a real connection between separately meaningful spaces. The lower station, upper station, summit, Jagged Pass and Fiery Path create a small network rather than a single teleport button. Access to the station can also be blocked by actors even while the transport infrastructure still exists.

Ouros transformation: preserve station access, line service, destination access and alternate physical routes as separate facts. A hostile faction blocking a station does not prove the line is mechanically broken. A working line does not prove the destination beyond the upper terminal is accessible.

The source also supports a useful social pattern: transport infrastructure can be an ordinary part of a mountain location and coexist with tourism, hiking, commerce and larger regional stories.

## Reusable narrative structures

### Service as a changing graph edge

The same line can be OPERATING, LIMITED, SUSPENDED, RESUMING or DECOMMISSIONED while the physical mountain, stations and alternate paths continue to exist. This creates a persistent travel graph instead of a boolean unlock.

### Interrupted journey without instant catastrophe

A carrier can stop or a trip can be interrupted without the narrative engine inventing injury, falling, panic, equipment failure or evacuation. Those consequences require separate evidence and authority.

### Temporary alternatives can become permanent social infrastructure

A closure can redirect hikers, workers, vendors, couriers or visitors through an older trail, shuttle point or market street. Even after service returns, some of those habits and relationships can remain.

### Station access and destination access are different

A lower station can be open while boarding is restricted. A trip can arrive while the summit beyond the station is closed. A destination can remain reachable by another authored route while the line is suspended.

### Public memory persists after technical recovery

A highly visible interruption can alter trust, local stories, business patterns, guide work and how residents describe the mountain. Repair does not erase social consequences.
