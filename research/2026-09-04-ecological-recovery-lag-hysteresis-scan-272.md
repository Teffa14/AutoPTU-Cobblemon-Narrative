## New public research

1. Sha et al. (2026), “Alternative stable states: Evidence from natural ecosystems and implications for ecological restoration,” Ecological Solutions and Evidence. The review emphasizes nonlinear recovery, feedback-maintained degraded states, asymmetric recovery/collapse thresholds, and the need for multiple lines of evidence before diagnosing alternative stable states or hysteresis. Source: https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2688-8319.70239

2. Beisner, Haydon & Cuddington (2003), “Alternative stable states in ecology,” Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment. Reversal or cessation of a perturbation can test whether a system returns toward its earlier state, but hysteresis specifically concerns a different return trajectory under reversed parameter conditions; persistence alone is insufficient to label every delayed recovery as hysteresis. Source: https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/1540-9295%282003%29001%5B0376%3AASSIE%5D2.0.CO%3B2

3. Moreno-Mateos et al. (2017), “Anthropogenic ecosystem disturbance and the recovery debt,” Nature Communications. Recovery can carry an interim deficit relative to a reference even when eventual recovery occurs; different biodiversity and ecosystem-function dimensions can remain below reference for different periods. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5263871/

4. Ferraz et al. (2020), “On the Need to Differentiate the Temporal Trajectories of Ecosystem Structure and Functions in Restoration Programs.” Structure and functions can recover at different rates, supporting dimension-specific recovery rather than a single ecosystem score. Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1940082920910314

## Reusable design lessons

Recovery needs a reference condition with provenance, not an assumed universal “normal.” A disturbance-cessation event starts an assessment window; it does not assert restoration. Response dimensions must remain separate. One dimension can recover while another lags. A persistent shift after cessation warrants a persistent-shift hypothesis; hysteresis requires stronger evidence about history dependence, feedbacks, or asymmetric reversal thresholds. A second active pressure must remain an alternative explanation until tested. Semantic time, not wall-clock ticks, chunk reloads, or restart count, governs assessment windows.
