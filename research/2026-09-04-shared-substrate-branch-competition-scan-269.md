## New public sources

Fukami (2015), Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics 46, “Historical Contingency in Community Assembly: Integrating Niches, Species Pools, and Priority Effects”, DOI 10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-110411-160340. Reusable lesson: order and timing can change later community outcomes through niche preemption and niche modification. Ouros should preserve arrival/history as evidence rather than collapsing a shared-site interaction into a timeless competition value.

Weidlich et al. (2021), Restoration Ecology, “Priority effects and ecological restoration”, DOI 10.1111/rec.13317. Reusable lesson: early occupants may reduce later access through resource preemption or may alter the niche in ways that inhibit or facilitate later occupants. The sign and mechanism of an interaction therefore need separate representation.

Gribben et al. (2009), Ecology Letters 12:1127–1136, “Behavioural interactions between ecosystem engineers control community species richness”, DOI 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01366.x. Reusable lesson: interactions among habitat-modifying organisms can change downstream community structure through behavior as well as physical structure. Ouros should preserve actor/branch-specific mechanisms rather than infer a single habitat-quality scalar.

Gagnon et al. (2020), Journal of Applied Ecology, “Facilitating foundation species: The potential for plant–bivalve interactions to improve habitat restoration success”, DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.13605. Reusable lesson: the same broad interaction can shift between positive and negative outcomes with environmental context. A relationship should therefore be context-bound and re-evaluable instead of globally tagged beneficial or harmful.

## Transformation for Ouros

CANON-ALIGNED: the global species interaction graph already allows `COMPETES_WITH`, `DISPLACES` and `FACILITATES`, but population truth, ecological interpretation and tactical truth remain separate.

PROPOSED: represent shared-substrate conflicts with explicit branch claims. Each branch names the substrate/resource it uses, the mechanism of use, evidence, temporal window, pressure state and interpretation state. A branch may facilitate one downstream condition while inhibiting another.

PROPOSED: never create a single “ecosystem health”, “dominance” or “competition score” that erases causal locality. A resource can be plentiful overall yet locally inaccessible to one branch. Conversely, spatial overlap alone does not establish limitation.

PROPOSED: preserve priority/history. Earlier occupation may matter only if evidence shows preemption or modification. Arrival order by itself does not prove competitive advantage.

PROPOSED: if one branch closes, re-evaluate the other branch and the shared substrate independently. Do not automatically promote the surviving branch, restore the resource, increase population, or infer habitat recovery.

UNCERTAIN: which actual Marea/Sendero species or habitat modifications participate in shared-substrate competition. Pass 269 keeps all concrete branch actors fixture-only.

UNCERTAIN: whether any future branch conflict maps to PTU cover, blocker, difficult terrain, hazard, zone, reaction, forced movement or combat displacement. Those semantics require exact engine admission evidence.

## Design consequence

A useful Ouros investigation can present two visible uses of one microhabitat. The player may first assume one branch is “winning”, then discover that the branches depend on different aspects of the same substrate or on different time windows. Intervention can alter one branch without globally repairing or degrading the ecosystem. The reduced encounter is observation and world-state only. Structured contest, forced displacement or terrain effects remain capability-gated.
