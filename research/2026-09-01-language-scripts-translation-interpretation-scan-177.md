## Source 1 — Ruins of Alph and Unown symbols

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Unown_symbols
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ruins_of_Alph

The Ruins of Alph use a distinct written form whose glyphs can be mapped to letters. In later games, inscriptions also function as hints that unlock hidden rooms. Different stages of player knowledge matter: seeing a symbol, recognizing a glyph, decoding a literal string and understanding what instruction or historical statement the string conveys are separate operations.

Reusable structures:
- transcription can precede comprehension;
- a script can be learnable through repeated examples rather than a single exposition dump;
- inscriptions can connect language study to physical exploration;
- solving one chamber can expand what is readable elsewhere;
- a research report can become a persistent tool rather than a one-use quest key.

Ouros translation:
Tideglass can maintain script/reference dossiers that grow through documented examples. A player may copy an inscription accurately before anyone knows its semantic meaning. Later interpretation should update the claim graph, not rewrite the original inscription record.

## Source 2 — Hoenn Sealed Chamber and Braille puzzles

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sealed_Chamber
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Braille

The Sealed Chamber separates a decoding aid from the operational instruction encoded by the text. The physical space, the written clue and the required action are coupled. Pokémon Adventures later uses a damaged inscription whose missing portion must be reconstructed from another document before the larger problem can be solved.

Reusable structures:
- damaged text can remain useful evidence without being complete;
- a reconstruction should preserve which characters are observed and which are supplied by inference;
- translation can require multiple custodians or records;
- an inscription may describe an action but does not itself guarantee that the proposed action is safe or currently applicable;
- later recovery of missing material can revise an earlier reading without erasing it.

Ouros translation:
A Tideglass record should distinguish `observed_glyphs`, `restored_glyphs`, `literal_reading`, `semantic_interpretation` and `confidence`. A field team can test an interpretation against the world, but a successful coincidence should not silently prove every part of the translation.

## Source 3 — Pokémon Adventures research forum and inscription interpretation

Source:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/PS134

A Pokémon Adventures chapter has Bugsy encounter an inscription and later report its translation to a research forum. The useful high-level pattern is institutional interpretation: a discovery can move from field observation to specialist reading to wider scholarly circulation rather than being understood instantly by the protagonist.

Reusable structures:
- interpretation can be attributed to a person or institution;
- scholarly disagreement and later correction are natural quest hooks;
- publication of a translation is an information event, not a canon-authority event;
- specialists can matter because of method and provenance, not because the story declares them omniscient.

Ouros translation:
Taro and Pia can preserve editions and source images while Nerea or another qualified actor may propose a reading. The archive should retain earlier readings, who proposed them and what evidence changed the current preferred interpretation.

## Source 4 — Legends: Arceus Old Verses

Sources:
- https://www.gamerguides.com/pokemon-legends-arceus/guide/digging-with-ursaluna/digging-info/how-to-find-all-20-old-verses
- https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/pokemon-legends-arceus-old-verse-locations/

Old Verses distribute historical material across multiple regions and require exploration capability to recover. Their value is cumulative: one fragment provides atmosphere and partial history, while a larger set allows stronger synthesis.

Reusable structures:
- fragments can be optional yet materially improve historical understanding;
- source location is part of provenance;
- collection order need not equal chronological order;
- inaccessible fragments can become reachable after traversal capability changes;
- synthesis can remain uncertain even after all fragments are found.

Ouros translation:
Historical text quests should avoid a single magic exposition tablet. Marea and later regions can preserve fragments in archives, survey markers, family copies, route infrastructure and recovered field notes. Each source keeps custody/location history.

## Source 5 — Mystery Dungeon Pokémon writing and alternate communication modes

Source:
- https://mysterydungeonwiki.com/wiki/Pkmn%3APok%C3%A9mon_Language

Mystery Dungeon depicts Pokémon writing, species-specific or unusual speech representation, and non-spoken communication such as Beldum using magnetism. The exact fiction differs across titles, so the reusable lesson is not a universal Pokémon language rule. It is that communication mode can vary by species, individual and setting.

Reusable structures:
- communicative competence is not identical to human speech;
- an observer can notice a recurring signal pattern before decoding it;
- gesture, rhythm, magnetism, scent, posture or other modes can carry information without becoming telepathy;
- a translation interface can be wrong or partial;
- a species-wide communication claim needs stronger evidence than one individual example.

Ouros guardrail:
Do not canonize a universal Pokémon language or grant human-language speech to a species from one external depiction. Any specific speech, Telepath, aura or special sensing claim must be checked against PTU/Caelo source material and the individual Pokémon record.

## Source 6 — Pokémon Ranger ruins built by people and Pokémon

Sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fiore_Temple
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jungle_Relic

Fiore Temple is described as having been built by people and Pokémon together. Jungle Relic combines an old site, a former Ranger use and later environmental change.

Reusable structures:
- an ancient site can contain both cultural and Pokémon-authored traces;
- later institutions can reuse an older place without fully understanding its original function;
- environmental change can alter how old markings are read or accessed;
- a modern training interpretation may differ from historical purpose.

Ouros translation:
A Marea marker or later Ouros ruin may have multiple authorship layers: original builders, later surveyors, maintenance crews and visitors. Interpretation should identify layer and date where possible instead of treating every visible mark as one coherent message.

## Cross-source design conclusions

1. `TRANSCRIPTION != TRANSLATION != INTERPRETATION != WORLD_TRUTH`.
2. Preserve raw observed material separately from restored/reconstructed material.
3. Attribute every interpretation to an actor/institution and evidence set.
4. Multiple plausible readings can coexist until evidence discriminates between them.
5. A puzzle clue should be redundant enough that failure to know one external alphabet never hard-locks the campaign.
6. Physical testing can support an interpretation but should not automatically prove its entire historical explanation.
7. Communication modes should be individual/species/context records, not universal assumptions.
8. Rare speech, telepathy or sensing must be explicitly sourced and mechanically gated.
9. Archives should retain superseded translations and correction history.
10. Translation work can create quests without requiring combat.
