# Mourning, Burial, Private Memory & Bereavement Research — Pass 191

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Nothing in this file is canon.
Date: 2026-09-01

## Research question

How can Ouros represent death, mourning, funerary practice, graves, private remembrance and post-loss continuity without turning every death into supernatural lore, automatically publicizing private grief, or letting Minecraft presentation state author a canonical death?

## Repository gap identified before research

The repository already has strong coverage for public memory, commemorations, archives, sacred-site stewardship, material provenance, care, cases, correspondence and persistent aftermath. The missing seam is the interval between a validated death and any later public legacy:

- who is allowed to know;
- what has actually been confirmed;
- what happens to personal effects;
- whether a farewell or burial occurs;
- which practices are private, household, institutional or public;
- whether a grave or memorial exists;
- how companion Pokémon and surviving actors continue afterward;
- how a later commemoration differs from the immediate response to loss.

This pass treats those as world-state and authorship problems first.

## Public Pokémon sources

### 1. Pokémon Tower / Lavender Town

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lavender_Town

Reusable structures:

- a burial site can be an ordinary destination visited by mourners;
- a place of remembrance can also contain wild Pokémon and operational hazards;
- private grief, public history and active criminal interference can coexist in one location without being the same plot;
- a specific deceased Pokémon can matter because of a specific relationship, not because every grave must produce a supernatural encounter;
- later redevelopment can relocate burial functions while preserving continuity through another institution.

Do not import:

- Marowak, Cubone, Team Rocket, Mr. Fuji, Silph Scope, the exact tower layout or the specific haunting;
- a rule that every violent death produces a Ghost;
- a rule that mourning sites necessarily contain Ghost-type Pokémon.

### 2. Celestial Tower

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celestial_Tower

Reusable structures:

- mourners can perform one simple repeatable act of remembrance;
- a memorial action can be emotionally meaningful without granting a battle bonus or supernatural effect;
- a major Trainer can revisit an old loss long after the death itself;
- an ordinary care obligation can interrupt competitive scheduling even when the player wants a battle now.

The important design lesson is temporal continuity. Grief does not need to be a one-scene quest state that permanently resolves after the player interacts once.

### 3. Hau'oli Cemetery

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hau%27oli_Cemetery

Reusable structures:

- people and Pokémon can share a funerary landscape;
- visitors arrive for different deceased individuals and carry different relationships to the place;
- memorial flowers and repeated visitation give loss a physical world footprint;
- a surviving companion Pokémon may have its own observable post-loss behavior;
- the deceased person's former possessions and the surviving Pokémon's history can intersect without automatically deciding ownership or emotional interpretation.

Do not import the specific widow, Machamp, accident or TM reward.

### 4. Memorial Hill

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Memorial_Hill

Reusable structures:

- a burial place can have historical prestige and draw visitors from outside the immediate settlement;
- proximity to a culturally significant site can influence where people choose burial or remembrance;
- present-day incidents may happen at a cemetery without making the cemetery itself the cause of those incidents;
- family status, public history and private bereavement can overlap while remaining distinguishable.

Do not import Alolan ruling families, Tapu Lele or the specific geography.

### 5. Broader Pokémon burial-ground pattern

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Death

The franchise repeatedly uses burial grounds across Kanto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova, Alola and later regions. Reusable high-level lessons:

- funerary landscapes are normal parts of the Pokémon world rather than one exceptional haunted location;
- some spaces are for Pokémon, some include people and Pokémon, and practices vary by region;
- repeated visits can matter years after the original death;
- remembrance can be quiet, institutional, familial, historical or culturally public;
- a grave can be important even when no supernatural event occurs there.

## PTU rules cross-check

Primary public PTU source:
Pokémon Tabletop United 1.05 Core PDF
https://peda.net/p/josajoki/fista/ohjeet/ptu/pokemon-tabletop-united-1.05-core%3Afile/download/c109e0ecc0ac41065575a4a324183b80189a2c70/Pokemon%20Tabletop%20United%201.05%20Core.pdf

The Core Rulebook contains explicit death conditions tied to Injuries and sufficiently negative Hit Points, plus a Coup de Grâce procedure for a helpless target. It also distinguishes friendly or sportsmanlike contexts from lethal thresholds in an important way.

Narrative consequence:

A canonical death caused by combat must come from authoritative PTU/AutoPTU state or an explicit human-authored world fact. Narrative must not infer death from:

- Fainted;
- zero HP alone;
- a Minecraft entity death animation;
- despawn;
- chunk unload;
- an NPC failing to path home;
- an absent companion Pokémon;
- a dramatic cutscene;
- a rumor of death;
- a grave marker whose subject identity is not validated.

This pass does not claim current AutoPTU-Java implements the complete PTU death family. The public rulebook proves the rule exists; implementation parity remains a separate evidence question.

## Community / campaign search

Searches were run for PTU campaign material involving character death, Pokémon death, funerals, cemeteries and memorial scenes.

One useful community signal came from r/PokemonTabletop discussions: players explicitly recognize that PTU already contains death rules rather than requiring a generic D&D-style death-save subsystem. This is community evidence only, not rules authority.

Source:
https://www.reddit.com/r/PokemonTabletop/comments/108eexy/player_death_mechanics/

The search did not produce a strong, attributable PTU actual-play funerary procedure worth importing. That absence is retained as an evidence gap rather than filled with unrelated tabletop customs.

## Transformed Ouros design lessons

1. Confirmation precedes mourning-state automation.

An overdue person, missing Pokémon, Fainted combatant or lost signal is not a deceased subject until authoritative state confirms it.

2. Private grief and public memory need separate objects.

A household may know and mourn a death before an institution publishes anything. A public record may later exist without exposing every private detail.

3. Funerary practice should be plural and locally authored.

Ouros should not silently assume burial, cremation, shrine use, bells, flowers, spirit belief, inherited equipment or religious officiation. Those require Caelo evidence or explicit canon approval.

4. Memorial objects are evidence of practice, not metaphysical proof.

Flowers, stones, ribbons, plaques, names, bells, kept tools, photographs and archived letters can show remembrance. They do not establish afterlife, Ghost presence, divine approval or supernatural efficacy.

5. Companion Pokémon require their own continuity.

After a validated human death, a Pokémon companion is still a persistent actor. Custody, care, ownership claims, preferred caretaker and observed behavior must remain separate. The inverse is also true when a Pokémon dies and a human survives.

6. Personal effects require custody and provenance.

Possessions do not become loot. Recovery, inventory, inheritance, institutional storage and final disposition must not collapse into Minecraft item pickup.

7. A farewell does not end grief.

A ceremony can close one operational phase while later dates, places, possessions and recurring events continue to carry meaning.

8. A grave is not an encounter generator.

Wild encounters, Ghost-type presence, vandalism, erosion, access problems or research questions must arise from actual world state rather than mandatory cemetery content.

9. Public interpretation can diverge from private memory.

A respected public figure may be remembered institutionally for one event while close associates remember ordinary details. Both can coexist without either becoming omniscient truth.

10. Player authorship needs a hard boundary.

Retired PCs and player-owned Pokémon cannot be killed, buried, assigned heirs, given posthumous beliefs or memorialized through invented future facts without explicit player/human canon authorization.

## Provenance separation

Everything above is research-derived design material.

No Marea cemetery, funerary rite, religious belief, burial custom, inheritance law, death-notification protocol, postmortem examination system, spirit doctrine or memorial institution is canonized by this file.

Any future Ouros implementation must continue to distinguish:

- validated death fact;
- cause-of-death claim;
- private notification state;
- public publication state;
- remains/body custody where applicable;
- personal-effects custody;
- companion-Pokémon continuity;
- mourning practice;
- funerary event;
- memorial/commemoration;
- historical interpretation;
- supernatural claim.
