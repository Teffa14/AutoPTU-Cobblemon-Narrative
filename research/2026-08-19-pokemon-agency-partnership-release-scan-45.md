# Pokémon Agency, Partnership, Release & Trainer Relationship Research — Pass 45

Status: research and provenance only. Nothing in this file is Ouros canon.

## Research question

Ouros already models social bonds, care, breeding, custody, conservation, workplaces, family, public memory and wild collectives. Those layers mention Trainer–Pokémon relationships, but they do not provide one authoritative narrative boundary for an individual Pokémon that changes Trainer, refuses cooperation, works temporarily with a human, returns to the wild, remains at a habitat, or continues a relationship after ownership/custody changes.

This pass asks a narrower question:

How can Ouros preserve individual Pokémon agency and continuity without inventing emotions, legal rights, obedience mechanics, Friendship bonuses or Loyalty values?

## Internal repository gap check

The repository tree was inspected before research. Relevant existing layers include:

- `design/social-bonds-mentorship-clubs-layer.md` — already states that Trainer/Pokémon bond state is distinct from human relationships and that mechanical Loyalty/Friendship must come from PTU/Caelo.
- `design/breeding-eggs-nursery-lineage-layer.md` — already separates custody, ownership claims, guardianship and juvenile care.
- `design/care-recovery-welfare-layer.md` — already allows care for wild Pokémon without automatic ownership.

What is still missing is a persistent individual-Pokémon association model that connects those systems.

## Source 1 — PTU GM advice: treatment and ownership norms are campaign questions

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/gm-advice-your-first-ptu-session/

The official PTU GM advice explicitly tells groups to decide how Pokémon are treated, what ethical norms surround capture, whether Pokémon remain outside Poké Balls in civilized areas, and how ownership is regulated.

The same article tells GMs to establish Pokémon as characters rather than battle tools. It also states that some wild Pokémon may be befriended without a fight, while warning that this approach will not work on every individual.

Reusable structure:

- capture rules and social norms are separate questions;
- battle victory need not be the only road to association;
- Pokémon interaction should exist before and outside combat;
- the world may contain multiple culturally valid expectations about Trainer/Pokémon relationships.

Ouros implication:

The narrative engine should not hard-code one universal interpretation of capture, ownership, Poké Ball etiquette or partnership before regional canon establishes those norms.

## Derived design lessons

### Association is not one field

At minimum, Ouros should keep these facts separate:

- physical location;
- Poké Ball / mechanical party state when authoritative;
- current custodian;
- ownership/registration claim when canon defines it;
- battle controller when applicable;
- residence;
- partnership history;
- observed cooperation/refusal events;
- authored or mechanically resolved Loyalty state;
- public belief about the relationship.

### Capture does not author personality

A successful capture event may change mechanical association. It does not automatically establish:

- trust;
- affection;
- respect;
- enthusiasm;
- fear;
- friendship;
- willingness to participate in every activity.

### Release is a transition, not deletion

A released Pokémon should keep its entity identity and history when the game has reason to preserve it.

It may later:

- remain in a known habitat;
- migrate;
- join a collective;
- be sighted by researchers;
- temporarily assist former companions;
- be cared for by another institution;
- form another association if canon/rules permit.

Release should not erase prior Chronicle events.

### Temporary cooperation deserves first-class state

A wild or institutionally managed Pokémon may assist with:

- rescue;
- traversal;
- survey;
- habitat defense;
- crisis response;
- transport;
- research;
- a single tactical encounter.

This should not silently create ownership or a permanent roster slot.

### Refusal requires evidence and bounded scope

If authoritative rules or authored behavior establish refusal, record exactly what happened.

Examples:

- refused one command;
- would not enter a location;
- avoided one person;
- left a battle;
- rejected a transfer;
- returned to a previous habitat.

Do not convert one event into a universal personality label.

## PTU/Caelo mechanical boundary

The project must re-open the supplied authoritative PTU/Caelo source set before implementing:

- Loyalty ranks and exact effects;
- Command checks connected to Loyalty;
- obedience/disobedience behavior;
- capture and release procedure;
- trade/transfer handling;
- Poké Ball ownership/registration assumptions;
- any Feature that modifies Loyalty or command behavior;
- any Caelo homebrew that changes these systems.

The narrative layer may store provenance and events. It must not calculate these mechanics from prose.

## Copyright / transformation boundary

Do not copy episode dialogue, scene prose, fan-RP characters, custom lore or distinctive plots.

Allowed reuse is structural:

- partnership can change;
- separation can be positive;
- temporary cooperation can exist without ownership;
- capture/association can precede trust;
- release can preserve history;
- individual Pokémon can have goals or behavior that do not perfectly match a Trainer's immediate objective.
