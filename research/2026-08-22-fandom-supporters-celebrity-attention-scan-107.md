# Pass 107 Research — Fandom, Supporters, Celebrity & Public Attention

Status: research/provenance only. Not canon. Do not treat source material below as PTU/Caelo rules.

## Why this pass

The repository already has battle institutions, Contests/performance, sports, music, media, public memory, tourism, sponsorships, markets and public-space systems. It does not yet have a dedicated authority for supporter communities, fan clubs, celebrity attention, parasocial assumptions, fan-made artifacts, crowd allegiance, anti-fandom, or how fame changes over time.

This gap matters because Ouros can produce famous Trainers, Coordinators, musicians, researchers, Gym Leaders and Pokémon. If attention is not modeled separately, narrative generation is likely to collapse several different states into one vague `reputation` score.

## Sources reviewed

Source: Pokémon Sword/Shield official site, “Meet Leon, the strongest Trainer in the Galar region.”
https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-gb/people-galar-region/champion-leon/

Reusable structure:
- competitive success can create a public figure;
- battle style itself can become part of a public persona;
- broadcasts and exhibition matches allow people who never met the person to form opinions;
- public affection can exist independently from private relationships.

### Pokémon Trainer Fan Club — fandom can switch targets over time

Source: Bulbapedia synthesis of FireRed/LeafGreen and Hoenn fan-club behavior.

Reusable structure:

- support can change after visible achievements or defeats;
- a player can become the subject of fandom without personally creating the group.

This is used as game-structure evidence, not as a claim that Ouros must contain a canonical “Trainer Fan Club.”

### Sports-fandom research

Source: Frontiers in Psychology (2025), “Is social identity theory enough to cover sports fans’ behavior?: additional perspective from identity fusion theory.”
https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1574520/full

Reusable high-level lesson:
- supporters can become attached not only to a team or public figure but also to one another;
- rituals, shared attendance and recurring traditions can make supporter groups persistent social networks;
- strong identification should never be treated as evidence that every member behaves identically.

This research is used to avoid simplistic “fan meter” design. Ouros must not proceduralize extremist behavior or assume violence from strong fandom.

## Design conclusions

1. Fame is not truth.
A famous claim can still be wrong. Public attention changes distribution and persistence of information; it does not alter world truth.

2. Fame is not friendship.
A fan may know many public facts about a Trainer while the Trainer has never met them.

3. Support is not uniform.
A supporter community needs internal variation: casual followers, regular attendees, archivists, artists, traveling supporters, critics, former supporters and people who only care about one activity.

4. Crowd state is event-scoped.
A stadium audience, market crowd or festival audience should not become a permanent faction merely because people cheered together once.

5. Public figures can have several audiences.
A Gym Leader might have local students, competitive fans, critics, music followers and residents who primarily know them through civic work. These audiences can disagree.

6. Fan-made artifacts need provenance.
Posters, scrapbooks, unofficial statistics, photographs, songs, costumes, banners and zines can become historical objects. They remain separate from official records.

7. Attention should produce world pressure through existing systems.
Examples: media coverage, tourism, event capacity, markets, sponsorship, public-space crowding, privacy concerns, misinformation or demand for exhibitions.

8. Parasocial inference needs a hard boundary.
The generator cannot decide that a public figure “owes” fans access, romance, friendship, explanations or private information.

9. Anti-fandom and criticism are not automatically hostility.
A critic, rival supporter group or skeptical journalist may oppose a public persona without becoming an antagonist.

10. Pokémon can become public figures too.
A persistent Pokémon may be famous because of battles, rescue work, performances or repeated sightings. Fame does not change ownership, custody, agency, Loyalty or battle mechanics.

## Copyright / transformation boundary

No protected dialogue, episode scripts, character arcs, event mysteries or distinctive plots are copied. Only abstract structures are retained: supporter institutions, mediated fame, multi-role public figures, recurring audience rituals, shifting support, fan artifacts and the distinction between public persona and private relationship.

## PTU / Caelo boundary

No PTU/Caelo mechanic is inferred from fandom. Public support does not grant Charm bonuses, Command bonuses, AP, Trainer Features, morale, Accuracy, initiative, healing, Loyalty or Contest effects.

The full project Caelo corpus was not reliably accessible in this runtime, so no Caelo-specific fan/reputation rule is asserted.
