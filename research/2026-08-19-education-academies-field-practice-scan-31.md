# Education, Academies & Field Practice Research — Pass 31

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon.

Date: 2026-08-19

## Scope

This pass studies education as a world system rather than another mentorship label.

Existing Ouros layers already cover:

- interpersonal mentorship and clubs;
- workplace training and staffing;
- battle institutions;
- science programs;
- travel and expeditions;
- homes/dorm-like residence;
- public memory and alumni-like continuity.

The missing layer is formal learning infrastructure: enrollment, curriculum, instruction, supervised practice, assessment, academic records, field placements, exchange programs, transfer state, graduation/exit state and institutional learning capacity.

This research does not establish Ouros school law, compulsory education, age requirements, tuition, credentials, graduation rules or PTU progression.

## Source 1 — Pokémon Tabletop campaign guidance

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-the-road-to-tomorrow/

Useful high-level patterns:

- PTU explicitly supports campaigns where PCs help build institutions that affect later society.
- The campaign seed includes the possibility of establishing academies specialized in teaching Pokémon and finding qualified teachers.
- It treats institution-building as a persistent consequence, not a decorative hub.
- PCs can eventually shift from being learners/adventurers to selecting, training or supporting newer Trainers.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

A school or academy can be an institution players influence over time. It can gain teachers, facilities, field programs and specializations because of world-state changes. Education should therefore connect to the civic, workplace, social and public-memory layers.

Copyright boundary:

Do not copy the source's specific recovering-region scenario or its factions. Use only the institutional-design principle.

## Source 2 — Naranja/Uva Academy and Paldea field learning

Sources:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/picturesque-perspectives-of-paldea-across-pokemon-horizons-the-series-and-the-video-games
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/a-look-at-pokemon-tcg-scarlet-violet-paldea-evolved-illustration-rare-cards

Observed structure:

- the player is formally enrolled at an academy;
- the academy provides subject-specific instruction;
- different teachers cover different domains such as Battle Studies and Home Economics;
- the academy provides knowledge about Pokémon and the region;
- the wider adventure moves beyond the campus rather than keeping all learning inside classrooms.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Formal education works best when classroom knowledge creates questions, preparation and context for field activity. A lesson should not automatically become a mechanical buff. It can instead unlock a supervised practicum, research question, safe field exercise, conversation topic, access request or assignment.

## Source 3 — Tera Training and aptitude battles

Sources:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/kitakami-unmasked-in-pokemon-horizons-season-2-the-search-for-laqua
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/horizons/2/showdown-the-paldea-elite-four

Observed structure:

- temporary students can enroll in a bounded training program without becoming ordinary full-time students;
- training includes practical battles supervised or framed by experienced Trainers/Gym Leaders;
- aptitude battles can evaluate learned skills through real practice;
- instruction, practical experience and assessment are distinct events.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Education should support short programs, certifications-in-progress, workshops, field schools and exchanges in addition to multi-year academies.

Assessment should produce evidence of demonstrated competency inside the institution, not automatically grant PTU Skill Ranks, Edges, Features or stat improvements.

- group activities and school-related quests coexist with personal exploration;

Model enrollment scope separately from residency and citizenship. A character may be:

- full-time student;

- visiting trainee;

- guest researcher;

- alumni;
- external evaluator.

Those labels should have explicit start/end windows and access scopes.

## Structural synthesis for Ouros

The highest-value reusable pattern is:

instruction -> supervised practice -> evidence -> feedback -> optional reassessment -> institutional record

This is distinct from:

mechanical PTU progression.

A student can demonstrate that an institution trusts them to conduct a survey, operate a piece of local equipment or lead a supervised expedition without the narrative layer claiming a specific Skill Rank.

## Curriculum should be a graph, not a level ladder

A curriculum may contain:

- required orientation;
- subject modules;
- prerequisites;
- electives;
- practical assignments;
- field placements;
- capstone projects;
- assessment opportunities;
- remedial/retry paths;
- independent study;
- visiting lectures;
- exchange modules.

Different characters can reach the same practicum through different prior evidence.

## Assessment should preserve uncertainty

An institution can record:

- attended;
- completed;
- observed;
- demonstrated;
- needs-supervision;
- reassessment-recommended;
- incomplete;
- waived-by-equivalent-evidence.

Those states are institutional judgments, not universal truth about the character.

## Field learning is the main bridge to Ouros gameplay

Strong field-practice structures include:

- ecology survey;
- supervised capture observation;
- route mapping;
- first-aid exercise;
- archive practicum;
- excavation documentation;
- infrastructure inspection;
- journalism assignment;
- contest production;
- shelter drill;
- research replication;
- public-service placement;
- supervised battle exercise.

When mechanics matter, the actual PTU/Caelo action must be resolved authoritatively.

## Failure should usually create learning state, not expulsion

Routine failed assessment can produce:

- instructor feedback;
- changed supervision level;
- targeted practice opportunity;
- reassessment;
- alternate demonstration route;
- peer support;
- delayed access to one responsibility.

Severe institutional consequences such as suspension or expulsion require authored policies and actual triggering events. They must not be invented by generic generation.

## Accessibility and alternative competency evidence

The system should not assume every learner proves competence through battle.

Possible evidence sources:

- field report;
- successful supervised procedure;
- oral explanation;
- practical demonstration;
- research contribution;
- planning document;
- team leadership;
- observation log;
- repair completed under supervision;
- battle result when the module actually concerns battle.

No accessibility accommodation, disability, age status or private learning need should be inferred for a PC without player-authored data.

## PTU/Caelo cross-check boundary

The supplied PTU Core guidance explicitly supports mission-based campaigns where PCs may be students at a Pokémon Trainer academy. The Core GM guidance also stresses meaningful interactivity and tasks using different PC Skills/Pokémon rather than passive narration.

Caelo distinguishes Social, Wild Encounter, PvP, Job, Raid, Contest, Gym and Dojo activities. Education can use those existing activity shapes as practica, but it must not invent a new mechanical reward layer over them.

The supplied character-creation material defines Skills, Edges and Features mechanically. A course title, exam score or school credential therefore cannot grant those mechanics without an authoritative PTU/Caelo rule.
