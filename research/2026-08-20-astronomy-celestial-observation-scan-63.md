Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Not Ouros canon. No PTU mechanic is established by this document.

## Why this pass exists

The repository already contains dedicated systems for seasonality, meteorology, photography, science, myth, archaeology, cartography, tourism, conservation and anomalous spaces. It did not contain a dedicated astronomical layer that separates predictable sky state, actual observation, cultural interpretation, Pokémon behavior, meteor falls, observatory operations and tactical PTU effects.

Repository search for astronomy/celestial/observatory/meteor terms did not return a dedicated internal layer before this pass.

A separate previously researched narrative catalogue outside this repository already contains an observatory + Minior/Clefairy ecological story. This pass deliberately does not reproduce that plot. It extracts a broader reusable systems grammar.

## Source findings

### 1. Minior supports real sky-to-ground ecological events

Official Pokémon Pokédex material states that Minior originate in the ozone layer and fall when their outer shell becomes heavy. The composition of atmospheric dust is associated with core color.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/minior

Reusable structure:

- a celestial-looking event can be a biological/ecological event;
- a falling object should not automatically be classified as an inert meteorite;
- observation, recovery and conservation can become separate activities;
- the same event can affect science, tourism, transport, wildlife and public memory.

Do not infer:

- magnetic effects;
- impact damage;
- atmospheric hazards;
- Minior spawning rules;
- a guaranteed Minior encounter after every meteor shower.

### 2. Hokulani Observatory turns a meteor shower into a social/scientific event

The official episode “Showering the World with Love!” takes students to Hokulani Observatory for a meteor shower that becomes a Minior shower. The observatory functions as a site for education, observation, explanation and emotional experience rather than only as a battle arena.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/21/episode-35-showering-the-world-with-love

Reusable structure:

- observatories can host school groups, researchers, technicians and visitors simultaneously;
- rare sky events can be public events with limited observation windows;
- a scientific explanation does not remove emotional significance;
- recurring observations can connect generations of observers.

### 3. Clefairy behavior can correlate with lunar state without becoming a universal lunar rule

The official Clefairy Pokédex describes gatherings and dancing on full-moon nights and associates those gatherings with an unusual local magnetic field.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/clefairy

The official episode “Clefairy and the Moon Stone” also links Mount Moon, Clefairy and Moon Stones while making protection of the Pokémon and their habitat part of the conflict.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/1/episode-6-clefairy-and-the-moon-stone

Reusable structure:

- lunar phase can be an authored environmental trigger for specific species or locations;
- an event can be predictable in timing but uncertain in exact behavior;
- observation access can conflict with capture, tourism or resource extraction.

Guardrail:

A full moon does not globally buff Fairy Pokémon, alter gravity, create a magnetic field or trigger Clefairy unless the authoritative PTU/Caelo rules or authored Ouros state explicitly support that exact effect.

### 4. Lunatone and Solrock support species-specific celestial associations

Official Pokédex material describes Lunatone as discovered at a meteorite impact site, active during full moons and associated with lunar phases. Solrock is described as drawing energy from sunlight and being strongest during the day.

Sources:
https://www.pokemon.com/es/pokedex/lunatone
https://www.pokemon.com/el/pokedex/solrock

Reusable structure:

- day/night and lunar state can matter to field observation;
- discovery location can influence scientific hypotheses without proving origin;
- a species can become historically associated with astronomy without being a celestial oracle.

### 5. PTU explicitly permits strange meteors as campaign-level mysteries

The public PTU campaign seed “The Road to Tomorrow” offers a strange meteor from outer space as one possible source of supernatural change on an expedition.

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-the-road-to-tomorrow/

Reusable structure:

- a meteor can be an inciting event rather than a one-room loot object;
- research teams, competing explanations and institutional consequences can sustain a long arc;
- the cause can remain unknown for a substantial period.

Copyright boundary:

Use only the abstract structure. Do not copy the campaign seed’s exact island, experimental organization, supernatural progression or prose.

### 6. Fangames show observatories as mixed institutions

Pokémon Forecast uses a mountain laboratory that combines weather station, observatory and seismic monitoring functions, with scientists, engineers and security staff sharing the facility.

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/forecast/

PokéROM Sanctuary includes an Observatory built around educational/math interactions and Pokémon discovery.

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/8519/

Reusable structure:

- observatories can overlap with meteorology, geology, education and public outreach;
- specialist facilities can contain ordinary workplace stories as well as rare discoveries;
- puzzles can teach a method of observation rather than act as arbitrary locks.

Do not copy either fangame’s characters, custom mechanics, weather conditions, facility layout or plot.

### 7. Dark-sky quality is a measurable world-state variable

NASA notes that light pollution and Moon brightness materially change what observers can see, and that weather, smoke, horizon direction and sky brightness affect observing conditions.

Sources:
https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/check-your-sky-quality-with-orion/
https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/how-to-find-good-places-to-stargaze/

NOIRLab documents dark-sky protection as both a scientific and cultural concern and describes observatory visits as an education/outreach function.

Sources:
https://noirlab.edu/science/events/websites/fm2
https://noirlab.edu/public/announcements/annee25018/

Reusable structure:

- sky visibility should be stateful rather than a binary “night = stars” switch;
- urban growth, festivals, infrastructure lighting, wildfire smoke and Moon phase can change observing quality;
- observatories can negotiate with nearby settlements over lighting without turning either side into villains;
- public outreach can be part of observatory identity.

### 8. Meteorite recovery makes provenance matter immediately

NASA’s July 15, 2026 report on a newly fallen meteorite emphasizes rapid recovery and contamination-aware handling because exposure after the fall can alter scientifically valuable material.

Source:
https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/astromaterials/nasa-study-of-pristine-meteorite-adds-to-story-of-ancient-asteroids/

Reusable structure for Ouros:

- a fresh fall can create a short scientific window;
- recovery location, time, handler and storage conditions matter;
- meteorite custody can intersect with science, museums, property claims, media and illicit markets;
- finding a fragment does not automatically determine ownership or scientific interpretation.

Do not import real-world meteorite ownership law.

## Cross-system lessons for Ouros

### Separate sky truth from observation

Use this chain:

celestial state → atmospheric/visibility state → observation opportunity → instrument/human observation → interpretation → publication/public belief.

Cloud, smoke, light pollution or a broken telescope can prevent an observation without changing the sky event itself.

### Separate astronomical events from Pokémon events

A meteor shower, Minior shower, Clefairy gathering and falling rock event may coincide but remain distinct objects until evidence links them.

### Preserve old predictions

An observatory forecast or predicted event window should remain versioned after the event. Later players can compare prediction against what happened.

### Preserve failed observation

A clouded-out observation, camera failure or null detection can still matter scientifically and narratively. The system should not fabricate a replacement spectacle because players arrived.

### Treat observatories as workplaces

Useful recurring roles include:

- astronomer;
- instrument technician;
- data analyst;
- night operator;
- guide/outreach worker;
- maintenance engineer;
- visiting researcher;
- student;
- conservation liaison;
- security/logistics staff.

These are narrative roles only. They do not grant PTU Skills, Edges or Features.
