# Ouros Narrative Research — Material Culture, Crafting & Economy — Pass 07

Status: Research only. Provenance and design evidence; not Ouros canon.

This pass follows the existing work on mission structure, dungeons, world agency, ecology, settlements, public memory and cases. The gap examined here is material culture: how resources, recipes, workshops, merchants, repairs, services, regional specialties and item histories can generate persistent stories without turning Ouros into a spreadsheet economy simulator.

## 1. Pokémon Legends: Arceus — field materials -> recipes -> workbench -> expedition readiness

Primary sources:
- https://legends.arceus.pokemon.com/en-gb/gameplay/
- https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Pokemon-Legends-Arceus-1930510.html

Observed structure:
- Exploration produces materials that can be combined into useful items.
- Crafting happens at specific workbenches in base camps and Jubilife Village.
- The Craftworks sells recipes and materials, while the general store offers a parallel path for resupplying.
- Crafted objects support field activity such as capture and stealth rather than existing as an isolated minigame.
- Recipe access expands over time, so production knowledge itself can be progression.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Crafting becomes narratively meaningful when material source, recipe knowledge, facility access and intended field use all connect to the world.

Ouros translation:
A crafted item candidate should be traceable through:
`material source -> recipe/knowledge -> workshop/tool access -> maker -> item -> later use/repair/loss`.

The system should not invent Legends: Arceus recipes. PTU/Caelo remains the mechanical source for any actual item creation.

## 2. PTU 1.05 — crafting already belongs to character identity

Public reference mirrors:
- https://pokemontabletop.fandom.com/wiki/Trainer_Edges
- https://pturpg.wikidot.com/consumables
- https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/pokemon_tabletop/apricorn-crafting-dc-t5931.html

Publicly indexed PTU 1.05 material includes crafting-oriented character options such as Apricorn Ball creation, Basic Ball creation, cooking and Gem Lore. It also includes a Poké Ball Tool Box used for crafting and repair. Apricorns, Berries, Herbs and similar resources have world-facing availability and item roles.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Production should not be a universal menu available identically to every character. It can be an expression of expertise, equipment access, gathered materials and social relationships with specialists.

Ouros translation:
- recipe knowledge and crafting permissions should come from actual PTU/Caelo character state or authored NPC services;
- a workshop can matter because the correct tools or specialist are present there;
- players without the relevant expertise can commission, trade, learn, gather or build relationships instead of being locked out of item access;
- repair can create continuity for meaningful equipment.

Mechanical guardrail:
Exact prerequisites, costs, actions, recipes and effects must be validated against the supplied project rule corpus. Public mirrors are discovery aids, not a higher-priority rules authority.

## 3. Supplied PTU Core Rulebook — Pokémon can participate in resource economies

The supplied CoreRulebook.pdf includes Special Capabilities that can create or obtain resources, including examples such as Honey Gather, Herb Growth, Heart Gift and Fortune. Other capabilities interact with terrain, movement and environmental state.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Pokémon can contribute to production and gathering through their actual capabilities rather than functioning only as combat units.

Ouros translation:
A resource opportunity may check for a governed capability on a specific Pokémon entity. The resulting item or opportunity must use the exact PTU/Caelo rule when implemented. The narrative system must never infer a resource-producing power merely from species flavor.

## 4. Supplied Caelo Player's Guide — shops and crafting already exist as living-world services

The supplied Caelo Player's Guide 1.5 includes explicit shopping and crafting surfaces, with commands for viewing shops and markets, buying items, viewing recipes and crafting. This is important because crafting is already treated as a service/activity inside a persistent social world rather than a detached character-sheet calculation.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A shop or workshop can be represented as a persistent world institution with inventory, recipe access, residents, dependencies and location state.

Ouros translation:
- preserve shop identity and physical location in Minecraft;
- attach service availability to residents/facilities rather than global menus where possible;
- let shortages, festivals, transport changes or resident absence alter what the player can actually access;
- keep exact Caelo prices and crafting rules separate until the project explicitly decides which homebrew carries into Ouros.

## 5. Supplied Caelo regional material — resources already belong to places

The supplied Caelo Region Location & Encounter List and merged regional material include location-specific item/fossil tables and excavation contexts. The region therefore already connects some resources to geography, hazards and exploration.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Materials should have provenance. A fossil, shard, herb, berry, salvage part or unusual natural resource becomes more interesting when the world remembers where it came from and why it was obtainable there.

Ouros translation:
Store origin information separately from mechanical item definition:
- source location;
- source event;
- finder/harvester;
- extraction method;
- date/world phase;
- ecological or institutional restrictions;
- later transformations.

## 6. Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Rescue Team DX — services make a hub operational

Primary source:
- https://mysterydungeon.pokemon.com/en-us/world/

Pokémon Square contains multiple facilities with distinct operational purposes, including separate Kecleon shops. The official material also presents storage and banking functions in the hub.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A settlement economy becomes legible when services have different owners, spaces and functions. One universal shop weakens local identity.

Ouros translation:
A settlement may expose services through specific residents or institutions:
- general goods;
- specialist equipment;
- storage;
- repair;
- food;
- transport;
- research supplies;
- crafting materials;
- commissions.

Not every settlement needs every service. Missing capability can itself create travel, trade or recruitment stories.

## 7. Festival Plaza — service ecosystems can grow through social participation

Official sources:
- https://www.pokemon.co.jp/ex/sun_moon/exchange/161004_01.html
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/strategy/the-first-timers-guide-to-the-alola-region

Festival Plaza ties visitor interaction to a growing collection of facilities and services. Shops, dyeing, food and training-related facilities coexist inside one social space, and development expands what the place can do.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Service growth can be social and institutional rather than only construction-based. A market district or Commons can become richer because new specialists, visitors, partnerships or reputation arrive.

Ouros translation:
Settlement capability should be calculated from people, facilities, supply routes and current state. Upgrading a market may mean attracting a dyer or specialist supplier rather than buying `market level +1`.

## 8. Real-world Pokémon Centers — regional craft identity as place-making reference

Primary source:
- https://corporate.pokemon.co.jp/en/business/shops

The Pokémon Company describes official Pokémon Centers whose concepts, merchandise and events are adapted to regional identity, including traditional local crafts and design references.

This is a real-world business source, not fictional Pokémon canon.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Material culture can make regions memorable. A town can be known for a local craft, material, food, pattern, tool style or artisan tradition that reflects ecology and history.

Ouros translation:
Regional identity can influence:
- workshop visual design;
- locally common materials;
- non-mechanical cosmetic variants;
- festival goods;
- commission themes;
- NPC professions;
- trade relationships;
- heritage disputes.

Do not copy real-world regional products into Ouros unless deliberately authored.

## 9. Eevee Expo fangames — crafting works best when drops and sidequests feed it

Sources:
- https://eeveeexpo.com/spee/
- https://eeveeexpo.com/released-games/
- https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/5935/

Public fangame descriptions show several recurring combinations:
- crafting from wild or exploration drops;
- refining low-value materials into useful supplies;
- sidequests that reward equipment or recipes;
- crafting systems that expand as progression advances;
- central hubs whose activities unlock over time;
- archaeology or region-specific exploration tied to new items.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Crafting should consume outputs already produced by exploration, ecology, jobs and dungeons. It should not require a parallel grind detached from narrative play.

Ouros translation:
A material should preferably enter the economy because of an existing game activity:
- gathered during exploration;
- recovered from a dungeon;
- grown by a settlement;
- produced through a legal Pokémon capability;
- salvaged after an event;
- traded from another region;
- rewarded by an institution;
- commissioned through a specialist.

Avoid designing arbitrary crafting drops merely to feed recipes unless they also make ecological/world sense.

## 10. Public Pokémon RP — commerce can create faction and community pressure

Source:
- https://forums.serebii.net/threads/losmia-horizon-a-pok%C3%A9mon-roleplay-pg13.672907/post-18927977

The public Losmia Horizon RP describes a corporation that provides widespread access to Pokémon supplies, food and other goods while also creating pressure on smaller independent shops. The specific company, setting and plot are not imported.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
An efficient service can be both beneficial and disruptive. Economic conflict does not need a cartoonishly evil merchant.

Ouros translation:
Possible tensions:
- chain supplier vs local artisan;
- cheap imports vs regional craft tradition;
- conservation limits vs material demand;
- reliable transport vs local self-sufficiency;
- emergency rationing vs ordinary commerce;
- faction sponsorship vs workshop independence.

A market conflict should be grounded in services, dependencies and affected residents rather than abstract ideology.

## 11. Public roleplay economies — work itself can be social content

Sources:
- https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/pokemon_roleplay/
- https://www.smogon.com/forums/threads/role-playing-approval-center-update-5-12-13.86090/

Public Pokémon RP communities have used shops, jobs, berry gathering, food businesses and message boards as recurring social activity. One Smogon RP proposal explicitly frames berry work as an interactive roleplay activity rather than passive currency generation.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A productive profession can generate scenes through customers, coworkers, suppliers, Pokémon helpers and changing demand. The valuable content is often the relationship network around production rather than the currency payout itself.

## 12. Design conclusion — item provenance can create callbacks

Across crafting, excavation, repair and trade systems, objects can retain narrative history.

A useful Ouros object record may distinguish:
- mechanical definition;
- physical instance;
- creator;
- source materials;
- owner/custodian history;
- repairs;
- public significance;
- linked Chronicle events.

This makes it possible for a repaired tool, tournament prize, expedition artifact or custom Poké Ball to reappear meaningfully later without granting unsupported mechanical bonuses.

## 13. Scarcity should create decisions, not chores

Persistent economies can become tedious when every item requires repetitive gathering.

Ouros should prefer scarcity that is:
- caused by understandable world state;
- temporary or addressable;
- visible before it becomes punitive;
- connected to alternate suppliers or solutions;
- capable of generating social, exploration or logistics content.

Examples:
- a storm closes a supply route;
- a specialist leaves town;
- a berry blight reduces one ingredient;
- a festival spikes demand;
- a faction buys up a material;
- a protected habitat limits harvesting.

The generator should avoid constant random shortages solely to simulate an economy.

## 14. Design conclusions from this pass

1. Items need instance provenance when they become story-significant.
2. Crafting should be capability/expertise/facility-aware rather than universally available.
3. Materials should originate from existing ecology, exploration, jobs, settlement production or trade.
4. Workshops need owners, dependencies and service state.
5. Repair creates object continuity and can preserve player attachment.
6. Recipe knowledge can be progression without becoming a second XP bar.
7. Regional craft identity strengthens place-making.
8. Markets can generate faction, labor and community stories without requiring villainy.
9. Service availability should depend on residents, facilities and supply routes.
10. Scarcity should be causal, legible and narratively productive.
11. Production professions should create social relationships, not only currency.
12. The world should distinguish mechanical item type from persistent item instance.

## PTU / Caelo mechanics boundary

The supplied PTU/Caelo corpus remains authoritative for executable crafting and item behavior.

Before any generated production action becomes mechanical content, validate:
- required Trainer Edge/Class/Feature;
- actual Skill prerequisites;
- required tool or facility;
- material inputs;
- Extended Action or other action requirement;
- item cost and effect;
- repair rules;
- Pokémon capability legality;
- Caelo-specific recipes or commands intentionally retained;
- current AutoPTU item implementation.

This pass does not create new recipes, item bonuses, prices, durability rules, harvesting yields or crafting DCs.

## Copyright and provenance guardrail

No external dialogue, distinctive characters, complete plots, custom recipes or copyrighted prose are imported. Sources are retained only to support high-level structural observations. Ouros materials, artisans, workshops, markets, commissions and regional traditions created from these lessons must be original and remain non-canon until reviewed.
