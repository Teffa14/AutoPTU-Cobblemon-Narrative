## Research question

How can Ouros represent recurring local practices, oral accounts, changing customs, inherited explanations and community participation without silently inventing a religion, a region-wide culture, a historical truth, a festival calendar or supernatural doctrine?

This pass deliberately uses `tradition` in the narrow world-state sense of a repeated or remembered practice supported by attributed evidence. It does not mean that every Marea resident shares the practice or assigns it the same meaning.

## External source 1 — Kitakami Festival of Masks

Source: Bulbapedia, `Festival of Masks`.
URL: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Festival_of_Masks
Accessed: 2026-09-01.

High-level observations:

- a recurring local festival can persist for generations;
- material practices such as masks, stalls and games make continuity visible;
- participation can include visitors as well as locals;
- a current public explanation of a tradition can differ from what later evidence suggests happened historically;
- the practice remains socially real even if part of its inherited explanation is wrong.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

A recurring practice and its claimed origin must be stored separately. Correcting an origin story does not retroactively erase past gatherings, objects, memories or relationships created through the practice.

Do not copy:

- Festival of Masks names;
- Loyal Three/Ogerpon story beats;
- mask-maker characters;
- specific masks, games or food stalls;
- the distinctive hidden-truth plot.

## External source 2 — Kitakami inherited public story versus concealed account

Sources: Bulbapedia, `Loyal Three`; Bulbapedia, myths/legends material covering Kitakami.
URLs:

- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Loyal_Three
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Myths_and_legends_involving_Legendary_and_Mythical_Pok%C3%A9mon

Accessed: 2026-09-01.

High-level observations:

- public memory can preserve one explanation for generations;
- a minority or family account can preserve a conflicting explanation;
- physical monuments and recurring celebration can reinforce the public version;
- later evidence can challenge interpretation without changing the fact that people historically acted on the older version.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

Oral accounts need teller, audience, date, scope and provenance. A majority version should not automatically overwrite a minority version, and a later correction should not rewrite what earlier residents genuinely believed or did.

This lesson is about provenance and social continuity, not about importing Kitakami's mythology.

High-level observation:

A repeated community activity can outlive or diverge from the historical event said to inspire it. The current rules and material setup of the practice are themselves things that can change over time.

Reusable Ouros lesson:

## Design structures extracted

### 1. Practice can be real while its explanation remains uncertain

Store the repeated action and its origin/meaning claims separately.

A community may genuinely repeat a route walk, shared meal, maintenance custom, seasonal gathering or phrase even if nobody can prove when it began.

### 2. Oral history needs provenance

An account should preserve:

- teller;
- audience;
- date/time;
- setting;
- whether it was firsthand, inherited or explicitly speculative;
- summarized claims;
- linked evidence;
- contradictions;
- later corrections.

Do not flatten several tellings into one canonical paragraph merely because they overlap.

### 3. Different tellings are not automatically deception

Variation can come from:

- memory;
- audience;
- emphasis;
- translation;
- family tradition;
- different source access;
- changed vocabulary;
- later evidence;
- practical versus ceremonial context.

The system should allow disagreement without spawning a villain.

### 4. Participation does not prove belief

A resident can attend because of hospitality, family, habit, curiosity, work, food, duty or friendship.

A visitor can participate respectfully without becoming a local or endorsing every associated story.

### 5. Physical traces can survive meaning changes

A marker, route object, table arrangement, token, old notice, recipe card, decoration or archive record may outlive the interpretation once attached to it.

Provenance and meaning remain separate.

### 6. A skipped instance does not erase a custom

Weather, work, mourning, transport or ecological conditions may cause one expected recurrence not to happen. That is historical continuity, not automatic cultural extinction.

### 7. Customs can change without a formal organizer

A repeated practice can drift through participant behavior. Not every change requires an institution, council vote or named festival director.

This is useful for Marea because it avoids inventing authority before canon establishes it.

### 8. Outsider labels are attributed claims

A visitor might call an old marker a shrine, monument, altar, grave, trail marker or lucky stone. Until local evidence establishes the category, the server records who used which label rather than canonizing the outsider's interpretation.

### 9. Pokémon presence is not supernatural validation

If wild Pokémon repeatedly appear near a gathering, that can be an ecological observation.

It cannot automatically prove:

- blessing;
- haunting;
- divine approval;
- ritual success;
- spirit identity;
- prophecy.

The ecology and supernatural-claim layers must remain separate.

### 10. Repetition should create world consequences

Useful persistent consequences include:

- changed wear on a route;
- stored objects returning each instance;
- updated notices;
- an oral account added to Tideglass;
- residents remembering who attended;
- a visitor returning because of a prior experience;
- a safety practice revised after a problem;
- an old version kept in the archive;
- a practice pausing for a season;
- one household or institution using a variant rather than the dominant version.

## Recommended Ouros seam

Create a `community tradition / oral history / repeated practice continuity` layer.

It should own:

- recurring-practice identity;
- individual instances;
- practice versions;
- participation evidence;
- oral accounts and variants;
- attributed meaning/origin claims;
- material traces linked to provenance;
- public/private scope;
- continuity through skipped or changed instances.

It should not own:

- PTU Skill progression;
- supernatural truth;
- religious doctrine;
- public-memory publication authority;
- funeral mechanics;
- visitor status;
- archive custody;
- language translation;
- battle resolution;
- festival adjudication.

## Strong first slice

`Two Tellings at Tideglass`

Tideglass contains two separately sourced accounts of the same older Marea work practice or storm-season habit. Most operational details overlap. One origin claim and one sequence detail differ.

Pia prepares a comparison. Taro preserves both tellings and their provenance. The player can locate another mundane record that narrows one part of the disagreement without forcing a single total answer.

Why this is strong:

- uses canon residents and institution;
- no new religion;
- no new settlement;
- no battle;
- no supernatural mechanic;
- no Skill check required;
- immediately tests oral-account provenance and variant continuity;
- can later feed public memory, education, visitor dialogue or festival development.

## Mechanically rich future pattern

`Community Route Walk at Glass Bend`

A recurring practical/community walk uses part of Sendero del Vidrio. Participants do not all need to assign the same meaning to it. Local wild activity creates an immediate safety problem.

The full version may involve noncombatant protection, corridor geometry, retreat, Interception, forced movement and environmental conditions.

The reduced version keeps the social practice and participants entirely in Narrative, moves noncombatants to a safe authored state, and compiles only a remaining immediate wild threat into a standard audited battle on stable terrain.

Battle output may establish only narrow tactical facts such as `IMMEDIATE_ROUTE_THREAT_WITHDREW` or `IMMEDIATE_PASSAGE_CLEAR`.

Battle cannot establish:

- what the practice means;
- whether it is ancient;
- whether everyone believes its story;
- whether it becomes a festival;
- whether a supernatural claim is true;
- whether a visitor is now local;
- whether the practice must recur next year.

## Research conclusion

Pokémon provides strong structural evidence for recurring practices whose public explanation, material form and historical interpretation can diverge over time. PTU community material supports folklore and local custom as campaign texture but does not provide an authoritative cultural subsystem. PTU itself supplies Skills that must remain mechanically authoritative when knowledge checks matter.

Ouros should therefore treat culture as persistent attributed practice plus evidence, not as a universal lore paragraph. That allows Marea to accumulate identity gradually while leaving religion, Caelo-wide custom and supernatural truth genuinely open.
