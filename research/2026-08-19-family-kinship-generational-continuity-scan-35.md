# Ouros Research Pass 35 — Family, Kinship & Generational Continuity

Status: research/provenance only. Not canon.
Date: 2026-08-19

## Why this pass

The repository already has systems for homes/households, social bonds, public memory, breeding/eggs/lineage, education, workplaces and legacy. It does not yet have a dedicated model for human kinship, guardianship, intergenerational continuity, inherited expectations, family-linked institutions or player-consented family relations.

This matters because a household is not necessarily a family; Pokémon biological lineage is not human kinship; and public legacy does not tell us who is related to whom.

## Sources and reusable structures

### PTU — generational campaigns

Source: https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-mysterious-ruins/

The Oran Valley campaign seed explicitly suggests a generational structure where characters can settle down, form families and later pass play to their children. The useful design lesson is not the specific town or ruin plot. It is that a persistent Pokémon campaign can move across generations while preserving location history, unresolved problems and institutional consequences.

Reusable structure:
- generation A changes a place;
- time passes;
- generation B inherits a changed world, not a reset world;
- prior PCs can become historical figures, relatives, mentors or absent influences without remaining the active protagonists.

### PTU — family legacy from a previous campaign

Source: https://pokemontabletop.com/over-there-a-world-war-one-pokemon-campaign-a-retrospective/

The retrospective describes a PC whose parents were characters from an earlier campaign. Legendary Pokémon recognized the family because of earlier events, while the new PC wanted to establish an identity independent of that legacy.

Reusable structures:
- inherited reputation is different from personal reputation;
- NPCs may know a family name while knowing little about the current member;
- family legacy can open doors, create pressure, produce mistaken assumptions or attract attention;
- a descendant should be able to reject, reinterpret or ignore inherited expectations.

### Pokémon official — family members with divergent paths

Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/celebrate-25-years-of-pokemon-with-memorable-moments-from-the-alola-region

The official Alola retrospective emphasizes that Lillie, Gladion and Lusamine belong to the same family while taking sharply different paths and occupying different organizations and roles.

Reusable structure:
- kinship does not imply faction alignment;
- relatives can possess different knowledge, loyalties, public reputations and objectives;
- one family can intersect several parts of the world without functioning as one unified actor.

### Pokémon official — sibling identity beside public fame

Sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/animation/seasons/25/episode-25-curtain-up-fight-the-fights
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/hop-zamazenta-and-sygna-suit-leon-eternatus-in-pokemon-masters-ex

Hop and Leon provide a useful high-level pattern where one sibling has an established public career and the other develops a separate competitive identity. Do not copy their personalities or story.

Reusable structure:
- family fame can create expectations without dictating mechanics;
- public comparison between relatives may exist even when the characters do not share goals;
- institutional records should distinguish personal achievements from family association.

### Fangame — siblings beginning the same journey differently

Source: https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/9507/

Pokémon Nimbus publicly describes two siblings beginning a journey from the same family context and competing with each other. This is useful only as a structural example of sibling-linked progression. No plot, names, dialogue or original characters should be reused.

Reusable structure:
- relatives may share an origin point but diverge in routes, teams, careers and relationships;
- sibling rivalry does not need hostility;
- shared family events can create callbacks available to both characters.

### Roleplay community — consent for pre-existing family relations

Source: https://www.fanfiction.net/topic/190447/150293963/1/STEP-ONE-FORUM-RULES-and-FAQs

This public Pokémon RP community explicitly requires players to consult other roleplayers before establishing sibling/parent/spouse relationships with their characters.

This supports a strong multiplayer guardrail for Ouros: the generator must never create a family relationship between player-controlled characters without explicit consent from all affected players.

### Roleplay community — family responsibilities as a reason to begin an adventure

Source: https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/lets-get-out-of-littleroot-town-discussion.30609/

A public character concept describes an older sibling who spent years helping care for younger siblings and only later becomes free to travel. The useful pattern is that family responsibilities can shape timing, profession and motivation without requiring tragedy or villainy.

Reusable structure:
- obligations can delay or redirect a journey;
- relatives can create ordinary, non-combat stakes;
- leaving home can be a meaningful transition even without severing family ties.

## Design lessons for Ouros

### Kinship must be explicit

Never infer parent, child, sibling, spouse, partner, guardian, adoption, ancestry or inheritance merely from:
- shared residence;
- surname;
- visual similarity;
- age difference;
- joint ownership;
- shared Pokémon;
- repeated caregiving;
- NPC dialogue from a third party.

Every kinship edge needs authored or evidenced provenance.

### Family is not a faction

A family should not have one shared relationship score, alignment or knowledge pool by default. Each member remains an independent actor.

### Inherited reputation is contextual

If a parent, sibling or ancestor was famous, another character may know that fact. This can affect expectations or initial recognition. It must not grant automatic trust, fear, skill, wealth, political authority or battle bonuses.

### Guardianship, residence and ownership are separate

A guardian may not own a residence. A resident may not be a guardian. A relative may not be a legal custodian. The world must not silently import real-world family law.

### Generational continuity should preserve agency

A descendant can inherit:
- records;
- unresolved claims;
- items with provenance;
- public expectations;
- invitations;
- enemies' assumptions;
- access to family archives where explicitly authorized.

A descendant does not automatically inherit:
- personality;
- loyalty;
- Trainer Classes;
- Pokémon ownership;
- guilt;
- debts unless canon defines them as transferable;
- political office;
- consent from previous characters.

### Retirement does not make a PC an NPC puppet

If a retired player character appears later, only confirmed state may be used. The generator cannot invent marriage, children, death, career changes, ideology or Pokémon disposition for that former PC without player-authorized canon.

## PTU/Caelo mechanical boundary

The supplied PTU/Caelo material already treats character background, skills, Features and Pokémon choices as explicit character-building state. Family background can provide narrative context but must not grant mechanical benefits unless a governing PTU/Caelo rule explicitly does so.

Do not invent:
- inheritance bonuses;
- family teamwork bonuses;
- sibling initiative effects;
- guardian reactions;
- legacy Features;
- automatic shared Loyalty;
- shared Trainer levels;
- shared inventory or Pokémon custody.

## Copyright/provenance rule

All source material above is used for high-level structural lessons only. Ouros proposals must use original people, locations, institutions and conflicts. Do not reproduce dialogue, distinctive character arcs or plot sequences from the sources.

## Research directions for later passes

- families operating businesses or Gyms across generations;
- adoption/guardianship structures only after Ouros civic/legal canon exists;
- inheritance of property/items after ownership rules exist;
- family archives and private information access;
- intergenerational Pokémon partnerships without treating Pokémon as inheritable property by default;
- succession in institutions versus succession inside a family;
- family-linked festivals and traditions without importing real cultural practices wholesale.
