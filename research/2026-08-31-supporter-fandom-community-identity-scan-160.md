# Ouros Research Scan — Supporters, Fandom, Community Identity & Public Attention

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Not established Ouros canon.
Date: 2026-08-31
Pass: 160

## Research question

What reusable structures can make supporters, fan groups, local followings and public attention persist across seasons without turning popularity into omniscient reputation, mechanical bonuses, universal crowd behavior or automatic faction membership?

This scan follows the existing authority boundaries in Narrative. Public Memory already owns what communities remember. Media/Communications owns information packets, publication, channels and delivery. Battle Institutions owns formal challenge circuits. Performance Production owns productions, performances and audience records. Social Bonds owns interpersonal relationships. Civic Governance owns authored collective decision procedures. This pass looks only at the missing connective tissue: durable supporter identity, group formation, participation, local customs, travel, artifacts, internal disagreement and changing relationships between a public figure/institution and the people who voluntarily follow them.

## Internal overlap findings

The full repository tree was inspected before selecting this topic. Targeted searches also checked supporter, fan club, fandom, publication, hospitality, weather, construction, civic governance and adjacent themes.

Existing layers already establish several hard limits:

- Public Memory may store public standing, audience reactions, records and contested memory, but it does not model the internal continuity of a supporter group.
- Media/Communications can publish reports, interviews, corrections and messages, but receiving information does not make an actor a supporter.
- Battle Institutions can define competitors, circuits, rankings and venues, but a competitor's following is not equivalent to their rank.
- Performance Production can record audience response to an episode, but one audience episode does not define a durable fan community.
- Ritual/Tradition can preserve inherited practices, but a new chant, travel custom or fan-made artifact does not become a historical tradition merely because supporters repeat it twice.
- Material Culture owns physical objects and provenance. A scarf, badge, homemade sign or photo album remains an object there even when this layer records its supporter meaning.

The new surface should therefore attach to those owners rather than replace them.

## Pokémon source patterns

### Galar League Cards — public identity can outgrow competitive results

Source: Bulbapedia, League Card.
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/League_Card

Galar's League Card material repeatedly separates battle record from public persona. Marnie has local celebrity and a personal cheering squad while showing little interest in fame. Gordie maintains a fan base through autographs, events and fan-supported photo albums. Raihan's public image includes popular selfies and harsh comments after losses. Leon's public identity extends far beyond a single match result.

Reusable structure:

- public attention can have multiple sources;
- supporter activity can produce artifacts and events of its own;
- a loss can change discourse without erasing support;
- a person can appreciate support while rejecting some forms of celebrity;
- local identity can attach to a public figure without making that figure the owner of the community.

Do not import individual Galar characters, dialogue, exact league structure, card mechanics or popularity claims into Ouros.

### Marnie and Team Yell — support can help, embarrass, obstruct or misrepresent

Sources:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Marnie
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Piers

Team Yell is useful structurally because supporter behavior and supported-person intent can diverge. The supported figure does not automatically authorize everything followers do.

Reusable structure:

`SUPPORTER_ACTION != SUPPORTED_PERSON_INSTRUCTION`

A fan group can create positive local identity, nuisance, access pressure, travel incidents, mutual aid, misinformation or embarrassment without those outputs becoming the public figure's authored actions.

This is especially important for faction attribution. A group wearing a Trainer's colors is not necessarily that Trainer's faction, employee base or command network.

### Gordie — fan production as independent cultural output

Source: Bulbapedia, Gordie.
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gordie

Gordie's fan base supports photo albums and values behaviors that outsiders might interpret differently. This suggests that supporters should be able to create derivative cultural objects with their own provenance and interpretation.

Reusable structure:

- fan-made album, sign, club newsletter or archive can exist independently of official media;
- supporter interpretation can be affectionate, critical, ironic, nostalgic or divided;
- creator identity and subject approval remain separate;
- circulation belongs to Media/Communications while object provenance belongs to Material Culture.

### Melony/Gordie town split — public affiliation can divide one settlement

Source: Bulbapedia, Melony.
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Melony_%28Gym_Leader%29

The useful pattern is a town developing divided public allegiance after a high-profile competitive/family dispute. Ouros should not reduce this to one settlement-wide opinion value.

Reusable structure:

- multiple supporter communities can coexist in the same place;
- households, clubs and workplaces need not align;
- a public disagreement can become part of local identity without becoming civil conflict;
- later reconciliation between principals does not automatically reconcile every supporter group.

### League Cards and fan mimicry — style can diffuse without formal membership

Source: Bulbapedia, League Card.
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/League_Card

Marnie's rare League Card notes fans imitating a local dialect associated with her. The high-level lesson is cultural diffusion: phrases, clothes, poses, training aesthetics, nicknames or rituals may spread through supporters without proving formal organization.

Ouros should record diffusion through observable adoption events, not a magic popularity radius.

### Piers after office — following can outlive an institutional role

Source: Bulbapedia, Piers.
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Piers

Piers continues public activity after leaving Gym leadership. The design lesson is that institutional office, competitive eligibility, performer identity and supporter relationship have different lifecycles.

`OFFICE_ENDED != FOLLOWING_ENDED`

`FOLLOWING_ENDED != PUBLIC_MEMORY_ENDED`

This supports retired Gym Leaders, former champions, past performers, researchers or expedition leaders whose public communities change instead of disappearing.

## Real-world supporter design patterns

### Supporters are heterogeneous stakeholders

Source: UEFA Supporter Liaison Officer material.
https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/026f-13d62b668a5d-d52d71c97e09-1000--uefa-supporter-liaison-guide-building-bridges-between-club/

UEFA's supporter-liaison material emphasizes dialogue between clubs and varied supporter groups rather than treating all fans as one homogeneous block. It also identifies relationships with club staff, transport, safety actors and counterpart groups.

Reusable structure for Ouros:

- supporter communities can have different interests and norms;
- an institution may maintain liaison relationships with several groups;
- away travel can create temporary coordination needs;
- communication channels can exist without granting governance authority;
- conflict between supporter groups does not imply conflict between the institutions or people they follow.

No real-world licensing, policing, stadium law or football governance rule is imported.

### Fan voice can be organized without becoming ownership or command

Sources:
https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0213-0f8a44bc31f5-412683f17cf4-1000--uefa-meets-with-fan-groups/
https://www.footballsupporterseurope.org/

The reusable lesson is organizational diversity. Support can remain informal, become a membership organization, create volunteer activity or develop a recognized liaison route. These are different states.

Ouros should preserve that distinction rather than promote every repeated fan gathering into a faction.

## Extracted design lessons

### 1. Followings need identity independent of the followed subject

A supporter group should have its own identity, origin, membership claims, internal practices and history. The supported person or institution is a subject of attachment, not necessarily the group's owner.

### 2. Membership must be evidenced

Wearing colors, attending a match or liking a public figure does not establish club membership. Membership may be informal, registered or deliberately ambiguous depending on authored world state.

### 3. Public attention has event provenance

A spike in attention should point to observable causes: a battle, performance, interview, rescue, controversy, retirement, transfer of office, publication or supporter-created event.

### 4. Support can persist through poor performance

Competitive losses, failed performances and scandals can change the composition or discourse of a following without forcing abandonment.

### 5. Supporters can disagree with the person they support

A group may oppose a retirement, strategy, sponsorship, venue move, public statement or faction alliance while remaining supporters.

### 6. Supporter-created artifacts deserve provenance

Homemade banners, scrapbooks, songs, photo collections, newsletters, badges, nicknames and memorial objects can become durable world history. Material Culture owns the object; Media owns circulation; this layer owns supporter meaning and group attribution.

### 7. Away support creates travel stories without automatic battles

A visiting group can need lodging, transport, route information, meeting places and liaison. Travel disruption can change attendance without implying hostility.

### 8. Crowd size must not become a truth detector

A large cheering section does not prove merit, guilt, innocence, popularity everywhere or institutional legitimacy.

### 9. Fan pressure and formal authority are separate

Supporter petitions, boycotts, attendance changes or public campaigns can become Civic Governance or institution inputs where appropriate. This layer records the activity and its provenance; the target system decides the actual outcome.

### 10. A following can split, merge or fade without retcon

Historical group identity should remain queryable after a split or dissolution. New groups can inherit some symbols or members without becoming identical to the predecessor.

## Encounter implications

Most supporter content should remain social, travel, media, public-event and relationship play.

Mechanically rich incidents can appear around crowd movement, route access, evacuation or protection, but these must declare exact dependencies. Large crowds must never be converted into ordinary Minecraft entity AI and then treated as authoritative narrative behavior.

Likely full-version dependency families include complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement, full turn/round lifecycle, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, AI tactical policy and Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support. Reduced versions should move spectators and noncombatants out of BattleSpec before initiative and use a static perimeter or corridor encounter.
