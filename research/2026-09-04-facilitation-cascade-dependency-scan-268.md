## New public sources

Angelini & Silliman (2014), *Ecology*, “Secondary foundation species as drivers of trophic and functional diversity: evidence from a tree–epiphyte system”, DOI 10.1890/13-0496.1. Reusable lesson: a primary habitat-former can enable a secondary habitat-former, which then changes conditions for other organisms. The useful abstraction is a dependency chain backed by evidence at each step, not the source species or site.

Yakovis & Artemieva (2017), *Scientific Reports* 7:237, “Cockles, barnacles and ascidians compose a subtidal facilitation cascade with multiple hierarchical levels of foundation species”, DOI 10.1038/s41598-017-00260-2. Reusable lesson: facilitation hierarchies can contain more than two levels. Multiple levels do not justify assuming that every upstream element directly facilitates every downstream beneficiary.

Thomsen et al. (2018), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, “Secondary foundation species enhance biodiversity”, DOI 10.1038/s41559-018-0487-5. Reusable lesson: secondary habitat-formers can have measurable effects beyond the primary structure. Ouros should therefore preserve intermediate dependencies instead of flattening a habitat chain into a single generic ‘good habitat’ flag.

New Pokémon Snap was reviewed only as a high-level exploration reference: repeated visits to an environment can expose different subjects, routes and behaviors as observation progresses. Ouros can reuse the loop of revisit -> compare -> reinterpret while keeping its own ecology, locations, characters and causal model.

## Transformation for Ouros

CANON-ALIGNED: ecology, observation, persistent modification history and population authority remain separate. A physical structure can persist without its creator. Recipient use alone remains insufficient to prove benefit or canon `FACILITATES`.

PROPOSED: represent a facilitation cascade as an explicit directed dependency graph whose edges each carry their own provenance, interpretation state and validity. A secondary modification may require a primary modification as substrate, shelter, retained material or access condition. A later modification may depend on the secondary one.

PROPOSED: never perform automatic transitive closure. Evidence for A -> B and B -> C supports a two-edge chain. It does not by itself establish a direct A -> C facilitation edge.

PROPOSED: distinguish current physical dependency from historical legacy. If A closes, B may close, degrade, persist autonomously or become uncertain according to B’s own physical validity and semantic horizon. History is preserved whichever outcome occurs.

UNCERTAIN: which Ouros species create primary or secondary habitat modifications, and which actual sites support them. No such behavior is canonized by this scan.

UNCERTAIN: whether any future cascade feature maps to PTU cover, blocker, difficult terrain, hazard, zone or other combat semantics. Minecraft geometry is presentation evidence only until an exact capability path is admitted.

## Design consequence

A useful Ouros investigation can expose the chain in reverse. The player first notices a beneficiary or secondary structure, then identifies what enables it, later discovers the older modification beneath it, and finally watches one layer degrade. The mystery is causal and environmental rather than a hidden quest flag. The reduced form requires only ecology/provenance/presentation. Battle effects remain separately gated.
