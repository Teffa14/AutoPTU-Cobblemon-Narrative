# Finance, Sponsorship, Patronage & Risk Research — Pass 40

Status: research only. Not Ouros canon.
Date: 2026-08-19

## Research question

How can Ouros make money, funding, sponsorship, patronage, prizes, economic risk and institutional finance produce persistent narrative consequences without becoming a spreadsheet simulator or silently inventing laws, prices, contracts and PTU mechanics?

## Existing-repo gap

The current repository already has strong systems for:
- material provenance, crafting and workshops;
- workplaces and staffing;
- markets, food, hospitality and supply routes;
- civic works and infrastructure;
- battle institutions and public competitions;
- media and public reputation;
- science, grants-adjacent institutions and field programs;
- crises and recovery;
- cases, custody and evidence.

What is still missing is a dedicated layer for who funds an activity, what the money is restricted to, whether a promised payment actually happened, what obligations accompany support, who bears financial risk, how sponsor influence differs from ownership/control, and how a project can be financially stressed without requiring a villain.

## Source 1 — Galar corporate sponsorship is explicitly part of public battle culture

Official Pokémon Sword/Shield material states that corporations in Galar sponsor Gyms and the Champion, with company logos appearing on uniforms, facilities and stadium signage. It also describes Galarian battling as mass entertainment whose major matches are broadcast across the region.

Source:
https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-us/people-galar-region/rivals/

Reusable structure:
- sponsorship can be visible in the environment;
- a battle institution may have several supporters rather than a single owner;
- a famous Trainer can accumulate many sponsors;
- funding, prestige, media visibility and athletic success can reinforce one another;
- sponsorship can exist alongside League authority without automatically becoming League authority.

Ouros adaptation:
A Gym, Contest venue, expedition, research program, festival or athlete may have funding partners. Their logos, plaques, equipment donations, public statements or naming rights can appear in Minecraft as world state. The sponsor relationship should remain a separate object from ownership, operational command and rules authority.

Copyright boundary:
Do not copy Galar corporations, logos, characters or sponsor names. Use only the structural fact that visible commercial sponsorship exists within Pokémon society.

## Source 2 — Poké Jobs connect institutions, work requests and rewards

The official Poké Jobs page says corporations and universities request Pokémon assistance, that jobs are accepted through Rotomi, and that successful work can produce rewards and experience.

Source:
https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-us/gameplay/pokejobs/

Reusable structure:
- institutions can publish work demand rather than directly hiring every actor permanently;
- a job request can include suitability requirements;
- completion creates both economic and developmental consequences;
- universities and corporations can coexist as requesters in the same network.

Ouros adaptation:
Temporary paid work, research contracts, institutional assignments and service calls can be represented as funding/work agreements connected to the existing JOB, workplace and institution systems. Exact payment and PTU rewards must come from authored rules/state, not the narrative generator.

## Source 3 — Join Avenue shows local economic development through people and services

Join Avenue begins mostly empty and develops as visitors become residents, open shops and attract more visitors. Shop composition can change and the avenue becomes more useful as its service ecosystem grows.

Source:
https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Join_Avenue

This is a secondary reference, not a PTU rules source.

Reusable structure:
- economic growth can be spatial and visible;
- new services depend on specific people being present;
- popularity and foot traffic can change which businesses survive;
- development can create opportunity costs because space is limited;
- a commercial district can become a persistent project rather than a static menu.

Ouros adaptation:
The settlement layer can use funding and tenancy decisions to explain why a clinic annex, café, workshop, equipment supplier or research office appears in a district. The finance layer should record who funded the opening and whether that funding carried obligations, but should not calculate a full simulated economy unless deliberately enabled.

## Source 4 — PTU campaign seeds treat economic effects as long-term world consequences

The official PTU campaign seed “The Road to Tomorrow” repeatedly connects institutions to regional development. In Talent Scouts, towns with League presence receive more attention and recover economically faster. In the Holon Institute concept, players can decide which facilities and specialties receive resources. In The Last Caravan, settlement restoration can eventually support merchants and goods moving between communities.

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-the-road-to-tomorrow/

Reusable structure:
- player decisions about institutional placement can redistribute opportunity;
- funding priorities can define what a research program becomes;
- infrastructure investment can create later trade and migration;
- economic benefit itself can become a political pressure without requiring corruption;
- a campaign can ask “who receives scarce institutional support?” rather than only “who wins the fight?”

Ouros adaptation:
Funding decisions should generate traceable world-state effects. A research lab funded for ecology may produce different services, staff demand and future quests than one funded for paleontology. A new Gym or transport hub may create lodging, vendors and jobs nearby while another town receives less attention.

## Source 5 — Discovery can create a local economic boom

The official PTU “Mysterious Ruins” campaign seed explicitly suggests that outside discovery of a supernatural/archaeological site can cause a population and economic boom, including merchants, tourism and a town economy built around expeditions.

Source:
https://pokemontabletop.com/campaign-seeds-mysterious-ruins/

Reusable structure:
- discovery can create demand before institutions are ready;
- economic growth can increase pressure on heritage, ecology, housing and infrastructure;
- a boom can produce honest opportunity, opportunism, speculation and public conflict at the same time;
- the same site can support researchers, merchants, tourists, residents and conservation concerns.

Ouros adaptation:
The finance layer can model investment interest, sponsorship offers, temporary grants, emergency spending and speculative projects triggered by a discovery. Whether those projects actually happen depends on civic, ownership, conservation, staffing and infrastructure state.

## Source 6 — PTU 1.05 has an explicit Money Matters / shop layer

Public mirrors of the PTU 1.05 Core Rulebook place “Money Matters” immediately before “Creating Shops” in the GM chapter and show examples of financial support, item rewards and shop availability.

Discovery mirror:
https://anyflip.com/deia/psdg/basic/451-500

This mirror is used only to confirm where the subsystem exists. The project-supplied PTU/Caelo source files remain authoritative for exact values and rules.

Design implication:
Ouros should not replace PTU money with a homebrew universal Favor/debt/sponsor resource. The narrative layer can track agreements and provenance around payments, but mechanical money, item prices, Trainer rewards and shop inventories must be validated against the governing PTU/Caelo material and actual project decisions.

## Source 7 — Pokémon Odyssey’s Favor is useful only as a design contrast

The current Pokémon Tabletop wiki for Pokémon Odyssey uses Favor as an abstraction of money, assets, favors and connections, and deliberately ignores many routine background expenses.

Sources:
https://pokemontabletop.com/wiki/index.php/Inventory
https://pokemontabletop.com/wiki/index.php/Gameplay_Structure

This is NOT PTU 1.05 authority and must not be imported as a rule.

Reusable design lesson:
Routine background expenses should often compress. Narrative focus belongs on spending/funding decisions that alter access, obligations, preparation, institutions or relationships.

Ouros adaptation:
Keep ordinary food, lodging and incidental costs out of the story unless the campaign specifically chooses to make them relevant. Track financial state when it creates a meaningful consequence: a research grant expires, a sponsor withdraws, an insurer disputes a claim, a prize is restricted, a workshop cannot replace a machine, or a public project has competing funding packages.

## Source 8 — Real Play! Pokémon sponsorship policy is a process reference, not world lore

Play! Pokémon has formal sponsorship rules for major events, including approval requirements and restrictions on sponsor categories.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/play-pokemon/about/tournaments-rules-and-resources

The real policy must not be copied into Ouros. The useful structural lesson is simply that sponsorship can be scoped, disclosed and conditional without transferring ownership or competitive authority.

## Synthesis — finance should describe obligations and risk, not simulate capitalism by default

The strongest reusable pattern is not “add banks and stock markets.” It is:

1. Something needs resources.
2. A person/institution offers support.
3. The support has scope and conditions.
4. Funds or assets may be restricted to a purpose.
5. Delivery can be delayed, partial or cancelled.
6. The recipient may gain capability while also taking on obligations or public expectations.
7. Failure does not automatically mean fraud.
8. A dispute should separate contract terms, evidence, public belief and actual payment state.
9. The world should remember who funded what and what happened afterward.

This creates stories from grants, prizes, sponsorships, relief funds, scholarships, loans, donations, insurance and patronage without requiring a full macroeconomic simulation.

## High-value narrative patterns

### Restricted support
A donor funds reconstruction but only for one category of work. The money exists, yet it cannot automatically solve every problem.

### Honest disagreement over priorities
Two research teams compete for the same grant because both projects are worthwhile.

### Sponsor pressure without villainy
A sponsor wants visibility and measurable outcomes. The Trainer wants independence. Neither side needs to be malicious.

### Null-result research
A grant-supported expedition finds nothing unusual. The honest result still matters and can affect renewal or institutional trust.

### Prize with downstream consequences
Winning a public event creates money, attention and sponsor interest, but not automatic friendship, political power or mechanical progression.

### Claims and recovery
A damaged workshop may have coverage, partial coverage or no relevant policy. The claim process should depend on authored agreement state, not a universal insurance system.

### Funding withdrawal
A project survives the loss of a major funder because staffing, local support, reserves or alternative partners already exist.

### Economic boom
Discovery drives visitors and money into a settlement, which can strain transport, habitat, housing, clinics and public memory.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:
- sponsor = evil corporation by default;
- rich NPC = secretly corrupt by default;
- all financial disputes resolved by combat;
- arbitrary debt traps used to force the player into quests;
- dynamic prices changing constantly with no player-facing reason;
- money functioning as a hidden morality score;
- sponsorship granting battle buffs;
- reputation automatically converting into cash;
- loss of funds deleting player-owned Pokémon, homes or core progression;
- invented taxes, interest rates, contract law, bankruptcy, securities or insurance rules without canon approval.

## PTU/Caelo mechanical boundary

Authoritative project files remain the PTU Core Rulebook, Pokédex material, Caelo Player's Guide, Caelo rulebook/errata, character-creation material and Caelo Region Location & Encounter List.

Pass 40 does not define:
- starting money;
- shop prices;
- Trainer battle payouts;
- Job payment values;
- contest or tournament prize values;
- salary rates;
- interest rates;
- insurance premiums;
- sponsor bonuses;
- grant amounts;
- discount formulas;
- item resale rules;
- debt enforcement;
- legal ownership;
- taxes;
- banking law;
- procurement law.

Caelo already treats Jobs and other activity types as distinct content categories. A funding agreement can explain why a Job exists, but it does not redefine the Job's mechanical reward.

External sources are used for high-level structures only. Do not copy corporate names, fictional sponsors, dialogue, plots, unique characters, item text or distinctive quest chains. Preserve source URLs in research notes and transform all Ouros candidates into original material.

## Next implementation questions

- Does Ouros use ordinary PTU money directly as the only mechanical currency?
- Which institutions can issue grants, prizes or sponsorships?
- Can players own businesses, or only participate in workshops/clubs/institutions?
- Does Ouros have banks or only safekeeping/payment services?
- Will insurance exist at all, and if so for which assets?
- Are loans/debt worth implementing, or should they remain authored story state only?
- How should sponsorship visibility appear physically in Minecraft?
- Which payments are public and which are private?
- How should multiplayer prevent one player's funding agreement from binding another player without consent?
- Which economic state changes need actual numeric balances versus simple qualitative states?
