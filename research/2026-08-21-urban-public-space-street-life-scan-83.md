# Urban Public Space & Street Life Research Scan — Pass 83

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon.

Inspected: 2026-08-21.

## Scope

This pass studies plazas, parks, station forecourts, sidewalks, pedestrian streets, waterfront promenades, small greens, arcades and other shared urban spaces as persistent narrative systems.

The goal is to fill a gap between existing Ouros layers for Architecture, Homes/Neighborhoods, Demography, Travel, Tourism, Events, Social Bonds, Accessibility, Light, Sound, Waste, Public Memory and Wild Pokémon ecology.

This pass does not define property law, public-order law, crowd-combat rules, urban PTU Terrain, civilian HP, protest mechanics, police powers, spawn bonuses or Minecraft crowd simulation.

## Existing-project gap check

The current branch was inspected before writing. Existing systems already cover:

- physical structures and adaptive reuse;
- households and neighborhoods;
- settlement growth and population cohorts;
- transport nodes and route services;
- visitor pressure and tourism;
- recurring events and public memory;
- lighting and soundscapes;
- accessibility;
- wild collectives and urban ecology;
- commerce, workplaces and hospitality;
- civic decisions and public works.

No dedicated design layer was found for the shared public realm itself: who uses a space, how uses change by time, how edges and access shape activity, how ordinary co-presence becomes callbacks, or how Minecraft should project a busy place without treating loaded NPCs as population truth.

## Public Pokémon sources

### Pokémon Legends: Z-A — Lumiose as mixed urban habitat

Official Pokémon Legends: Z-A material describes Lumiose as an urban redevelopment project intended to create a city shared by people and Pokémon. It explicitly places shopping arcades, cafés and restaurants alongside waterfronts, parks and newly created wild zones.

Sources:

- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-au/story-world/lumiose-city
- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/news/adventure
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-video-games/pokemon-legends-z-a

Reusable design lesson:

A dense city does not need to separate “human content” and “Pokémon content” into different maps. Public life, commerce, habitats, research and encounters can overlap spatially while remaining different systems.

Ouros adaptation:

A public-space record may reference human activity patterns and Pokémon-use observations simultaneously. One does not automatically define the other.

### Time-programmed use of the same city

Official Z-A material establishes a strong day/night contrast: daytime supports urban exploration, preparation and wild-zone activity, while the nighttime competition changes where and how Trainers use parts of the city.

Source:

- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/news/adventure

Reusable design lesson:

The same geometry can have different programmed uses at different times.

Ouros adaptation:

A square can be a commuter shortcut in the morning, lunch space at midday, market in the afternoon and event venue at night. These are time-program revisions, not separate copies of the location.

Do not import Z-A Royale rules, battle-zone mechanics or progression.

### Centrico Plaza and visible urban identity

Official Lumiose material treats Centrico Plaza, cafés, museums, boulevards, stations and other recognizable places as part of the city’s public identity. The official Legends site also describes a café master specifically associated with Centrico Plaza.

Sources:

- https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/illuminating-lumiose-city
- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/story-world/characters/grisham

Reusable design lesson:

A public place gains identity through repeated uses, edges, institutions, recurring people and memories rather than through scenery alone.

### Urban Pokémon numbers can themselves become research

Official material for Mable states that the number of Pokémon in Lumiose has increased since redevelopment began and that the research lab studies their habits and the reason for the increase.

Source:

- https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/story-world/characters/mable

Reusable design lesson:

Redevelopment can change observed Pokémon presence without the narrative generator immediately declaring a cause.

Ouros adaptation:

New parks, food waste, lighting, water, shelter, traffic changes and route connectivity may become competing hypotheses. Loaded Cobblemon entities remain observations/projections, never the authoritative population estimate.

## Public Pokémon roleplay material

### Pokécharms as a large public RP corpus

Pokécharms currently exposes a public Pokémon RP forum with thousands of threads and a long-running archive of collaborative stories.

Sources:

Reusable design lesson:

Shared places are useful because they generate intersections without requiring every participant to share the same quest.

### Converted public places as social infrastructure

A public Pokécharms RP, “A Transformed World,” describes converted shops, a café, community meetings, employment and activism sharing the same neighborhood fabric.

Source:

- https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/a-transformed-world.21637/page-2

Only the structural lesson is reused:

A café, community room, storefront or park edge can support many unrelated social functions over time. Ouros must not copy its characters, transformation premise, organizations or prose.

### Location creation inside collaborative city RP

A public Pokécharms RP permits participants to create new places inside existing towns/cities when those places make sense within the current plot.

Source:

- https://forums.pokecharms.com/threads/behind-closed-doors-mature.19896/

Reusable design lesson:

Player-created micro-locations can be powerful when they inherit district constraints instead of spawning anywhere without consequence.

Ouros adaptation:

Future player-authored kiosks, club corners, memorial benches, gardens or meetup sites should link to an existing public-space ID and require the appropriate building/permission systems rather than becoming free-floating lore.

## Urban social-infrastructure research

### Public life exists in ordinary spaces

Latham and Layton’s work on social infrastructure emphasizes that public urban life occurs through a broad range of ordinary places and facilities: sidewalks, plazas, playgrounds, libraries, pools, cafés and similar spaces.

Source:

- https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10076089/1/Latham_et_al-2019-Geography_Compass.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

Not every socially important place needs to be a major landmark. The bench beside a route, station steps, a corner café edge or small playground may accumulate more recurring interactions than a monumental building.

Ouros adaptation:

Chronicle callbacks should be able to anchor to small shared spaces, not only named institutions and quest hubs.

## Synthesis for Ouros

The strongest reusable structure from this pass is a public-space identity that sits between physical architecture and human/Pokémon activity.

A useful causal chain is:

physical layout / access / edges
→ time-programmed use
→ co-presence and activity observations
→ recurring patterns
→ pressures or opportunities
→ institutional/player response
→ revised use
→ public memory.

A second important chain is:

redevelopment / transit / event / ecology change
→ altered public-space use
→ new human/Pokémon intersections
→ observation and competing explanations
→ future quests or design changes.

## Copyright and transformation boundary

No dialogue, distinctive characters, episode plots, fangame quest text or RP prose should enter Ouros.

Pokémon canonical names are retained only in research notes for attribution. Proposed Ouros content must use original locations, institutions, actors and causal combinations.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

No public-space observation in this pass creates a PTU mechanic.

Crowding does not create Accuracy penalties.

A park does not create Grassy Terrain.

A bench does not create Cover.

A pedestrian street does not become Slow Terrain.

A festival crowd does not grant morale.

A visible wild Pokémon does not prove population abundance or ownership.

Any Skill check, capability use, Move interaction, item effect, Trainer Feature, hazard, status, movement rule or battle objective requires authoritative PTU/Caelo validation and implementation evidence.

The complete primary Caelo corpus was not reliably retrievable in this runtime, so this pass makes no new Caelo-specific mechanical claim.

## Engine-facing implication

Public-space content can progress substantially before rich tactical support exists because schedules, crowd cohorts, access, edges, memories and Pokémon-use observations belong to world state.

Battles in public spaces should snapshot a bounded, already-cleared tactical area. Minecraft must not improvise crowd collisions, civilian HP, trampling, moving obstacles, cover, terrain or reaction rules.
