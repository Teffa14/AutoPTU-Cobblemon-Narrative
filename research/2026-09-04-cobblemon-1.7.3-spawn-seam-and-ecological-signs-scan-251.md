### Verified findings

`CobblemonEvents.ENTITY_SPAWN` is cancellable. `POKEMON_ENTITY_SPAWN` is a filtered projection of that event for `PokemonEntity`.

The important correction is event scope. `SpawnEvent` explicitly says it is fired for entities spawned using `BestSpawner`. It is therefore unsafe to describe `POKEMON_ENTITY_SPAWN` as an event that observes every PokemonEntity entering a world.

`SingleEntitySpawnAction.run()` provides stronger ordering evidence. It creates the entity, posts `ENTITY_SPAWN`, and only executes `world.addFreshEntity(e)` inside the success branch. For this verified path, cancellation occurs before world insertion.

`PokemonSpawnAction` uses that single-entity action path for a spawned Pokemon.

Spawn Rules provide earlier `filter` and `location` gates that can reject generic spawn candidates before direct presentation.

Cobblemon separately exposes `POKEMON_SENT_PRE` and `POKEMON_SENT_POST`. This supports treating owned party send-out as a distinct lifecycle surface rather than forcing it into a wild-spawn classifier.

### Design consequence

Pass 250's broad phrase "entity-wide" was too strong. The corrected Ouros model is versioned and path-specific:

BestSpawner natural candidate -> early spawn rule -> cancellable BestSpawner spawn event -> Ouros admission validation.

Owned send-out, entity restore/load, commands and third-party creation remain separate paths until independently verified.

This is safer than guessing an origin from the physical PokemonEntity after it exists.

## Source C — PTU gameplay structure

Source:

- https://pokemontabletop.com/wiki/index.php/Gameplay_Structure

The public tabletop material includes a camp-danger outcome where danger may be averted while players still wake to signs of what could have happened, such as tracks. The reusable structural lesson is that an encounter system can preserve ecological evidence even when no direct actor remains present.

Ouros transformation:

- direct entity presentation and environmental evidence remain independent outputs;
- suppressing a generic Cobblemon spawn does not erase the persistent population;
- a cancelled generic candidate does not itself create a track/sign evidence root;
- Ouros may generate a separate sign from persistent ecology when the evidence policy permits it.

No PTU wording, specific random table or encounter is imported as Ouros canon.

## Source D — public PTU campaign log

Source:

- https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-527075.html

The campaign log repeatedly lets players see unsafe or damaged environmental conditions and decide whether to interact, scavenge or avoid a wild Pokemon. The useful design lesson is consequence visibility: environmental state should create readable choices, and risks should be encoded as actual rules/state when they matter rather than existing only in a GM's intention.

Ouros transformation:

A projection failure or suppressed generic spawn should not become an invisible world reset. The persistent layer can still expose habitat signs, resource depletion, recent disturbance or route changes through the existing observation pipeline. Those signs need explicit provenance and cannot be invented from an adapter cancellation callback.

## New reusable pattern — path-specific authority

A Minecraft entity lifecycle should be decomposed by creation path rather than treated as one universal callback problem.

Candidate paths for future verification:

- BestSpawner natural/world spawning;
- Ouros-requested BestSpawner projection;
- owned party send-out / recall;
- entity save/load or world restore;
- command-created Pokemon;
- fishing, bait or snack-specific creation;
- third-party integration creation.

Each path needs a declared source of identity, cancellation/reconciliation semantics and a regression. Only then can it be marked controlled.
