# Pass 72 Research — Decomposition, Fungi, Deadwood & Nutrient Cycling

Status: RESEARCH ONLY. Not established Ouros canon. External sources are retained for provenance; no protected prose, named plot, distinctive character or source-specific quest is imported.

Date: 2026-08-21

## Why this pass exists

The repository already has dedicated systems for soil condition, vegetation dynamics, wildfire recovery, waste streams, interspecies relations, conservation and scientific observation. None of those layers owns the transition from dead organic material through decay, decomposer activity, habitat formation and nutrient return.

That gap matters because a persistent forest should not treat a fallen tree as either decorative Minecraft blocks or instant disappearance. The same object can remain important for years as substrate, shelter, scientific evidence, path obstruction, cultural landmark and eventually soil organic matter.

This pass therefore studies the ecological and narrative structure of decomposition while explicitly keeping PTU Move/Ability effects separate from ordinary fungal presence.

## Source findings

### Official Pokémon species material

Pokémon's official Pokédex supports several distinct mushroom/fungal relationships rather than one generic "fungus behavior".

Morelull lives in dark forests, produces spores with a specific battle-relevant effect, and its caps can be eaten by other forest Pokémon before regrowing. This supports recurring resource/foraging observations, but the Pokédex Ability text makes clear that Effect Spore is a defined Ability rather than a property that should be granted to every fungal patch.

Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/morelull

Shroomish prefers damp forest shade and scatters toxic powder when threatened. Again, this is species behavior and must not become a generic environmental Poison rule.

Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/shroomish

Toedscool occupies muggy forests, while Toedscruel can form colonies deep within forests and has its own feeding behavior. This gives useful inspiration for persistent fungal-like Pokémon colonies whose presence can be surveyed independently from non-Pokémon fungi.

Sources:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/toedscool
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/toedscruel

Paras and Parasect provide a very different fungal relationship: the mushroom and host relationship affects nutrient acquisition and behavior. Pass 51 already used this family for host-parasite design, so Pass 72 does not duplicate that system; it only preserves the boundary that fungal associations can be parasitic as well as decomposer or mutualistic.

Sources:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/paras

A Pokémon animation episode centered on Morelull shows a forest interaction in which energy taken from people is later shared with an old tree. The reusable lesson is not the episode's plot. The useful structural idea is that an initially alarming biological observation can have a second ecological function and should be investigated before assigning motive or harm.

### Forest Service: fungi, dead wood and nutrient cycling

A USDA Forest Service review describes fungi as decomposers, pathogens and mycorrhizal partners. Fungi associated with down wood can derive nutrients from decaying organic material; pathogenic fungi can also create canopy gaps and cavity habitat, while mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic relationships with roots that exchange resources.

Source: https://apps.fs.usda.gov/r6_decaid/documents/The%20Role%20of%20Fungi%20in%20Forest%20Ecosystems%20and%20Wood%20Decay%20170418b.pdf

A Forest Service wood-decomposition synthesis describes woody debris as a major detrital pool and an important part of food webs and carbon/nutrient cycling. It also stresses that moisture, temperature and decomposer communities affect the rate of decay.

Source: https://www.srs.fs.usda.gov/pubs/gtr/gtr_srs262.pdf

Forest Service guidance on organic debris notes that coarse woody debris can affect soil moisture and become integrated into soil over time, with relevance to mycorrhizal development. Another Forest Service synthesis describes logging residues as a long-duration nutrient pathway rather than material that disappears immediately after harvest.

Sources:
https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_other/rmrs_2009_schnepf_c001.pdf
https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/257505

### Scientific caution: fungal networks are not automatic sentient communication

For Ouros, mycorrhizal association should be represented as ecological state/evidence unless a Pokémon-specific or authored supernatural rule says otherwise. The project must not turn real fungal-root nutrient exchange into a universal conscious "forest internet" that lets trees or Pokémon exchange thoughts.

This is important because the system already contains psychic, Aura and communications layers. Fungal ecology must not bypass those authority boundaries through metaphor.

## Reusable design structures

1. A fallen tree can persist through multiple decay revisions instead of disappearing after one season.
2. Deadwood can simultaneously affect habitat, route geometry, moisture, scientific value and later nutrient state.
3. Fungal fruiting bodies are observations, not the full organism and not automatic proof of ecological function.
4. Decomposer activity can be seasonal and moisture-dependent without requiring per-block simulation.
5. Removing deadwood can solve one access/safety problem while reducing habitat or research value elsewhere.
6. A "diseased-looking" tree can require diagnosis before the system labels a fungus pathogenic.
7. Mycorrhizal association can be a scientific hypothesis or verified ecological relationship without becoming psychic communication.
8. A decomposition study can run for years and generate callbacks as the same log changes state.
9. Wild Pokémon may repeatedly use a decomposing structure as shelter, food source, lookout or route landmark without the system inferring ownership or emotion.
10. Forest restoration can include retaining selected deadwood rather than resetting a site to visually clean conditions.

## Boundaries with existing Ouros layers

Soil owns surface/soil-condition state. Pass 72 may emit organic-matter or biological-activity observations into that layer; it does not replace soil assessments.

Flora owns live vegetation communities and recruitment. Pass 72 can report dead plant material and nutrient-return links; it does not decide vegetation succession by itself.

Interspecies Ecological Relations owns persistent Pokémon-species/population relationships. Pass 72 can produce observations such as repeated feeding or shelter use around deadwood; the relation edge remains owned there.

Wildfire owns burn patches and post-fire event history. Pass 72 can track the decay of killed standing/down wood after a fire without rewriting fire severity.

Waste owns human waste and treatment flows. Naturally fallen organic material is not automatically municipal waste.

Material Culture owns harvested/processed materials. Removing a log for timber or craft use requires an explicit transfer from ecological deadwood state into material provenance.

Science owns hypotheses, datasets and publication. A mushroom identification, decay-rate claim or mycorrhizal interpretation must remain evidence-backed rather than becoming world truth on first observation.

## PTU / Caelo mechanical boundary

Available project-file evidence identifies concrete mechanics named `Spore` and `Effect Spore`. `Spore` puts a target to sleep; `Effect Spore` has a contact-triggered chance to inflict a defined status. These examples are narrow mechanics, not permission to give generic mushrooms environmental status effects.

The complete primary Caelo corpus was not reliably retrieved for this pass. No new Caelo-specific fungal, spore, foraging, poison, sleep, Naturewalk or terrain rule is asserted.

The following remain prohibited unless exact governing rules and runtime support are verified:
- ambient mushrooms automatically causing Sleep, Poisoned, Paralysis or Confusion;
- fungal spores creating tactical zones;
- deadwood automatically becoming Rough or Slow Terrain;
- rotten logs collapsing as forced movement or damage;
- mushrooms acting as Food Items or healing items because they look edible;
- mycorrhizae granting communication, buffs or shared senses;
- decomposition changing Pokémon stats;
- fungus exposure creating disease or outbreak state without evidence.

## AutoPTU evidence relevant to this pass

Python AutoPTU remains the rules oracle while Java is incomplete. The available Python project file exposes specific forest tile mappings for some Moves/effects and concrete status/Ability behavior, but that does not establish a generic fungal-environment subsystem.

AutoPTU-Java was inspected separately in the Pass 72 engine snapshot. Its live work continues to strengthen authoritative initiative/runtime projection. That does not verify spore clouds, dynamic deadwood, environmental status application, objective-aware wildlife withdrawal or Minecraft ecological projection.
