# Ouros Research Scan — Tourism, Visitors & Destination Pressure — Pass 47

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon. External works are used only for high-level structures and design lessons.

Date: 2026-08-20

## Why this pass

The repository already has systems for travel, transport, hospitality, food, public events, conservation, protected areas, museums, sacred sites, media, seasonal cycles, mapping, housing, workplaces and interregional mobility. What it did not yet model directly is the visitor layer between those systems: why people temporarily arrive, what they expect, where they stay, how their presence changes a place, how residents react, and how a successful destination can create pressure on infrastructure or Pokémon habitat.

This pass therefore studies tourism, resorts, sightseeing, guided travel, event visitors, visitor carrying capacity and destination reputation without assuming modern real-world tourism law or economics for Ouros.

## Source scan

### 1. Pokémon Legends: Z-A — Lumiose sightseeing and Hotel Z

Source: The Pokémon Company, official Legends: Z-A site.

URL: https://legends.pokemon.com/en-us/story-world/lumiose-city

High-level structure:
- the protagonist begins as a visitor on a sightseeing trip;
- a hotel becomes a persistent base of operations rather than permanent residence;
- cafés, restaurants, shopping arcades, parks and waterfronts coexist in one destination;
- redevelopment changes the experience of both residents and visitors.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A visitor can have a temporary home base and meaningful local relationships without becoming a resident. A hotel or lodge can be a narrative hub connected to transport, events, guides, local businesses and changing city state.

Do not copy Hotel Z, Lumiose characters or plot.

### 2. Official Alola visitor guides

Sources: The Pokémon Company.

URLs:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/strategy/the-first-timers-guide-to-the-alola-region
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/remember-the-region-alola-spotlight
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/a-visitors-guide-to-johto

High-level structure:
- regions are experienced through optional attractions and activities, not only the main quest;
- repeat visitors can have different opportunities from first-time visitors;
- sightseeing, competitions, beaches, cultural places, shopping and Pokémon activities coexist;
- a visitor guide is itself an information product that selects what outsiders are likely to see.

Reusable lesson:
Destination knowledge should be versioned and perspective-dependent. A public visitor guide can be useful and still be incomplete, promotional, outdated or designed for a specific audience.

### 3. Pokémon Concierge — resort as a social institution

Sources: The Pokémon Company official articles.

URLs:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/watch-the-new-trailer-for-pokemon-concierge-coming-to-netflix-on-december-28-2023

High-level structure:
- the Pokémon Resort exists primarily for rest, leisure and temporary stays;
- staff members have ongoing work lives while guests rotate through;
- people and Pokémon can participate in low-stakes social stories with no battle requirement;
- the same facility can create recurring relationships with visitors without ownership or permanent residence.

Reusable lesson:
Hospitality can produce character arcs, slice-of-life events, Pokémon behavior observations and staff progression without needing a crisis or competition.

Do not copy Haru, Psyduck, resort scenes or dialogue.

### 4. Resort and event lodging around competitive circuits

Source: Lakeside Resort / Hotel Grand Lake documentation and Pokémon animation references.

URL: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lakeside_Resort

High-level structure:
- a major competition can temporarily transform lodging demand;
- competitors, media and supporting characters share the same hospitality district;
- pre-event social interaction can occur outside the formal contest venue.

Reusable lesson:
Large events should affect occupancy, transport, staffing, restaurants, media and public spaces. The event layer should create temporary destination state instead of spawning only the tournament bracket.

Sources:
- PokéHeroes public Pokéresort RP: https://pokeheroes.com/forum_thread?id=37056
- Serebii public Pokémon Mystery Island RP: https://forums.serebii.net/threads/pokemon-mystery-island.667298/

High-level patterns:
- visitor and employee perspectives can coexist in the same destination;
- resort staff create recurring local continuity while guests create rotating stories;
- weather or transport disruptions can trap or redirect visitors and expose service dependencies;
- a major attraction can create housing pressure, travel coordination problems and venue congestion;
- tourist centres and information desks provide non-combat onboarding into a region.

Reuse only the structural patterns. Do not copy characters, mysteries, distinctive locations or plots.

### 6. Fangame structure — tourism growth as regional pressure

Source: Pokémon Parallax public project page.

URL: https://eeveeexpo.com/parallax/

High-level pattern:
The setting presents a remote island region experiencing expanding tourism alongside institutional and social change.

Reusable lesson:
Tourism growth can be treated as a world-state driver that intersects with League development, transport, housing, jobs, local identity and security without making tourism inherently good or bad.

Do not copy the Kimlik Islands, characters or storyline.

### 7. Visitor carrying capacity and conservation pressure

Sources:
- Rahman et al., 2026, visitor carrying capacity in an island marine protected area: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213078026000459
- Erdoğan, 2025, carrying-capacity assessment at a heritage site: https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/ahtr/article/1483060
- Ecological Informatics, 2025, tourism pressure and wildlife overlap: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574954125004509

High-level lessons:
- visitor pressure is not only a raw headcount;
- ecological sensitivity, safety, management capacity and visitor experience all matter;
- hotels, visitor centres and trailheads can concentrate pressure spatially;
- seasonal peaks matter;
- visitor-management zones can be more useful than treating a whole destination as equally sensitive.

Ouros adaptation:
Use qualitative pressure bands and causal indicators rather than pretending to simulate exact tourism economics or ecology. Visitor pressure should be connected to actual transport capacity, accommodation, events, trail access, habitat sensitivity and staffing.

### 8. Games can alter destination interest

Source: Tourism Management, 2026, study of game-linked destination spillover.

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517725002407

Reusable lesson:
Stories and media can change where people want to travel. In Ouros, public battle footage, discoveries, festivals, research publications, myths or famous Trainers can make a place newly desirable. That change should propagate through the existing media and communication layers before visitor numbers change.

## Derived Ouros design lessons

### A. Visitor status is temporary world state

A character may be:
- local resident;
- returning visitor;
- first-time visitor;
- event attendee;
- competitor;
- researcher;
- pilgrim or heritage visitor if canon supports the tradition;
- worker on temporary assignment;
- stranded traveller;
- guest of an institution.

These labels describe the current relationship to a place. They do not imply citizenship, wealth, ignorance, social status or legal rights.

### B. Destination reputation should be causal

A location becomes popular because information traveled.

Possible causes:
- public event;
- famous battle;
- media feature;
- scientific discovery;
- seasonal phenomenon;
- restoration success;
- new transport link;
- famous resident;
- newly accessible ruin;
- photography or social publication;
- rumor.

Popularity should not appear because a generator decides a place needs activity.

### C. Visitor pressure must remain spatial

A region can have high visitation while one sensitive valley remains quiet. A single trailhead can be overloaded while the rest of a park is stable.

Model pressure by destination zone, route, venue or attraction.

### D. Success can create new problems without becoming punishment

A festival, restoration project or research discovery can legitimately increase visitors. That can create jobs, revenue, cultural exchange and services while also creating crowding, waste, route stress, wildlife disturbance or resident frustration.

The game should present tradeoffs rather than automatically turning growth into a villain plot.

### E. Routine hospitality should compress

Checking into an ordinary functioning hotel, eating breakfast and receiving basic directions should not become mandatory quests.

Expand the experience when something meaningful changes:
- overbooking during an event;
- infrastructure failure;
- an accessibility problem;
- visitor pressure on habitat;
- a missing reservation or delivery with real consequences;
- a recurring guest relationship;
- staff training or capacity decision;
- conflicting interpretations of a sacred or heritage site;
- a route closure that strands travellers;
- a player-authored leisure goal.

### F. Visitor knowledge is not local knowledge

Public guidebooks, attraction maps, media posts and guide services should produce actor knowledge records. They may be accurate, incomplete or stale.

A tourist seeing a site does not automatically learn its hidden ecology, history or institutional context.

## PTU/Caelo boundary

This pass adds no new PTU mechanics.

The existing repository already treats travel capabilities, Skills, Trainer Features, food effects, social checks, healing, hazards and combat as external authoritative mechanics. Tourism state must not invent:
- lodging bonuses;
- rest/healing benefits;
- travel-speed bonuses;
- guide Skill bonuses;
- crowd penalties;
- attraction buffs;
- pilgrimage effects;
- encounter-rate modifiers;
- Pokémon happiness/friendship gains;
- service prices or hotel income formulas.

When visitor incidents create a battle, use the permanent engine capability map and an explicit encounter implementation contract.
