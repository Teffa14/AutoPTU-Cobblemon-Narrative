# Ouros Narrative Research — Wild Collectives, Territory & Group Ecology — Pass 11

Status: Research only. Provenance and design evidence; not Ouros canon.

This pass follows the existing work on observation, ecological causality, individual persistent Pokémon, travel ecology, settlements and world agency. The repository already knows that wild Pokémon can migrate, nest, compete, form hierarchies and react to disturbance. The missing layer examined here is the group itself: when a pack, herd, flock, school, colony, roost or temporary aggregation should persist as a world actor rather than being regenerated as unrelated encounter rolls.

The goal is richer ecology without anthropomorphizing every wild population or inventing tactical bonuses outside PTU/Caelo.

## 1. Official Pokémon species material — group organization is not one universal pattern

Primary sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/mightyena
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/passimian
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/wishiwashi
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/falinks
- https://www.pokemon.com/es/pokedex/combee

The official Pokédex descriptions show several structurally different kinds of collective behavior.

Mightyena can coordinate as a pack around a leader and pursue prey together. Passimian groups can organize hunting parties and share what they gather with the wider group. Wishiwashi demonstrates a very different model: individuals signal one another under threat, aggregate into a school, and may disperse again when wounded. Falinks is more extreme because multiple component creatures operate as one Pokémon entity under a brass leader. Combee shows yet another pattern, with large nighttime aggregations around colony life.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
`wild group` must not mean `pack with alpha`.

A collective can differ in:
- persistence;
- membership stability;
- leadership;
- reason for grouping;
- communication;
- territoriality;
- cooperation;
- seasonal behavior;
- response to danger;
- whether it is mechanically one Pokémon or many separate Pokémon.

Ouros translation:
The narrative layer should record the observed collective pattern first. It should not generate a leadership hierarchy, family relation, social rank or permanent territory merely because multiple Pokémon of one species appear together.

## 2. Official Pokémon species material — group behavior can change with pressure

Primary sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/poochyena
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/wishiwashi

Poochyena may pursue prey as a pack but flee when the target retaliates. Wishiwashi can form a school in response to danger and scatter again after sufficient injury.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A collective needs a behavioral state, not only a member count.

Useful state changes can include:
- gathering;
- dispersal;
- coordinated pursuit;
- retreat;
- defensive clustering;
- migration;
- relocation;
- temporary fragmentation;
- reforming after pressure passes.

This lets encounters resolve through retreat, dispersal, protection or environmental change instead of requiring every member to be defeated.

## 3. Official Pokémon species material — territorial conflict can alter group composition

Primary source:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/bisharp

The official Bisharp entry describes groups of Pawniard commanded by Bisharp and defeated groups being absorbed after territorial disputes.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Some species may support collective merge, split or transfer of members after conflict. That outcome should be species-grounded rather than a universal ecological simulation rule.

Ouros translation:
Where authored biology supports it, a territorial event can update:
- group identity;
- member estimates;
- leadership state;
- home range;
- rival-group relationship;
- later encounter composition.

The exact Bisharp/Pawniard structure should not be generalized to unrelated species.

## 4. Official Pokémon species material — sleeping, foraging and hunting groups need not equal battle squads

Primary sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/wynaut
- https://www.pokemon.com/es/pokedex/combee
- https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/passimian

Wynaut can cluster together to sleep. Combee can gather in very large numbers at night. Passimian can send only a subset of a larger group out to forage or hunt.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
The group visible in one encounter may be only a temporary operational subset of a larger local collective.

Ouros translation:
Separate:
- persistent collective;
- currently visible subgroup;
- tactical encounter participants.

This prevents a six-Pokémon battle from implying that the player has encountered or removed an entire local population.

## 5. PTU Core Rulebook — Pack Mon is a real mechanical concept and must not be improvised

Internal source:
- supplied `CoreRulebook.pdf`, Special Capabilities / Pack Mon.

The supplied PTU Core Rulebook includes a `Pack Mon` capability with explicit mechanical consequences around wild Pokémon of related forms/levels, fear and dominance interaction between qualifying Pack Mon users.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Narrative group state must not replace or silently expand the PTU capability.

Ouros translation:
- when an encounter or social interaction depends mechanically on Pack Mon, query the actual Pokémon capability data;
- do not give a species Pack Mon because its Pokédex description sounds social;
- do not give a group extra obedience, fear, initiative, damage or coordination bonuses from narrative collective state alone;
- narrative leadership and PTU Pack Mon are separate concepts unless the governing rules explicitly connect them.

## 6. PTU GM guidance — territorial groups are valid encounter premises

Source:
- https://pokemontabletop.com/gm-advice-your-first-ptu-session/

Public PTU GM advice uses situations such as territorial groups blocking a route, wild Pokémon harassing another injured Pokémon, or creatures taking items as early encounter premises. It also encourages environmental interaction and alternatives to fighting until knockout.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
A collective can create an encounter because of behavior and current need, not because the random table selected `battle`.

Possible triggers:
- territorial boundary crossed;
- nest or shelter approached;
- food source contested;
- injured member protected;
- player blocks migration path;
- group attempts to drive another creature away;
- player-created disturbance changes normal behavior.

The resulting situation may become BATTLE, OBSERVE, RETREAT, PROTECT, RESCUE, NEGOTIATE where communication is plausible, or simple route-choice content.

## 7. Eevee Expo — overworld behavior can communicate state before combat

Sources:
- https://eeveeexpo.com/flux/
- https://eeveeexpo.com/shifting-skies/

Pokémon Flux publicly describes overworld encounters with behavior patterns and emotion displays. Pokémon Shifting Skies emphasizes seasonal exploration and scouting wild Pokémon before committing to capture.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Group state should be legible in Minecraft before an AutoPTU transition where possible.

Ouros translation:
Potential player-facing signals include:
- clustering or spacing;
- repeated movement route;
- sentry-like position only when behavior supports it;
- nest/roost use;
- feeding behavior;
- coordinated retreat;
- warning display;
- seasonal presence;
- absence from a normally occupied site.

The player should be able to infer `this group is protecting something` or `this aggregation is temporary` from evidence, not from an omniscient UI label.

No fangame behavior AI, custom species, loot or combat mechanics are imported.

## 8. Mass outbreaks and horde encounters — abundance can be an event state

Reference sources:
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mass_outbreak
- https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Horde_Encounter

Main-series Pokémon has repeatedly represented short-lived abundance as outbreaks/swarms and represented battles against several wild Pokémon at once through horde-style encounters.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
High local abundance does not automatically mean a permanent colony, territorial invasion or ecological crisis.

Ouros translation:
A `wild_aggregation_event` should be able to exist separately from a persistent `wild_collective`.

Potential causes:
- migration stop;
- food bloom;
- seasonal breeding/nesting period;
- weather refuge;
- disturbance elsewhere;
- mass movement along a corridor;
- authored outbreak phenomenon.

Only validated evidence should promote a temporary aggregation into a persistent group identity.

## 9. Public ecosystem experimentation in Pokémon fangame development

Source:
- https://eeveeexpo.com/threads/8194/

A public Pokémon Essentials development discussion explores simulating seasonal differences in wild behavior and longer-term changes to Pokémon populations.

Reusable lesson for Ouros:
Ecological simulation becomes expensive very quickly. The useful part is stateful change with visible consequences, not simulating every creature continuously.

Ouros translation:
Track a small number of narratively relevant collectives and population states. Avoid persistent identity for every spawned wild Pokémon.

A group receives persistent identity when one or more of these are true:
- players have interacted with it repeatedly;
- it controls or depends on a meaningful location;
- its movement changes routes or settlement life;
- it is central to an observation/research arc;
- a named persistent Pokémon anchors it;
- a faction or institution actively monitors it;
- its state can create future consequences.

## 11. Core design conclusions

1. Wild collectives need several archetypes; `pack` cannot be the universal template.
2. Persistent group identity should be selective rather than attached to every spawn.
3. Population abundance, persistent collective and tactical encounter are separate objects.
4. Leadership defaults to unknown unless species data or observed/canonical facts establish it.
5. Home range and territory should allow overlap; they are not faction-style political ownership.
6. Group states can include retreat, dispersal, migration and fragmentation, so conflict does not always resolve through knockout.
7. Temporary aggregations such as outbreaks should not automatically become permanent communities.
8. Mixed-species associations need explicit observed relationships; co-location does not imply friendship or symbiosis.
9. Group-scale memory should store observed behavioral adaptation, not human-like motives such as vengeance unless authored.
10. Removing one member does not automatically remove the collective. The impact depends on the member's actual role and group structure.
11. Encounter generation should derive a visible subgroup from persistent ecology rather than instantiate unrelated opponents.
12. World Pulse can evolve a small number of relevant collectives between player interactions, but changes need causes and visible evidence.
