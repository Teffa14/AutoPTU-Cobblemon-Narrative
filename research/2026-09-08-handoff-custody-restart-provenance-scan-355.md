## New public source: U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service evidence handling policy

Source: https://www.fws.gov/policy-library/e1445fw3

The public policy requires an accurate record of every person who possesses or controls an evidence item. For transfers, it requires the chain-of-custody record to document the transfer, the people involved and the reason for it.

Reusable structure for Ouros:

A physical object can have a current holder and a historical custody sequence at the same time. Recovering only the current holder after restart loses causal evidence. Recovering only the historical transfer without the authorization that permitted it loses the reason the transfer was legitimate.

Transformation boundary:

Ouros does not import law-enforcement procedures, evidentiary standards, forms, signatures or legal consequences. The reusable design lesson is limited to identity-stable chronological custody and explicit transfer provenance.

## High-level Ouros design extraction

A durable handoff record should preserve at least:

- stable authorization identity;
- the accepted request that justified the handoff;
- exact resource identity;
- provider, receiver and accountable actor;
- authorized place and time window;
- the actual transfer event, if one occurred;
- actual from/to actors, place, time and condition provenance;
- whether the authorization has already been consumed by a transfer.

After restart, none of those facts should be reconstructed from the current inventory view. Current possession can corroborate a record but cannot manufacture the missing historical event.

Future-dated authorization is legitimate because an appointment can be scheduled ahead of world time. A completed transfer dated after the checkpoint time is causally impossible and should fail closed.
