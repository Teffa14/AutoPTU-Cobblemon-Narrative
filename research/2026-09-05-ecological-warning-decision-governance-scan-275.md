# Ecological warning decision-governance scan — Pass 275

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE. No canon effect.

## Question

How should Ouros turn a probabilistic ecological warning into monitoring or management choices without treating uncertain knowledge as an automatic order, and without confusing ecological thresholds with policy thresholds?

## New public sources

1. Nichols, Eaton and Martin, “Thresholds for conservation and management: structured decision making as a conceptual framework,” USGS (2014), https://www.usgs.gov/publications/thresholds-conservation-and-management-structured-decision-making-a-conceptual. Reusable structure: ecological thresholds, utility thresholds and decision thresholds are different objects. A measured ecological state does not itself specify the management action; objectives, alternatives, models and monitoring participate in the decision.
2. Lyons et al., “Monitoring in the context of structured decision-making and adaptive management,” Journal of Wildlife Management / USGS (2008), https://www.usgs.gov/publications/monitoring-context-structured-decision-making-and-adaptive-management. Reusable structure: monitoring can establish current state, evaluate action effectiveness and reduce decision-relevant uncertainty. Monitoring design should be driven by the actual decision rather than accumulated without purpose.
3. Williams, “Reducing uncertainty about objective functions in adaptive management,” USGS / Ecological Modelling (2012), https://www.usgs.gov/publications/reducing-uncertainty-about-objective-functions-adaptive-management. Reusable caution: disagreement may concern objectives as well as ecological models. Two institutions can share the same evidence and reasonably prefer different actions because their objectives or tolerances differ.
4. Stantial et al., “Qualitative value of information provides a transparent and repeatable method for identifying critical uncertainty,” Ecological Applications / USGS (2023), https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70240937. Reusable structure: limited monitoring effort should target uncertainties whose resolution can materially improve decisions, rather than attempting to eliminate every unknown.
5. Addison et al., management-threshold framework in Conservation Biology (2015), https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cobi.12544. Reusable structure: implementation ranges and monitoring thresholds can depend on decision-maker risk tolerance. Risk tolerance belongs to governance, not to ecological truth.
6. Allen and Gunderson, “Pathology and failure in the design and implementation of adaptive management,” Journal of Environmental Management / USGS (2011), https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70003322. Reusable caution: uncertainty should neither be ignored nor used as a blanket reason to avoid decisions. Safe-to-fail and revisable actions are useful when systems are uncertain.
7. Community PTU campaign example, “Sharing My Orre Region Campaign,” r/PokemonTabletop (2022), https://www.reddit.com/r/PokemonTabletop/comments/xcgcx0. Reusable high-level pattern only: habitat investigations can be episodic missions that expose environmental causes and allow occasional participants to contribute evidence. Ouros does not import the campaign’s characters, locations, encounter content or plot.
8. Community Pokémon roleplay example, Ranger outreach AMA, r/PokeMedia (2025), https://www.reddit.com/r/PokeMedia/comments/1lkhz6n. Reusable high-level pattern only: a human-built site can become an ecological-management dispute where stakeholders disagree over whether continued Pokémon use represents habitat value or environmental harm. The specific hotel, characters and setting are not imported.

## Transformed Ouros lesson

Ouros should keep at least four records separate:

- ecological evidence: what the world-state and observations support;
- decision objective: what a responsible actor is trying to protect or optimize;
- risk posture: how much uncertainty or downside that actor accepts before acting;
- chosen action: monitor, restrict, test, protect, intervene, defer or reverse.

A Pass 274 warning can inform a decision without commanding one. Likewise, absence of a supported warning does not prohibit precaution if other authorized evidence or objectives justify it.

Monitoring has an opportunity cost. A useful research action should identify which uncertainty could change the next decision and whether the information can arrive before the decision horizon closes.

## Proposed use in Marea/Sendero

Use the same fixture-only resilience warning from Pass 274. Two provisional management actors see the same evidence. One prioritizes avoiding irreversible habitat loss and accepts temporary access restrictions. Another prioritizes maintaining access and prefers a short matched-monitoring cycle before restriction. Their disagreement is stored as different objective/risk records rather than contradictory ecological truth.

The player can collect one high-value comparison that resolves a decision-critical uncertainty. Low-value observations may still enrich the archive but do not automatically advance the management state.

A temporary action must have a semantic review horizon and explicit rollback/reassessment conditions. Minecraft barriers, signs or NPC schedules are presentation of an authorized decision and never create PTU terrain or movement rules by themselves.
