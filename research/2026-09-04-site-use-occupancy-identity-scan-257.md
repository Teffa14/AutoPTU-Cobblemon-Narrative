## Public-source findings

USGS occupancy work separates ecological presence from detection. MacKenzie et al. (2002) states that nondetection at a site does not imply absence unless detection probability is 1. Riddle et al. (2010) further shows that prior detections can alter subsequent detection probability and bias abundance/occupancy inference if ignored. Reusable lesson for Ouros: an empty observation window is evidence about presentation/detection, not automatically an ecological departure or demographic event.

Sources:
- USGS, MacKenzie et al., 2002, “Estimating site occupancy rates when detection probabilities are less than one”: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/estimating-site-occupancy-rates-when-detection-probabilities-are-less-one
- USGS, Riddle et al., 2010, “Effects of prior detections on estimates of detection probability, abundance, and occupancy”: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/effects-prior-detections-estimates-detection-probability-abundance-and-occupancy

Site fidelity is real but variable rather than a universal identity key. Morrison et al. (2021) found large interspecific and contextual differences in site fidelity, with environmental predictability influencing return tendency. A recent shorebird study likewise found generally high fidelity while still observing meaningful variation between species and individuals. Reusable lesson: repeated use of a place can strengthen a location-use hypothesis, but the location cannot itself identify the user.

Sources:
- USGS, Morrison et al., 2021, “Drivers of site fidelity in ungulates”: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/drivers-site-fidelity-ungulates
- Schwemmer et al./Movement Ecology, 2023, “Site fidelity of migratory shorebirds facing habitat deterioration”: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10740345/

A 10-year golden-crowned sparrow study explicitly analyzes spatial fidelity together with demographic turnover and social change. It shows that animals may repeatedly use small home ranges while turnover in associates changes those patterns. Reusable lesson: place history and individual history need separate ledgers.

Source:
- “Loss of flockmates weakens winter site fidelity in golden-crowned sparrows”: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10410770/

## Transformed Ouros lessons

CANON-ALIGNED: projection/detection is not demography; internal individual identity stays separate from player-facing evidence; all projected Pokémon must come from already-counted sources.

PROPOSED: add a site-use ledger whose records say that a counted source used an observation micro-site during a bounded window. The ledger is ecological history, not territory ownership and not an identity registry.

PROPOSED: same-site recurrence can contribute only weak contextual evidence to Pass 253 recognition. It cannot by itself produce `CONFIRMED_BY_DIEGETIC_MARKER` or silently alias two sources.

UNCERTAIN: Fletchling-specific site fidelity, perch reuse, territorial ownership, nest defense, turnover rate, and seasonal persistence require species/canon evidence before becoming rules.
