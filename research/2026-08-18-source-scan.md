# Ouros Narrative Research — 2026-08-18

Status: Research only. This file is provenance and design evidence, not Ouros canon.

## Internal PTU / Caelo findings

The PTU Core Rulebook explicitly supports three broad campaign modes: central plot, character-centric arcs, and sandbox activity. It also recommends alternating periods of relative calm with periods where a larger plot demands action. Standard Pokémon activities such as catching Pokémon and challenging Gyms should be woven into larger plot developments instead of treated as disconnected loops.

The same GM section recommends sessions that give players meaningful choices, connect different player interests and Pokémon capabilities, and provide self-contained satisfaction while also contributing to a larger whole. It also recommends maintaining short dossiers on PCs so character backgrounds, interests, goals, and capabilities can feed future sessions.

Caelo already models a persistent living-world campaign through distinct activity containers: Social, Wild Encounter, PvP, Job, Raid, Contest, Gym, and Dojo. Social play is intentionally unrestricted to encourage roleplay and character development. Jobs, battles, raids, exploration and milestone activities therefore provide natural delivery channels for Ouros content without requiring every narrative beat to become a combat encounter.

Caelo location design also demonstrates that locations can encode mechanical state. Toxic Ravine, for example, is not only descriptive scenery; its poison-gas condition changes combat state for Pokémon present there. This supports an Ouros principle that important locations should have a gameplay identity in addition to visual lore.

## Public source scan

### Pokémon Tabletop community side-quest design

Source: https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/pokemon_tabletop/50-side-quests-t1729.html
Source: https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/pokemon_tabletop/random-side-quests-t1633.html

Reusable lesson: side content works best as modular situation seeds rather than fixed scripts. Public Pokémon Tabletop discussions explicitly frame side quests as a way to break railroading, refresh pacing, and let players enjoy the world between major plot beats.

Ouros use: maintain a large library of situation templates whose actors, locations, factions, Pokémon and consequences are resolved against current world state at runtime.

### Pokémon Tabletop campaign structures

Source: https://pokemontabletop.fandom.com/wiki/Campaign_Structure
Source: https://pokemontabletop.fandom.com/wiki/Getting_Started

Reusable lesson: PTU campaigns can support conventional League play, mission-driven organizations, academies, exploration teams, unusual settings and dungeon-oriented campaigns. The important structural property is that Pokémon training/capture and character goals remain connected to the campaign premise.

Ouros use: avoid making the League the only progression spine. Support occupations and organizations as parallel adventure generators.

### Eevee Expo fangame systems

Source: https://www.eeveeexpo.com/released-games/
Source: https://www.eeveeexpo.com/expo-news/
Source: https://www.eeveeexpo.com/resources/709/

Reusable lessons found across public project descriptions:
- faction and NPC reputation can change dialogue and gameplay;
- side quests can reveal secrets and alter how a location is understood;
- NPC populations can change as the story advances, making settlements feel alive;
- quest journals and explicit objective state help players manage optional content;
- interconnected compact locations can support meaningful backtracking when previously inaccessible passages or states open later.

Ouros use: represent reputation, location state, unlocked routes and NPC availability as explicit world-state variables rather than prose-only lore.

### Pokémon roleplay and campaign anecdotes

Source: https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-333811.html
Source: https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-482353.html

Reusable lesson: emergent player jokes, unexpected captures, rival behavior and memorable encounters can become long-term material when the GM reincorporates them later. A campaign world becomes personal when events created at the table return with changed context.

Ouros use: preserve notable player-created events in a Chronicle system and allow them to seed future NPCs, rumors, encounters, monuments, grudges and callbacks.

### Episodic fanfiction structure

Source: https://forums.serebii.net/threads/episodic-fanfiction.481628/

Reusable lesson: relatively independent episodes can each provide a complete local story while progressively building characters and a larger climax.

Ouros use: quests should usually have a satisfying local resolution plus optional outbound edges to other arcs. Avoid chains where every quest exists only to unlock the next quest.

### Journey-fic community lessons

Source: https://www.bulbagarden.net/threads/writers-workshop-frequently-asked-questions.307706/
Source: https://bulbagarden.net/threads/journey-fic-discussion.215295/

Reusable lesson: the classic badge/villain/League journey is familiar but prone to repetition, extreme scope and unfinished long-form structure.

Ouros use: treat badges as one possible character goal. Build shorter arcs with explicit closure, then let larger stories emerge from connected completed arcs.

### Pokémon Adventures structural lesson

Source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Adventures
Source: https://community.pokemon.com/en-us/discussion/6415/which-pokemon-adventures-arc-do-you-recommend

Reusable lesson: a shared world can advance through multiple protagonist groups and regional arcs while previous characters and consequences reappear later. Time and continuity give old events renewed meaning.

Ouros use: create a Chronicle layer that allows later seasons, regions and player groups to inherit world state without requiring every player to follow one continuous main quest.

### Narrative graphs and level design

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09294

Reusable lesson: narrative structures can be represented as graphs and constrained by level design. This is directly useful for procedural or semi-procedural quest construction.

Ouros use: quests and arcs should be data structures with nodes, preconditions, consequences and cross-links to locations rather than only prose documents.

## Research exclusions

This pass does not copy prose, dialogue, original characters or complete plots from manga, fanfiction, fangames, roleplay posts or campaign logs. External works are being used only to identify high-level structures, design failures, recurring patterns and system ideas.

Private Discord content, paywalled text and access-controlled material are out of scope unless supplied directly by its owner with appropriate permission.
