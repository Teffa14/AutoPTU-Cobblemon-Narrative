# Ouros Astronomical Observation, Observatories & Celestial Events — Research Scan 170

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE. Not canon. No mechanic in this file overrides PTU/Caelo or engine evidence.
Date: 2026-08-25

## Why this pass exists

The repository already has authority for Timekeeping, Lightscapes, Metrology, Meteorology, Remote Sensing, Science, Sacred Sites and public/community monitoring. A repository-wide audit did not find a dedicated authority for observing the sky itself: observatories, repeated observing programs, celestial predictions, observation sessions, candidate transients, follow-up, ephemeris revisions, public observing events and the distinction between a meteor in the atmosphere and recovered physical material.

This pass therefore treats astronomy as an evidence-generating world system. It does not make celestial folklore true, grant orbital technology to Ouros, or turn PTU Moves and Abilities with cosmic names into environmental rules.

## New public-source lessons

### 1. A prediction is a versioned scientific product, not future truth

NASA/JPL CNEOS documents that near-Earth-object orbit solutions are constrained by observations and can change materially as new observations extend the data arc. Early solutions can have wide uncertainty regions; later follow-up narrows them. Objects can appear concerning under limited data and later leave a risk list because the orbit is better constrained, not because the physical past changed.

Reusable Ouros structure:

`candidate observation -> provisional object link -> solution revision -> predicted observing window -> follow-up observations -> revised solution -> institutional/public interpretation`

This supports stories where an observatory was correct to issue a cautious notice under the evidence available at the time and later revises it without corruption, fraud or retcon.

Sources:
- NASA/JPL CNEOS, Sentry: Earth Impact Monitoring: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/sentry/intro.html
- NASA/JPL CNEOS, NEO Close Approaches / uncertainties: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/ca/neo_ca_info.html
- NASA/JPL CNEOS, NHATS observing opportunities: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats/intro.html
- NASA JPL, How a Speck of Light Becomes an Asteroid: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/how-a-speck-of-light-becomes-an-asteroid/

### 2. Distributed observers can create century-scale scientific memory

The American Association of Variable Star Observers maintains a database containing tens of millions of observations accumulated over more than a century, including visual, photographic and digital measurements submitted by many observers. Quality control, comparison sequences, method metadata and long time series matter as much as a single striking observation.

Reusable Ouros structure:

- professional and community observatories can share a program without becoming one institution;
- an old observation can remain scientifically useful even when later instruments are more precise;
- gaps, method changes and comparison-reference revisions belong in Chronicle;
- a quiet observation can become important decades later as a baseline.

Sources:
- AAVSO International Database: https://www.aavso.org/index.php/aavso-international-database/
- AAVSO Data Access: https://archive.aavso.org/index.php/data-access
- AAVSO Research Portal: https://www.aavso.org/index.php/research-portal

### 3. Follow-up changes confidence without changing the observed sky

NASA/JPL describes discovery confirmation as a distributed process. Follow-up observers attempt to recover the same candidate, submit additional positional observations and extend the observation arc. Some candidates remain uncertain; some are confirmed; some disappear from concern as the solution improves.

Ouros should therefore preserve:

- raw observation identity;
- candidate-object linkage separately from accepted object identity;
- observation arc and coverage;
- follow-up request and response;
- accepted/rejected/unresolved candidate state;
- solution revision and supersession;
- public notice versions.

No single telescope should be omniscient.

### 4. Pokémon canon supports celestial ecology and institutions, but only narrowly

The official Pokémon Pokédex states that Minior forms in the ozone layer and falls when its outer shell becomes heavy. This supports authored stories about unusual high-altitude or falling-Minior observations, recovery sites and ecological/scientific interest. It does not prove that every meteor is Minior, that Minior is an astronomical sensor, or that falling Minior should use meteor-impact damage.

Source: official Minior Pokédex: https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/minior

The official Lunatone Pokédex states that it was discovered at a meteor-strike site and associates its activity/power with the full moon. This is species-specific lore. Ouros may author Lunatone observations around lunar phase if the regional canon chooses to, but this cannot become a universal moon-phase stat modifier, spawn rule or PTU mechanic without rules evidence.

Source: official Lunatone Pokédex: https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/lunatone

An official Pokémon anime recap describes Sophocles working at Mossdeep Space Center on a research-satellite return, with Ampharos helping provide light after a technical problem. The reusable structure is `scientific institution -> technical mission -> equipment failure -> improvised support -> mission recovery`; it does not authorize satellites, astronautics or Pokémon-powered guidance systems in Ouros until technology canon says they exist.

Source: Pokémon Ultimate Journeys recap, episode “Lighting the Way Home!”: https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/pokemon-ultimate-journeys-the-series-part-1-recap-quiz

### 5. Cosmic terminology in Pokémon/PTU must remain mechanically scoped

Pokémon and PTU contain Moves, Abilities and flavor with names or effects associated with moonlight, sunlight, gravity, meteors, stars and space. Similar wording is not a world-system API.

Guardrails for this pass:

- `Moonlight` does not define lunar illumination or an observatory bonus;
- `Gravity` does not define celestial mechanics;
- `Meteor Mash`, `Comet Punch` or similar names do not simulate meteors/comets;
- Lunatone/Solrock lore does not create regional buffs;
- Minior presence does not prove a meteor shower;
- Psychic or Occult Education does not become automatic astronomy expertise;
- a telescope is not a PTU Item unless the authoritative ruleset defines it as one.

The accessible File Library search did not recover the requested Caelo primary corpus for astronomy. A prior research package confirms PTU 1.05 as the public baseline used by the project, but that package is a secondary project research artifact rather than Caelo canon. No Caelo astronomy rule is inferred from it.

## Distinction from existing Ouros authorities

Timekeeping owns clocks, timestamp correction and local display conventions.

Lightscapes owns sky brightness, artificial light and nocturnal visual environment.

Meteorology owns cloud, weather and atmospheric observing conditions.

Metrology owns calibration, reference standards, uncertainty and measurement traceability.

Remote Sensing owns observations of the physical world from elevated/remote platforms, not astronomical targets.

Science owns hypotheses, datasets, analyses, publications and research interpretation.

Sacred Sites / Mythology / Public Memory own cultural claims, traditions and celestial stories.

Material Culture / Geology / Museums own recovered physical meteorites or other objects once they become terrestrial specimens.

Astronomy Pass 170 owns the observing program, sky-target/event identity, observing session, candidate detection, follow-up, derived celestial solution, visibility prediction and revision history.

## Reusable design lessons

1. The same event can be predicted correctly but not observed locally because cloud, light pollution, equipment failure or horizon obstruction prevented observation.
2. Two observatories can disagree while both raw observations remain valid because timestamps, references, filters, calibration or coverage differ.
3. A transient can be scientifically important even if it never becomes a quest or battle.
4. Public attention can surge before scientific certainty. The public-notice revision should survive in Chronicle even after the solution changes.
5. A recovered meteorite is a separate physical-object workflow from the luminous meteor observation that preceded it.
6. Historical star charts can preserve valid observations while their coordinate conventions or interpretations become obsolete.
7. Community observers can fill gaps, but duplicate reports and shared source dependence must be preserved.
8. A long quiet series is valuable evidence. Do not generate a spectacular anomaly every observing season.
9. A constellation or celestial festival can remain culturally stable while the scientific interpretation changes.
10. Minecraft sky rendering is presentation only. It cannot authoritatively decide eclipse timing, object position, observing conditions or scientific detection.
