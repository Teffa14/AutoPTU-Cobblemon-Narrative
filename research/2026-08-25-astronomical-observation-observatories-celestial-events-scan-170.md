## New public-source lessons

NASA/JPL CNEOS documents that near-Earth-object solutions change as additional observations extend the data arc. Early solutions can be highly uncertain; follow-up can narrow or substantially revise predicted paths. An object leaving a risk list after more observations is a revision of knowledge, not a change to the physical past.

Sources:
- Sentry: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/sentry/intro.html
- Close-approach uncertainties: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/ca/neo_ca_info.html
- NHATS follow-up opportunities: https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats/intro.html
- NASA JPL, How a Speck of Light Becomes an Asteroid: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/how-a-speck-of-light-becomes-an-asteroid/

Reusable structure for Pass 63:

`candidate observation -> provisional linkage -> follow-up request -> additional observations -> revised solution -> revised prediction -> public/scientific handoff`

A cautious early notice can be reasonable and later superseded without implying fraud or incompetence.

The AAVSO International Database shows how distributed observers can maintain astronomical series over more than a century using multiple methods and quality-control processes. This supports Ouros programs where community and institutional sites contribute to the same long-term record without becoming one organization.

Sources:
- AAVSO International Database: https://www.aavso.org/index.php/aavso-international-database/
- AAVSO Data Access: https://archive.aavso.org/index.php/data-access
- AAVSO Research Portal: https://www.aavso.org/index.php/research-portal

Reusable structure:

- observing coverage matters;
- a quiet observation can become a valuable future baseline;
- older observations can remain useful after instruments improve;
- multiple reports derived from one source are not independent corroboration;
- gaps and method changes belong in Chronicle rather than being silently interpolated.

NASA/JPL also describes discovery confirmation as a distributed process: follow-up observers attempt to recover the same candidate, submit additional measurements and improve the solution. Some candidates remain uncertain or are reclassified. This supports explicit `UNRESOLVED`, merge/split and artifact states rather than automatic discovery confirmation.

## Pokémon-specific source lessons

The official Minior Pokédex states that Minior forms in the ozone layer and falls when its outer shell grows heavy. This supports authored observations of high-altitude or falling Minior, but does not imply that every meteor is Minior or that falling Minior uses meteor-impact mechanics.
Source: https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/minior

The official Lunatone Pokédex associates the species with a meteor-strike discovery site and full-moon activity/power. This can motivate a regional observational study if canon chooses, but it does not create global lunar stat modifiers, spawn schedules or automatic Sleep effects.
Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/lunatone

An official Pokémon anime recap describes Sophocles at Mossdeep Space Center assisting with a research-satellite return after a technical problem. The reusable structure is `scientific institution -> technical mission -> equipment failure -> improvised support -> recovery`. It does not authorize satellites or equivalent technology in Ouros until Technology canon does.
Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/pokemon-ultimate-journeys-the-series-part-1-recap-quiz

## Mechanical guardrails

Cosmic wording in Pokémon/PTU is not a world-system API. `Gravity`, `Moonlight`, `Meteor Mash`, `Comet Punch` and similar names cannot define Ouros astronomy. Psychic/Occult Education does not become astronomy expertise by analogy. A telescope is not a PTU Item unless the authoritative ruleset defines one.

Minecraft sky rendering remains presentation only. Vanilla moon phase, tick time, particles or shaders cannot authoritatively create or measure a celestial event.
