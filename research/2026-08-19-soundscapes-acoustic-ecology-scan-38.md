# Soundscapes, Acoustic Ecology & Auditory Information Research — Pass 38

Status: research/provenance only. Nothing in this file is automatically Ouros canon.
Date: 2026-08-19

## Why this pass

The repository already has dedicated layers for performance culture, media/communications, wild ecology, conservation, infiltration, technology, seasonal change, investigation and public memory. A full-tree inspection found no dedicated soundscape/acoustic-ecology layer and no repository search hits for a persistent acoustic model.

That leaves a useful gap: sound can be environmental information, species behavior, infrastructure, navigation, disturbance, evidence, accessibility and place identity without being a Contest, a song performance or a radio publication.

The goal of this pass is therefore to study sound as world state and information while keeping all PTU Sonic Move/Ability effects inside the authoritative rules engine.

## Source 1 — Exploud as biological communication and historical infrastructure

Official Pokédex material states that Exploud communicates with its own kind through multiple sounds and that people historically used Exploud voices to communicate between distant cities.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/exploud

Reusable structure:
- Pokémon vocal behavior can have social meaning independent of combat;
- species behavior can become part of human infrastructure or historical practice;
- an old communication system can leave physical traces, traditions, abandoned stations or route names long after technology changes;
- hearing a call does not automatically reveal its meaning unless the listener has learned or observed that pattern.

Do not copy a canon city, character or plot. Do not assume every Exploud in Ouros willingly serves as infrastructure.

## Source 3 — Chimecho and differentiated call repertoires

Official Pokédex material describes Chimecho using several kinds of cries to communicate with one another and also using sound offensively.

Source:
https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/chimecho

This is useful for ecology, field research and mystery design without turning every cry into a Move.

## Source 7 — Audio-cue puzzles need recovery and accessibility

A public Pokémon fangame discussion for Pokémon: Blast Processed Version describes a cave puzzle where players follow a sequence of sound cues; an incorrect route resets progress. The creator later published the path because the puzzle was harder to interpret than intended.

Source:
https://eeveeexpo.com/blast-processed-version/

Reusable structure:
- sound can be a navigation clue rather than background decoration;
- a sequence-based audio puzzle should expose state clearly and provide a reset/recovery path;
- mandatory audio-only clues create accessibility problems and should have an equivalent visual, textual, haptic or log representation;
- difficulty should come from interpretation, not from inaccessible presentation.

No sound assets, melodies, puzzle sequence or distinctive setting are copied into Ouros.

## Source 8 — Sound can build spatial understanding

BatSight studies navigation through spatialized audio cues in a physical maze and reports that musical sonification can support navigation and immersion.

Source:
https://journal.seriousgamessociety.org/index.php/IJSG/article/view/718

Reusable structure:
- directional or changing audio cues can support navigation;
- different cue families can represent different environmental information;
- players can form a mental map from sound when the signal is consistent;
- Ouros can use spatial audio as a presentation layer while retaining non-audio alternatives.

This is an accessibility/design reference, not a source of Pokémon mechanics.

## Source 9 — Silence can also be information

Research in the Journal of Sound and Music in Games discusses silence as an affordance and how reduced soundtrack density can make body/environment sounds more meaningful.

Source:
https://online.ucpress.edu/jsmg/article/2/4/36/118798/Negative-Ecologies-or-Silence-s-Role-in-Affordance

Reusable structure:
- absence of an expected sound can be an observation;
- quiet can identify a place, time, threat, migration change or infrastructure shutdown;
- silence should only become a clue when a baseline exists;
- "the forest is silent" is not automatically proof of danger.

## Source 10 — Ambient sound supports place identity even without a gameplay signal

Research on environmental music and acoustic ecology argues that ambient audio contributes to the stability and identity of game spaces even when it does not communicate a direct gameplay state.

Sources:
https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/ambient-ecologies-environmental-music-in-video-games/
https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/situating-gaming-as-a-sonic-experience-the-acoustic-ecology-of-fi

Reusable structure:
- not every sound should become a quest clue;
- recurring ambient signatures help a location remain recognizable across visits;
- changes to a familiar soundscape can carry narrative weight precisely because a stable baseline existed first;
- the world can have audible identity without turning sound into constant UI information.

## Cross-system synthesis for Ouros

A durable acoustic model should distinguish:

1. what physically produced a sound;
2. the sound event that occurred;
3. where/when it was potentially audible;
4. what a specific observer actually heard;
5. what that observer thinks caused it;
6. what a recording captured;
7. what institutions or other actors later learn from the observation/recording;
8. whether any PTU mechanic was involved.

This prevents several bad shortcuts:
- hearing a cry does not identify the individual Pokémon automatically;
- a recording does not prove motive;
- absence of calls does not prove population collapse;
- a loud sound does not automatically create damage or a status;
- music does not automatically change emotions;
- a species known for sonic behavior does not automatically possess every Sonic Move;
- battle Soundproof does not automatically make an actor unable to hear ordinary world audio unless the governing rules explicitly establish that interaction.

## New reusable structures

### Acoustic baseline

Locations can accumulate expected sound patterns by time, season and world state. A baseline is evidence built from repeated observations, not a hand-authored universal truth unless canon says so.

### Acoustic anomaly

An anomaly is a difference between expected and observed sound state:
- expected nesting calls absent;
- machinery rhythm changes;
- a warning bell fails;
- an unfamiliar repeated call appears;
- a normally loud district becomes quiet;
- a known call appears at the wrong season or location.

An anomaly creates an investigation candidate, not a conclusion.

### Call library

Field researchers can associate recurring call signatures with species, individual Pokémon, group behavior or environmental context. Confidence and provenance matter.

### Listening station

A fixed or mobile observation site can collect acoustic records over time. It should integrate with Science, Conservation, Media and Technology layers rather than create a separate research economy.

### Acoustic landmark

A recurring audible source can help navigation and place identity: surf, machinery, bells, wingbeats, a waterfall, a colony, a train crossing, a market or a public announcement system.

### Quiet zone

A quiet area can exist for habitat, care, memorial, residential or research reasons. Quiet-zone policy is governance/world state; it is not a PTU Silence effect unless an authoritative mechanic says so.
