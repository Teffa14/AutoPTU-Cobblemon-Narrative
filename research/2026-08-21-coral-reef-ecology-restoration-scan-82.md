# Pass 82 — Coral Reef Ecology, Stress & Restoration Research Scan

Status: research/provenance only. Nothing in this file is established Ouros canon or an authoritative PTU/Caelo rule.

Inspected: 2026-08-21.

## Why this pass exists

The internal repository was inspected before research. `design/maritime-coasts-depths-layer.md` already recognizes `REEF` as a marine-habitat label and includes a reduced `Reef Passage Disturbance` encounter. Fisheries, Conservation, Water Quality, Tourism, Photography, Freshwater, Estuaries, Coastal Geomorphology and Wild Collectives also cover adjacent concerns.

No existing layer owns persistent reef structure, coral-condition history, bleaching/stress observations, recruitment, rubble/stability, reef-complexity change, restoration cohorts, nursery/outplant provenance or long-term recovery. Pass 82 fills that gap instead of duplicating Maritime.

## Research questions

This scan asked:

- How can a reef become persistent world state rather than a decorative biome?
- Which Pokémon relationships are specific enough to support ecology without becoming invented mechanics?
- How can reef degradation and recovery remain uncertain, causal and multi-year?
- Which restoration concepts can be translated into original Ouros institutions and quests without copying real communities or projects?
- Which encounter ideas remain legal with the current AutoPTU-Java capability map?

## Source findings

### Pokémon — Toxapex and warm-current competition

Official Pokédex material for Toxapex states that it battles Bruxish over areas where warm ocean currents flow and that it senses tidal flow through its spikes.

Source: https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/toxapex

Reusable design lesson:

A reef species relationship can depend on a local physical feature such as a current boundary. Ouros can record observations like `Toxapex and Bruxish repeatedly contest the same warm-current edge` without turning warm currents into an automatic battle modifier or declaring every local Toxapex territorial.

Copyright boundary:

Use the high-level ecological structure only. Do not copy Pokédex prose into Ouros fiction.

### Pokémon — Corsola as environmental history

The official Sword/Shield site describes Galarian Corsola as resembling a form whose ancestral population lived in a sea that no longer exists in the same condition.

Source: https://swordshield.pokemon.com/it-it/pokemon-galar-region/corsola/

Reusable design lesson:

Reef organisms can carry evidence of environmental history. A regional coral-associated Pokémon population may become an archaeological/ecological clue without implying that every reef decline creates a regional form or Ghost type.

Guardrail:

Do not create a Corsola extinction, regional form, meteor event or Ghost transformation in Ouros merely because the source demonstrates that environmental history can matter.

### Pokémon roleplay — reef as multi-zone destination

A public Pokémon RP setting, `The Legend of the Isles of Doom: Stranded`, divides an island environment into reef/atoll, deeper slope, freshwater interior and other zones, with different access expectations and Pokémon presence.

Source: https://forums.serebii.net/threads/the-legend-of-the-isles-of-doom-stranded-a-pokemon-rpg.112819/

Reusable design lesson:

A reef is more interesting when it has internal structure: sheltered lagoon, crest, outer slope, channels, rubble field, nursery zone, deep edge, atoll or exposed reef flat. These zones can drive exploration and different observations without copying the source location, name or plot.

### Public Pokémon RP — development pressure around a reef

`Azure Grand Dillema`, a public Bulbagarden RP sign-up, places a major resort development among coral-reef scenery.

Source: https://bulbagarden.net/threads/azure-grand-dillema-a-pokemon-rp-sign-ups.135034/post-4122936

Reusable design lesson:

Tourism, construction, reef access, employment and conservation can all be legitimate stakeholders around the same place. Ouros should avoid a default `developer = villain` framing. The conflict should come from actual footprint, access, waste, transport, anchoring, lighting, visitor pressure, habitat use and stakeholder goals.

### NOAA — restoration needs explicit goals and interventions

NOAA's coral-restoration planning guidance distinguishes restoration goals from individual interventions. Examples include propagation/outplanting, macroalgae removal, substrate preparation and structural interventions, with design choices depending on site conditions, stress exposure and monitoring.

Source: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/27219/noaa_27219_DS1.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

Ouros should represent a `REEF_RESTORATION_PROJECT` with a stated objective, baseline, intervention history, cohorts/sites and monitoring plan. `Restoration active` must not equal `reef recovered`.

The project should be able to fail partially, succeed in one zone, need maintenance after a storm, or be revised because the original method did not work at a given site.

### NOAA — structural complexity is ecologically meaningful

NOAA's restoration monitoring guide treats reef structural complexity/rugosity as a measurable ecological dimension. Disturbance can reduce complexity; restoration can seek to increase or preserve it; repeated surveys are needed to detect change.

Source: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/27135/noaa_27135_DS1.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

Do not reduce reef health to one percentage. Ouros can separately track coarse structural complexity, living-cover observations, recruitment observations, rubble/stability, habitat use and disturbance history.

For Minecraft, structural state can determine which physical reef revision is projected. It must not directly create PTU cover or movement costs unless the battle snapshot has verified rules for them.

### NOAA — restoration must be evaluated over time

NOAA coral-restoration guidance emphasizes baseline/reference comparisons, repeated monitoring and post-disturbance reassessment. Historical restoration reports also show recovery being limited by substrate and local conditions even when corals are transplanted.

Sources:
- https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/27135/noaa_27135_DS1.pdf
- https://taskforce.coralreef.noaa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RtC2005-full_report.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

A reef-recovery arc should persist for seasons or years. The game can revisit the same transects after heat stress, storms, vessel damage, water-quality changes or restoration work. A restored site should retain its intervention provenance and the possibility of later regression.

### NOAA Coral Reef Watch — heat stress and bleaching are observed, monitored states

NOAA Coral Reef Watch documents the use of sea-surface-temperature and heat-stress products to prioritize field monitoring and restoration decisions during severe bleaching events.

Source: https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/doc/NOAA_NESDIS_Impacts_Briefing_CRW_Coral_Bleaching_2024.pdf

Reusable design lesson:

A marine heatwave should enter Ouros through Meteorology/Ocean condition state, monitoring and reef observations. A forecast or heat-stress alert does not itself prove bleaching at every reef. A visually pale Minecraft reef does not apply tactical damage.

## New conceptual distinctions for Ouros

The research supports keeping these states separate:

1. Reef physical structure.
2. Living coral/organism condition observations.
3. Thermal or water-quality stress exposure.
4. Bleaching or disease observations.
5. Recruitment observations.
6. Rubble/substrate stability.
7. Species/population/collective use.
8. Restoration intervention history.
9. Public interpretation and tourism reputation.
10. Tactical battle environment.

A reef can retain high physical complexity after living cover falls. A visually recovering site may still have low recruitment. A restored coral cohort can survive while the surrounding habitat remains degraded. A storm can damage structure without producing the same biological response at every site.

## Narrative structures worth reusing

### Longitudinal reef atlas

Players and institutions survey the same stations over multiple years. Changes become meaningful because previous versions exist.

### Stress event followed by delayed consequences

A heatwave, sediment pulse or storm can end while the reef continues to show altered recruitment, habitat use or structural change.

### Restoration as institutional work

Nursery/outplant programs can generate staffing, transport, monitoring, funding, scientific disagreement, maintenance and public communication stories without becoming a crafting minigame.

### Multiple legitimate stakeholders

Fishers, ferry operators, dive guides, residents, researchers, conservation groups and developers can all have reasonable goals that overlap spatially.

### Apparent recovery that needs verification

A colorful visual state may create optimistic public reports before repeated surveys establish whether the change is durable.

### Species relationship as clue, not universal rule

A Toxapex/Bruxish conflict at a current boundary can reveal a changing local condition. It does not define those species everywhere.

## PTU / AutoPTU boundaries checked in this run

The public PTU resource page continues to identify PTU 1.05 resources and the system's Core/Pokédex material as the rules source: https://pokemontabletop.com/downloads-and-resources/

The available Python AutoPTU evidence confirms concrete `Swim`, terrain-label and `ocean/wetlands` feature paths. Examples include `can_swim()` and Wilderness Guide/Natural Fighter logic for specific terrain categories. Those are exact mechanics, not a generic reef subsystem.

No reliable primary Caelo reef-specific rule text was recovered in this run. No Caelo-specific reef modifier is asserted.

Do not infer from this research:

- reef = PTU Terrain;
- coral branches = cover;
- rubble = Rough Terrain;
- bleaching = Status;
- heat stress = Weather damage;
- Toxapex = automatic territorial AI;
- Corsola presence = healthy reef;
- Corsola absence = dead reef;
- Mareanie/Toxapex = automatic coral damage;
- Water type = Swim capability;
- reef nursery = Pokémon breeding nursery;
- restoration = healing;
- reef complexity = tactical elevation.
