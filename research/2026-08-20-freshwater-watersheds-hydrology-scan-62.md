# Freshwater, Watersheds & Hydrology Research — Pass 62

Status: RESEARCH / provenance only. Not Ouros canon. External material is used for high-level structures and design lessons only.

Date: 2026-08-20

## Why this pass

Existing Ouros layers already cover maritime travel, meteorology, crisis response, conservation, sanitation/pollution, agriculture, infrastructure and geology. They do not yet provide one shared model for freshwater movement across a catchment: headwaters, tributaries, lakes, wetlands, floodplains, reservoirs, groundwater, irrigation diversions and downstream effects.

This pass focuses on that missing connective layer.

### Real-world hydrology used only as systems-design reference

11. Australian Government DCCEEW — Wetlands and changes in water flows
    https://www.dcceew.gov.au/water/wetlands/publications/factsheet-wetlands-water-changes

    Design lesson: a water regime includes timing, frequency, duration, magnitude and variability, not only “wet” or “dry.” Floods can reconnect floodplains and wetlands; low flows and dry periods can also be ecologically meaningful. Dams, extraction and drainage changes can alter those patterns.

12. Australian Government DCCEEW — Water for the environment
    https://www.dcceew.gov.au/cewh/manage-water/water-for-environment

    Design lesson: managed flows can be used to restore connections, support movement/breeding and keep systems functioning through dry periods. In Ouros, this becomes a world-state planning pattern, not a copy of Australian institutions or policy.

13. Australian Government DCCEEW — Return flows
    https://www.dcceew.gov.au/cewh/manage-water/planning/return-flows

    Design lesson: one release can serve several downstream ecological purposes in sequence. This supports a catchment graph where the same water-state change propagates through multiple locations rather than disappearing after one quest.

14. Australian Government DCCEEW — Wetlands and water quality
    https://www.dcceew.gov.au/water/wetlands/publications/factsheet-wetlands-water-quality

    Design lesson: wetlands can slow flows, retain sediment and affect downstream water quality. Cleaning one visible reach does not prove downstream recovery if connected wetland state remains degraded.

15. U.S. Geological Survey — Rivers Contain Groundwater
    https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/rivers-contain-groundwater

    Design lesson: streams may gain water from groundwater, lose water to groundwater, or do both at different reaches/times. This prevents Ouros from treating surface rivers and aquifers as unrelated systems.

16. U.S. Geological Survey — Groundwater/Surface-Water Interaction
    https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/groundwatersurface-water-interaction

    Design lesson: water and dissolved material move between surface and subsurface systems over different timescales. A pollution or drought story can therefore have delayed effects without requiring supernatural explanation.

## Reusable narrative structures

### Catchment causality

Represent freshwater regions as connected reaches and storage nodes. A change upstream should be eligible to affect downstream flow, sediment, access, habitat, water quality and service state only through explicit edges.

### Water-regime history

A river or wetland needs more than a current level. Preserve coarse histories such as high-flow window, low-flow period, seasonal drying, reservoir release, diversion, flood pulse and reconnect/disconnect events.

### Competing legitimate uses

Freshwater can simultaneously support settlements, farms, workshops, transport, wetlands, research sites and Pokémon habitats. Conflict can arise without any side being a villain.

### Surface / groundwater uncertainty

A spring may weaken because of reduced recharge, pumping, geological change or a local blockage. The system should allow several hypotheses until evidence resolves them.

### Reservoir memory

A reservoir can have a pre-inundation history. Former roads, structures, property claims, archaeological context and Pokémon habitat may persist beneath or around the current water body.

### Floodplain as active geography

Floodplains should change route availability, habitat connections and settlement pressure. A flood need not be a disaster in every location; some ecosystems depend on periodic connection.

### Infrastructure as a modifier, not the entire system

Dams, gates, pumps, levees, irrigation channels and treatment works should modify a natural catchment graph. They should not replace the underlying river identity.

## Design direction for Ouros

Create one persistent freshwater layer that other systems consume:

catchment graph → water regime → infrastructure operations → observation/forecast → ecological/service consequences → player-facing opportunities.

This allows a small upstream decision to become future content downstream while keeping every consequence traceable.
