# Music Traditions, Ensembles, Repertoire & Recordings Research — Pass 103

Status: research/provenance only. Not Ouros canon. External stories, communities and games are inspiration sources, not rules sources.

## Why this pass exists

The current branch inventory was reviewed before research. Ouros already has dedicated layers for Contest/performance careers, soundscapes/acoustic ecology, festivals/ceremonies, media, archives, education, clubs, workplaces, material culture, fashion and public memory. Those systems can all mention music, but none owns persistent musical culture itself: compositions, repertoire versions, ensembles, instruments, rehearsal lineage, oral transmission, field recordings, regional styles, arrangements, authorship claims, song archives or the history of a tune moving between places and generations.

This pass therefore models music as durable cultural state. It does not replace the Contest subsystem, the performance-event layer or acoustic ecology. It also does not make ordinary music mechanically equivalent to PTU Musician Songs or Sonic Moves.

## New-source scan

### 1. PTU 1.05 Musician class

Sources:
- public PTU 1.05 Core mirror, Musician section;
- PTU class index.

https://anyflip.com/deia/psdg/basic/151-200
https://pturpg.wikidot.com/classes

Reusable facts:
- Musician is an actual PTU Trainer Class, associated with Charm and Focus;
- its Songs are explicit combat mechanics with action/AP timing and areas of effect;
- the class interacts with Sonic Moves and later Features/Abilities;
- the rules deliberately support several musical character concepts rather than one genre.

Ouros adaptation:

Musicians can exist as ordinary professions, hobbyists, ensemble members, teachers, composers or community performers without possessing the PTU Musician class. Conversely, a Trainer who mechanically has Musician may use its legal battle effects when AutoPTU supports them, but that does not grant cultural authority, fame, composition skill, employment, an audience or ownership of songs.

Mechanical guardrail:

Do not treat an ordinary song, rehearsal or concert as a PTU Musician Song. Do not apply Song of Courage, Song of Might, Song of Life, Soundproof, Drown Out, Sing, Supersonic or other effects unless exact PTU/Caelo rules and Java runtime support authorize them.

### 3. Village Bridge variable scoring

Source: Bulbapedia, Village Bridge.

https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Village_Bridge

Reusable pattern:

Individual local musicians add distinct parts to the same location music. The place gains musical identity through layered participation rather than one centralized star performer.

Ouros adaptation:

A settlement tune can have several documented parts, local variants and performers. A returning player may hear a changed arrangement because one musician moved away, another joined, a new instrument became common or a younger player learned a different version. The composition identity can persist while the arrangement changes.

No lyrics, names or distinctive musical material are copied.

### 9. Public PTU campaign longevity and recurring cultural anchors

Source: Pokémon World Tour: United public podcast listing.

https://podcasts.apple.com/au/podcast/pokemon-world-tour-united/id1154176782

Useful pattern:

A long-running PTU campaign can return to recurring cultural dates and places years later. This supports music as a continuity device: the same song, venue, ensemble or annual program can acquire different meaning after major campaign events.

Ouros adaptation:

Repeated performances should retain edition history. Old recordings, former members and changed arrangements can make time visible without retconning earlier versions.

### 10. Living heritage should evolve rather than be frozen

Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, “Safeguarding without freezing.”

https://ich.unesco.org/en/safeguarding-00012

Reusable design lesson:

Living cultural practices remain viable through transmission and recreation. Safeguarding does not mean preserving one supposedly pure version forever. Community involvement and consent are central, and some practices can legitimately change or disappear.

Ouros adaptation:

A regional song tradition should support variants, new arrangements, apprentices, loss, revival and disagreement. The system should not create a hidden `authenticity_score` that declares one modern performance true and another false.

Cultural-use guardrail:

This source is used only to understand living-heritage structures. Real musical traditions, sacred practices, Indigenous knowledge, instruments and communities must not be reskinned into fictional Ouros cultures.

### 11. Ethnomusicology archives and field recordings

Sources:
- UCLA Ethnomusicology Archive;
- Library of Congress Archive of Folk Culture.

https://schoolofmusic.ucla.edu/facilities/ethnomusicology-archive/
https://wwws.loc.gov/folklife/archive.html

Reusable patterns:
- recordings have provenance and collection context;
- archives may hold audio, video, photographs, notes and commercial releases together;
- a recording can preserve a historical version without being the only valid version;
- access and description are separate archival problems.

Ouros adaptation:

A `MUSIC_RECORDING` should preserve performer, location, date/window, recording context, source medium, archive custody, derivative copies and identification confidence. Old recordings can support research into a vanished venue, former dialect, changed arrangement or individual Pokémon call without automatically proving every catalog claim.

### 12. Rights and permissions around recorded performance

Source: Library of Congress, guidance for copies of unpublished Archive of Folk Culture recordings.

https://wwws.loc.gov/folklife/recordering.html

Reusable design lesson:

Possessing or preserving a recording does not automatically mean unrestricted publication rights or performer consent.

Ouros adaptation:

Archives, broadcasters, performers and collectors can have separate custody, access and publication states. Ouros should not assume a recording found in an archive can be broadcast, monetized or remixed. Exact legal systems remain canon decisions; the design only preserves the distinction.

## Design synthesis

Pass 103 should add a music-culture layer with these separations:

1. musical work identity;
2. composition/authorship claim;
3. arrangement or regional variant;
4. repertoire membership;
5. ensemble identity and membership history;
6. instrument instance and provenance;
7. rehearsal state;
8. performance event reference;
9. recording instance and provenance;
10. archive/publication state;
11. audience/public-memory state;
12. acoustic event projection;
13. PTU mechanical effect, normally none.

A song can survive after its composer is unknown. Two arrangements can both be legitimate. A field recording can preserve one performance without defining the tradition. A famous performer can play a work they did not compose. A Pokémon can participate musically without becoming owned, mechanically buffed or assigned the Musician class.

## Reusable narrative structures

### The tune with three histories

Three towns perform recognizable versions of the same melody and each has a different origin story. Musicological comparison, archives, migration history and old recordings can narrow relationships without requiring one community to be declared fraudulent.

### The missing part

An ensemble piece survives, but one part was transmitted orally by a performer who is no longer available. Players can search archives, former students, old recordings or related regional versions. Recovery may produce several plausible reconstructions rather than one magic answer.

### Venue changes the music

A hall closes, a bridge is rebuilt or a neighborhood changes. The same ensemble adapts instrumentation and arrangement to a new space. Architecture and public-space history become audible without turning acoustics into combat bonuses.

### Pokémon participation without ownership

Wild or institutional Pokémon may contribute recurring sound or rhythm to a local practice. Their absence, migration or refusal changes the music. This is an observation and relationship problem, not a capture objective.

### Recording becomes evidence

A recording made for artistic reasons later becomes useful evidence for seasonal timing, a machine sound, an individual Pokémon call, a vanished building or an old public announcement. The recording remains incomplete and context-bound.

### Revival creates a new tradition

A nearly forgotten repertoire is reconstructed by students. The revival is historically informed but visibly modern. Years later, that revival itself has become a tradition with its own alumni and variants.

## PTU / Caelo / engine boundary notes

Do not invent or generalize:
- Musician Songs;
- Sonic keyword behavior;
- Soundproof or Drown Out effects;
- Sing/Supersonic/Hyper Voice/Perish Song effects;
- Charm or Focus DCs for ordinary performance;
- Contest bonuses from music;
- musician damage/support bonuses;
- stage-equipment electrical rules;
- instrument weapon stats;
- morale, Friendship or Loyalty gains from listening;
- emotional status effects from ordinary songs;
- healing from lullabies;
- crowd buffs;
- rhythm-based initiative;
- Pokémon obedience from music;
- compulsory reaction to Meloetta or other musical species.

The AutoPTU Python repository contains a structured Trainer-class catalog including Musician material, which is useful as oracle evidence. Current AutoPTU-Java search did not surface concrete Musician/Song implementations. Trainer Features/perks therefore remain a capability dependency whenever an encounter expects mechanical Musician effects.

## Copyright / transformation policy

No protected lyrics, melodies, dialogue, characters, routines or plots are copied. Franchise examples establish broad precedents. Community sources contribute only structural lessons. Original Ouros material must use new compositions, traditions, institutions, performers, histories and conflicts.
