# Research Scan — Pass 87 — Open Ocean, Oceanography & Pelagic Ecology

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Not Ouros canon. External sources are inspiration or factual references, not PTU/Caelo rules authority.

## Scope

This pass investigates a gap left between the existing Maritime, Fisheries, Reef, Estuary, Meteorology and Ecology layers: persistent open-ocean physical state.

The focus is on:

- currents and water masses;
- oceanographic fronts;
- upwelling and downwelling;
- stratification and mixed layers;
- thermoclines;
- pelagic habitat;
- plankton and larval transport;
- productivity hotspots;
- open-ocean observation networks;
- marine tourism and fisheries responses;
- how ocean state can change observations without becoming a tactical rule by default.

This pass does not define Swim, underwater visibility, drowning, currents in battle, fishing checks, marine Weather, rare-spawn modifiers or PTU movement rules.

## Source 1 — Mantyke: regional water temperature can matter without becoming a battle effect

Source: Pokémon Pokédex — Mantyke

https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/mantyke

The official Pokédex notes that Mantyke in Galar appear more sluggish and suggests colder regional seas as a possible cause.

Reusable lesson:

- marine populations can differ by region because the water mass itself differs;
- a biological observation can be correlated with temperature without proving the exact mechanism;
- local ocean conditions can become research hooks independent of combat.

Do not import:

- automatic Speed penalties from cold water;
- species-wide temperature formulas;
- a rule that any cold sea makes Mantyke Slowed.

## Source 6 — NOAA: plankton are transported by currents

Source: NOAA Ocean Service — What are plankton?

https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/plankton.html

NOAA defines plankton operationally as organisms carried by tides and currents when they cannot move effectively against them. Some organisms are planktonic only during early life stages.

Reusable lesson:

- currents can move biological cohorts without requiring individual pathfinding;
- eggs/larvae/juveniles may have different spatial dynamics from adults;
- loaded Cobblemon entities should not be the authority for pelagic transport.

## Source 7 — NOAA: ocean fronts can concentrate productivity and organisms

Source: NOAA Fisheries — The Plankton Scope

https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/science-blog/plankton-scope

NOAA describes oceanographic fronts as nutrient-rich features where organisms can concentrate for feeding, nursery use and breeding.

Reusable lesson:

- a front can be a temporary ecological hotspot with no visible coastline or structure;
- boats, researchers, fishers, predators and tourism may all converge on the same moving feature for different reasons;
- a front should be versioned in space/time rather than represented as a permanent marker.

## Source 8 — NOAA: upwelling links wind, colder water and productivity

Sources:

https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/upwelling/

https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/upwelling.html

NOAA describes upwelling as deeper, colder, nutrient-rich water rising toward the surface when surface water is displaced. It can occur along coasts and in the open ocean and often supports high biological productivity.

Reusable lesson:

- Meteorology can drive an oceanographic process without becoming the same state object;
- productivity can increase because physical conditions change, not because the system spawned a quest reward;
- a productive region can later affect Fisheries, Wildlife, Tourism and Science.

Do not turn upwelling into a direct encounter-rate multiplier. Any Cobblemon projection must be buffered, coarse and anti-exploit.

## Source 9 — NOAA: fronts and shelf-break features can propagate through the food web

Source: NOAA Integrated Ecosystem Assessment — Northeast Phytoplankton

https://www.integratedecosystemassessment.noaa.gov/regions/northeast/northeast-phytoplankton

NOAA describes stratification, localized upwelling and thermohaline fronts as important controls on phytoplankton and higher trophic levels. It also notes subsurface chlorophyll maxima that may not be visible from satellites.

Reusable lesson:

- surface appearance is incomplete evidence of the water column;
- remote observation and in-situ sampling can disagree without either being fraudulent;
- fronts can create localized food-web effects that move or disappear.

## Source 10 — NOAA: upwelling and stratification are distinct physical processes

Source: NOAA GFDL — Reconciling Ocean Productivity and Fisheries Yields

https://www.gfdl.noaa.gov/research_highlight/reconciling-ocean-productivity-and-fisheries-yields/

The source explains how stable stratification can separate nutrient-rich deeper water from the well-lit surface layer, while winds, cooling and upwelling can reconnect them.

Reusable lesson:

- `STRATIFIED` and `PRODUCTIVE` should never be synonyms;
- a warm surface layer can coexist with different water below;
- thermocline depth can become a scientific observation that affects sampling strategy before it affects any encounter.

## Source 11 — NOAA: oceanographic fronts are not permanent walls

Source: NOAA repository research on submesoscale currents and seasonal productivity

https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/49930

The study demonstrates that smaller-scale currents and eddies can alter nutrient transport and productivity differently across coastal and offshore areas and seasons.

Reusable lesson:

- ocean features should have revisions, confidence and lifetimes;
- an old cruise track may be scientifically valuable because the front moved;
- the same process can produce opposite biological responses in different places.

## Source 12 — NOAA: plankton sampling can reveal transport and nursery connectivity

Source: NOAA Fisheries — Neuston and Bongo Plankton Nets

https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/science-blog/neuston-and-bongo-plankton-nets

Sampling surface and deeper plankton can inform productivity, spawning areas, stock size and larval transport.

Reusable lesson:

- Open Ocean should support depth-tagged sample provenance;
- Fisheries recruitment and Reef/Estuary nursery connections can depend on transport evidence;
- a larval cohort should not become an exact adult population forecast.

## Source 13 — NOAA: blooms depend on physical processes and can move

Source: NOAA Fisheries — Phytoplankton of the Northwest U.S. Shelf Ecosystem

https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/science-data/phytoplankton-northwest-us-shelf-ecosystem

NOAA lists vertical mixing, upwelling, currents, tides, temperature, nutrients and light among drivers of phytoplankton abundance and composition.

Reusable lesson:

- a bloom requires observation and identification rather than being inferred from green water;
- transport can move a bloom away from its source region;
- Health Surveillance, Fisheries and Air/Water communication systems can consume bloom alerts without redefining them.

This pass does not create toxin or disease mechanics.

## Design lessons for Ouros

### 1. Open ocean should have persistent state even when nobody is there

The server should be able to remember:

- a current regime;
- a front position;
- a water-mass boundary;
- a stratification profile;
- an upwelling episode;
- a bloom observation;
- a pelagic hotspot;
- a research transect;
- an old route observation.

It should not simulate every water parcel.

### 2. Ocean state should be coarse and versioned

A useful model needs enough detail to produce consequences without becoming an ocean circulation simulator.

Recommended scales:

- region;
- corridor;
- front;
- observation station/transect;
- depth band;
- time window.

### 3. Biological concentration is evidence, not a guaranteed encounter table

A productive front may increase observation opportunities in world logic, but should not directly map to `rare_spawn_chance += X`.

### 4. Fronts should move

A front can be:

- mapped yesterday;
- missed today;
- rediscovered farther offshore;
- split into multiple features;
- gone next season.

That makes old data and field expertise useful.

### 5. Vertical structure matters

Surface water, mixed layer, thermocline, deeper water and seafloor can be physically and biologically different.

The narrative system can store depth bands without assuming AutoPTU supports 3D combat.

### 6. Research and fishing can read the same ocean differently

A fisher may care about catch distribution.

A scientist may care about temperature, plankton or nutrient structure.

A ferry operator may care about sea state and route reliability.

A tourist guide may care about Wailord sightings.

None of those views is the whole ocean.

### 7. Drifters need provenance

Plankton, larvae, debris, floating samples and tagged objects can move far from origin. Discovery location and origin claim must remain separate.

### 8. An oceanographic anomaly should not automatically become supernatural

Unexpected warm water, missing plankton, shifted sightings or strange drift should first create observations and hypotheses.

Legendary or anomalous explanations require authored canon evidence.
