# Research Scan 137 — Textiles, Garments, Uniforms & Wearable Material Culture

Status: RESEARCH / PROVENANCE ONLY. Not Ouros canon.
Date: 2026-08-23

## Scope and non-duplication check

Before writing, the current narrative repository inventory was reviewed against existing systems. Breeding/Eggs was explicitly rejected because Pass 17 already covers it. No dedicated layer was found for textiles, garments, uniforms, tailoring, alterations, fit, wearable maintenance, or long-lived clothing history.

Relevant existing authority boundaries were reviewed:

- Manufacturing owns repeatable production processes, lots, work-in-progress, quality disposition, release, rework and recall.
- Supply Chains owns procurement, inventory, allocation, storage, freight and availability.
- Material Culture remains the authority for persistent physical item identity and provenance.
- Markets owns listings, negotiation, sale and transaction handoff.
- Credentials owns actual permissions/eligibility/authority.
- Workplaces owns roles and staffing.
- Identity owns names/presentation/aliases.
- Museums owns accession, conservation and exhibition after an item enters a collection.
- Pokémon Agency owns Pokémon identity, partnership, custody and consent/behavioral observations.
- AutoPTU/PTU/Caelo remain authoritative for mechanical equipment, accessories and Fashionista effects.

The missing responsibility is the wearable-specific lifecycle between material production and ordinary use: fiber/textile provenance, construction, fit, alteration, repair, care, uniform issue/return, secondhand continuity and visual presentation without inventing authority or mechanics.

## Public-source findings

### 1. Leavanny and Sewaddle — clothing as a Pokémon-authored behavior

Official Pokédex material states that Leavanny fashions clothing from woven leaves. Sewaddle chews leaves to make its own clothing after imitating the garment Leavanny first gave it.

Reusable design lesson:

- some Pokémon may have species-authored relationships with making or wearing material coverings;
- construction can be observable behavior and can produce persistent physical objects;
- learned imitation may be relevant to the Social Learning layer when evidence exists;
- the behavior does not imply Fashionista Features, crafting bonuses, universal tailoring competence, or combat equipment.

Sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/leavanny
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/sewaddle

### 2. Wooloo — renewable fiber can become a regional material economy

The official Sword/Shield site states that Wooloo fleece regrows after shearing and is used for clothing, carpets and other textiles, with Galar treating it as a regional specialty product.

Reusable design lesson:

- Pokémon-derived fibers can connect ranching, consent/care, processing, manufacturing, markets and cultural identity;
- a fiber batch should preserve source/provenance rather than becoming generic cloth immediately;
- availability and regional popularity can matter without assigning mechanical armor or environmental resistance;
- shearing/harvesting rules must remain authored and cannot be generalized to other Pokémon.

Source:
- https://swordshield.pokemon.com/en-au/pokemon-galar-region/wooloo/

### 3. Burmy/Wormadam — biological coverings are not ordinary garments

Official Pokédex material states that Burmy rapidly remakes its cloak from nearby materials if the cloak is damaged, and Wormadam's cloak becomes part of its body after evolution.

Reusable design lesson:

- visually wearable material may be biological/species state rather than an independent inventory item;
- the adapter must not strip, transfer, sell, repair or equip a Burmy/Wormadam cloak as if it were an ordinary garment;
- the same visual category can require different authority models depending on species biology.

Sources:
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/burmy
- https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/wormadam

### 4. PTU Fashionista — clothing can have exact mechanics, but only when the rules say so

The official PTU development blog explicitly discusses the Fashionista Class as a mechanical class that needed rework alongside Contests. The public PTU 1.05 core material also identifies Fashionista as a Trainer Class. Community reproductions/homebrew discussions show why the boundary matters: Fashionista recipes and equipment can carry actual game effects.

Reusable design lesson:

- ordinary clothing in Ouros must remain narrative/material state unless a specific PTU/Caelo item, Feature, recipe or equipment rule is validated;
- a uniform, coat, hat, costume, tailoring skill or fashion profession cannot inherit Fashionista effects by resemblance;
- visual customization in Minecraft cannot create a PTU equipment state by itself.

Sources:
- https://pokemontabletop.com/a-fresh-start-and-ptu-1-05-news/
- https://peda.net/p/josajoki/fista/ohjeet/ptu/pokemon-tabletop-united-1.05-core%3Afile/download/c109e0ecc0ac41065575a4a324183b80189a2c70/Pokemon%20Tabletop%20United%201.05%20Core.pdf

Community material was consulted only to understand how mechanically dangerous it would be to blur ordinary clothing and Fashionista equipment. No homebrew mechanics are imported.

### 5. Textile conservation — wear, light, handling and pests create histories

The US National Park Service textile-care guidance treats long-term preservation as dependent on environmental control, handling and pest management. It specifically identifies light, temperature, relative humidity, air pollution and handling as preservation concerns.

Reusable design lesson:

- a historical garment should accumulate condition observations and conservation history rather than a single permanent durability number;
- repair, alteration, restoration and conservation are different events;
- preserving an old garment does not make it mechanically tougher;
- a visibly intact textile may still have conservation concerns that Minecraft appearance cannot determine.

Source:
- https://www.nps.gov/subjects/museums/upload/MHI_AppK_TextilesObjects.pdf

## Cross-source synthesis for Ouros

A useful textile lifecycle is:

```text
fiber/material source
→ material batch
→ textile/fabric batch
→ garment design/specification
→ construction
→ fitting/issue
→ wear/use
→ cleaning/care
→ repair/alteration
→ reassignment/secondhand transfer
→ retirement/storage
→ possible archive/museum accession
```

Each transition should preserve provenance and version history. The same coat can pass through multiple owners, repairs and institutions without becoming a new item.

## Guardrails derived from the scan

Do not infer:

- uniform worn → credential/authority;
- insignia visible → membership or current rank;
- costume → profession;
- clothing style → gender, origin, ethnicity, ideology, wealth, religion or faction;
- expensive-looking clothing → actual wealth;
- repaired garment → lower or higher PTU stats;
- winter coat → cold immunity;
- raincoat → Weather immunity;
- protective-looking garment → Armor mechanics;
- Fashionista-like appearance → Fashionista Class;
- Leavanny present → free tailoring service;
- Wooloo fleece → universal renewable harvesting permission;
- Burmy/Wormadam cloak → transferable inventory item;
- Pokémon wearing an accessory → consent to continued use;
- Minecraft skin/armor slot → authoritative PTU equipment.

## Candidate system handoffs

- Material Culture: stable item identity, provenance, ownership/custody references.
- Manufacturing: repeatable textile/garment production runs and lots.
- Supply Chains: fiber/fabric inventory, allocation and transport.
- Markets: listings, secondhand sales, consignments.
- Workplaces: dress requirements and role context.
- Credentials: actual authority independently of uniform appearance.
- Identity/Public Persona: presentation without inferring private identity.
- Festivals/Performance: costumes and event-specific presentation.
- Museums: accession/conservation of historically significant garments.
- Pokémon Agency: Pokémon participation and wearable consent/behavior.
- Climate/Weather/Care: context only; no automatic mechanical protection.
- AutoPTU/PTU/Caelo: exact mechanical equipment, accessories, Fashionista recipes/Features.

## PTU/Caelo validation boundary

Public PTU material confirms Fashionista exists as a mechanical class. That is enough to require a strict boundary, but not enough to claim Java parity for the class or its item catalog.

The complete project-specific Caelo corpus was not reliably recoverable in this runtime. Super PTU Online Helper was not exposed as an invokable capability. No Caelo-specific equipment, Fashionista, armor, accessory, clothing, crafting or environmental-protection rule is inferred here.

## Research result

The research supports a dedicated wearable-material-culture layer because textiles are simultaneously persistent objects, social signals, manufactured goods, repairable artifacts and potential PTU equipment. Keeping those dimensions separate prevents Minecraft visuals and narrative clothing from silently creating authority or battle mechanics.
