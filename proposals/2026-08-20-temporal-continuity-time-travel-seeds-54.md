# Ouros Temporal Continuity Seeds — Pass 54

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level temporal-story structures. No external characters, dialogue or plots are imported.

These seeds assume the default Ouros rule that unusual clocks or spaces do not prove time travel. A seed becomes physically temporal only after canon review.

## 1. The Letter Dated Next Thursday

A municipal archive receives a sealed letter whose material, handwriting and custody history look ordinary, but the document is dated seven days ahead and describes a minor infrastructure failure.

The immediate story is verification, not prevention. The described failure might happen, fail to happen, or be a deliberate prediction rather than a message from the future.

## 2. The Bridge Photograph

A research team finds a photograph showing a rebuilt bridge with a design nobody has approved yet.

The image is authentic as a physical object. Its source temporal context remains unresolved.

## 3. Five Minutes at the Clocktower

A small district appears to repeat the same five-minute sequence around one clocktower while the surrounding city continues normally.

Only explicitly scoped objects reset. The rest of the server never rolls back.

## 4. The Missing Edition

A newspaper archive contains editions 141, 142, 144 and 145. Edition 143 appears in a private collection, but it reports events no local institution remembers.

Possible explanations include forgery, catalog error, alternate-context provenance or a record from a future that never became current history.

## 5. The Orchard's Two Harvest Logs

Two legitimate farm ledgers cover the same season and both have continuous provenance, yet they record different harvest dates.

The investigation must first rule out clerical duplication, calendar differences and copied records before proposing temporal divergence.

## 6. A Pokémon Seen Before It Was Caught

An old photograph appears to show a player's persistent Pokémon years before its documented first meeting with that Trainer.

The system must not rewrite ownership, capture history or relationship state until identity and temporal provenance are established.

## 7. The Researcher Who Knows the Result

A visiting scientist predicts the exact negative result of an experiment before the first measurement is taken.

They may have unusual information. They may also simply understand the system better than everyone else.

## 8. The Ferry That Arrived Twice

A harbor log records the same ferry arrival twice with the same vessel, crew and cargo IDs but different timestamps.

The investigation focuses on records, clocks and physical cargo before any supernatural hypothesis.

## 9. The Unopened Memorial

A traveler describes a memorial that does not yet exist and knows several names proposed for it.

The information cannot cause those people to become casualties. It may instead influence prevention, public debate or the memorial's eventual design.

## 10. The Possible Champion

A future-context record depicts an established NPC as regional Champion.

Current NPC career state remains unchanged. The record becomes a possible-future claim, not a promotion.

## 11. The Empty Museum Case

A museum display contains a label for an artifact that has not yet been discovered.

The institution can investigate the label without creating the artifact in current world state.

## 12. The Second Survey Team

An expedition reaches a ruin and finds markers bearing its own institute's modern inventory codes, weathered as if they had been there for decades.

The markers can be authentic without proving who placed them or when.

## 13. The Old Route, New Map

A map appears to show a trail that will only become physically possible after a planned dam project changes water levels.

The route remains inaccessible until world state actually supports it.

## 14. The Repeated Rescue Request

A rescue office receives the same request at the same local timestamp on three different days, each with small factual differences.

The office keeps each message as a separate information packet rather than overwriting the previous one.

## 15. The Pokémon That Remembers a Building

A persistent Pokémon repeatedly navigates toward a structure that does not exist in the current settlement but appears in historical plans.

Observed route choice is factual. The system does not invent the Pokémon's memory or temporal experience as explanation.

## 16. The Future Weather Station

A sealed sensor is recovered carrying a dataset with timestamps months ahead.

The data can be compared against later weather without treating it as guaranteed prophecy.

## 17. The Tournament Bracket That Never Happened

A complete bracket lists matches, results and injuries for an event whose format was changed before opening day.

Institutions investigate provenance. No participant receives those results in their actual battle history.

## 18. The Duplicate Signature

Two versions of an agreement contain identical valid signatures but materially different clauses.

Custody, document versioning and temporal provenance must be resolved before accusing anyone of fraud.

## 19. The Closed Loop Garden

A botanical research garden repeats one afternoon until a bounded exit condition is met.

Plants, instruments and local NPC routines reset according to an explicit loop domain. Player progression and unrelated world state do not.

## 20. The Reward That Cannot Be Farmed

A looped dungeon contains a unique historical token.

The first successful recovery writes an idempotent reward claim. Later iterations can show the object in source-context state but cannot produce additional owned copies.

## 21. The Rival's Warning

A rival claims to have fought the player in another future and warns about a tactic.

The rival's current tactical AI cannot read hidden moves because of that claim. Only information explicitly transferred and legally knowable can enter scouting state.

## 22. The Clinic Record From Tomorrow

A care facility receives a patient record for a Pokémon that is currently healthy.

The record never creates an Injury, diagnosis or status. It can motivate monitoring if privacy and consent permit.

## 23. The Evolved Pokémon in the Old Film

An archival film appears to show a Pokémon in an evolved state before the Evolution event in current history.

Pass 53 identity and Evolution history remain authoritative until temporal provenance is proven.

## 24. The Versioned Ruin

A ruin appears in three historically distinct physical states through a controlled temporal window.

Researchers can document each state. They cannot remove unique objects from all three states unless canon explicitly defines how temporal custody works.

## 25. The Town That Remembers Nothing

One traveler returns from a temporal event with memories of an evacuation that local residents never experienced.

The traveler can provide testimony. The town does not receive rewritten memories automatically.

## 26. The Prediction Market Problem

A public future claim causes businesses and travelers to change behavior, which itself changes whether the predicted event occurs.

The arc studies information-driven causality without requiring supernatural determinism.

## 27. The Safe Future That Became Unsafe

Players see a possible future where a route is safe and later make a current decision that alters traffic, ecology and maintenance.

The future record becomes stale rather than false or fraudulent.

## 28. One Building, Three Histories

A civic hall has three documented renovation histories. Each is internally consistent and supported by different records.

The story can resolve as archive error, institutional cover-up, or actual temporal divergence depending on evidence and canon review.

## 29. The Temporal Quarantine

A research institute discovers that objects crossing one anomaly may carry inconsistent dates or biological states.

The institute establishes a cautious intake protocol without assuming pathogens, radiation or mechanical damage.

## 30. The Last Stable Minute

A temporal window becomes less stable over several world events. Researchers can observe the decline, prepare equipment, limit visitors and decide whether the final opening should be used at all.

The climax can be a decision to let the phenomenon close permanently.

# Longer arc A — The Calendar With Missing Days

Phase 1: Several institutions independently report timestamp inconsistencies.

Phase 2: Most cases receive ordinary explanations, leaving a smaller cluster tied to one geographic corridor.

Phase 3: A controlled observation confirms a temporal offset without identifying its cause.

Phase 4: The region creates rules for records, visitors and custody crossing the anomaly.

Phase 5: Evidence suggests that one future observation has already influenced current decisions.

Phase 6: Players choose whether to intervene further, observe without interference or close access.

The arc can finish without exposing a Legendary or permitting permanent free time travel.

# Longer arc B — The Town That Happened Twice

A settlement exists in public records in two materially different historical forms after the same old disaster.

Players reconstruct both evidence chains through archives, family records, archaeology, maps and institutional memory.

If temporal divergence is eventually approved as canon, the two histories become explicit contexts with provenance. If not, the arc can resolve through layered historical mistakes or deliberate record manipulation.

The design remains playable under either answer for most of its duration.

# Longer arc C — The Loopkeepers

A bounded site enters a recurring temporal loop.

Early play is about discovering reset boundaries and retained information.

Middle play becomes institutional: researchers, residents, rescue teams and visitors disagree about acceptable experiments and risk.

Late play asks whether escaping the loop requires changing a causal event, accepting the site's permanent closure or preserving the loop for study.

No loop participant receives unlimited rewards or automatic battle retries with free progression.

# Encounter contract B — Tomorrow's Evacuation

REDUCED version:

Treat the record as a future claim. Players investigate and prepare. Any battle occurs normally on current world state. Later compare the predicted event against what actually happened.

FULL version:

Maintain a formal possible-future simulation whose state can be compared against current interventions.

Additional dependency:

- authoritative future-state projection/causal-delta system — BLOCKING/unimplemented.

No time travel is required for the reduced version.

# Encounter contract D — Archive From Tomorrow

Narrative premise: a future-dated archival record describes three events not yet observed.

Preferred implementation is non-combat.

The quest uses archives, provenance, actor knowledge, science/case investigation and temporal-claim validation. A battle is added only if current world state independently creates one.

# Promotion rules

None of these seeds establish:

- physical time travel in Ouros;
- a time-traveling Legendary;
- deterministic prophecy;
- timeline deletion;
- automatic memory rewriting;
- resurrection;
- de-aging;
- battle rewind;
- repeated reward farming;
- duplicate Pokémon/items;
- alternate evil versions of PCs.

Any seed promoted toward canon needs explicit temporal rules, provenance, player-agency review and implementation feasibility.
