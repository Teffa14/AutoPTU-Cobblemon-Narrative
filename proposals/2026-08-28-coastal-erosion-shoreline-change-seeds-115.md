# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Coastal Erosion, Shoreline Change & Recovery — Pass 115

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. These are worldbuilding and adventure candidates derived from Pass 115 research. They do not establish Ouros geography, institutions, exact Pokémon roles or battle mechanics.

## Design rule

A changing coastline should create persistent local history rather than a disposable disaster scene.

Every seed keeps separate:

1. what physical change was directly observed;
2. what actors believe caused it;
3. what maps/notices currently say;
4. what downstream systems have decided;
5. what remains uncertain.

## Situation — The Beach Grew and Disappeared

Two nearby neighborhoods argue about whether the last season “took the beach away” or “made the beach bigger.”

Both reports can be correct.

One shoreline sector lost its familiar upper beach while another received a new deposit.

Adventure value:

- compare photographs from stable viewpoints;
- identify which stretch each witness means;
- reconcile tourism brochures with current access;
- document a newly used Pokémon resting area;
- hand ecological interpretation to Conservation;
- avoid reducing the entire coast to one erosion score.

## Situation — The Old Stairs End Above Nothing

A public stairway still descends toward the coast, but the final section no longer reaches the same usable beach edge shown in older photographs.

Questions:

- is the structure physically sound?
- is the lower access currently authorized?
- was the coast altered, the stairs altered, or both?
- does another route provide accessible entry?
- are old public signs still directing visitors here?

Facility Maintenance owns structure condition. Travel/Accessibility owns practical use. The coastal layer preserves the physical-change evidence.

## Situation — The New Sandbar Has Regulars

A recently documented sandbar or deposited edge is repeatedly used by several wild Pokémon.

Residents quickly invent explanations about why the Pokémon “made” it or “knew it was coming.”

The quest can focus on patient observation:

- when did the landform first appear in records?
- when did each Pokémon begin using it?
- does use vary with ordinary tide state?
- are food/resting conditions different?
- is public access disturbing the new behavior?

No species-wide geomorphic capability is inferred.

## Situation — The Temporary Boardwalk Became the Main Entrance

A temporary path was installed after a coastal access point changed. Months later, visitors prefer the temporary route because it passes a café, lookout or market stall that grew around the new foot traffic.

When the original access is technically recoverable, reopening it becomes a civic question rather than an automatic reset.

Possible consequences:

- one business depends on the new flow;
- residents near the temporary path want quiet restored;
- a Pokémon has adopted a routine around the temporary structure;
- Accessibility finds the old and new routes serve different users;
- a tourism map still shows only the former entrance.

## Situation — The Map Still Has the Old Beach Name

A historic beach name survives on maps and in family stories even though the visible coastline has changed enough that newcomers use another name for the present-day public space.

Adventure value:

- compare map editions;
- interview residents from different generations;
- distinguish a place-name from a precise shoreline polygon;
- identify whether both names remain socially valid;
- preserve old records without forcing a single “correct” label.

## Situation — The Object Was Under the Sand for Decades

A changed coastal edge exposes an old sign, foundation, mooring piece, memorial object or cargo fragment.

The discovery creates several independent questions:

- what is it?
- when was it deposited or buried?
- who currently has custody?
- is it heritage, lost property, evidence, debris or ordinary infrastructure?
- can the public approach the location safely?

Combat, if any, should never convert the object into automatic loot. Found Property, Archives/Heritage, Case Authority or another governing layer handles custody.

## Situation — The Road Reopened, the Lookout Did Not

A minor coastal road is reopened after cleanup and inspection, but an adjacent lookout remains restricted because its access edge has changed.

Residents summarize the entire area as either “open” or “closed.”

The quest reconciles scopes:

- road segment state;
- parking/turnout state;
- footpath state;
- lookout state;
- beach access state;
- public notice freshness.

This creates useful friction without requiring anyone to lie.

## Situation — The Dune Came Back Somewhere Else

After a prior episode, residents celebrate that the beach/dune system “recovered,” but repeat observations show a new stable form rather than restoration of the old outline.

Possible story threads:

- old property/access assumptions no longer match reality;
- a previous path is not sensible to rebuild;
- a new sheltered pocket becomes habitat;
- an old observation post now overlooks the wrong feature;
- local memory treats the former line as culturally important.

Recovery should close a crisis without erasing morphology history.

## Situation — The Pokémon Waits at the Former Landing

A recurring Pokémon still visits the place where a small boat landing used to meet the beach, even though the current landing has shifted.

Possible explanations remain open:

- learned routine;
- food associated with past traffic;
- social attachment to a worker/person;
- another ecological feature at the old site;
- coincidence in observation times.

The behavior becomes a character/world-continuity thread rather than a supernatural coastal sensor.

## Situation — The Brochure Is Only Two Years Old

A tourism brochure shows a path and beach width that no longer match current conditions.

The brochure is recent enough that visitors assume the mismatch means fraud or negligence.

Investigation may show:

- the map was accurate when printed;
- a later event changed one sector;
- the tourism office received an update but old copies still circulate;
- a private website copied an older diagram;
- residents use the same path name for its replacement alignment.

This seed links Cartography, Tourism, Public Notices and local memory.

## Situation — The New Channel Has Two Names

A small breach or newly persistent channel appears in several records. Field staff, fishers, residents and mapmakers each use a different informal name.

The story can revolve around identity and provenance:

- are all names referring to the same feature?
- did the channel shift after its first observation?
- did an older drainage cut already exist nearby?
- does a historic local name refer to a broader area?

No hidden `true_name` is required until an authority chooses one for its own records.

## Situation — The Cliff Photograph Is Real

An old photograph appears to show a path continuing around a coastal cliff where no path exists today.

Possible explanation space:

- shoreline/cliff-edge change;
- slope failure recorded elsewhere;
- perspective confusion;
- a retired engineered walkway;
- different route naming;
- photograph metadata error.

The investigation should cross-link Slope Instability, Cartography and Public Works rather than assuming erosion from appearance alone.

## Mystery — Six Shorelines, One Afternoon

Evidence set:

- a harbor worker's photograph;
- a tourist's photo from another sector;
- a ranger observation;
- a public webcam frame;
- a ferry passenger's description;
- a map screenshot.

They appear to show six different “shorelines” on the same afternoon.

Possible resolution:

They observed different sectors, different features and different water conditions. Some record visible water edge; another records dune/backshore position; another uses an old map layer.

The mystery teaches scope and evidence type without a villain.

## Mystery — Four Reopenings, Three Access Points

Four notices announce that “beach access has reopened” on different dates.

Investigation finds separate meanings:

- service/emergency access reopened;
- one footpath reopened;
- temporary public walkway opened;
- original public stairs reopened later.

No notice is necessarily false. Each applies to another access object.

## Mystery — The Missing Marker

A shoreline observation marker seen in older photographs is gone.

Possible explanations:

- removed by maintenance;
- buried by deposition;
- lost during an event;
- replaced under a new identifier;
- photograph mislocated;
- moved to preserve the monitoring line.

The quest can remain low-stakes science/history while adding durable world provenance.

## Exploration — The Line Behind the Dunes

Several decades of maps and photographs show a beach edge and access path moving relative to buildings that still exist.

Current executable version:

- begin from stable building/road landmarks;
- compare dated map editions;
- find old access furniture, signs or foundations;
- interview residents and workers;
- photograph current conditions from historical viewpoints;
- note Pokémon behavior without causal inference;
- use only already-verified paths;
- keep unstable or unknown coastal sectors outside accessible geometry.

Future rich version may add dynamic tide windows, surf, unstable sand or cliff edges only after exact mechanical support exists.

## Exploration — The Buried Promenade

An older promenade or service edge survives in fragments behind or beneath later sediment, vegetation and rebuilt public space.

The objective is historical reconstruction rather than treasure extraction.

Possible discoveries:

- a former business entrance now inland or buried;
- old route signs facing the wrong direction;
- a maintenance hatch associated with retired infrastructure;
- photographs showing a once-open view now blocked by new terrain/vegetation;
- a Pokémon routine tied to a surviving sheltered corner.

All excavation, custody and structural work require owner-system approval.

## Longer arc — A Town Learns Its Shoreline

Phase 1: Ordinary coast

Establish familiar routes, cafés, public beach access, harbor workers, tourism habits, wildlife observations, family photographs and one or two recurring Pokémon.

Phase 2: Small discrepancy

A familiar marker, path edge or beach shape no longer matches a recent map. The first story can remain mundane and investigative.

Phase 3: Documented change

A weather/marine/slope or other authored event leads to new observations. Different sectors change differently. Downstream systems issue their own decisions.

Phase 4: Temporary life

Alternate access, new service points, observation posts and detours create new social routines.

Phase 5: Recovery without reset

Some functions return quickly. Others move permanently. Maps update at different times. Habitat interpretation continues.

Phase 6: Historical reuse

Months or years later, a newly exposed object, another modest change or an old witness statement makes the accumulated record relevant again.

The arc has no `coast_recovery_level`. Each shoreline, route, business, habitat and institution keeps its own state.

## Encounter — Newly Exposed Structure Perimeter

Full premise:

A newly exposed object draws competing actors before custody/safety review finishes.

Full version can require protect/hold/withdraw objectives, interactables, changing access, reactions, forced movement, environmental zones and tactical AI.

Reduced version:

Freeze the object's custody state outside combat. Keep it outside the tactical grid. Resolve a normal static battle nearby. The result does not transfer ownership, prove identity or authorize excavation.

## Encounter — Former Beach Road Diversion

Full premise:

A changed shoreline has altered a minor path/road approach and conflict occurs around the temporary route.

Full version can require moving noncombatants, route objectives, changing restricted cells, reactions, environmental zones and objective-aware policy.

Reduced version:

Road/Travel chooses the legal detour first. All noncombatants remain off-grid. Battle occurs at one static verified junction. The coast remains narrative world state during combat.

## Implementation rule

No coastal proposal may cause the Minecraft adapter to invent missing PTU rules.

If the full version needs surf displacement, overwash, collapsing edges, soft-sand movement, spray visibility, water-depth changes, delayed environmental phases, reactions or objective-aware AI, the dependency remains explicit.

The reduced version must preserve the narrative premise using static reviewed geometry and owner-system world-state consequences.
