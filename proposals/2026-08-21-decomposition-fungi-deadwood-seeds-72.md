# Pass 72 Ouros Seeds — Decomposition, Fungi, Deadwood & Nutrient Cycling

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from research patterns, not adaptations of any single external story.

## 1. The Fallen Giant

A tree that fell before the campaign begins becomes a persistent landmark. Over several years its decay state, cavities, fungal fruiting and wildlife use change, giving old players an environmental callback without changing the location's identity.

## 2. The Mushroom Year

A wet season produces an unusually large fruiting event across several forest sites. The visible mushrooms are real; the cause of the exceptional year remains a research question rather than an automatic supernatural event.

## 3. Missing Fruit Bodies

A fungus that normally fruits at a known time produces nothing visible this year. The system records the absence as an observation, not proof that the organism disappeared.

## 4. Trail Versus Habitat

A rotten trunk blocks part of a popular trail but has become shelter for multiple wild Pokémon. Reroute, partial removal and retention are all plausible options.

## 5. The Hollow Winter

A standing snag is repeatedly used during cold weather. A safety inspection later questions whether it can remain beside a public route.

## 6. Three Logs, Three Rates

Three trees fell in the same storm but decay very differently. Researchers compare shade, moisture, contact with soil and decomposer evidence instead of assuming an error.

## 7. The Clean Forest Debate

Residents want storm debris removed because the forest looks neglected. Ecological surveys show that some pieces are already important habitat. The dispute concerns management values, not heroes versus villains.

## 8. Orchard Pruning Pile

An orchard's annual pruning pile becomes a repeated decomposer study site. The project remains agricultural world state until an actual PTU item or encounter is created.

## 9. Old Bridge Timber

Timbers removed during bridge reconstruction are stacked nearby. Years later they have ecological, historical and material value at the same time, forcing a decision about reuse versus retention.

## 10. The Wrong Diagnosis

A declining tree has visible mushrooms and is immediately blamed on fungal disease. Later evidence shows the tree was already stressed by another factor; the fungal role remains uncertain.

## 11. Colony Under the Ferns

A persistent Toedscruel colony is discovered in a part of the forest that visitors rarely enter. Its territory and observed behavior are tracked without turning the colony into an automatic combat encounter.

## 12. Caps After Rain

Morelull caps are repeatedly observed after wet nights and are eaten by local Pokémon. Harvest pressure from visitors creates a management question, but no Food Item mechanics exist until rules validate them.

## 13. The Spore Scare

A public rumor says a mushroom bloom is putting travelers to sleep. Investigators must separate actual species observations, reported symptoms and PTU-capable Pokémon from ordinary fungi.

## 14. The Nurse Log Survey

Seedlings repeatedly establish beside one decaying trunk. Scientists investigate moisture, substrate and shelter conditions without declaring a single causal mechanism too early.

## 15. After the Burn

A wildfire left standing dead trees and down wood. Several years later different patches show different decomposition, cavities and wildlife use, connecting Pass 64 recovery state with Pass 72.

## 16. The Storm-Fall Maze

A windstorm creates a tangle of downed wood. Travel initially reroutes around it. Months later parts are cleared while others become intentional deadwood habitat.

## 17. Timber Yard Succession

An abandoned timber yard gradually shifts from industrial residue to ecological site. Some old stacks retain provenance that matters to the town's history.

## 18. The Cavity Census

A long-term survey records which dead trees develop cavities and which species use them. The project can run quietly for years and later influence conservation decisions.

## 19. Fungi Without a Name

Researchers can document morphology, substrate and season for a recurring fungal occurrence before anyone confidently identifies it.

## 20. Two Honest Models

Two research teams agree on all observations but disagree about what controls decay rate at a restoration site. Both interpretations remain active until more evidence arrives.

## 21. Compost Is Not Magic

A community garden begins using locally produced compost. Soil and plant response are monitored as observations; the game never grants automatic yield or healing bonuses.

## 22. River Log Relocation

A flood carries a large dead tree downstream into a new reach. The same persistent object now affects freshwater geometry, habitat and navigation at a different location.

## 23. The Removed Snag

A hazardous standing dead tree is removed beside a school path. Later surveys show that some wildlife shifted elsewhere. The removal was still reasonable given the evidence available at the time.

## 24. Mushroom Tourism

A spectacular seasonal bloom becomes locally famous after photographs spread online. Visitor pressure becomes more consequential than the fungi themselves.

## 25. The Quiet Decomposer Plot

A small research plot is intentionally left untouched for years. Its main payoff is longitudinal evidence and callbacks rather than rewards every season.

## 26. The Workshop Transfer

A storm-fallen tree is partly retained on site and partly transferred to a workshop. Both portions preserve shared provenance but develop separate histories.

## 27. The Forest Floor Archive

Researchers compare decades of photographs and samples to reconstruct how deadwood abundance changed after logging, storms and restoration.

## 28. Root Association

A restoration team finds strong evidence of fungal-root association around some plantings but not others. The finding affects research priorities, not communication or psychic mechanics.

## 29. The Misplaced Marker

An old research tag appears on the wrong decaying log after a flood rearranges the site. Correcting identity becomes a provenance problem rather than a simple catalog edit.

## 30. Shelter After Release

A previously partnered Pokémon is observed using a decaying log as shelter after release. The observation joins that Pokémon's persistent history without assigning motive or implying renewed ownership.

# Long Arc A — The Fallen Giant Years

Year 1: a major tree falls and blocks a route.

Year 2: the route is partially rerouted; researchers establish baseline observations.

Year 3: fungal fruiting and cavity formation become noticeable.

Year 4: several wild Pokémon repeatedly use the site.

Year 5: a safety concern forces a new management review.

Year 6+: parts of the object become integrated into soil while its location remains a remembered landmark.

The arc has no required villain and does not need a boss encounter. Its payoff is persistent transformation that older characters can recognize.

# Long Arc B — After the Logging Road

A former extraction road contains compacted soil, leftover wood, regrowing vegetation and altered drainage. Over years, players can contribute surveys, rerouting, selective deadwood retention, restoration and historical documentation. Different segments can recover differently without the system forcing one ideal endpoint.

# Long Arc C — The Mushroom Year Archive

An exceptional fruiting season triggers photography, public interest and scientific sampling. Later seasons are quieter. The archive becomes valuable precisely because the original event is not repeated on demand. Years afterward, a new bloom can be compared against the first rather than treated as a fresh procedural quest.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Rot Log Passage

FULL intent:
A decaying trunk is both route obstacle and habitat. Wildlife can retreat through gaps; unstable sections may change pathing; any actual spore/status interaction must come from a verified Move, Ability or hazard.

Required permanent capability families:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement incl. push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL if structural/environmental damage is introduced
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL if a legal status effect is invoked
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

REDUCED version:
Resolve the management decision before battle. Freeze the log as static legal geometry. Keep habitat occupants not participating in combat outside the grid. No collapse or ambient spores occur.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Hollow Trunk Refuge

FULL intent:
Wild Pokémon are using a hollow snag as temporary refuge while another disturbance approaches. The tactical problem is to preserve an exit corridor rather than KO every wild Pokémon.

Additional blockers:
- objective semantics such as PROTECT_ROUTE / WITHDRAW — BLOCKING outside current verified battle scope;
- autonomous retreat/protection policy — AI tactical policy BLOCKING;
- changing refuge geometry — terrain/hazards/zones BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:
Resolve refuge occupants and evacuation as overworld state. If hostile combatants create a fight, run it on a fixed nearby map with the refuge and noncombatants outside the grid.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Fruiting Survey

FULL intent:
Researchers need to survey an unusual fruiting event before a large visitor group enters the site. Wild Pokémon can move through the area and some specific Pokémon may legally possess spore-related Moves or Abilities.

Mechanical rule:
Ordinary mushrooms never create Sleep, Poisoned, Paralysis, visibility penalties or tactical zones by description alone.

REDUCED version:
Survey, samples, visitor timing and fungal observations remain overworld state. Any battle uses only legal combatants and their actual PTU sheets on a static arena.
