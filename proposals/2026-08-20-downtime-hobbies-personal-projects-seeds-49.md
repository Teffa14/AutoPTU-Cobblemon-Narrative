# Ouros Downtime, Hobbies & Personal Projects Seeds — Pass 49

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from research patterns. No external plot, character or prose is transplanted.

Date: 2026-08-20

## Design intent

These seeds create reasons to care about ordinary places, recurring habits and personal projects between major arcs. They should remain optional, compress repetition and avoid turning every hobby into a profession, competition or stat progression track.

## 1. The Empty Bench at Dawn

A resident has used the same riverside bench most mornings for months. One morning they are absent.

The system records only the deviation. Possible explanations remain unresolved until evidence appears: changed shift, rain, travel, new routine, private appointment or something more serious.

Useful connections: routines, workplaces, weather, cases, communications.

## 2. Sketchbook of One Street

A character chooses one ordinary street and sketches it repeatedly over a year.

The project slowly records shop changes, seasonal decorations, recurring Pokémon, construction and residents. The completed sketchbook can later function as personal memory and secondary historical evidence without becoming an authoritative map.

## 3. The Long Bicycle Repair

An old bicycle has sentimental provenance but no special battle properties. Repair requires several ordinary milestones: inspect the frame, locate compatible parts, complete workshop work and test the result.

Supply interruptions or an artisan's schedule may delay progress without creating a villain.

## 4. The Corner Card Table

Several residents regularly play cards outside a small shop. Participation changes naturally as work shifts, travel and age alter their routines.

Players may join. The table can become a recurring social landmark without becoming a formal club or gambling system.

## 5. Favorite Warm Stone

A known Pokémon repeatedly chooses the same sun-warmed stone during quiet afternoons.

The system records the repeated location choice. It does not label the place emotionally, infer ownership or grant Friendship.

A later construction project can make the behavior relevant because the stone lies inside the proposed work zone.

## 6. The Community Puzzle Shelf

A café keeps a shelf of physical puzzles and games donated by customers. People borrow and return them informally.

Over time the shelf gains provenance, missing pieces, handwritten notes and recurring users. No puzzle provides mechanical bonuses.

## 7. Lantern Repainting

A neighborhood repaints old festival lanterns in small batches throughout the year instead of preparing everything immediately before the festival.

Participation is voluntary. The gradual project can show who contributes and which old lanterns carry historical significance.

## 8. The Balcony Garden

A resident keeps a tiny noncommercial garden at home.

The garden can change appearance over time and attract observed Pokémon, but it does not use farm-economy yields unless explicitly connected to the agriculture subsystem.

## 9. Postcards From Places Already Visited

A player starts collecting postcards only after visiting the represented places.

The collection becomes a travel memory object. Old editions can preserve locations or businesses that later changed.

## 10. Rain Changes the Plan

A character with a regular outdoor hobby chooses an indoor alternative during bad weather.

The purpose is to make routine flexible. Missing a scheduled leisure activity never creates a punishment meter.

## 11. The Closed Footpath

A familiar walking route closes for repairs. The player can accept the detour, investigate why the work is taking longer, or simply change their routine.

The closure remains owned by travel/public-works state rather than becoming a mandatory quest.

## 12. The Unfinished Scarf

An NPC has worked on the same handmade gift for weeks. The object changes phase gradually.

If the recipient changes location or the relationship changes, the creator may pause, repurpose or finish it. The system does not infer why without authored evidence.

## 13. Scrapbook From Before the Journey

A retired traveler keeps a scrapbook of tickets, photos and notes from older trips.

The scrapbook contains personal interpretation, not guaranteed canonical truth. It may point to outdated routes or places whose current state must be checked independently.

## 14. Shared Star-Watching Night

Several people occasionally gather on a hill because it has a clear sky and easy access.

No formal event organizer exists. Over time the gathering may develop recurring habits, equipment caches or informal etiquette. It becomes a formal institution only if participants choose to create one.

## 15. Personal Map Annotations

A player maintains private annotations on a regional map: favorite viewpoints, quiet rest places, reliable shade, memorable encounters and personal route notes.

Cartography owns geographic accuracy. Downtime owns the personal curation project.

## 16. Amateur Fossil Sketches

A hobbyist visits museum displays and sketches fossils from public viewpoints.

They do not acquire fossils, gain excavation authority or become a paleontologist through repetition. Their drawings may later reveal how an exhibit changed over time.

## 17. Recipe Notebook Without Mastery XP

A character records local recipes encountered during travel.

Food/crafting layers own actual recipes and mechanical effects. The notebook tracks provenance, variations and personal notes without granting Chef progression.

## 18. The Quiet Fishing Spot

A small shoreline is popular with casual fishers and sketchers because it is normally calm.

If ecology changes, the absence or appearance of Pokémon becomes observable only because a baseline exists. No fishing roll or encounter table is invented by this layer.

## 19. Reading Circle Without a Club Charter

Three residents often read at the same library table and sometimes discuss books.

They remain an informal recurring group. If they later request space, recruit members or establish rules, the social/clubs layer can promote the state.

## 20. Room-by-Room Decoration

A player gradually personalizes a room through several approved objects rather than a single “upgrade”.

Minecraft presentation can change visually while housing retains authority over the room and item provenance remains intact.

## 21. The Photo Album With Missing Months

A resident maintains a neighborhood photo album but stopped adding entries for several months.

The gap is a fact, not proof of tragedy. Work, travel, equipment failure or simple loss of interest are all possible until evidence resolves it.

## 22. Former Partner at the Hobby Site

A released former partner Pokémon is later observed near a place the Trainer frequently visits.

The system does not infer that the Pokémon came to see the Trainer. Ecological range, food, shelter or coincidence remain possible. Pokémon Agency owns any future interaction.

## 23. The New Work Shift

An NPC disappears from an old evening routine after accepting different workplace hours.

This seed exists to reward correct causal reasoning: the deviation has an ordinary explanation and can still change who now occupies the shared social space.

## 24. The Neighborhood Display Shelf

Residents begin leaving lawful, low-value souvenirs and small craft pieces on a protected public shelf.

Over time it becomes a tiny community tradition. Custody, permissions and preservation state remain explicit so the shelf never becomes free loot.

## 25. The Half-Finished Model Lighthouse

A resident builds a scale model of a lighthouse over many months. Historical maps, photographs and current maintenance records disagree on some details.

Finishing the model can become an investigation into architectural change without requiring a crisis.

## 26. The Monthly Walk That Changes Meaning

A group takes the same short route once a month. Seasons, construction, migration and changing participants alter the context.

The system tracks concrete changes and shared events. It does not assign nostalgia or friendship unless authored.

## 27. A Hobby Passed On

An NPC wants to give an unfinished personal project to another person who has shown interest.

Transfer requires acceptance. The recipient can continue, alter, archive or decline it. A handoff does not create mentorship automatically.

## 28. Pokémon Play Space

A settlement maintains a voluntary open area where partnered Pokémon can spend supervised free time.

Observed interactions can feed Pokémon Agency and social evidence. The area does not grant Friendship, XP or recovery bonuses without governing mechanics.

## 29. The Box of Unsorted Keepsakes

A household has accumulated ordinary tickets, photographs, programs and small objects over decades.

Sorting them can become a personal archive project. Some items may connect to existing public-memory records; others remain meaningful only to the household.

## 30. The Hobby Nobody Monetizes

A highly skilled NPC makes beautiful miniature buildings for enjoyment. Several people suggest selling them, but the NPC explicitly declines.

The world respects that choice. Skill and recognition do not force a hobby into the economy layer.

# Long Arc A — A Year of Small Things

A settlement is followed across one full regional cycle through recurring personal projects rather than one central villain.

Early phase:

- several NPCs and players begin optional projects;
- routines establish baselines;
- seasonal sites become familiar;
- small personal objects gain provenance.

Middle phase:

- weather, work schedules, migrations and public events interrupt some routines;
- a few projects pause or change direction;
- characters learn which activities matter enough to resume;
- quiet Chronicle facts begin producing callbacks.

Late phase:

- several projects reach milestones near the same seasonal transition;
- the town looks physically different because of accumulated small changes;
- players can review a year of personal history without the system declaring a universal emotional conclusion.

The arc succeeds if the place feels lived-in even if no boss appears.

# Long Arc B — The Corner Table

An informal gathering at one shop evolves over several years.

Phase 1:

A few workers play games after shifts.

Phase 2:

A traveler joins temporarily. One regular changes jobs. Another teaches the rules to a newcomer.

Phase 3:

The shop changes ownership. The table is moved during renovation. Participants decide whether to keep meeting.

Phase 4:

The gathering may remain informal, become a club, migrate to another venue or end naturally.

Nothing forces institutionalization. Its history remains available as a local social thread and memory anchor.

# Long Arc C — The Long Repair

A personally important but mechanically ordinary object is restored over a long period.

The project interacts with:

- workshop capacity;
- material provenance;
- transport delays;
- seasonal weather;
- old photographs;
- a retired artisan's knowledge;
- budgeting if the owner chooses paid work;
- housing/display state after completion.

The final result has historical and personal provenance. It receives no invented PTU bonus.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Picnic Site Disturbance

Narrative premise:

A recurring picnic site overlaps with a recently shifted wild-Pokémon route.

Full version:

- visible wild subgroup moves through the area;
- encounter can support withdrawal, route-clearing or protect-space objectives if authoritative systems exist;
- environmental zones and picnic objects can affect navigation only when rules validate them;
- wild AI can prefer escape or passage instead of defeat-all combat.

Exact dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement if interception/forced displacement is used;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle for phase-sensitive versions;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if the site has tactical environmental state;
- move-specific behavior, abilities, items and Trainer Features/perks only when a specific legal mechanic is selected;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for withdrawal/protection/objective behavior;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Resolve the migration conflict in overworld/wild-collective state. Remove picnic objects from battle authority. If combat occurs, use a static legal arena and ordinary supported battle resolution, then write the result back to route and routine state.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Evening Walk Chokepoint

Narrative premise:

A character's habitual walk intersects an obstructed route and an unrelated battle-capable threat.

Full version:

- dynamic route blockers;
- alternative exits;
- interception if actors attempt to pass;
- scenario-aware AI;
- possible REACH_EXIT/WITHDRAW objective.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement for the intended version;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle when timed;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Choose the detour outside battle. If conflict cannot be avoided, instantiate a conventional static encounter. The routine record simply notes that the usual route changed.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Shoreline Hobby Night

Narrative premise:

A small recurring gathering near the water observes unusual Pokémon activity.

Full version:

- shoreline land/water geometry;
- optional current/weather state if PTU/Caelo validates it;
- autonomous retreat behavior;
- investigation-to-battle handoff;
- objective other than defeat-all where supported.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality including ordinary supported Swim movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle when timed effects matter;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for currents, weather or dynamic shoreline state;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for retreat/objectives;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Observation and leisure remain overworld state. Do not invent fishing mechanics. If a legal battle occurs, use static supported land/Swim geometry and normal battle resolution.
