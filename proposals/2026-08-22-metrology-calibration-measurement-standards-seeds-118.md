# Proposed Ouros Seeds — Metrology, Calibration & Measurement Standards — Pass 118

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Requires review before promotion.

These concepts use `design/metrology-calibration-measurement-standards-layer.md`. They do not create PTU/Caelo bonuses, Skills, DCs, equipment effects or canonical regional standards.

## 30 worldbuilding and quest candidates

### 1. Three Gauges, Three Rivers

Three gauges in the same catchment appear to disagree after a storm. All three are functioning. The discrepancy comes from the fact that the river changed differently at each site. The investigation teaches players not to search for one broken device when the measured world is spatially variable.

### 2. The Benchmark Under the New Wall

A century-old survey mark still exists, but a later retaining wall was built around it. Modern crews can no longer access it directly. Old maps remain useful while a new reference network must be established without pretending the old mark never existed.

### 3. The Instrument That Was Right Yesterday

A portable sensor passes its scheduled check, is dropped during transport and begins producing a small bias the next morning. The calibration record remains correct for the moment it was issued.

### 4. The Famous Wrong Number

A museum display repeats a measurement from a pioneering expedition. The number was correctly transcribed but used an old local reference convention. The public story is culturally important even after a modern conversion is published.

### 5. Four Sensors Agree

Four independently maintained instruments agree on an anomaly while the official reference station disagrees. The reference station, not the field sensors, becomes the subject of the investigation.

### 6. The Sensor That Attracts What It Measures

A wildlife monitoring instrument emits a faint sound/light that changes local Pokémon behavior. The dataset is real but method-affected. Research Ethics and Sound/Light layers become relevant before continuing the study.

### 7. Calibration Caravan

A traveling technical team carries reference equipment through settlements that cannot maintain their own laboratory. Their seasonal route becomes an institutional tradition and a natural source of small travel stories.

### 8. The Cold-Morning Bias

One weather station reads low only during a narrow dawn window. The cause could be mounting, local shade, condensation, electronics or genuine microclimate. Repeated comparison is more useful than a single repair quest.

### 9. The New Standard Arrives Late

A regional standards revision becomes official, but several remote institutions continue using the previous version for months because updated references have not arrived. Both datasets remain internally interpretable if their version is recorded.

### 10. The Map That Shifted North

A new survey reference makes old coordinates appear offset. Nothing physically moved. The story becomes one of Cartography, Archives and institutional communication rather than anomalous geography.

### 11. The Missing Reference Weight

A small reference artefact disappears from a workshop. The production line pauses certain inspections. The eventual explanation may be misfiled custody, legitimate loan, accident or theft; Cases only activates if evidence warrants it.

### 12. The Factory With One Bad Station

Two inspection benches accept a production lot while a third rejects it. Manufacturing puts the lot on hold. Metrology determines whether the discrepancy belongs to the product, the instrument, the fixture, the method or the environment.

### 13. The Spring Fell Six Centimeters

A spring gauge reports a sharp decline. A later survey discovers that the local reference marker itself subsided. Groundwater still needs independent evidence before anyone concludes the aquifer recovered or declined.

### 14. The Observatory Clock Problem

Two observatories record the same transient event at slightly different times. The mystery concerns time-reference history and software updates rather than the sky event itself.

### 15. The Old Mine Uses Its Own North

A historical mine map uses a local orientation convention established before regional surveying. Modern rescue teams must reconstruct that convention before trusting headings underground.

### 16. The Technician’s Notebook

An experienced technician kept informal comparison notes for decades. The notes are not an official standard, but they reveal exactly when a long-lived sensor began drifting.

### 17. The Measurement That Became Coarser

An instrument is later found outside its precise specification. Review concludes that old measurements cannot support fine-grained analysis but remain valid for broad seasonal trends. The dataset loses precision without becoming worthless.

### 18. The Broken Seal That Proves Nothing

A calibration case arrives with damaged packaging. The reference equipment itself may still be fine. Supply Chains handles custody/condition; Metrology handles the comparison result.

### 19. The Pokémon Beside the Magnetometer

A group of Magnemite appears regularly near a magnetic station. Some staff immediately blame them for anomalies. Historical data show the anomaly predates their arrival. Their presence remains a real observation without becoming causal proof.

### 20. Rotom Likes One Gauge

A Rotom repeatedly enters a specific piece of equipment. The staff learn how to detect and record those visits. The behavior creates a maintenance/calibration question without giving Rotom universal control over the network.

### 21. The River Mark Everyone Trusts

Residents use a painted wall mark as a flood reference. The formal gauge is newer and more precise, but the community mark has a much longer historical memory. Both records become valuable for different questions.

### 22. The Competition of Methods

Two research groups measure the same ecological variable using different methods. Neither method is fraudulent; each has a different detection limit and disturbance profile. The campaign can let players contribute evidence without forcing a winner immediately.

### 23. The Reference Room Flood

A minor building flood affects the room where standards are stored. No battle is required. The work is triage: preserve records, inspect custody, determine which references need re-verification and keep unaffected services operating.

### 24. The Gauge That Became a Landmark

A retired river gauge remains physically present and becomes part of local memory. It no longer provides operational measurements, but its old datum and photographs help reconstruct historic floods.

### 25. The Wrong Unit on the Public Board

A public display converts a valid institutional measurement incorrectly. The raw observation and sensor are fine. Media/Communications issues a correction while the original display remains part of Chronicle.

### 26. The Calibration Due During the Crisis

A station reaches its planned check date during a regional emergency. The institution must choose whether to keep using it with explicit uncertainty, replace it, or rely on neighboring stations. No option is automatically irresponsible.

### 27. The Portable Lab That Never Returned

A field calibration kit was loaned to a remote expedition and its return record is incomplete. Years later it appears in an archive facility with several undocumented comparison notes attached.

### 28. The Survey Marker Moved by a Pokémon

A persistent Pokémon-built structure or burrowing event shifts a physical benchmark. The Pokémon did something observable; that does not make it malicious or make every survey after the event wrong.

### 29. The Two Regions’ Temperature Records

Neighboring regions kept long climate records under different historical conventions. Researchers need an explicit conversion/uncertainty project before merging them into one regional series.

### 30. The Dataset That Improved After Failure

A failed instrument comparison exposes weak provenance throughout a monitoring program. The institution responds by improving records, reference checks and archive practices. The failure produces durable competence rather than only a culprit.

## Long arc A — Five Years of Drift

Year 1: a network of environmental stations appears consistent.

Year 2: one site develops a small discrepancy that is dismissed as local variability.

Year 3: comparison campaigns show that two device families drift differently under specific conditions.

Year 4: old datasets are reviewed. Some records remain fully usable, some need correction, and some support only coarse conclusions.

Year 5: the region has a stronger reference network, improved technician training and a transparent archive of corrections.

The arc works without sabotage or villainy. The antagonist, if any, is premature certainty.

## Long arc B — The Meridian Standard

Several settlements historically use local survey references. Rail, water, cartography and construction expansion make cross-regional comparability increasingly important.

The arc can involve:

- old benchmarks;
- mapping expeditions;
- institutional negotiations;
- archive research;
- traveling reference standards;
- communities worried that adopting a shared reference will erase their older records;
- a final standardization that preserves conversion history rather than declaring older systems “wrong.”

This arc connects Cartography, Railways, Water Service, Architecture, Archives and Interregional Recognition.

## Long arc C — The Archive of Numbers

A research archive contains a century of measurements from changing instruments and standards. A modern anomaly forces researchers to re-read the old record.

Progression:

- identify which instruments produced which records;
- reconstruct calibration/reference history;
- distinguish genuine environmental changes from changes in method;
- discover a few old corrections that were never propagated to public summaries;
- publish a revised historical series that preserves the raw archive.

The resolution should make the past more legible, not rewrite it.

## Hard design restrictions

Do not add:

- generic Technology Education bonuses for calibration;
- calibration Skill DCs without PTU/Caelo source text;
- Accuracy/range bonuses from scopes or measuring tools;
- initiative bonuses from clocks;
- automatic Weather/Terrain from monitoring instruments;
- instrument HP or battle destruction rules;
- Pokémon-based “perfect sensors” without authored and validated mechanics;
- false-data villains as the default explanation for discrepancies;
- automatic criminal cases from an out-of-tolerance result;
- silent rewriting of old measurements after a new calibration/reference revision.

These proposals remain candidates until canon and mechanics review.
