# Crisis, Rescue & Recovery Seeds — Pass 12

Status: NON-CANON fictional Ouros candidates for game design. These do not establish real emergency procedures, PTU mechanics, locations or institutions.

## 1. Conflicting Forecasts

A settlement receives different predictions about an approaching fictional weather event. Players can verify routes, inspect vulnerable services and decide which preparations deserve limited attention. Later public trust should remember reasoning and evidence, not only whether a prediction happened to be correct.

## 2. The Gym Shelter

A Gym suspends challenges and temporarily becomes a community support hub because its building and location are useful. Adventures can involve supplies, communication, Pokémon accommodation and reopening the normal Gym schedule. The Gym Leader gains no automatic legal authority from this role.

## 3. Two Failed Routes

A landslide closes the primary road. The obvious detour later becomes unavailable for an unrelated reason. Players must update route knowledge, scout alternatives and help reconnect settlements rather than discover one permanently correct path.

## 4. Missing Traveler

A traveler does not arrive after a regional disturbance. World state stores last verified location, intended route and known companions, but does not invent injury or survival status. Investigation narrows the search through evidence.

## 5. The Empty Nesting Ground

Before a storm reaches the coast, a normally occupied Pokémon nesting area becomes unexpectedly empty. Possible explanations include seasonal movement, another species, human disturbance or coincidence. Investigation can improve ecological knowledge without turning Pokémon behavior into supernatural prediction.

## 6. Firebreak Choices

A fictional wildfire threatens several different assets. Players may support route access, protect a settlement edge, help relocate wild Pokémon or secure communications. Exact fire behavior and every PTU interaction remain undefined until mechanical review.

## 7. Cascading Blackout

A power outage reveals dependencies. A workshop stops, transport signaling fails and a communications node loses backup capacity. Players can repair the original source or create temporary workarounds for selected services.

## 8. Outdated Map

A public emergency map is old. One bridge changed, a construction project blocks another path and a previously open area became protected habitat. Discovering the mismatch can lead to a permanent map-maintenance project after the incident.

## 9. Silent Relay

A remote communications relay stops reporting during severe weather. The initial problem is verification and restoration. Sabotage becomes a case only if evidence later supports it.

## 10. Suspended Ferry

A harbor transport service stops because conditions become unsafe. The familiar hub temporarily changes function: stranded travelers need information, cargo needs rerouting and local services experience unusual demand. No battle is required.

## 11. Success Creates a Second Problem

Players clear a blocked route successfully. Heavy traffic then crosses an area where wild Pokémon had taken temporary refuge. The ecological issue is recorded as a causal consequence of reopening the road rather than a punishment for helping.

## 12. After the Water Recedes

A previously flooded district becomes accessible. The immediate incident is finished, but recovery generates new content: damaged services, displaced Pokémon, lost records, rebuilding choices and habitat assessment.

## 13. Rebuild Somewhere Else

A beloved public facility is badly damaged. Residents disagree over whether to rebuild in place, relocate, preserve part of the site or replace it with a different service. Each position can have understandable motives.

## 14. Mutual Aid

A nearby settlement experiences a crisis while the player's current location remains stable. Local actors can contribute transport, supplies, specialists or temporary accommodation. Future relations can remember the actual contribution rather than applying a generic reputation bonus.

## 15. Familiar Cave, New Role

A cave normally used for exploration becomes a temporary refuge during dangerous weather. Its geography remains familiar while exits, NPC distribution, wild behavior and objectives change. Later visits can preserve traces of the event without permanently converting the cave into a disaster dungeon.

## 16. The Old Preparedness Cache

Months earlier, players helped establish a remote supply cache. A later incident makes that quiet-world action relevant. The cache may be well maintained, partly used, expanded by locals or difficult to reach because the route changed.

## 17. Repair Reveals Another Story

Post-crisis infrastructure work exposes an archaeological layer, old utility tunnel, hidden habitat or evidence relevant to another system. Recovery can therefore bridge into myth, ecology or investigation when existing world state supports the connection.

## 18. False Alarm, Real Consequences

A warning proves unnecessary. No major hazard occurs, but the event still reveals weak communications, outdated signs or poor access to a gathering point. Different NPCs update trust differently rather than receiving one universal modifier.

## 19. Tournament Interrupted

A public competition faces a credible hazard warning. Organizers can postpone, relocate or alter the event. Public-memory state later records what was decided and how people interpreted it, even if the danger never fully arrives.

## 20. Wildlife Corridor Conflict

A damaged human route diverts traffic into a path used by migrating Pokémon. The resulting jobs can focus on rerouting, observation, temporary separation or faster infrastructure repair rather than treating wildlife as enemies.

## 21. Harbor Inventory Confusion

A port survives an incident but cargo handling becomes disorganized. Missing goods can create logistics and provenance problems. The generator must not convert every mismatch into theft without evidence.

## 22. New Town Center

Temporary services cluster in a previously minor neighborhood during recovery. If the state persists long enough, vendors, transport and residents may gradually treat it as a new center of activity. The crisis changes urban geography through consequence rather than instant scripted rebuilding.

## 23. Local Knowledge Versus Official Data

Regional maps recommend one route while local residents know a seasonal terrain problem may affect it. Players can compare current observation, official information and lived experience. Neither source is automatically correct.

## 24. Response-Team Rivalry

Two fictional response groups develop different priorities: one emphasizes rapid infrastructure restoration, another ecological protection. Both can contribute and both can make errors. Their disagreement can become a long-running professional rivalry without requiring a villain.

## 25. Recovery Anniversary

Years later, a settlement marks a major past event through remembrance, preparedness activities, rebuilt infrastructure and stories from residents. Public memory may differ from the Chronicle's confirmed facts.

## 26. No Boss Required

A major crisis arc concludes through coordination, exploration, route restoration, information, Pokémon assistance, social choices and recovery rather than a final enemy. This seed exists to ensure Ouros can create memorable large-scale arcs whose climax is not automatically combat.

## Implementation boundary

Before use, every seed needs a defined fictional cause, location, affected systems, information model, world-state transitions, supported Minecraft presentation and explicit PTU/Caelo + AutoPTU validation for any movement, hazard, Skill, battle or Pokémon capability interaction.

These concepts do not authorize invented hazard damage, environmental status effects, carrying rules, medical mechanics, evacuation checks or new Pokémon powers.
