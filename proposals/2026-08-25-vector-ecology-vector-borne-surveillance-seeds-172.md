# Vector Ecology & Vector-Borne Surveillance Seeds — Pass 172

Status: NON-CANON / PROPOSED
Date: 2026-08-25

These concepts extend `design/vector-ecology-vector-borne-surveillance-protocol.md`. They do not establish a canonical pathogen, vector species, institution, transmission cycle or control method.

## Thirty original candidates

### 1. The Positive Pool, Quiet Clinic
A wetland trap line yields a pathogen-positive pooled sample, but local clinics report no matching health cluster. The useful mystery is why the evidence streams differ, not how quickly the party can find something to fight.

### 2. Cases Without Positive Traps
A neighborhood health investigation has several compatible cases while the vector programme repeatedly tests negative. Investigators must examine coverage, timing, trap selectivity, alternate exposure routes and the possibility that the initial vector hypothesis is wrong.

### 3. The Trap Upgrade Broke the Trend
A new trap design captures three times as many specimens as the old network. Public commentary calls it an explosion until an analyst notices that the method itself changed.

### 4. The Pool With Forty Specimens
A famous “positive vector” story spreads through the city, but the laboratory result came from one forty-specimen pool. No one can identify which individual carried the target.

### 5. The Species Everyone Blamed
A conspicuous Poison-type population lives beside the first reported cases and becomes the public suspect. Standardized surveillance eventually finds stronger evidence elsewhere, forcing institutions to correct their own communications.

### 6. The Lighted Station
A night transit stop reports many insect-like Pokémon after new lighting is installed. The increase in visibility and attraction is real; whether vector density changed remains unresolved.

### 7. The Empty Trap Was Broken
Three consecutive “zero” nights become part of a reassuring public dashboard. Maintenance later discovers the trap fan had failed on the first night.

### 8. The Warm Night Shift
A familiar local population appears active later into the season during a sequence of warm nights. Diel Activity and Climate can document timing shifts while Vector Surveillance asks whether monitoring windows still cover meaningful activity.

### 9. Two Labs, One Pool
Two laboratories produce different results from related subsamples. The case becomes about method revision, sample custody and uncertainty rather than fraud.

### 10. The Culvert Everyone Sprayed
A culvert is treated as the obvious source because it holds water. Follow-up shows the highest vector detections are associated with a different microhabitat, while the culvert intervention created unrelated ecological effects.

### 11. The Festival Bite Reports
A summer festival generates a huge spike in public bite reports. Standardized trapping changes little, suggesting observer density and exposure opportunity rose more than the underlying vector population.

### 12. The Night Workers Saw It First
Maintenance staff report a persistent biting problem weeks before daytime surveys detect anything. The institution learns that surveillance coverage has a work-shift bias.

### 13. The Pool Retested Ten Years Later
An archived pooled sample is tested with a later method and produces a new result. The original season is not rewritten; Chronicle gains a revised interpretation with explicit analytical provenance.

### 14. The Host Moved
A sudden cluster of host-contact observations appears farther north. Telemetry suggests the monitored hosts changed their route, while vector abundance may have remained stable.

### 15. The Vector Moved
The inverse case: host movements are stable, but standardized vector detections expand into a new area. Biosecurity and ecology must decide whether this represents spread, previously unsampled presence or another explanation.

### 16. The Rain Filled Everything
Stormwater creates hundreds of temporary water pockets after a severe storm. Surveillance expands, but the first week finds no meaningful vector increase; preparedness still matters even without a crisis.

### 17. The Control Worked on the Wrong Metric
Trap catches decline after an intervention, while pathogen positivity among the smaller catch does not. The programme cannot call the intervention a complete success from abundance alone.

### 18. The Control Changed the Habitat
An environmental modification reduces one target habitat but creates a different wet edge used by another species. The second species is not automatically a vector; the programme records the ecological handoff before making new claims.

### 19. The Public Trap Network
Residents volunteer to host simple monitoring devices. Community Science improves geographic coverage but reveals inconsistent servicing and duplicate reports that must be handled before aggregation.

### 20. The Sensitive Roost
Evidence suggests a possible transmission interface near a protected roost. Public-health usefulness and conservation-sensitive location privacy come into direct tension.

### 21. The Golbat Rumor
Several Golbat are seen feeding near an affected herd. Their feeding behavior is real, but the programme has no evidence of pathogen carriage or transmission; the rumor becomes a test of evidence discipline.

### 22. The Insect That Wasn't Involved
A visually insect-like Pokémon dominates public photos from the affected park. Standardized sampling and laboratory evidence repeatedly fail to connect it to the health signal.

### 23. The Wrong Season Baseline
A programme compares early spring detections against a midsummer baseline and declares an anomaly. Reanalysis corrects the comparison without altering any field record.

### 24. One Positive, Three Possible Routes
A confirmed host infection and a positive vector pool exist in the same district. Shared water, direct contact and vector exposure all remain plausible until further evidence resolves—or fails to resolve—the transmission route.

### 25. The Trap Avoidance Hypothesis
Counts decline around long-running trap stations but not at new stations. Researchers debate real population change, habitat shift, equipment drift and learned avoidance without promoting any explanation prematurely.

### 26. The Crossing Introduced Nothing
A transport hub begins enhanced vector surveillance after a neighboring region reports a concern. Months of good coverage find nothing unusual. The programme documents prevention work without manufacturing a smuggling or invasion plot.

### 27. The Rehabilitation Release Question
A rehabilitated wild Pokémon returns to an area with known vector activity. Care and Rehabilitation own the release outcome; Vector Surveillance records exposure opportunity without declaring infection.

### 28. The Intervention Nobody Needed
A dramatic control proposal reaches review after one alarming report. Better sampling shows the original signal was a combination of duplicate public reports and a selective trap, preventing a needless environmental intervention.

### 29. The Quiet Year
An entire surveillance season produces expected detections, no pathogen-positive samples and no health cluster. Years later it becomes the best pre-change baseline in the region.

### 30. The Missing Link Stays Missing
A multi-year investigation accumulates vector detections, occasional pathogen-positive pools and sporadic health cases but never obtains enough evidence to establish the exact local transmission chain. `UNRESOLVED` remains an acceptable endpoint.

## Longer-term arcs

### Five Summers at Reedglass Marsh
Year one establishes a trap network and weak baseline. Year two changes access after flooding. Year three produces the first positive pooled sample without matching health cases. Year four adds a method revision and community observations that improve coverage while breaking naive comparability. Year five finally shows that several apparent “changes” came from different combinations of ecology, method and exposure opportunity. The Marsh becomes a long-lived research landscape rather than an outbreak vending machine.

### The Missing Link
A clinic cluster, a positive pooled sample and host-contact observations all appear within the same broad season. Investigators repeatedly improve the evidence graph but cannot prove that these events belong to one transmission chain. Later years may strengthen, weaken or reject the hypothesis. The story works even if no final culprit is discovered.

### The Trap Network Learns the City
A small urban surveillance programme begins around markets and parks, then expands toward night-shift workplaces, stormwater corridors, transit hubs and underserved districts. Each expansion reveals previous coverage bias. Over time the institution becomes better at saying “we do not know” and better at preventing unnecessary interventions.

Full version: a research team must recover time-sensitive trap/sample equipment while a wild migration crosses the same wetland corridor. The intended scene has non-hostile movement objectives, protected areas, possible water or weather constraints and a threat that may be unrelated to the migrating Pokémon.

### Research Station Screen Failure During Peak Activity

Full version: a damaged physical exclusion screen or access barrier exposes an indoor research area during a high-activity observation window. Staff must secure samples and leave safely while the underlying cause remains uncertain.

Dependencies: complete movement BLOCKING for evacuation; AI tactical policy BLOCKING for `EVACUATE`, `REACH_EXIT`, `PROTECT_SAMPLE`; adapter/playback BLOCKING. Terrain/hazards becomes BLOCKING only if broken structures or environmental exposure changes battle legality. Any exact Move/Ability/Status required remains under its PARTIAL family.

Reduced version: Architecture closes the room and Vector Surveillance marks the sampling window compromised. Staff evacuate before combat. Any later battle occurs in a stable adjacent area and cannot establish infection or repair the screen.

### Two Positive Pools, No Cases

Non-combat investigation. Vector Surveillance, Outbreak/Health, Metrology, Timekeeping and Science compare sample provenance, coverage, test revisions and health-surveillance data. Valid endpoints include `SUPPORTED`, `WEAKENED`, `REJECTED` or `UNRESOLVED`; no battle capability is required.

## Reusable NPC / institution ideas

A field entomology-equivalent specialist who is excellent at designing surveys but refuses to interpret clinical cases outside their mandate.

A clinic analyst who keeps reminding the vector team that a positive pool is evidence about the sample, not a diagnosis of the neighborhood.

A night-shift maintenance worker whose observations reveal a systematic daytime sampling bias.

A public-information officer who learns to publish coverage gaps and uncertainty instead of converting every detection into an alarm.

A conservation steward who supports health monitoring but challenges proposals that would expose sensitive roost or nesting coordinates.

A laboratory team whose reputation improves because it documents inconclusive and invalid results rather than forcing every test into positive/negative.

## Canon guardrail
