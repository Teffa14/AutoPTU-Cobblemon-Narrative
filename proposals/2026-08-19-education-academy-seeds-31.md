# Ouros Education & Academy Seeds — Pass 31

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original structures derived from research patterns. Nothing here establishes schools, age rules, credentials, tuition, PTU progression or legal authority.

## 1. The First Field Week

A new intake spends its first week learning the region through short supervised assignments rather than remaining on campus.

Possible activities:

- map a safe route;
- interview a local worker;
- observe a wild population;
- visit a clinic or workshop;
- document one public-service dependency;
- complete a supervised legal battle.

The week ends with a portfolio, not a single final exam.

## 2. The Course Nobody Can Run

A popular field module is suspended because its only qualified supervisor has been reassigned to crisis response.

The institution must decide whether to postpone it, bring in a guest, reduce scope or create an equivalent project.

No villain is required.

## 3. Transfer Credit Dispute

An exchange student completed equivalent work elsewhere, but the host academy's records use different evidence standards.

The conflict is about documentation and institutional trust, not whether the student is objectively competent.

## 4. The Practicum That Changes the Route

Students surveying an old path discover that public route information is outdated.

Their assignment can update the travel layer and later affect transport, conservation and settlement decisions.

## 5. Guest Instructor Week

A working specialist visits for several days.

The guest may be:

- a Ranger;
- a researcher;
- an artisan;
- a contest performer;
- a mechanic;
- a veteran battler;
- a journalist;
- a conservation steward.

Their class creates new opportunities, but their occupational title grants no automatic PTU mechanics.

## 6. Failed Aptitude, Better Plan

A student performs poorly in a supervised battle assessment.

The response is a review of observable choices and a new practice plan rather than a hidden buff, humiliation arc or automatic expulsion.

## 7. Field Notes from the Night Class

An evening course repeatedly observes Pokémon behavior that daytime researchers have never recorded.

The result can create a science question without assuming the night students are automatically correct.

## 8. The Campus Clinic Rotation

Learners shadow a care facility during a busy period.

Their educational objective is documentation and supervised support. Diagnosis, healing and Medicine mechanics remain authoritative.

## 9. The Missing Field Report

A practicum was completed, but the submitted evidence cannot be located.

Possibilities include:

- filing error;
- failed handoff;
- damaged archive;
- wrong version;
- accidental duplicate identity;
- deliberate removal only if evidence supports it.

## 10. Reassessment Day

Several students return for different reassessments at the same time.

Each needs a different form of evidence, creating a compact social and practical event without requiring a tournament.

## 11. School Without a Battle Track

A small regional institution focuses on ecology, care, crafts, history and civic service.

Battle instruction is available elsewhere.

This prevents every academy from becoming another Gym.

## 12. Battle School Without General Education

A specialist institution teaches battle practice only.

Students still depend on external institutions for research, care, law, ecology or technical learning.

## 13. Community Evening Courses

A settlement offers short courses for working adults and visiting Trainers.

Programs change seasonally based on local needs.

No age assumptions are required.

## 14. The Field-Trip Reroute

A planned trip becomes impractical because of weather, migration or transport disruption.

The instructor must decide whether to cancel, reroute or replace the practicum with a local equivalent.

## 15. The Student Project That Outlives the Students

A cohort begins a long-term survey, archive or restoration project.

Later intakes inherit the records and continue it.

The project can become institutional memory rather than disappearing when the original PCs leave.

## 16. Peer Teaching Session

Students who demonstrated different strengths each lead a short practical station.

The event records observed teaching and collaboration without assuming permanent mentor relationships.

## 17. The Old Syllabus

A module still teaches a route, technology or ecological assumption that was once correct but is now outdated.

Players can help document the change and trigger a curriculum review.

## 18. Visiting Exchange Cohort

A temporary group arrives with different routines, terminology and training assumptions.

The story focuses on equivalence and adaptation rather than one school being objectively superior.

## 19. The Capstone with No Combat

A final project asks learners to solve a persistent local problem using research, logistics, negotiation or service design.

The project can still produce encounters if world state creates them, but combat is not a mandatory rubric item.

## 20. The Capstone with a Supervised Battle

A battle-focused program uses an AutoPTU result as one evidence source inside a broader review.

The transcript remains authoritative. The evaluator cannot alter dice/results to fit the expected grade.

## 21. Alumni Return

Former students return for an institutional anniversary, exhibition or field project.

They can reveal how the program changed without the system inventing what happened to retired PCs.

## 22. Dorm Is Not Destiny

A residence group develops its own informal traditions over time.

Membership creates repeated contact but does not assign personality, social status or predetermined rivalry.

## 23. The Shared Equipment Problem

Several practical courses depend on one facility or device.

A maintenance issue forces scheduling tradeoffs between research, technical instruction and community service.

## 24. Independent Study

A learner proposes a topic the current curriculum does not cover.

An advisor helps define evidence and safety boundaries. Completion can later justify creating a new elective if the institution chooses.

## 25. The Supervised Wild Encounter

A field course encounters a territorial or otherwise relevant wild Pokémon group.

The educational record tracks preparation, observation and decisions. The battle itself, if one occurs, is resolved by AutoPTU.

## 26. Public Demonstration Day

Students present projects to residents, employers, clubs and other institutions.

Public reception belongs to public-memory state; academic completion belongs to education state.

A project can satisfy one and fail at the other.

## 27. The Placement Nobody Expected

A learner assigned to a prestigious host institution discovers that the actual useful work is ordinary record-keeping, maintenance or observation.

The arc values competence and institutional knowledge rather than constant spectacle.

## 28. A Teacher Is Also Busy Elsewhere

An instructor is simultaneously a Gym Leader, researcher, parent, artisan or civic representative.

Conflicting commitments change availability and force the institution to use staffing redundancy.

## 29. Curriculum Review Board

A recurring institutional process reviews outdated modules using evidence from alumni, instructors, field partners and students.

This is not automatically a political scandal. Reasonable people can disagree about what should remain required.

## 30. The School That Grows from a Club

A small player-created club accumulates projects, facilities, archives and mentors over many arcs.

If canon review approves it, the organization can eventually become a formal training institution.

The transition requires explicit governance and staffing rather than an automatic level-up.

# Long Arc A — The Regional Field Institute

Stage 1: A temporary seasonal field school opens with limited staff and borrowed facilities.

Stage 2: Learners produce useful ecological, route and public-service evidence.

Stage 3: Host institutions disagree about which specializations deserve permanent funding.

Stage 4: A crisis tests whether the program's training actually helps local response.

Stage 5: The region decides whether to close, repeat, expand or permanently establish the institute.

Possible persistent results:

- new facility;
- recurring seasonal program;
- archive of field observations;
- employment pipeline;
- revised route/conservation state;
- alumni network.

No outcome is preselected.

# Long Arc B — Two Schools, One Practicum

Two institutions use different methods but share one field location.

The conflict develops through:

- incompatible schedules;
- different evidence standards;
- different safety assumptions;
- shared limited facilities;
- student comparisons;
- public reputation.

Neither institution is automatically corrupt or incompetent.

A later joint project may produce an equivalency framework, a permanent rivalry, specialization boundaries or a merged program.

# Long Arc C — The Cohort After the Disaster

A major regional crisis disrupts an academic term.

Instead of canceling the campaign, the institution adapts:

- classes compress;
- staff are reassigned;
- field placements become emergency support only where authorized;
- facilities become shelters or service hubs;
- missed practicals are reassessed later;
- student projects document recovery.

Months later, the cohort becomes part of the institution's public memory because their education was shaped by recovery rather than because they were declared heroes.
