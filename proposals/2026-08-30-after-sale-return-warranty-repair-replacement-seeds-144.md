# Ouros Proposal Seeds — After-Sale Return, Warranty, Repair & Replacement — Pass 144

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Nothing in this file is established Ouros lore.
Date: 2026-08-30

These seeds use the proposed after-sale continuity extension without deciding that warranties, returns or standardized consumer remedies exist everywhere in Ouros.

Pokémon are excluded from product-return semantics. They may appear as workers, companions, witnesses or environmental actors only under their own agency rules.

## The Repair Was Finished Yesterday

A familiar repair counter tells a customer that a field camera was repaired yesterday. The customer insists that it cannot be finished because nobody called them.

Both claims can be true.

The repair record shows technical completion yesterday. Quality verification happened this morning. The pickup notice has not yet been sent.

Useful consequences:

- the player can carry information without adjudicating fault;
- the workshop gains a visible backlog of finished-but-not-released items;
- later callbacks can reference a process change where completion and customer notification are displayed separately.

## The Replacement Is Here, the Original Is Not

A replacement device arrives before the original unit has been returned.

Possible reasons:

- advance replacement was explicitly authorized;
- a courier delay affected the original return;
- the original is still needed until a field expedition ends;
- the replacement was allocated early but cannot yet be issued;
- records from two same-model units were mixed.

Do not decide theft or bad faith until evidence supports it.

## Three Repairs, Three Causes

A survey instrument returns to the same workshop for the third time in a year. Locals joke that the maker never fixed it correctly.

The records show three different problems:

- cracked external housing after a fall;
- corroded connector after a wet expedition;
- calibration drift discovered months later.

The instrument itself becomes a story object because its continuity matters. Each repair can leave cosmetic history without granting a mechanical bonus.

## The Old Shop Promise

A shop changed operators six months ago. A customer arrives with a written promise from the former operator concerning a commissioned tool.

The successor recognizes the item and remembers the customer, but nobody has established whether the old promise transferred with the business.

The interesting question is provenance and authority, not whether one side is dishonest.

Possible outcomes:

- the successor voluntarily honors it;
- the former operator remains responsible;
- an agreement record resolves the scope;
- the evidence remains incomplete;
- the parties negotiate a new remedy without deciding the old obligation.

## The Exchange Program Nobody Else Uses

One regional workshop maintains two tool configurations for different terrain conditions and allows regular users to exchange one for the other when their expedition needs change.

Nearby shops do not offer the same practice.

This deliberately avoids a universal return rule. The program can become part of the workshop's identity and create recurring NPC relationships without turning into a menu-only service.

## The Loaner Everyone Recognizes

A workshop keeps one battered temporary replacement camera. During a prolonged repair backlog it passes through several researchers, couriers and inspectors.

The camera becomes locally recognizable because of stickers, a replaced strap and a distinctive case.

Its ownership never changes. Custody does.

Later story hooks can arise when:

- two people remember using it on different dates;
- a photograph is traced to the loaner's unique damaged lens hood;
- the loaner is overdue because its current holder is stranded by a transport disruption;
- the shop finally retires it and preserves it as a local object of memory.

## The Receipt Is Gone, the Record Is Not

A customer lost the paper or token they received at purchase. The shop still has a transaction record linked to the exact item instance.

This seed exists to prevent automatic fraud assumptions from missing physical proof.

Alternate version:

The customer has a genuine receipt but it refers to another same-model unit purchased by a household member. The case remains unresolved until item provenance is checked.

## The Policy Changed During the Storm

A provider temporarily broadened its service practice during a major disruption because replacement parts could not reach the region. After routes recovered, the normal policy returned.

Months later several residents remember different rules because they interacted with the shop during different policy versions.

The contradiction becomes environmental history rather than a continuity error.

## The Recall and the Broken Unit

A batch recall becomes public while one customer already has a repair case open for a unit from that batch.

Do not collapse the two histories.

Possible states:

- the customer's reported symptom matches the recall issue;
- the unit belongs to the batch but has an unrelated defect;
- batch membership is uncertain because the visible casing was replaced previously;
- the repair case closes while the recall process still requires another action;
- recall eligibility provides a different remedy than the earlier service promise.

## The Wrong Replacement in the Right Box

A replacement shipment reaches the correct service counter with the correct outer label, but intake reveals a different item instance inside.

Potential causes include:

- packing error;
- label applied to the wrong parcel;
- two handoffs occurred in reversed order;
- provider records referenced a model rather than an instance;
- deliberate substitution, but only if evidence supports it.

Courier owns the shipment history. Material Culture owns item identity. The after-sale case links the discrepancy to the intended replacement.

## The Repaired Tool Has a New Scar

A repaired field tool functions correctly but returns with a visible patch, replaced grip or differently colored panel.

One NPC dislikes the change because the old damage had personal meaning. Another sees the patch as evidence that the object survived an important expedition.

The dispute is cultural and emotional before it is mechanical.

## The Refund Was Approved Last Week

A provider's case record says refund approved. Finance has no settled payment yet.

Neither system is necessarily wrong.

Possible causes:

- settlement is queued;
- recipient information needs correction;
- payment route is unavailable;
- authorization expired before execution;
- a later amendment changed the amount or destination.

The scene should never display `refund approved` as equivalent to `money received`.

## The Workshop Cannot Reproduce the Fault

A customer reports intermittent failure. The workshop repeatedly tests the item and cannot reproduce it.

Useful story directions:

- the failure depends on environmental conditions at the customer's field site;
- another component used with the item is involved;
- the symptom occurred only under a specific sequence of actions;
- the item is currently functioning and the cause remains unknown;
- the report was mistaken without being deceptive.

A good outcome can be `UNRESOLVED / MONITOR` rather than forced diagnosis.

## Two Identical Cases, Different Remedies

Two same-model devices fail within the same week. One receives repair; the other receives replacement.

The apparent inconsistency may come from:

- different underlying causes;
- parts availability changing between cases;
- different policy versions;
- one unit having prior repair history;
- one provider handling each case under separate agreements;
- a genuine inconsistency requiring later review.

The narrative system should preserve the facts before deciding why outcomes differed.

## The Item Was Replaced, Then Found

A replacement was issued after an original object was believed lost in transit. Weeks later the original turns up in a misrouted storage area.

This creates several distinct questions:

- who currently owns each item;
- whether the replacement was conditional;
- who has custody now;
- whether a refund or insurance claim also exists;
- whether one item should be returned;
- whether the old object's story significance makes its recovery emotionally important.

No answer should be inferred from physical possession alone.

## The Workshop Moved, the Cases Stayed

A repair counter relocates after building damage. Open service cases continue at a temporary site while repaired items remain stored at the old workshop until safe transfer is possible.

This connects Place Reference, Commercial Service, Facility Maintenance, Courier and after-sale continuity without making the relocation itself a fetch quest.

## Mystery: Five Times the Item Was “Returned”

Five records use the word “returned” differently:

- customer requested a return;
- courier accepted the parcel;
- provider warehouse received it;
- repair center sent it back;
- customer collected it.

The mystery is solved by restoring event precision rather than picking one record as false.

## Mystery: Four Serial Marks, One Instrument

A historic instrument appears under four identifiers across twenty years.

Possible explanation:

- one institutional asset number;
- one maker mark;
- one replacement casing number;
- one later archive catalog number.

Possible alternate conclusion:

Two records may actually refer to another same-model instrument. If evidence cannot resolve the collision, `ACCEPTED_AMBIGUITY` is valid.

No universal serial-number scheme is introduced.

## Mystery: The Repair That Happened Before the Purchase

A workshop ledger seems to show a repair three months before the customer's acquisition record.

Possible explanations:

- pre-owned item with prior history;
- refurbishment before resale;
- acquisition date refers to transfer into the current household rather than first manufacture/sale;
- same-model identity collision;
- old ledger copied under a new numbering system.

The solution should come from provenance, not a mandatory time-travel or forgery twist.

## Mystery: The Replacement That Was Never Issued

A replacement asset is marked allocated. The customer says they never received it. Staff remember preparing it.

Evidence chain:

- allocation record;
- shelf placement;
- pickup notice;
- custody event;
- courier event if any;
- later inventory observation.

A prepared item can remain unissued. `ALLOCATED` must never be treated as handoff proof.

## Dungeon / exploration concept: The Closed Service Basement

A former service depot beneath an old commercial block contains:

- shelves tagged under multiple historical numbering schemes;
- repaired casings from discontinued equipment;
- an intake counter disconnected from the present storefront;
- courier labels from a route that no longer exists;
- a locked records room whose contents remain private unless lawful access is established elsewhere;
- safe public areas where environmental history can be read visually.

The exploration can run without combat. Its puzzle is chronology and provenance.

If a tactical encounter is added, use a static safe area and keep stored controlled property outside BattleSpec.

## Dungeon / exploration concept: The Workshop With Two Front Doors

A repair workshop expanded into a neighboring building over decades. The old intake door, new pickup door and loading entrance all have different historical signs.

Players reconstruct how objects moved through the site during three eras.

Useful connections:

- Place Reference;
- Commercial Service;
- Courier;
- Material Culture;
- Public Notices;
- after-sale cases.

The physical layout tells a history without requiring exposition.

## Long arc: A Town Learns What “Fixed” Means

Phase one establishes ordinary routines. Residents know which workshop repairs field equipment, which shop exchanges certain tools and which institution maintains a pool of temporary loaners.

Phase two introduces unrelated small failures. Some are repaired quickly. One cannot be reproduced. One waits for parts. One receives a replacement.

Phase three adds a regional disruption that delays shipments and forces temporary policy changes. Old and new cases overlap. Loaners become scarce. A recall may affect one batch without explaining every failure.

Phase four restores normal logistics, but the service ecology does not reset. The workshop keeps one emergency practice that proved useful. A repaired object becomes locally famous. One old case remains unresolved because the evidence genuinely cannot establish what happened.

The arc can remain entirely non-villainous. Its value comes from persistent objects, institutions and memory.

## NPC / faction candidates

The Intake Specialist

Remembers objects and their condition better than customers' names. Useful as a recurring provenance witness. No mechanical appraisal authority is implied.

The Traveling Repairer

Works between small settlements where permanent workshops are not viable. Their route becomes part of local calendars. Exact repair capability must be grounded in PTU/Caelo mechanics.

The Cooperative Tool Pool

An institution that owns a small set of temporary replacement assets used by multiple members. Custody and scheduling matter more than ownership transfer.

The Maker Who Keeps Every Old Panel

Stores replaced cosmetic components because each tells part of an object's history. Can support Material Culture, museum donation or memorial stories later.

The Successor Operator

Inherited a storefront and its customers but only some archived service records. Their central conflict is continuity under incomplete institutional memory.

- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL

## Encounter seed: Replacement Handoff Chokepoint

Full intended version:

A replacement is physically ready for a later handoff while a threat blocks the route between secure storage and the pickup area.

Rich protection/escort semantics remain dependent on PARTIAL/BLOCKING movement, lifecycle, terrain/reaction, tactical-policy and adapter families.

Reduced version: READY.

Keep replacement asset outside BattleSpec. Resolve a conventional static encounter nearby. Victory creates `IMMEDIATE_HANDOFF_APPROACH_CLEAR`. World-state owners perform later custody/ownership events.

## Encounter seed: Workshop Retrieval Perimeter

Full intended version:

A technically completed repair waits for pickup while severe weather residue or another threat affects the exterior approach.

If active hazards, changing zones, weather phases, generalized reactions or forced movement matter, the full version remains blocked by those exact families.

Reduced version: READY.

Freeze weather/hazard semantics before combat or move the encounter to reviewed static geometry. Keep the repaired item and noncombatants outside BattleSpec. Victory creates `IMMEDIATE_WORKSHOP_APPROACH_CLEAR` only.

## Canon questions

Before promotion, Ouros still needs explicit answers for each relevant region/provider:

- whether returns are offered at all;
- whether exchanges exist;
- whether warranties/guarantees/service contracts exist;
- who makes those promises;
- whether proof of acquisition is required;
- whether promises follow the item, buyer or transaction;
- whether repair, replacement, reissue or refund are possible remedies;
- who chooses a remedy;
- whether original-item return is required for replacement;
- whether temporary loaners exist;
- whether successor providers inherit old cases;
- whether refurbished/pre-owned markets exist;
- how private service records are handled;
- which exact PTU/Caelo mechanics govern repairs;
- which named shops, workshops, makers, cooperatives or institutions belong in canon.

Until decided, every seed above remains optional NON-CANON material.
