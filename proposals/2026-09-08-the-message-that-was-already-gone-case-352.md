# The Message That Was Already Gone — Pass 352

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-08

## Premise

A shared field instrument was reassigned after two legitimate bookings collided. The displaced team later insists that nobody told them.

The operational scheduler shows that their reservation stopped governing the resource. The communication queue no longer has the message in pending work because the event completed hours earlier.

The dispute begins after the live message is already gone from the queue.

## Full investigation structure

The player can reconstruct the sequence from independent evidence:

1. the original reservation;
2. the later allocation conflict;
3. the authority record that selected one obligation;
4. the displacement application;
5. the notice obligation created for the affected holder;
6. the communication event ID;
7. the archived terminal envelope showing sender, receiver, channel and transport times;
8. the delivery status;
9. the receiver’s current memory-access state;
10. any later retransmission, correction or acknowledgement.

Several outcomes remain possible without rewriting history.

### Correct recipient, accessible memory

The archived envelope and private knowledge history agree that the holder received the update. The player can then investigate misunderstanding, later contradictory information, or a subsequent planning failure without pretending the notice never existed.

### Correct recipient, inaccessible recall

The archived transport and claim ledger show historical receipt, while the memory-retrieval owner currently returns the claim as inaccessible or content-only. The NPC can sincerely say they do not remember the notice.

Historical delivery therefore does not become an omniscient lie detector.

### Wrong recipient

The archived envelope shows that a real message reached another actor. The transport succeeded for that recipient, but the displacement notice obligation for the booking holder was never satisfied.

A later corrective message must be a new communication event.

### Terminal channel failure

The archive preserves who the attempted message was for even though delivery failed. The notice remains outstanding. The failed attempt can explain why the office believed it had acted while the field team never received the information.

## Player-facing hooks

The player may be asked to settle an equipment dispute, reconstruct why a survey was missed, determine whether a second trip is necessary, or identify whether the failure occurred in allocation, transport, receipt, memory or replanning.

The interesting choice is not “who is lying?” by default. The player identifies which causal layer failed and decides what practical repair is still possible.

Possible repairs include:

- send a correction;
- reschedule the missed work;
- find another technically ready resource;
- document the failure for later policy review;
- change a local communication procedure if a competent authority adopts that change;
- leave the dispute unresolved when evidence is insufficient.

## Long-term arc potential

Repeated disputes of this type can create evidence for an institutional communication review. That future arc should consume actual histories of wrong-recipient delivery, failed delivery, late correction and inaccessible recall.

Frequency alone must not automatically punish staff, change policy or trigger procurement. A competent authority and explicit world decision remain required.
