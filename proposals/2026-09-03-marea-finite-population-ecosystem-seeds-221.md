# Marea finite-population ecosystem seeds — pass 221

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-03

## Scope

These seeds turn pass 221's ecosystem-scale population authority into playable situations. They preserve current Marea canon and do not assign new species, population totals, diet values or ecosystem boundaries.

The recurring premise is simple: the ecosystem contains a finite population, while characters experience only partial observations of it.

## Seed: Two Baits, One Population

Nerea and Ema are comparing two observation points inside the same broader ecological envelope. One uses a source-verified attractant appropriate to the target species; the other uses an unbaited control or a different approved observation method.

After several visits, raw encounter counts make both stations look unusually active at different times. The useful question is whether abundance changed, the same individuals redistributed, detectability changed, or different subsets of the population use each site.

Player actions can include checking unique markings, comparing timestamps, reading tracks, changing observation effort, removing the attractant, or revisiting after a quiet interval.

Consequences can include a corrected field report, a misleading local rumor about a population boom, or a temporary access decision that later proves overbroad.

Guardrail: neither station has its own independent spawn budget. Both draw from one finite ecosystem ledger.

## Seed: The Biome Edge That Wasn't

A working field map inherited a hard boundary where the Minecraft terrain palette changes. Nerea's repeated observations suggest that the same ecological population and resource flows cross the apparent line.

The player walks transects on both sides, records resource continuity, tracks movement and compares older field notes. The final interpretation may describe a broad ecotone, a habitat patch within one ecosystem, or genuine evidence for a boundary. No answer is forced before the evidence supports it.

The quest can update ecological cartography without changing Minecraft blocks. If a later world redesign requires moving canon anchors, that becomes a separate explicit migration proposal.

## Seed: Everybody Counted the Same Fletchling

Several residents report a familiar Fletchling during one day: Lia sees it near the route, Pia notices a bird at the archive approach, and Ema records one at a transect. The reports initially look like three separate animals.

If authoritative state places the persistent Sendero Fletchling across those locations at plausible times, the player can discover that multiple reports describe one individual. If the evidence does not support identity, the records remain separate observations.

The quest teaches that detections are not population units and gives persistent individuals narrative value without making every wild Pokémon named.

## Seed: When the Resource Moves

A source-verified dietary resource becomes less available in one habitat patch and more available elsewhere inside the same ecosystem. Wild observations shift spatially.

Possible explanations include ordinary foraging movement, competition, seasonal resource change, disturbance, migration outside the sampled patch, or poor detectability. Demographic decline is only one hypothesis and cannot be asserted from fewer sightings alone.

The player can compare resource surveys, old notes, current traces and repeated observations. Later systems may allow sustained resource deficits to influence reproduction or survival, but this seed does not invent those rules.

## Seed: A Lure Can Empty the Other Camera

A bait station becomes successful enough that another observation point appears suddenly quiet. The apparent local decline may be an observation artifact created by the first team's intervention.

This seed gives provisioning a cost even when no Pokémon is harmed: one research method can contaminate another dataset.

It also creates an institutional conflict that does not require villains. Nerea may care about clean longitudinal data; another researcher may prioritize obtaining a close observation quickly; Mara may care about public access and wildlife concentration.

## Reduced encounter: Reserve, Project, Observe

The reduced version preserves the narrative premise without waiting for full battle support.

Flow:

```text
ecosystem ledger
-> one available member reservation
-> Cobblemon-compatible physical projection
-> ordinary movement/observation
-> provenance record
-> actor unload/release of projection reservation
-> ledger total unchanged
```

A player can visit several stations, wait for another time window, compare detections, identify a persistent individual when evidence allows, and record baited versus unbaited observations.

This version supplies no PTU Item effect, capture modifier, friendship, healing, demographic growth, off-screen combat or autonomous tactical battle policy.

## Long-term arc: The First Real Marea Ecosystem Map

Nerea's longitudinal project can eventually become the vehicle for defining the first full 1:1 ecosystem envelope around the implemented Marea district.

The arc can proceed through ordinary field work rather than a single exposition quest:

- map habitat patches around existing anchors;
- identify transition belts;
- extract source-backed species diets;
- record resource distributions;
- estimate movement corridors;
- distinguish persistent residents from transient migrants;
- compare human land use with wildlife activity;
- maintain uncertain population estimates for characters while the server keeps ground truth;
- propose any required coordinate migration only after physical scale evidence exists.

This creates a worldbuilding pipeline in which adding a species means placing it into a functioning ecological system rather than attaching another spawn JSON to a nearby decorative biome.
