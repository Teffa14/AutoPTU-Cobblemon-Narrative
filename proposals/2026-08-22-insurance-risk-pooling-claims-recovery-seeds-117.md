# Ouros insurance, risk pooling, claims & recovery seeds — Pass 117

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Every entry requires continuity, originality, PTU/Caelo and implementation review before promotion.

These candidates assume the proposed Insurance, Risk Pooling, Claims & Recovery layer. They deliberately avoid premium formulas, universal liability, Pokémon valuation, property law, fraud-detection bonuses or battle effects.

## Candidate 01 — The Roof Was Already Leaking

A warehouse roof fails during a major storm.

Inspection records show a minor pre-existing leak, but the catastrophic failure happened only after the storm.

The story is a scope/causation review, not automatic fraud or automatic coverage.

## Candidate 02 — The Paid Claim, Unfinished Bridge

A bridge replacement claim was approved months ago.

The funds arrived. The project is still incomplete because the specified beams are unavailable.

Supply Chains and Public Works now matter more than the claim itself.

## Candidate 03 — Three Claims, One Flood

A single flood creates three claims from the same district:

- one structure was physically damaged;
- one business remained intact but lost access for two weeks;
- one museum moved its collection before impact and suffered almost no loss.

The event is common. The consequences are not.

## Candidate 04 — The Mutual That Survived Small Storms

A town repair mutual has handled routine storms successfully for decades.

A region-wide disaster creates claims from nearly every member at once.

The problem becomes prioritization, reserve depletion and outside mutual aid.

## Candidate 05 — The Missing Claim Photograph

An old claim file references a photograph that no longer appears in the archive.

The claim may still be valid because structure inspections, repair records and payment events survive independently.

## Candidate 06 — The Wrong Building Number

A claim lists the address used before a street renumbering project.

A modern map makes it look like the claim names the wrong building.

Historical address revisions resolve the discrepancy.

## Candidate 07 — The Claim That Predates the Map Correction

A river migration changed the channel after an old claim was filed.

The claimed riverside location seems impossible on the current map but is correct for that year's geometry revision.

## Candidate 08 — The Insured Ferry That Never Sank

A ferry remains physically intact but service stops for six weeks because a landslide blocks the only access road to the terminal.

Whether interruption support applies depends on the agreement version, not on narrative intuition.

## Candidate 09 — The Heritage Window

A historic hall loses a one-of-a-kind stained window.

A payout can fund a replacement. Public Memory and Architecture still record that the original was lost.

## Candidate 10 — The Claim With Two Owners

Two institutions both believe they are the legitimate owner of a research instrument because custody changed years ago without a clean transfer record.

Coverage review must wait for the relevant ownership/custody facts without deciding them itself.

## Candidate 11 — Emergency Repair Before Approval

A water utility cannot wait for claim review and performs an emergency repair immediately.

The later question is what support can be reimbursed under the agreement, not whether the repair was narratively allowed to happen.

## Candidate 12 — The Reserve That Was Used Last Winter

A cooperative emergency fund appears unusually low before hurricane season.

The reason is public: it paid for winter road repairs after a severe freeze.

Preparedness decisions now have memory.

## Candidate 13 — The Museum Stabilization Claim

A flood reaches museum storage.

The first authorized support pays for drying, temporary relocation and stabilization.

Full restoration remains a separate future project with separate provenance.

## Candidate 14 — The Honest Inventory Mismatch

A warehouse claim says twelve units were lost. The inventory database says ten.

Receiving records reveal two units arrived physically but were never reconciled into the system before the event.

The mismatch produces investigation without villainy.

## Candidate 15 — The Claim That Was Filed Twice

A local office files a claim, then the regional office files another after communications recover.

Both describe the same loss.

The correct outcome is merging records, not accusing either filer of deception.

## Candidate 16 — The Uncovered Upgrade

A storm destroys an old pedestrian bridge.

The agreement supports equivalent replacement. The town chooses an accessible wider bridge and funds the upgrade through a separate public-works source.

The final structure has two funding histories.

## Candidate 17 — The Orchard Mutual

Several orchard operators share a cooperative weather-recovery fund.

One member proposes expanding coverage after a new drought pattern appears.

The story is whether members want to change the pool before the next season, not a pricing minigame.

## Candidate 18 — The Pokémon Center Backup Generator

A backup generator keeps critical systems online during a blackout.

The facility still files a small claim for damaged electrical equipment, but no business-interruption component occurs because the redundancy worked.

Preparedness changes consequences through actual infrastructure state.

## Candidate 19 — The Claim With No Villain

A mountain lodge loses most of a season's bookings after wildfire smoke repeatedly closes the access road.

The building never burns. No actor caused the business loss intentionally.

## Candidate 20 — The Former Workshop, Current Apartments

An old policy file still labels a building as a workshop even though it was converted into housing years ago.

The current agreement version, occupancy and structure use must be checked before interpreting the old record.

## Candidate 21 — The Insurer's Assessor Finds a Nest

An assessor enters a storm-damaged roof space and discovers an active Pokémon nest.

The claim survey pauses while Conservation and Pokémon Agency determine the appropriate next step.

Coverage review does not create authority over the animals.

## Candidate 22 — The Boat Pool

A small harbor uses a mutual pool for emergency vessel repairs.

A single unusual storm damages several boats and the repair yard itself.

The bottleneck becomes skilled labor and parts even before money runs out.

## Candidate 23 — The Old Claim Solves a New Mystery

A decades-old claim contains photographs and repair estimates from before a district was rebuilt.

Those records later help Cartography or Architecture reconstruct the earlier street geometry.

The claim becomes historical evidence beyond finance.

## Candidate 24 — The Wrong Cause, Correct Loss

A claimant reports that a lightning strike damaged a relay.

Sensor logs later show the relay failed from wind-thrown debris.

The equipment damage is real even though the initial cause claim was wrong.

## Candidate 25 — The Payment That Went to the Old Account

A claim is approved correctly, but the payment handoff uses an obsolete institutional account record.

The finance problem is separate from the claim decision.

## Candidate 26 — The Community Fund Chooses Prevention

After repeated small floods, a community recovery pool votes to fund drainage improvements instead of waiting to reimburse another round of repairs.

The decision becomes a Public Works project with its own evidence and outcomes.

## Candidate 27 — The Released Partner Is Not a Lost Asset

A Trainer's former partner was voluntarily released years earlier and later appears near a damaged orchard.

No claim can list the Pokémon as lost property or replacement value simply because the Trainer once had a relationship with it.

## Candidate 28 — The Venue Claim After Cancellation

A festival venue is undamaged, but a severe weather forecast causes a legitimate cancellation before visitors arrive.

Whether any interruption support exists depends on authored coverage. The forecast itself does not become a loss event.

## Candidate 29 — The Claim Review Corrects Itself

An old claim was partially denied because a key record could not be found.

Years later, archive digitization recovers the missing document.

A bounded review changes the decision while preserving the original chronology.

## Candidate 30 — The Recovery Ledger

A regional archive preserves decades of claim notices, damage photos, repairs, denied components, payouts and later rebuilding.

Researchers can use the ledger to study how the region's infrastructure and risk changed over time without treating every historical decision as correct.

# Long arc A — Five Storms at Meridian Mutual

Year 1: a small town mutual handles a routine wind event comfortably.

Year 2: members add a shared equipment reserve after discovering that parts, not money, caused the longest repair delays.

Year 3: a larger storm produces simultaneous roof, road and service-interruption losses.

Year 4: the mutual changes its agreement version and funds prevention work in two repeatedly affected locations.

Year 5: another storm tests whether the earlier adaptations actually changed recovery time.

The arc records reserve history, member decisions, claims, preventive projects and unresolved tradeoffs without requiring an antagonist.

# Long arc B — The Claim Archive Under the City

An old institutional archive contains decades of recovery records from a district that repeatedly changed use.

Early files describe workshops and rail yards. Later files describe apartments, markets and public spaces. Several claims use addresses and boundaries that no longer exist.

Over multiple investigations, the archive becomes useful to Architecture, Cartography, Public Memory, Supply Chains and Cases. Some discrepancies are errors. Some reflect legitimate historical versions. A small number may justify a separate investigation.

The archive never becomes omniscient because each claim only records what that process captured at the time.

# Long arc C — After the Great Flood, Who Pays for What?

A major flood damages several settlements in one watershed.

Immediate crisis response uses emergency reserves and mutual aid.

Later, each affected asset follows a different recovery path: public grant, local mutual, private coverage, institutional reserve, volunteer repair or no external support.

Years later the region has different bridge designs, relocation decisions, restored wetlands, altered business districts and public memories because financial recovery mechanisms interacted with real physical and social constraints.

The arc is about persistent consequences, not maximizing payouts.

# Encounter contract 1 — Storm-Damaged Depot Claim Survey

Narrative premise:

An assessor and depot staff need to document a storm-damaged freight facility while a separate Pokémon disturbance makes part of the site unsafe.

FULL version:

The assessor moves through several inspection points while workers withdraw and combatants may block routes. Unsafe zones are represented only if authoritative terrain/hazard rules exist.

Primary dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement for dynamic escort/withdrawal;
- action economy/initiative;
- full lifecycle and damage/status families as used by actual Moves;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if the damaged depot has live tactical hazards;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for `PROTECT`, `WITHDRAW`, `REACH_INSPECTION_POINT`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

REDUCED version:

Close the unsafe section in world state. Move assessor/workers off-grid. Preserve inspection observations already collected. If combat remains, use a static dry/clear arena. Resume claim work afterward. The battle cannot determine coverage or valuation.

A mutual-aid reserve has emergency pumps and generators ready for dispatch, but access to the loading lane is blocked by an unrelated encounter.

FULL version:

Equipment transfers and workers may move through protected lanes while combatants contest the chokepoint.

Primary dependencies:

- AI tactical policy for `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `PROTECT`, `WITHDRAW`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback;

# Encounter contract 3 — Harbor Claim Evidence Recovery

Narrative premise:

A storm scattered physical records and instrument data around a damaged harbor office. Investigators need evidence for multiple claims while wild Pokémon are also using the changed shoreline.

FULL version:

Search teams move among evidence points while Pokémon can withdraw through changing routes. Any live water/current/shore hazard needs authoritative environmental support.

Primary dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement/interception/forced movement for movement objectives;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if shoreline state matters tactically;
- AI tactical policy for `SEARCH`, `WITHDRAW`, `DO_NOT_DESTROY_EVIDENCE`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback;
- normal battle families for actual combat resolution.

REDUCED version:

Recover evidence locations in overworld state first. Keep documents/instruments outside battle authority. If a confrontation occurs, freeze one validated shoreline geometry and run an ordinary battle. Evidence provenance remains unchanged by the battle result.

# Permanent non-inferences for Pass 117

Do not infer:

- damage -> coverage;
- coverage -> payout;
- payout -> repair;
- denial -> corruption;
- discrepancy -> fraud;
- maintenance issue -> negligence;
- claim review -> criminal investigation;
- business interruption -> building destruction;
- insurance institution -> ownership of insured assets;
- mutual pool -> government authority;
- Pokémon involvement -> liability;
- captured Pokémon -> asset valuation;
- Pokémon Injury -> monetary depreciation;
- battle victory -> valid claim;
- battle transcript -> financial valuation;
- Minecraft block destruction -> authoritative loss without domain handoff;
- repaired visual model -> financially or historically restored state.
