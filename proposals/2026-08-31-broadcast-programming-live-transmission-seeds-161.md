# Ouros Broadcast Programming & Live Transmission Seeds — Pass 161

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS
Canon effect: NONE
Date: 2026-08-31

Every named concept below is an original candidate for review. No station, presenter, program, region, technology, historical event or media institution becomes Ouros canon through this file.

## Design intent

These concepts exercise the new broadcast continuity layer without turning broadcasting into omniscience, reputation math, remote combat authority or an automatic quest dispenser.

They are deliberately compatible with several future technology levels. A concept described as radio, television or livestream-like should be adapted only after Ouros canon establishes the channel available in that region.

## The Episode Everyone Remembers Differently

A well-known local program covered a major public event years ago. Three surviving audience groups remember different moments as the defining scene.

The investigation finds that all three memories have provenance:

- one settlement received the original live feed;
- another got a delayed regional version with a local insert;
- a third mostly knows a later rebroadcast edited for length.

The mystery is lineage rather than fraud.

Possible outputs include a reconstructed transmission tree, newly catalogued recordings and a public exhibit that explicitly preserves the variants.

## The Weather Bulletin That Reached One Valley Late

A weather report was prepared correctly and transmitted on time. A network incident delayed delivery to one service sector. Travelers there made decisions using an older bulletin.

The story can connect Media, Communications Network, Travel and Infrastructure without requiring the original forecaster to be wrong.

Hard boundary:

`CORRECT_REPORT + LATE_DELIVERY != FALSE_REPORT`.

## The Live Battle With a Dead Camera

A formal battle continues under AutoPTU authority after one field camera or relay path fails.

People physically present know the battle continued. Remote viewers receive only a partial feed. Later commentary, eyewitness reports and an official result fill some gaps.

The missing footage never becomes permission for Narrative to invent unseen tactical events.

## The Host Who Left, the Show That Continued

A presenter strongly associated with a long-running local program leaves the role. The program retains its title and regular slot but changes tone under a successor.

Older residents refer to periods of the same show by presenter. Newer viewers assume the predecessor's format was always the original one.

Potential follow-up years later: a retrospective discovers an even earlier pilot period that neither generation remembered.

## The Regional Feed That Cut Away

A regional broadcast is covering a public festival when one feed switches to an urgent local bulletin. Other regions continue seeing the festival.

Later arguments about “what was on air at that moment” are resolved by identifying the feed rather than declaring one witness mistaken.

## The Rebroadcast After the Road Reopened

A travel documentary or field report was recorded before a route closure. Its original broadcast was postponed. By the time it airs, the road has reopened in a changed configuration.

The program is historically accurate about when it was filmed but potentially misleading if viewers assume it is current.

A responsible station may add a new framing segment without altering the original recording.

## The Archive Has the Studio Feed, Not the Air Feed

An archive preserves a clean studio master of a famous transmission. Community testimony remembers a regional interruption absent from that recording.

The contradiction disappears after the players establish that the preserved object is a studio master rather than a captured air feed.

This seed is useful for teaching archive provenance without making the institution incompetent.

## The Emergency Override That Never Reached the Coast

An operator initiates a priority bulletin according to an authored local process. The main service transmits it, but one coastal sector is already isolated by an infrastructure failure.

The investigation must distinguish issuance, network transmission, sector availability and audience receipt.

The story does not invent universal emergency-broadcast law.

## The Call-In Guest Wasn't Who Listeners Assumed

A caller or remote guest discusses a public incident. Later listeners attach an identity to the voice that was never established by the original program.

The archived transmission proves what was said but not necessarily who spoke.

Claims/Investigation handles identity evidence. Broadcast continuity preserves the transmission and attributed label used at the time.

## The Program About a Festival From Three Years Ago

A recurring cultural program revisits a festival using archived footage, new interviews and a present-day narration track.

Different temporal layers coexist in one episode.

The episode can reveal how a tradition changed without claiming the older footage represents current practice.

## The Interview Recorded Before Everything Changed

A public figure records an interview about an upcoming decision or challenge. Before airtime, the underlying situation changes.

The broadcaster may air it with updated framing, postpone it, edit it or abandon it. Each choice creates different public information history while leaving the original interview recording intact.

## The Series That Outlived Its Sponsor

A long-running program loses the organization that had funded or advertised around it. The show continues under a different production arrangement.

Finance/Contracts owns any actual commercial relationship. Broadcast continuity owns the program's format and transmission history.

The old sponsor remains visible in archived episodes without implying a current relationship.

## Five Times Someone Said “I Saw It Live”

Five statements all use the same phrase but mean different things:

- physically present at the event;
- watched the real-time regional feed;
- watched a delayed-live feed;
- saw an immediate replay shortly afterward;
- saw a later rebroadcast and remembers it as contemporaneous.

The mystery is solved by reconstructing temporal and delivery provenance rather than accusing witnesses of lying.

## The Program With No Surviving First Episode

A beloved show clearly existed before its earliest surviving recording. Schedules, advertisements, presenter contracts, listener letters and later anniversary material all imply earlier transmissions.

The absence of an air copy remains an archival gap.

The project must never generate a fabricated first-episode transcript merely to fill it.

## The Presenter Who Corrects Themself Years Later

A presenter once repeated a claim that later evidence disproved. Years afterward, the same program airs a retrospective where the presenter explains the correction.

Both transmissions remain historical objects. The correction links through Media information revision rather than overwriting the earlier episode.

## The Quiet Hour

A small regional broadcaster keeps a low-production program devoted to route conditions, local events, community notices and ordinary interviews.

Most episodes generate no quest at all.

Its narrative value comes from baseline continuity. When a familiar bridge closes, an old correspondent retires, a seasonal migration changes or a town festival moves date, players notice because the program previously established ordinary life.

## The Field Reporter Who Misses the Story

A crew reaches a location after the decisive event. They can interview witnesses, inspect public evidence and report what is known, but they cannot retroactively observe the event.

A later audience may nevertheless remember the reporter as having “covered it live.”

This seed keeps observation provenance important.

## The Battle Commentary Everyone Quotes Wrong

A famous line attributed to a commentator appears in fan culture, merchandise and supporter chants. The archived air feed contains a similar but different statement.

Possible explanations include paraphrase, regional feed variation, a rehearsal recording, later dramatization or simple collective compression.

No explanation is selected until evidence supports it.

## The Broadcast From the Temporary Relay

A communications outage causes a temporary relay to support a limited local program for several weeks. When normal service returns, the temporary setup is removed.

Years later, the short-lived broadcasts are culturally important because they contain local voices absent from the main regional archive.

Communications Network owns relay state. Archives owns surviving recordings. Public Memory owns later significance.

## The Program That Changes Language by Region

A single recurring show has region-specific presentation or translation variants while referencing the same underlying information packets.

Differences in phrasing may later create divergent public interpretations.

Language/Translation should own translation provenance if that layer is invoked. No automatic equivalence or mistranslation is invented.

## The Broadcast That Became Evidence

A recording captures a detail later relevant to an investigation. The recording enters a case through an explicit evidence/custody path.

Broadcast continuity establishes what kind of recording it is and when it was made. Case/Evidence determines evidentiary handling. The existence of footage does not make every interpretation of it true.

## Long arc — The Region Learns the Same Event Through Different Feeds

Phase one establishes several ordinary programs with distinct audiences, presenters and regional reach.

A major but non-apocalyptic world event then unfolds over several days. One channel has a field reporter. Another relies on official statements. A local station loses service for part of the event. A recurring cultural show gets preempted. A battle broadcast captures only one tactical slice before a feed failure.

Players can directly witness only some events. Other facts arrive through attributable packets and transmissions.

Later episodes correct early assumptions. Regional archives preserve different air copies. Communities remember different moments because they genuinely received different information.

The finale can be a retrospective, inquiry, exhibition or anniversary rather than a villain confrontation.

No single feed becomes the “truth channel.” Chronicle remains authoritative.

## Long arc — The Program That Becomes an Institution

A small local show begins with a narrow purpose: route conditions, field observations, interviews or community notices.

Across years:

- presenters rotate;
- format changes;
- the program covers crises and quiet seasons;
- a reporter becomes a familiar NPC;
- a temporary hiatus creates replacement programming;
- old recordings enter an archive;
- younger characters grow up treating the show as part of normal regional life.

The payoff is continuity itself. A future player can hear a new host introduce a segment whose origin is traceable to something an earlier party helped establish.

## Long arc — The Broadcast Battle Era

If canon later establishes widespread battle broadcasting, the world can develop history around coverage without changing battle mechanics.

Possible developments include:

- early broadcasts with limited camera positions;
- growing regional coverage;
- specialist commentary careers;
- archive collections of formally significant matches;
- disagreements between commentator narratives and tactical logs;
- supporter watch events;
- a famous match whose feed failed before the result;
- later reanalysis using authoritative battle records.

AutoPTU remains the result authority throughout.

## NPC archetypes

The Continuity Producer remembers program history and prevents new staff from accidentally treating old segment conventions as current rules.

The Field Correspondent is excellent at gathering attributable observations but refuses to claim things they did not witness.

The Retired Presenter remains culturally associated with a show without retaining institutional authority.

The Archive Technician knows the difference between studio master, air capture, regional feed and edited replay.

The Local Weather Voice is famous in one valley and almost unknown elsewhere.

The Battle Commentator understands strategy but cannot alter official rulings or AutoPTU outcomes.

The Substitute Host becomes unexpectedly beloved during a temporary absence, creating social complexity without requiring interpersonal hostility.

The Network Liaison explains coverage state and refuses to promise delivery outside verified sectors.

The Documentary Editor works across old and new footage and preserves dates instead of making archival material appear current.

## Faction and institution hooks

A research institution may provide a recurring field segment without owning the channel.

A Gym may permit formal match coverage without granting commentators access to private scouting data.

A civic body may issue notices through a channel without owning the broadcaster.

A supporter organization may organize watch events without becoming an official program partner.

An archive may preserve recordings from several politically or culturally opposed broadcasters without endorsing their claims.

A remote settlement may value a tiny local station more than a major regional network because it carries useful route and weather information.

## Encounter seed — Relay Rooftop Perimeter

The full version uses height, weather, edge danger, forced movement and route-control behavior. It remains blocked by hazards/reactions, complete-movement incompleteness, lifecycle/policy gaps and playback.

Reduced version uses a safe bounded static platform. Relay state stays with Communications Network. AutoPTU may establish only `IMMEDIATE_RELAY_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

## Encounter seed — Field Crew Withdrawal Corridor

The full version needs escort/withdrawal, possibly equipment carrying, complete movement and tactical policy.

Reduced version extracts the crew and gear before initiative and resolves a static hostile perimeter. AutoPTU may establish only `IMMEDIATE_FIELD_EXIT_CORRIDOR_CLEAR`.

## Encounter seed — Broadcast Battle Coverage

This seed deliberately has a mechanically modest form.

A normal, individually audited AutoPTU battle can occur while a separate broadcast transmission observes it. The broadcast adds no combatant, action, target, modifier or objective.

A synchronized rich spectator implementation remains blocked by Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

The narrative premise survives without waiting for that adapter work.

`CAMERA_LINE != PTU_LINE_OF_SIGHT`

`VIEWER_REACTION != PTU_MODIFIER`

`COMMENTATOR_ADVICE != TRAINER_ORDER`
