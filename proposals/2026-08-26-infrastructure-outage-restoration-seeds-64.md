# Ouros Infrastructure Outage & Restoration Seeds — Pass 64

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No settlement, utility operator, technical standard or regional technology is established by this file.

## The Block That Never Went Dark

A district outage affects almost every building except one narrow block. Residents assume the surviving block has a private generator. Operators instead find that an old bypass still connects it to a different distribution zone.

The useful discovery is the real network topology. The old bypass may be safe, temporary, undocumented or historically significant; none of those states should be assumed before inspection.

## Power Back, Lift Closed

Electric service returns to a multilevel public building, but its lift remains unavailable. Visitors complain because the lights are clearly on.

The local facility operator explains that supply restoration and equipment verification are separate. Players can help obtain access, records or the appropriate inspection without turning the scene into an invented repair Skill check.

This creates a visible accessibility consequence and a clean handoff to Facility Maintenance and Accessibility.

## The Clinic on Backup

A neighborhood loses normal power while the clinic stays open under backup operation. The waiting room appears normal, but several nonessential services are unavailable and staff are monitoring the fallback state closely.

The story can remain entirely preventative. Players may help trace the upstream issue, deliver an existing required part, carry an outage notice or resolve an unrelated access problem before the backup state becomes critical.

No numeric runtime, fuel consumption or medical consequence is invented.

## Three Dark Streets, Two Causes

Three adjacent streets report outages within the same hour. Early public discussion treats them as one event. Field observations show that two share a distribution fault while the third is a local building failure.

The investigation rewards dependency tracing instead of dramatic assumptions. Public communications may later need a correction explaining why one area remains offline after the main network returns.

## The Service That Came Back First

A small settlement restores water pumping before communications. A larger neighboring settlement does the opposite because its infrastructure and dependencies differ.

The point is to show regional variation without declaring one universal correct restoration order. The technical graph exposes what each place can do; local authority and procedures determine the actual sequence.

## The Old Shutdown Map

A technical archive contains a decades-old isolation map. Current workers know several labels are obsolete, but nobody has yet reconciled every modification.

Players can compare field markers, operator memory, maintenance records and current service observations. The final product is an updated dependency/switching map, not a hidden treasure.

## The Night Market Half-Lit

A planned evening market overlaps a partial district outage. Emergency lighting and one service line remain available, but refrigeration, decorative lighting and several vendor systems have different dependencies.

Event Operations must decide what can safely open based on actual availability. Infrastructure records supply state; it does not decide whether the event proceeds.

## The Quiet Pumping Station

Residents associate the pumping station with constant mechanical noise. One morning it becomes silent, but household water service has not yet changed because storage is still supplying the zone.

The silence is an early observation, not proof of failure. Players can report it, inspect authorized public areas or speak with operators before the downstream effect appears.

This uses soundscape knowledge as operational evidence without granting supernatural diagnostic certainty.

## The Backup That Became Normal

A remote service has run on a temporary fallback for so long that residents assume it is the permanent system. A restoration project now proposes returning to the original network.

Players discover that routines, work schedules and perhaps Pokémon habitat use have adapted around the fallback state. Reconnection becomes a world-state transition that may need ecology, staffing and public-information handoffs.

## The Reconnected Habitat Corridor

A de-energized technical corridor has been quiet for months and is now used regularly by wild Pokémon. Repair work is complete and the operator is ready to reconnect the line.

The technical system can be ready while the ecological situation still needs review. Conservation may recommend timing, exclusion measures or another route. No Pokémon receives an invented electricity resistance or hazard effect.

## The Wrong Green Light

A control-room indicator shows a downstream zone as restored. Field observations from the same zone report no usable service.

Possible causes include stale telemetry, partial restoration, a local downstream problem or incorrect mapping. The puzzle is solved by evidence reconciliation, not by declaring the dashboard or eyewitness automatically correct.

## The Morning After Restoration

A town-wide service returned overnight. The next morning is not a victory cutscene. Different systems perform restart checks, businesses reopen at different times, an elevator remains under verification, a courier depot clears backlog and residents remove temporary workarounds.

This seed exists to make recovery playable after the dramatic failure has ended.

## The Nonessential Load Everyone Uses

A fallback plan labels one service as nonessential. In practice, residents use it to reach another service that is considered critical.

The discovery does not automatically prove that the plan was negligent. It reveals a dependency the formal map did not capture. Operators and civic actors can revise future fallback procedures.

## The Manual Board

During a communications outage, a utility crew switches from a digital status board to a hand-maintained physical board at its depot. The board becomes a local information hub where crews mark isolated zones, access blockers and completed checks.

Minecraft can represent this visibly without requiring a functioning in-world internet stack. When digital systems return, preserving the manual board as outage history may create a later archive/museum callback.

## Long arc: A District Learns Its Dependencies

First visit: players learn the normal services of one district without an incident.

Second visit: a modest outage affects two services while a third continues through fallback. The player sees that visible darkness is only one symptom of network state.

Third visit: restoration succeeds, but one downstream facility stays limited until its own verification is complete.

Fourth visit: an after-action review finds an undocumented dependency and updates the fallback plan.

Later callback: a different incident occurs. The revised plan from the earlier arc changes which services remain available. The world remembers preparation without granting any combat buff.

## Mystery: Six Reports, Four Zones

Six outage reports reach an operator. They appear to describe six failures.

Investigation reveals:
- two reports refer to the same service zone from different streets;
- one is a facility-local fault;
- one is a stale report from before a switching event;
- two describe genuinely separate affected zones.

The objective is to build a correct incident extent map before dispatching scarce crews or making public claims.

## Encounter contract — Switchyard Access Restoration

Narrative premise:

Operators need physical access to a distribution node that remains safely isolated. Pokémon activity after the outage obstructs the approach.

Full version:
- reach/protect objective;
- potentially changing technical zones;
- validated hazards only if PTU/Caelo and AutoPTU support them;
- interception/forced movement where legal;
- tactical opponents that understand access denial or withdrawal;
- synchronized Minecraft state.

Capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when used
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

Reduced version:

Operators isolate the node before the tactical scene. Run a static battle in a safe access area using individually verified mechanics. No live electricity, interactable switches, escort logic or forced displacement is simulated. After combat, the authorized restoration action and verification occur in world state.

## Encounter contract — Flooded Conduit Isolation

Narrative premise:

Water has entered a technical corridor and an unrelated Pokémon incident prevents inspection.

Full version:
- water/terrain state that can change during the scene;
- unsafe zones;
- withdrawal or protection objective;
- possible forced movement only with governing rules;
- objective-aware tactical AI;
- live infrastructure-state playback.

The major blockers are complete movement, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, AI tactical policy and Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Evacuate workers and close the corridor first. Treat water condition as noncombat world state. Any battle occurs in an adjacent static space. Pumping, inspection and service restoration happen afterward through the infrastructure/facility systems.

## Noncombat scene — Restoration Board

Players join an operator team after a multi-zone outage has already been bounded.

They receive:
- current zone states;
- active backup states;
- blocked access points;
- unresolved dependency questions;
- downstream service requests.

They can help verify observations, locate missing records, deliver information or solve access dependencies. If priority decisions exceed the operator's mandate, the scene explicitly hands them to civic/crisis authority instead of using a Persuasion roll to decide public policy.
