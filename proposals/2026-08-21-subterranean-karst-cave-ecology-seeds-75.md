# Ouros subterranean, karst & cave ecology seeds — pass 75

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research structures. No external plot, character, faction, dungeon or distinctive lore is copied.

## Design intent

These seeds treat caves as persistent world systems with hydrology, ecology, mapping history, access, research, archaeology and infrastructure. They should not default to random encounters, rare loot or universal darkness penalties.

### 01 — The Passage That Only Exists After Rain

A dry cave corridor becomes a temporary underground stream after sustained rain. Old maps disagree because both survey teams visited in different seasons. Players can help reconcile geometry, hydrology and access history.

### 02 — The Missing Dawn Exit

A Zubat roost that normally disperses through one entrance stops using it. The entrance remains physically open. Possible explanations include changed light, construction vibration, airflow, disturbance or an alternate exit discovered by the colony.

### 03 — Heart Marks in the Closed Gallery

A chamber sealed for safety years ago contains wall marks consistent with repeated Woobat use. The finding creates tension between reopening, research, conservation and visitor access without proving the chamber is occupied today.

### 04 — The Spring That Changed Taste

Residents report that a spring fed by a cave system tastes different after roadwork uphill. Water quality observations, runoff provenance and old sinkhole maps must be compared before anyone blames the nearest workshop.

### 05 — Three Maps of One Cave

A miners’ map, a university survey and a rescue-service map disagree on several edges. Each is internally consistent with its purpose and date. Reconciliation reveals one sealed passage and one route that never existed outside rumor.

### 06 — The Newly Opened Sinkhole

A sinkhole appears after heavy rain near farmland. It may connect to a known cave, a new void or only a shallow collapse. The site becomes simultaneously a safety issue, research opportunity and potential groundwater pathway.

### 07 — The Quiet Roost

A known evening chorus drops sharply for one week. No dead Pokémon are found. The players can investigate visitor pressure, food availability outside the cave, seasonal timing and weather before anyone declares a population collapse.

### 08 — The Mine Broke Into Somewhere Older

Routine work exposes a natural chamber behind a mine wall. The chamber contains biological use evidence and geological features. Work pauses while ownership, access, safety, conservation and research are reviewed.

### 09 — The Cave That Feeds the Marsh

A wetland remains healthy through a dry period because an underground flow persists. Later, debris blocks part of the system. The problem first appears as a wetland/ecology change rather than a cave incident.

### 10 — The Underground Ferry of Bats

A large cave has two openings several kilometers apart. Seasonal bat-like Pokémon movements make the system function as a biological corridor. A proposed visitor route may interrupt only one part of that movement.

### 11 — Fossil Context Lost

A fossil was removed decades ago without adequate location data. A later expedition finds matching strata in several chambers, turning an old museum specimen into a provenance investigation rather than a treasure hunt.

### 12 — The Tracer That Never Arrived

A hydrology team introduces a safe tracer at an approved surface point, but it never appears at the expected spring. The result may indicate an unmapped branch, storage delay, sampling failure or a wrong model. The null result matters.

### 13 — The Tourist Gate That Changed the Cave

A well-intentioned gate installed years ago improves visitor control but may have altered airflow or wildlife passage. The story focuses on evidence, redesign and institutional learning rather than corruption.

### 14 — Carbink at the Cut Face

Excavation exposes a Carbink in a newly opened chamber. The event creates questions about habitat, continued work, custody if the Pokémon is injured, and whether the exposure is unique or part of a larger underground population.

### 15 — The Flood Debris Archive

Seeds, branches, feathers and man-made debris accumulate in one chamber after flood pulses. Long-term sorting reveals changes in the surface catchment over several years.

### 16 — The Cave School

A regional academy uses a stable entrance zone for geology and navigation field practice. Students never enter sensitive deeper sections. A new collapse forces the institution to redesign the course without turning the lesson into a combat gauntlet.

### 17 — The Old Lantern Route

Historic markers show where travelers once crossed a mountain through caves. Modern mapping finds that some lantern hooks lead toward a route now blocked by sediment. The route may have changed naturally rather than being sabotaged.

### 18 — A Chamber Nobody Owns

Two institutions access the same chamber from different entrances. One treats it as a water-monitoring site, the other as part of a heritage trail. Neither automatically controls the whole system.

### 19 — The Underground Orchard Roots

Deep roots from an old orchard reach a shallow cave zone and support a local microhabitat. Changes to the orchard years later alter the cave gradually, linking Flora, Decomposition and subterranean ecology.

### 20 — The Silent Ultrasound Station

A listening station records fewer nocturnal calls but cameras still show cave use. The problem may be sensor placement, altered flight paths or behavioral change. This prevents soundscape data from becoming direct population truth.

### 21 — The Rescue Cache from Twenty Years Ago

An old emergency cache is discovered in a side chamber. Most contents are unusable, but its placement reveals a route once considered standard. Archive records can explain why the route was abandoned.

### 22 — The Seasonal Underground Lake

A chamber alternates between dry basin and lake depending on groundwater state. Different Pokémon use the same physical space at different times of year. No one encounter table describes it permanently.

### 23 — The False Bottom

A cave pool believed shallow has a deeper connection discovered during survey. The discovery changes maps and hydrological interpretation, but underwater traversal remains restricted until actual capability and safety rules are known.

### 24 — The Sacred Chamber and the Service Tunnel

A culturally important cave site lies near a modern maintenance tunnel. The problem is designing access and infrastructure without collapsing stewardship, engineering and belief into one authority.

### 25 — The Cave After the Fire

A wildfire never enters the cave, yet months later ash, sediment and changed nutrient inputs appear after rain. The story links Wildfire, Soil, Freshwater and subterranean ecology through delayed consequences.

### 26 — The Surface Dump Nobody Remembered

Pollution appears underground near an old sinkhole. Archival land-use records reveal several possible sources across decades. The nearest current business may be completely uninvolved.

### 27 — The Chamber That Became a Refuge

A previously little-used chamber begins showing repeated occupancy after surface habitat is disturbed. Conservation staff must decide whether to limit access before the long-term pattern is understood.

### 28 — The Unmapped Airflow

Explorers detect a steady draft in a dead-end chamber. It supports the possibility of another connection, but no tunnel is generated automatically. Investigation may confirm a crack, shaft, inaccessible void or nothing useful.

### 29 — The Cave Festival That Outgrew the Cave

A local tradition near a broad entrance becomes popular enough that visitor pressure starts affecting access and habitat. The community can move parts of the celebration outside instead of simply banning the event.

### 30 — The Cave Atlas Never Finished

A research institution has published five editions of the same cave atlas over thirty years. Each edition contains corrections, closures and discoveries. Players can add a sixth without implying the cave has finally been fully mapped.

## Long arc A — Under One Watershed

Year 1: players survey several apparently separate caves, springs and sinkholes.

Year 2: a drought changes spring flow and reveals that one assumed connection may be wrong.

Year 3: a pollution event travels through only part of the network, providing new evidence.

Year 4: a collapse opens a new passage and changes the hydrological model again.

Year 5: several institutions publish a shared map with confidence levels rather than pretending complete certainty.

The arc can generate science, travel, conservation, infrastructure and case content without requiring a central villain.

## Long arc B — The Deep Route

An old cross-mountain cave route is rediscovered gradually.

Early play focuses on archives and entrance surveys. Later visits add mapping, rescue caches, wildlife observations, a former mine section, seasonal water and eventually a usable route. Reopening remains a civic/travel decision rather than the automatic reward for reaching the far entrance.

## Long arc C — The Roost Years

A major cave roost is observed over many seasons.

Players may contribute sound recordings, photography, visitor management, surface-habitat work and migration observations. Some years show high activity, others low activity. The system accumulates baseline evidence before it can reasonably label an anomaly.

## Encounter contract — Sinking Passage Survey

Full version:
- water rises during the encounter;
- multiple route choices;
- safe/high-ground objectives;
- legal Swim versus non-Swim movement;
- current/forced movement only if supported;
- REACH_EXIT or WITHDRAW tactical goals;
- objective-aware AI.

Required permanent capability families:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED baseline;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED baseline;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:
Resolve water level and accessible route before combat. Freeze one chamber state. Keep surveyors outside the grid. Do not simulate currents, drowning or changing elevation.

## Encounter contract — Roost Entrance Disturbance

Full version:
- visitors/noncombatants near the entrance;
- wild Pokémon may withdraw through legal exits;
- protect/withdraw goals;
- verified light/visibility rules if available;
- objective-aware AI.

Reduced version:
Resolve crowd movement and roost evidence in overworld state. If wild Pokémon defend the entrance, only actual participants enter a static battle. The whole roost never becomes a combat roster by default.

## Encounter contract — Collapsed Mine-Natural Junction

Full version:
- rescue zone;
- unstable passage edges;
- possible debris only through verified hazards;
- workers/noncombatants;
- changing access state;
- tactical AI aware of rescue/withdrawal goals.

Reduced version:
Resolve structural stability and rescue corridors first. Use one validated chamber for a conventional battle if needed. Keep workers, fossils, samples and unstable geometry outside tactical authority.

## Encounter contract — Deep Pool Crossing

Full version:
- underwater or partially submerged route;
- legal movement by individual capabilities;
- exit objective;
- possible changing water state;
- no assumed drowning or pressure rules.

Reduced version:
Treat the pool as a world traversal gate. Validate legal passage before any battle. If combat occurs, choose either a dry ledge or fixed shallow-water map supported by current movement legality.

- continuity review;
