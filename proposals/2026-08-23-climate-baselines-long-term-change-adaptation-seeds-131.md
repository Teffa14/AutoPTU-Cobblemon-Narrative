# Ouros Worldbuilding Candidates — Pass 131: Climate Baselines, Long-Term Change & Adaptation

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-23

All concepts in this file are candidates. They do not establish regional climate history, species responses, institutions, battle mechanics or future events until promoted through the project’s canon process.

The common design rule is simple: long-term change should emerge from dated observations and persistent consequences. A dramatic storm is not automatically a climate story. A climate story can exist for years without a battle.

## 1. The Year the Normal Changed

A regional weather service publishes a new baseline edition. Nothing about last summer changes, but newspapers now describe its temperature anomaly differently because the comparison period changed.

Use: teach players that baseline revisions change context, not history.

## 2. Two Valleys, One Regional Trend

A regional assessment shows warmer summers overall. One high valley has changed little while a neighboring basin shows much hotter nights.

Use: prevent a regional average from flattening local reality.

## 3. The Missing Highland Station

An old climate summary excluded a high-elevation station because its record was too incomplete. A later reconstruction adds enough verified observations to revise the regional picture.

Use: Metrology + Archives + Alpine without accusing the original analysts of concealment.

## 4. The Guidebook’s Typical Winter

A travel guide from twenty-five years ago describes a reliable winter route over frozen ground. The guide is authentic. The route is now unreliable in most winters.

Use: Library/Travel/Cryosphere continuity.

## 5. Five Earlier Blooms

An orchard’s flowering window has arrived earlier for five observed springs, but the observation method changed midway through the series.

Use: Flora + Phenology + Metrology. The story is about comparability before causation.

## 6. The Ferry Season Moves

A river ferry that historically began service after spring high water now regularly opens earlier. Operators revise staffing while scientists still debate how persistent the change will be.

Use: Freshwater + Workplaces + Transport adaptation.

## 7. Warm Nights, Ordinary Days

A city’s daytime temperatures have changed little, but night cooling has weakened in several districts.

Use: Urban Heat. The relevant adaptation may be shelter hours, shade, ventilation or staffing rather than a battle hazard.

## 8. The Fire Crew Starts in Bloom Season

Emergency-service staffing begins several weeks earlier than it did a decade ago after repeated changes in dryness and fire history.

Use: Wildfire + Emergency Services + Workplaces. No fire needs to occur during the player visit.

## 9. Reef Warning, No Bleaching

A reef experiences repeated heat-stress warnings, but local surveys do not document bleaching in one monitored season.

Use: keep exposure, forecast/warning and observed ecological response separate.

## 10. The Baseline Reef Photograph

A museum owns an old coastal photograph taken before standardized reef surveys existed. Scientists use it as contextual evidence while carefully recording its limitations.

Use: Photography + Museums + Reef Ecology + Science.

## 11. The Snowline Archive

Three generations of mountain photographers unintentionally created a useful record of late-summer snow patches.

Use: public memory becoming scientific context without pretending every photo is a calibrated measurement.

## 12. The Reservoir Built for Another Pattern

A reservoir still receives roughly similar annual precipitation, but more arrives in fewer events and at different times. Operators investigate whether old operating assumptions still fit.

Use: Freshwater + Public Works. Do not turn annual totals into a complete hydrological model.

## 13. The Spring That Became Seasonal

A spring remembered as perennial now stops flowing in some late summers. Groundwater monitoring is too recent to identify one cause confidently.

Use: Groundwater + Memory + Climate attribution uncertainty.

## 14. The Migration Arrives Before the Festival

A civic festival was historically scheduled near the arrival of a migratory population. Over several years the wildlife window shifts earlier while the public holiday remains fixed.

Use: Wildlife Migration + Festivals + Seasonality. The community can split the ecological observation from the civic date.

## 15. The Festival Moves With the Flowers

A different observance deliberately follows a bloom window rather than a fixed date. Organizers publish the date only after phenology observations reach a threshold they trust.

Use: successful adaptation can be administrative and low-drama.

## 16. The Old Rail Maintenance Calendar

A mountain railway still performs a major maintenance cycle on dates chosen decades earlier. Repeated shoulder-season weather disruptions prompt a review.

Use: Rail + Meteorology + Workplaces. The timetable is institutional history, not evidence of incompetence.

## 17. The Fisher Calendar Drifts

Fishers’ traditional seasonal observations shift over many years, but catch records are confounded by changed gear, regulations and effort.

Use: Fisheries + Climate + Metrology. Avoid attributing every catch trend to temperature.

## 18. Lake Turnover Comes Early

A limnology program records earlier turnover in several years. A single unusually early event had occurred decades before, preserved in an old field notebook.

Use: Limnology + Archives. Extremes can precede a trend.

## 19. The Treeline Does Not Move Everywhere

One exposed slope shows little woody expansion while a sheltered aspect changes markedly.

Use: Alpine/Forest layers. Elevation alone never determines response.

## 20. The Town Adapted Before Certainty

A settlement adds water-source redundancy after several drought years even though the long-term attribution remains debated. Years later, the redundant system proves useful for a completely different outage.

Use: adaptation actions can have benefits outside the scenario that motivated them.

## 21. The Climate Archive Was a Lighthouse Ledger

A lighthouse keeper’s decades of ordinary notes about fog, visibility and service interruptions become useful historical context after the station closes.

Use: Archives + Maritime + Meteorology. The ledger is observational evidence, not a perfect climate instrument.

## 22. The Tourism Season Splits

A destination once had one dominant visitor season. Warmer shoulder months gradually create two smaller peaks with different ecological pressures.

Use: Tourism + Demography + Ecology. More visitors are not automatically good or bad.

## 23. The Quiet Decade

A monitoring network produces ten years of data with no dramatic crisis and no major quest. The series later becomes essential for understanding a real shift.

Use: reward continuity without constant incident generation.

## 24. The Climate-Sensitive Collection Label

A museum label claims a species historically lived much lower on the mountain. The specimens are real, but later work shows the old locality names were geographically broad.

Use: Taxonomy + Museums + Identity/Place Names + climate attribution caution.

## 25. The Range Shift That Was a Survey Shift

A species seems to have moved north only because modern surveys finally sample an area that historical expeditions rarely visited.

Use: Science + Survey Effort. A good false positive for climate interpretation.

## 26. The Range Shift That Survives Review

A separate population has strong comparable observations showing a persistent distribution change after survey-effort corrections.

Use: demonstrate that skepticism should improve evidence, not prevent conclusions forever.

## 27. The Adaptation That Creates a New Conflict

A town raises a riverside path after repeated flooding. The new structure improves access but changes shade, public-space use and wildlife movement.

Use: adaptation has cross-layer consequences and no perfect solution.

## 28. The Old Crop Still Grows

A traditional crop remains viable, but its reliable harvest window has narrowed. Growers debate changing schedules, varieties, irrigation or land use.

Use: Food/Ag + Phenology + Water. No automatic yield formula.

## 29. The Historical Coral Story

An old collection shows that a coastal ecosystem once supported a different community. The cause of the historical transition is not assumed from the collection alone.

Use: high-level inspiration from official Corsola climate-history precedent without copying species/form extinction.

## 30. The Baseline Everyone Forgot Was Provisional

A widely quoted historical figure came from a sparse early network and was marked provisional in the original technical notes. The number was not fabricated; its uncertainty was gradually lost in public retellings.

Use: Metrology + Media + Public Memory + Science.

# Longer arcs

## Arc A — Thirty Years in Five Surveys

Year 0: an institution establishes a coarse regional baseline from uneven historic records.

Year 4: new stations reveal strong local differences. The baseline remains useful but needs spatial caveats.

Year 9: phenology and snow records begin shifting in the same direction, while annual precipitation shows little change.

Year 15: infrastructure planners compare several future scenarios and adopt two low-regret adaptations.

Year 22: one adaptation works as intended; another produces an ecological tradeoff and is revised.

Year 30: a new baseline edition is published. Old reports remain in the archive with their original reference periods.

Core value: a climate arc with no singular villain, no one decisive battle and no requirement that every predicted future happen.

## Arc B — The Valley Moves Uphill

A long mountain valley accumulates evidence of change across different systems: snow duration, flowering, tourism schedules, a migration stopover, treeline patches and water demand.

Different slopes and settlements respond differently. Some species show no clear distribution change. One famous “range shift” is eventually explained by survey bias. Another survives repeated review.

Over years, trail placement, public works, emergency staffing and research priorities adapt. The physical mountain remains the same persistent place while its expected seasonal use changes.

Core value: climate is expressed through interacting local histories rather than a world-level apocalypse switch.

## Arc C — The Climate Archive

An interdisciplinary project tries to reconstruct a century of regional change from weather stations, lighthouse logs, museum specimens, photographs, lake notebooks, flowering records, ferry schedules and oral/public memory.

Every source has different precision and bias. Several cherished local stories prove broadly correct but wrong in detail. A famous “record year” changes rank when a station move is accounted for. A forgotten quiet decade becomes the strongest comparison period.

The project ends with several conclusions and several questions still open. Its biggest result may be an infrastructure/adaptation plan rather than a revelation about a Legendary.

Core value: investigation, archives, institutions and environmental worldbuilding converge without requiring a conspiracy.

# Promotion guard

None of these candidates becomes canon merely by existing in this file. In particular, this pass does not establish:

- that Ouros is warming or cooling;
- that any region has climate change underway;
- any historical climate catastrophe;
- any Corsola population or regional form;
- any climate-caused extinction;
- any Legendary cause;
- any battle Weather or environmental damage;
