# Ouros paleontology, taphonomy & fossil-locality seeds — Pass 147

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. These are original Ouros candidates derived from Pass 147 research. Nothing here overrides canon or PTU/Caelo mechanics.

## 30 worldbuilding and adventure candidates

1. The Roadcut That Opened Overnight
A storm exposes a fossil-bearing layer beside a newly upgraded road. The urgent problem is documentation and traffic management before erosion and repair work destroy context.

2. Two Bones, Two Layers
Two apparently matching bones are found meters apart but in different strata. The museum wants a single display; field scientists refuse to combine them without stronger evidence.

3. The Famous Skull, the Quiet Sediment
A spectacular skull attracts press attention while tiny plant remains and sediment structures provide the stronger evidence for reconstructing the ancient environment.

4. The Trackway Under the Footpath
A popular walking route crosses a fossil trackway that had been hidden by sediment. Conservation, Tourism and Land Tenure now need an access solution that does not erase either history.

5. The Fossil Everyone Already Named
A specimen has carried a local nickname for sixty years. New preparation reveals anatomy inconsistent with the museum label, but changing the label has public consequences.

6. The Jacket Nobody Opened
A field jacket collected decades ago remains in storage because preparation capacity was never available. Modern imaging suggests it may contain several associated specimens rather than one.

7. The Riverbank Locality
A migrating river exposes fossils each dry season and destroys others during floods. The locality itself becomes a long-term monitoring site rather than a renewable loot source.

8. The Quarry Shift Change
Workers report a bone-like structure during extraction. Production can continue elsewhere, but the exact layer must be recorded before the face changes.

9. The False Mass-Death Story
A dense accumulation of bones is publicly interpreted as a single catastrophe. Taphonomic abrasion and mixed orientations suggest transport and reworking instead.

10. The Fossil With Modern Roots
Tree roots penetrate a fossil-bearing layer and complicate stabilization. Flora restoration and paleontology need a shared plan rather than treating the roots as simple damage.

11. The Burn Marks Question
Dark alteration on a fossil is called evidence of ancient fire in an early paper. Later work must distinguish heat alteration, mineral staining and modern exposure without forcing a dramatic answer.

12. The Missing Locality Card
A museum specimen is scientifically important but its exact collection site was lost. An old photograph, field notebook and rail timetable may narrow the source to one of three valleys.

13. The Quarry That Became a Classroom
A depleted extraction pit retains an exposed stratigraphic wall and becomes a field-learning site rather than being completely backfilled.

14. The Trace Without a Body
A long trackway has no matching body fossils. Several maker hypotheses remain plausible for years.

15. The Body Without a Track
A nearly complete fossil is preserved in fine sediment but there are no trace fossils. The absence remains a preservation question, not proof of behavior.

16. The Broken Cast
A famous museum display is a cast, not the original. Damage to the cast causes public concern while the original specimen remains secure in storage.

17. The Ancient Nursery Hypothesis
Several juvenile fossils occur together. The site may represent a nursery, transport concentration or preservation bias; the Chronicle records the debate instead of selecting a convenient answer.

18. The Fossil Under the Station
Renovation beneath an old station reveals a fossil layer below much younger human infrastructure. Archaeology and paleontology share the excavation grid but maintain separate context records.

19. The Coastal Cliff Season
A fossil-bearing cliff can only be surveyed safely during a narrow seasonal window. Each year exposes and removes different material.

20. The Preparation Reveal
Months of preparation expose a second skull beneath the matrix of a specimen already catalogued as one individual.

21. The Reconstruction Everyone Drew Wrong
A widely reproduced public reconstruction used an early anatomical assumption. New evidence changes the pose while the old posters remain historically valid artifacts.

22. The Fossil Donation With No Context
A private collection donates impressive specimens with minimal locality data. The museum can preserve them but must classify their scientific value separately from display value.

23. The Microfossil Year
A research season produces no dramatic skeletons but radically improves the ancient-environment model through tiny samples and repeated measurements.

24. The Restorer Request
A museum receives a request to revive a Fossil Pokémon. The actual conflict is authorization, custody, welfare planning and scientific loss if destructive processing is required.

25. The Living Result
A canon-authorized restoration succeeds. The new Pokémon immediately leaves the collection system and becomes a persistent living individual with separate agency, care and identity records.

26. The Wrong Layer in the Old Paper
A respected publication cites a specimen from Layer C. Digitized field notes show the original collector wrote Layer B. The correction affects regional correlation but does not invalidate all earlier work.

27. The Fossilized Burrow Network
A set of trace fossils preserves repeated burrows across multiple layers. Researchers debate whether the same species, same behavior or only the same environmental opportunity is represented.

28. The Protected Site Nobody Visits
A fragile locality remains important even though no quest occurs there for years. Monitoring records normal erosion, stable access restrictions and no major discoveries.

29. The Former Partner at the Dig
A released former partner Pokémon is observed near an active fossil locality. Its presence is recorded without inventing motives, ownership, archaeological interest or capture eligibility.

30. The Paleo Atlas
Decades of localities, sections, museum specimens, revised identifications and public reconstructions are assembled into a regional atlas whose historical editions remain accessible.

## Long-form arcs

### Five Layers at Meridian Basin
Year 1 begins with a roadcut exposing a fossiliferous layer. Year 2 adds a second locality across the river. Year 3 preparation revises one specimen identification. Year 4 a flood destroys part of the original exposure but exposes a lower layer. Year 5 correlation work shows the two sites were never exactly contemporary. The arc connects Geology, River Geomorphology, Museums, Taxonomy, Metrology and Public Memory without requiring an antagonist.

### The Specimen With Three Lives
A single fossil is first field-jacketed, later prepared, then exhibited as one taxon. Years later new imaging and comparison lead to a revised determination. A cast of the original exhibit meanwhile becomes culturally famous. If restoration is ever canon-authorized, the specimen may face a fourth phase where destructive processing and the creation of a living Pokémon become an ethical decision rather than an automatic reward.

### The Ancient River Project
Multiple fossil localities across a valley gradually reconstruct an extinct river system: trackways, plant impressions, isolated bones, channel sediments and one unusually complete specimen. Modern hydrology, transport and settlements occupy the same landscape. The story becomes a dialogue between ancient and current rivers rather than a dungeon chain.

## Encounter contracts

### Roadcut Fossil Rescue — FULL

Narrative premise: a fresh exposure is at risk while traffic, workers and wild Pokémon move through the area.

Full tactical version requires:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement for workers/wildlife crossing and interception;
- action economy/initiative;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_TECHNICIAN` and `REACH_EXIT`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions only if unstable rock, traffic lanes or active collapse have validated tactical mechanics.

Reduced version:
- close the road and move workers/wildlife through world state;
- document/stabilize the fossil outside combat;
- if conflict remains, freeze a safe static arena away from the fossil face;
- AutoPTU resolves only the combatants;
- fossil context survives regardless of battle result unless a separate world-state event says otherwise.

### Trackway Protection at Low Water — FULL

Narrative premise: a temporarily exposed fossil trackway crosses a route used by people and Pokémon.

Full tactical dependencies:
- base movement legality;
- complete movement for route-crossing objectives;
- AI tactical policy for `CROSS`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_ROUTE`;
- adapter/playback;
- environment family only if water level or slippery/fragile surfaces gain exact validated tactical effects.

Reduced version:
- establish a temporary reroute in Travel/Land Tenure;
- record the trackway before any combat;
- use a separate static encounter if a confrontation occurs nearby.

### Preparation Lab Emergency — FULL

Narrative premise: an equipment failure interrupts preparation of a fragile specimen while a separate Pokémon-related threat emerges.

Full dependencies:
- targeting/range/LoS;
- base movement;
- complete movement if staff/equipment evacuation is tactical;
- AI tactical policy for `EVACUATE`, `PROTECT_STAFF`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`;
- adapter/playback;
- hazards/reactions only if equipment, dust, debris or unstable supports have rules-backed tactical effects.

Reduced version:
- power down equipment and secure the specimen first in world state;
- evacuate staff;
- battle occurs in a static adjacent workspace;
- the specimen’s condition and preparation history are assessed afterward.

### Restoration Authorization Review — NON-COMBAT BY DEFAULT

Narrative premise: a scientifically valuable fossil is proposed for revival/restoration.

Dependencies:
- no battle engine required;
- Museums, Research Ethics, Pokémon Agency, Science, Taxonomy and any future restoration authority decide the case;
- if restoration is not rules/canon-authorized, the outcome can remain `NOT_AUTHORIZED` or `UNRESOLVED` without forcing a quest.

## Guardrails for all concepts

- Fossils do not respawn because a Minecraft chunk reloads.
- Battle victory does not determine species identification, taphonomy or stratigraphic age.
- `Paleontologist` remains mechanically unavailable until its PTU/Caelo contract and runtime mapping are verified.
- Restored Pokémon are living actors, not collection objects.
- Fossil abundance is not ancient population abundance.
- Missing fossils do not prove ancient absence.
- Rock/Ground typing, ancient Moves, ancient Abilities and prehistoric behavior are not inferred from sediment or morphology alone.
- No custom fossil-search rolls, excavation DCs, restoration timers, item rewards or damage hazards are introduced here.
