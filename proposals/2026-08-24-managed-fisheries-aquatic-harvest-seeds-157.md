# Ouros Candidate Seeds — Managed Fisheries & Aquatic Harvest — Pass 157

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Research-derived candidates only.
Date: 2026-08-24

These candidates assume the authority boundaries in `design/managed-fisheries-aquatic-harvest-layer.md`. They do not establish that Pokémon are routinely harvested for food, that any particular fishery institution exists, or that fishing access creates capture rights.

## 30 worldbuilding / quest candidates

### 1. The Harbor Learns to Count Effort

For years, Glasswater Harbor celebrates stable annual landings. A new analyst notices that crews now spend much longer on the water for the same reported result. The investigation becomes a debate about methods, technology, route distance and changing distribution rather than a simple "the population is collapsing" answer.

### 2. Two Boats, Same Catch

Two crews return with nearly identical totals. One worked a sheltered bay for an hour; the other spent most of the day offshore. The discrepancy becomes a practical lesson in why landing totals without effort cannot stand in for abundance.

### 3. The Survey Saw Plenty, the Harbor Saw Little

Independent survey stations report normal observations while harbor landings fall. Possible explanations include weather, changed access, market demand, gear, timing or spatial redistribution. No theft or conspiracy is assumed.

### 4. The Harbor Saw Plenty, the Survey Saw Little

The reverse disagreement appears. Concentrated catches near one aggregation create confidence among local crews while standardized surveys show weak results elsewhere. The story rewards maintaining both evidence streams.

### 5. The Quiet Catch Year

Landings fall sharply, but so does effort because a popular festival, rail project and several repairs keep many boats home. The year's data remain valuable but cannot be compared naively to the previous season.

### 6. The Closed Week That Became Tradition

A one-week closure has been observed for generations and is now part of local identity. Migration records suggest the spawning/run window has gradually shifted. The community must decide whether a cultural calendar and a management calendar should remain identical.

### 7. The Spawning Run Nobody Fishes

A spectacular recurring aggregation exists in a protected reach. Its value is tourism, science and public memory rather than harvest. The main design challenge is managing crowds and access without turning the Pokémon into an encounter farm.

### 8. The One-Day Opening

A tightly scoped community fishing day returns after several quiet seasons. The story concerns monitoring, event rules, local tradition and what happens after the event rather than maximizing catches.

### 9. The Bycatch That Wasn't a Pest

A non-target Pokémon repeatedly interacts with a particular method. Some workers call it a nuisance. Researchers suspect the activity simply overlaps a migration corridor. Gear review and route timing become more important than removal.

### 10. The Release That Was Never a Landing

A known persistent Pokémon was hooked and released years ago, but later summaries accidentally count the event as a harvest record. Correcting the ledger changes historical effort/catch interpretation without changing the Pokémon's identity or history.

### 11. The Former Partner at the Estuary

A released former partner is photographed near a recurring fishery. The observation does not establish why it is there, whether it participates in the same population, or whether it has any interest in its former Trainer. Pokémon Agency owns any later relationship.

### 12. The Landing Ledger Disagrees With the Survey

A decades-old handwritten harbor ledger and a research archive disagree. Both turn out to be internally coherent but measure different things. The resolution improves provenance instead of choosing a "winner."

### 13. The Small Fish Year

Crews report that interactions are increasingly with smaller individuals. The world records this as an observation requiring biological follow-up; it does not convert size distribution directly into a stock-status conclusion.

### 14. The Juvenile Year

A survey sees many juveniles but fewer adults. Optimists call it recovery; pessimists call it warning. Ouros preserves recruitment and adult-abundance assessments separately until later seasons clarify the pattern.

### 15. The Storm Year Without a Stock Collapse

Landings collapse after repeated storms, but independent surveys suggest the population did not. Harbor access, safety closures and route disruption become the actual story.

### 16. The Closure That No Longer Matches the Run

A seasonal control begins and ends on familiar dates, but Migration records show the movement window increasingly straddles the boundary. Updating the policy may be politically awkward even when the ecological evidence is straightforward.

### 17. The Old Net Method

An old method is remembered as efficient and culturally important. Reintroducing it for a heritage event requires reviewing non-target interactions and present habitat before assuming historical practice remains appropriate.

### 18. The Gear Change Changed the Data

A new method reduces handling time and changes catchability. Apparent abundance rises immediately in fishery-dependent records. The assessment team must avoid reading an instrumentation/method change as an ecological jump.

### 19. The Market Kept Selling After the Boats Stopped

Fishing activity pauses, but market stalls still have product from stored inventory, another region or prior landings. Market availability must not be used backwards as proof that the local fishery is active.

### 20. The Market Label Outlived the Management Unit

A famous regional label remains popular after scientists revise the boundaries of the stock/management unit it originally referred to. Markets, Public Memory and Fisheries now describe the same tradition using different scopes.

### 21. The River Stock Was Counted Twice

River and estuary institutions each counted the same migrating population as separate local units. Migration and Conservation Genetics evidence later suggests substantial overlap. Historical assessments remain valid as records of what each institution knew then.

### 22. The Stock That Crosses Jurisdictions

One aquatic population uses an inland river, estuary and coastal corridor. Three management bodies have different calendars, data quality and local priorities. The long-term arc is coordination, not institutional merger.

### 23. The Fishery Without Boats

A managed resource is accessed from shore, piers and seasonal footpaths. Maritime is irrelevant to most activity, reminding the system that `fishery` does not imply vessel traffic.

### 24. The Pier Camera Changed the Count

A camera system improves observation of landings, making reported activity appear to jump. The new number may be more complete rather than larger in physical reality.

### 25. The Landing Site Moved

Erosion or redevelopment relocates the harbor landing point. The fishery continues, but Supply Chains, Markets, Transit and historical records now need a new location revision.

### 26. The Protected Bay Still Has Activity

A no-harvest bay still contains research surveys, rescue work, public observation and transit. Closure of one activity does not mean the location is empty or inaccessible for every purpose.

### 27. The Emergency Measure Became Expected

During a past crisis, a temporary resource-use measure helped feed or supply a settlement. Years later, some actors treat it as normal practice despite the original end condition having passed. The conflict is institutional memory and transition, not villainy.

### 28. Three Records, Three Meanings

A harbor receipt, a fisher log and a scientific survey all contain the number "12" for the same date. One means landed batches, one means hours of effort and one means observed individuals. Identity/provenance prevents a false correlation.

### 29. The Fishery Archive Has Missing Effort

Catch records survive from an early era but effort logs do not. Modern analysts can describe landings accurately while leaving abundance inference data-limited. The honest outcome is uncertainty.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Pier Three

A season passes with ordinary monitoring, routine catches/releases and no crisis. Years later, that normal period becomes an important baseline. The Chronicle records it without generating a quest.

## Longer-term arcs

### Five Seasons at Glasswater Fishery

Year one relies almost entirely on harbor landings. Year two adds independent surveys after catches appear stable but effort rises. Year three introduces a migration timing shift and a narrowly scoped closure review. Year four brings a storm season that suppresses effort and tests whether the institutions learned not to confuse catch with abundance. Year five is deliberately quieter: the new assessment process works, routine decisions happen without PCs and the campaign sees institutional maturity.

Possible callbacks include a recurring fishing festival, the same survey station, several persistent Pokémon and old ledgers whose interpretation changes without retconning their data.

### The Stock That Crosses Jurisdictions

A population moves from an inland system through an estuary into coastal waters. Freshwater researchers, estuary monitors and coastal crews have each treated their observations as locally complete. Migration, tagging/photography and possibly Conservation Genetics gradually show that the units overlap more than assumed.

The climax does not need to be a boss. It can be the creation of compatible monitoring and management revisions that preserve local authority while acknowledging one connected ecological process.

### The Harbor Learns to Count Effort

A settlement has generations of catch stories but weak effort data. Players can help standardize logbooks, recover old route records, compare gear revisions and build a survey partnership. The strongest ending is not a perfect population number; it is an institution that understands what it can and cannot infer from its own records.

### Landing Discrepancy at Harbor Market

Primarily non-combat.

Players compare landing receipts, effort logs, supply-chain provenance, market stock and independent survey data. The correct outcome may be:

- reporting lag;
- stored product;
- changed effort;
- two management units using different definitions;
- unresolved discrepancy.

A battle cannot establish abundance, fraud or provenance.

## Engine guardrails for every proposal

Do not use Minecraft fishing as a substitute for PTU Fishing.
Do not infer population abundance from loaded aquatic Cobblemon.
Do not interpret capture/KO/despawn as fishery removal.
Do not make a released Pokémon owned, capturable or known-unharmed.
Do not turn nets, hooks or lines into Stuck/Restrained without exact validated rules.
Do not implement currents or deep water as forced movement/drowning in the adapter.
Do not treat Schooling, Pack Mon, Swim or Water typing as fishery-management mechanics.
