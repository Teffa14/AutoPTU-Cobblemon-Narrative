## 01 — First Safe Crossing

A pass that normally opens early in the cold season remains closed after repeated field observations disagree with the public timetable. Local businesses expect the crossing to open; route crews refuse to certify it yet. Players can survey markers, compare historical opening dates and help maintain the lower alternate path.

Useful links: Travel, Meteorology, Cartography, Workplaces, Public Memory.

## 02 — The Snowpack That Does Not Match the Forecast

A forecast correctly predicted snowfall, but one slope accumulated far more wind-transported snow than nearby monitoring stations expected. The story is about resolving local variation rather than proving that the forecast institution failed.

Useful links: Meteorology, Science, Cryosphere.

## 03 — Last Cabin Before the Pass

An old route cabin becomes important after a transport service suspends operations. Its heater, supplies, logbook and maintenance history all belong to different institutions. The cabin can later become a permanent survey post if the region chooses to fund it.

Useful links: Travel, Architecture, Infrastructure, Finance.

## 04 — Fresh Snow, Missing Snom

A research team expects Snom activity after fresh snowfall but records almost none. Explanations can include observation timing, human traffic, changed food availability, a route closure that displaced observers, or an actual ecological shift. Absence remains evidence, not conclusion.

Useful links: Observation, Science, Ecology, Tourism.

## 05 — Snover Below the Old Snowline

Several Snover appear lower than older field guides describe. A new survey compares snowline, food availability, seasonal timing and old observations. The discovery can lead to a correction of the guide without implying a regional crisis.

Useful links: Seasonality, Science, Archives, Public Information.

## 06 — The Empty Berry Bench

A winter storm destroys a high-elevation berry patch used by several wild groups. Competition appears farther downslope near a route shelter. Players can investigate food state, temporary feeding pressure and alternate habitat rather than treating one group as the aggressor.

Useful links: Wild Collectives, Food Ecology, Conservation, Travel.

## 07 — Ice Reveals the Fossil Bed

A retreating ice edge exposes a fossil-bearing layer for the first time in recorded memory. Scientific urgency, custody, conservation and route safety all become relevant before anything is excavated.

Useful links: Geology, Science, Archives, Custody.

## 08 — The Moving Survey Stakes

Markers installed beside a glacier no longer line up with the newest map. The markers were not moved by vandals; the ice edge and reference geometry changed. Older measurements become more valuable rather than obsolete.

Useful links: Cartography, Science, Public Memory.

## 09 — The Lake Beneath the Ice

A seasonal lake is visible in summer but becomes an apparently featureless frozen basin in winter. A historical photograph suggests an old route crossed it. The question is whether that route was ever formally authorized, not whether the current surface looks walkable.

Useful links: Freshwater, Archives, Credentials, Travel.

## 10 — New Blue Water

Glacier retreat exposes a small lake that did not appear on older maps. Researchers must establish outlet connections and monitor change before local media turns it into a new tourist attraction.

Useful links: Freshwater, Media, Tourism, Science.

## 11 — The Road That Exists Only in Winter

A frozen seasonal route shortens travel between two communities. Its availability depends on an explicit route decision each year. When the crossing opens late, commuters and deliveries shift to other services.

Useful links: Travel, Demography, Workplaces, Supply Chains.

## 12 — The River That Starts Too Early

Snowmelt begins earlier than historical local records suggest. The immediate river level looks manageable, but downstream farms and wetlands are more concerned about late-season supply. The arc unfolds months after the first observation.

Useful links: Freshwater, Agriculture, Science, Seasonality.

## 13 — The Rope Route in Retreating Ice

An old expedition route becomes unusable because its anchor points were placed in ice that no longer reaches the same location. The solution can be a new route, not a repair of the historical one.

Useful links: Travel, Architecture, Public Memory.

## 14 — Footprints Without a Return Track

A field team finds a sequence of Pokémon tracks that enters an area where wind later erased the surface. The missing return trail is not treated as evidence of disappearance. The quest teaches uncertainty through snow as an imperfect record.

Useful links: Observation, Cases, Wildlife.

## 15 — The Frozen Landing

A ferry landing becomes unusable when ice accumulates in a different pattern than expected. The ferry itself is functional. The harbor authority, route operator and weather service therefore report different but compatible states.

Useful links: Maritime, Travel, Infrastructure, Communications.

## 16 — School Snow Survey

Students maintain a simple long-term snow observation route under supervision. Years later, their records become useful to professional researchers because the observation method remained consistent.

Useful links: Education, Science, Archives.

## 17 — Festival Route Revision

A winter event traditionally uses a frozen promenade. Organizers move one activity to land after the route assessment changes. The event still happens, preserving cultural continuity without pretending the old route is safe.

Useful links: Public Events, Accessibility, Tourism, Cryosphere.

## 18 — Observatory Snow Blind Spot

Accumulated snow blocks access to a remote instrument and changes the local horizon. The astronomy program still has usable data from other stations. Recovery is a logistics and maintenance problem rather than a celestial mystery.

Useful links: Astronomy, Workplaces, Travel.

## 19 — Warm Room Supply

A mountain clinic maintains a small warm recovery room for cold-route incidents. A delayed supply shipment reduces capacity during a busy travel window. No medical effect is invented; the narrative problem is staffing, space and transport.

Useful links: Care, Travel, Supply Chains, Crisis.

## 20 — Avalanche Path Habitat Survey

A recurring avalanche corridor also supports an open habitat used by several species. A proposed barrier may protect a road while changing that habitat. The decision requires engineering, ecological and route evidence.

Useful links: Conservation, Public Works, Infrastructure, Science.

## 21 — The Glacier Front Picnic Problem

A viewpoint becomes popular after a widely shared photograph. Visitors move closer to a changing ice edge than the existing path was designed to support. The response can involve route redesign and visitor management rather than closing the entire valley.

Useful links: Tourism, Photography, Public Works, Cryosphere.

## 22 — The Newly Exposed Wall

Retreating ice exposes part of a constructed wall. Archaeologists, geologists and local historians disagree on its age and purpose. Physical observation remains separate from historical interpretation.

Useful links: Archaeology, Geology, Archives.

## 23 — Permafrost Under the Warehouse

A warehouse floor begins shifting seasonally. Maintenance records, drainage observations and frozen-ground measurements point to a foundation problem. Nobody needs to have sabotaged the structure.

Useful links: Architecture, Infrastructure, Workplaces.

## 24 — Tourism Moves Downhill

A high winter route closes for a season, moving visitors toward a lower town that did not prepare for the demand. Lodging, transport and wildlife pressure change without any population suddenly becoming permanent residents.

Useful links: Tourism, Demography, Travel, Ecology.

## 25 — Meltwater Orchard

An orchard depends partly on a late-season stream supplied by mountain snow and ice. Several low-snow years force the community to compare irrigation, crop choices and watershed restoration rather than blaming one upstream actor.

Useful links: Freshwater, Agriculture, Governance.

## 26 — Two Maps, Both Correct

Two expedition maps show the same glacier edge in different places. A public dispute ends when archival dates show both maps were accurate when produced. The correction becomes an educational exhibit.

Useful links: Cartography, Archives, Public Memory.

## 27 — Abandoned Lift Station

A small lift system stops serving its original route after snow patterns and visitor use change. The station can be demolished, preserved or converted into a field-research and rescue outpost.

Useful links: Architecture, Adaptive Reuse, Travel, Science.

## 28 — The Ice Cave Sounds Different

Long-term acoustic recordings show an ice cave's resonant pattern changing across seasons. Researchers investigate geometry, meltwater and opening size. Sound is evidence, not proof that the cave is collapsing.

Useful links: Soundscape, Science, Geology.

## 29 — Former Partner on the Snow Route

A previously released Pokémon is observed repeatedly along a seasonal snow route. Its appearance is recorded as an individual sighting. The system does not infer that it returned for its former Trainer or wishes to rejoin the party.

Useful links: Pokémon Agency, Photography, Wildlife.

## 30 — The Cache Under New Snow

A maintenance crew discovers an old emergency cache while replacing route markers. Its contents, ownership and age are unclear. The objects may document an older crossing network rather than become loot.

Useful links: Material Provenance, Archives, Travel, Custody.

# Long Arc A — Five Winters at North Pass

Year one establishes the route, shelters and baseline observations.

Year two opens late and sends traffic through a lower settlement.

Year three includes a large but non-catastrophic avalanche that changes one habitat opening and damages route infrastructure.

Year four focuses on redesigned markers, a new monitoring partnership and different tourism pressure.

Year five allows players to compare all previous records and decide whether the historical route should remain the region's primary crossing.

The arc is about cumulative world knowledge and adaptation. It does not require escalating into a boss plot.

# Long Arc B — The Glacier That Moved the Valley

A glacier's retreat gradually exposes rock, a fossil site, a new lake and an old built structure over several world years.

Each discovery belongs to a different system: geology, science, freshwater, archaeology, tourism and governance.

The large story emerges from their shared spatial cause rather than a predetermined conspiracy.

The glacier never needs supernatural agency.

# Long Arc C — The Last Snowline Atlas

Several schools, guides, researchers and residents contribute observations to an atlas of seasonal snowline and cold-route state.

Early editions contain gaps and contradictory labels. Later editions preserve those errors and corrections instead of overwriting them.

Wildlife observations, route openings, water timing and cultural events gradually become connected through the same archive.

The endpoint can be a durable regional institution rather than a final battle.
