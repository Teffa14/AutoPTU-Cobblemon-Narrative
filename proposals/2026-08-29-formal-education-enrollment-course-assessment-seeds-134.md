# Ouros Formal Education, Enrollment, Course & Assessment Seeds — Pass 134

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.
Date: 2026-08-29

Everything below is candidate material. Names, institutions, regions, schedules, rules and characters remain unapproved until promoted through canon review.

## Design goal

Create educational places that feel like persistent parts of the world rather than quest dispensers. Their records, alumni, classrooms, practical sites and student projects should survive staff changes, crises, transfers and player absence.

Academic state remains separate from PTU progression. A class may create a story opportunity; it does not automatically grant Skill Ranks, Edges, Features, Moves or Trainer Levels.

## Seed — The Class Photograph With One Extra Student

An old class photograph includes a person absent from the enrollment register.

Initial interpretations may include clerical error or falsification. The actual explanation can be mundane: the person attended for several weeks as a temporary guest during a regional disruption and was never part of the permanent enrollment ledger.

Evidence channels:

- photograph date;
- temporary placement record;
- attendance roll;
- host-family or dorm log if canon permits;
- later transfer note;
- alumni recollections.

Persistent consequence:

The institution adds a better archival link between temporary placements and class-year material.

## Seed — The Field Report Was Finished, the Course Was Not

A learner completed a difficult ecological observation project and gave a public presentation. Years later, an archive lists the course as incomplete.

The discrepancy comes from another requirement that remained unresolved, not from the quality of the fieldwork.

This supports a recurring Ouros principle: visible achievement and institutional completion answer different questions.

## Seed — Two Schools, One Semester

A storm, wildfire, infrastructure closure or other canon-authored event displaces a small group of students to a host institution.

The home school preserves the primary enrollment. The host creates local participation records. Months later, two archives describe the same period differently because one tracks enrollment and the other tracks attendance.

No fraud is required.

## Seed — The Old Battle Court Is a Garden Now

A current courtyard garden occupies the outline of a former training court.

Environmental clues:

- old drainage channels;
- embedded boundary markers;
- photographs on a hallway wall;
- alumni stories;
- a storage room still containing obsolete signage;
- plants intentionally arranged around the old court geometry.

The site can become an exploration/history scene without combat.

## Seed — The Student Who Keeps Showing Up After Graduation

A recent graduate still appears on campus several days each week.

Possible authored explanations:

- alumni volunteer;
- club mentor;
- temporary project assistant;
- sibling or friend connection;
- paid staff role;
- use of a public facility;
- unresolved capstone archive work.

The generator must not infer employment or authority from presence alone.

## Seed — The Exchange Student's Course Has Three Names

A course has one title at the home institution, another at the host institution and a third shorthand name used by students.

The story problem is recognition and provenance, not whether the learner secretly failed.

The player can compare:

- course identifiers;
- syllabus dates;
- assessment scope;
- instructor records;
- transfer packet;
- learner's own notes.

## Seed — The Certificate Was Printed Six Weeks Later

An alumnus says they finished in spring. The commemorative certificate carries a summer date.

Both can be correct.

Completion, approval, printing, presentation and archival entry are separate events.

This can anchor a historical investigation where exact timing matters.

## Seed — The Practical Site Reopened, the Class Did Not Return

A field station or training ground reopens after repairs. The course continues somewhere else because instructors found the alternate site better suited to the new curriculum.

The repaired location survives as a lightly used institution-owned place with old project boards, faded markings and occasional alumni visits.

## Seed — The Rental Pokémon Recognizes the Alumni

An institution-custodied teaching Pokémon responds familiarly to a returning alumnus.

This supports a social callback without changing ownership.

Required boundary:

- recognition can be authored from documented repeated contact;
- the Pokémon remains under its authoritative custody state;
- no capture or party-transfer assumption follows.

## Seed — The Missing Assignment Exists in Another Archive

A project is absent from the central student file but preserved by the department that hosted the practical.

The records answer different questions:

- student file proves program history;
- department archive proves work performed;
- course record proves assessment;
- research owner proves findings.

The quest becomes reconstruction of a record graph.

## Seed — The School Closed, the Club Survived

An older educational institution no longer operates. One student club continued as an independent community organization in the same town.

The club keeps traditions, trophies and some former equipment but no longer possesses the school's authority or full archive.

This directly connects the formal-education extension with the existing clubs layer.

## Seed — The Teacher Changed, the Rubric Did Too

A course changes instructor mid-term. Both instructors' assessment schemes are preserved.

Some learners completed work under the earlier rubric and others under the revised one.

The institutional question is whether local policy accepted both, not which instructor was “right.”

This creates a low-stakes records mystery that can later matter when an alumnus needs proof of a particular practical requirement.

## Seed — The Field Class That Never Reached the Field

A practical class was scheduled for a conservation site but a route closure prevented arrival.

The attendance register shows the students present at the institution. The fieldwork record shows no activity at the site. A later rescheduled exercise used a different location.

Useful distinction:

`PRESENT_FOR_CLASS != PRESENT_AT_FIELD_SITE`

## Seed — The Dorm Room Assigned to Nobody

A room remains labeled in old plans but is missing from current residential assignment records.

Possible explanation:

- converted to storage;
- accessibility adaptation;
- staff room;
- temporary isolation room under an old policy;
- merged into adjacent rooms during renovation;
- numbering scheme changed.

Do not default to a hidden student or conspiracy.

## Mystery — Five Times the Student “Transferred”

Five records use the word transfer:

1. application submitted;
2. records sent;
3. host placement began;
4. home enrollment ended;
5. course recognition completed.

Different NPCs quote different dates. All may be correct within scope.

## Mystery — Three Attendance Lists, Two Campuses

A temporary practical program uses a main campus and a field station. One list tracks morning check-in, another tracks departure for the field site, and a third tracks return.

A learner appears on two lists and not the third.

Possible explanation:

They attended the morning seminar but were reassigned before field departure.

## Mystery — The Graduate Who Never Took the Famous Class

Local memory says every graduate of a certain era took a famous course.

One alumnus did not.

Archive review shows the course was elective during that specific term or the learner completed an alternative requirement under a temporary curriculum version.

The mystery corrects public memory without humiliating the alumnus.

## Exploration — The Annex With Two Generations of Whiteboards

A disused educational annex contains modern whiteboards mounted over older chalkboards, storage cabinets with multiple numbering systems and a hallway map showing classrooms that no longer exist.

Players can reconstruct three curriculum eras through:

- room labels;
- equipment;
- archive photos;
- retired course manuals;
- alumni annotations;
- renovation records.

No new mechanics required.

## Exploration — The Field Station Beyond the Closed Footbridge

A former school field station remains physically intact but is no longer used by current classes because the access route changed years ago.

Its interior preserves:

- specimen drawers or observation logs if the appropriate owner permits;
- weathered project boards;
- old safety signage;
- route maps;
- student sketches;
- later maintenance notes.

The site can support environmental storytelling and archive-driven quests without implying current authorization to use it.

## NPC archetype — Registrar Who Refuses Easy Answers

They rarely say “yes” or “no” without specifying the record type.

Typical distinctions:

- enrolled here;
- attended here;
- completed this course;
- completed this program;
- received this recognition;
- was present on this date.

Their value comes from precision, not secret power.

## NPC archetype — Field Instructor With a Long Memory

Remembers practical exercises, route changes and recurring student mistakes. Their memories are useful but remain testimony, not authoritative archival truth.

## NPC archetype — Returning Alumnus From an Older Curriculum

Knows a version of the institution that no longer exists. They can help interpret old spaces while misunderstanding current procedures.

## NPC archetype — Exchange Coordinator With Two Calendars

Maintains the relationship between home and host institutions. Many apparent contradictions come from different term boundaries and reporting schedules.

## NPC archetype — Student Project Archivist

A student or staff member maintains old capstone projects, displays and project maps. Their role can connect current learners with forgotten local history.

## Faction dynamic — Curriculum Traditionalists and Practical Reformers

One group values inherited courses and institutional identity. Another wants more field placements and applied learning.

Neither side needs to be villainous.

World consequences can include:

- room reuse;
- different field-site partnerships;
- alumni reactions;
- club changes;
- new schedules;
- preserved legacy projects.

## Faction dynamic — Home Institution and Host Institution

During exchange or emergency placement, both institutions may want the learner to succeed while disagreeing about record format, timing or requirement equivalence.

This is administrative friction, not automatically institutional hostility.

## Long arc — A Town Learns in More Than One Place

Phase 1 establishes an ordinary educational institution through routines rather than exposition: students commute, staff teach, clubs meet, practical classes visit nearby sites and alumni appear at community events.

Phase 2 introduces a bounded disruption to one building or route. Some courses move temporarily, one field project uses a partner institution and a small cohort receives host placements.

Phase 3 follows the consequences rather than the emergency. Records arrive at different times. A student project survives at the host. One temporary classroom proves useful and becomes permanent. A club begins sharing space with a community organization.

Phase 4 introduces a curriculum revision. Older alumni interpret the changes through their own experience. Players can help preserve useful history without freezing the institution in time.

Phase 5 returns years later. The world contains layered evidence of several educational eras: renamed rooms, former field sites, old photographs, alumni NPCs, revised course paths and institutions that learned from prior disruptions.

No phase requires academic progression to become a PTU reward track.

## Encounter — Field Class Withdrawal

Full version:

A practical class is interrupted near a field site. Instructors must move the group out through a constrained route while a separate tactical threat is resolved.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED baseline
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — PARTIAL
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for protected withdrawal lanes or active environment
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING

Reduced version: READY.

The class is withdrawn before BattleSpec creation. Students, instructors, teaching Pokémon, records and field samples stay outside tactical state. A conventional static battle at the perimeter determines immediate access only.

## Encounter — Practical Site Access Diversion

Full version:

A class cannot reach a scheduled training ground because an unrelated encounter occupies the only safe approach.

Rich dependencies include escort/Intercept, possible protected-route reactions, tactical policy and semantic playback.

Reduced version: READY.

Students wait at a safe location. Explicit combatants resolve a conventional encounter. Education later decides whether enough time remains to conduct the practical.

Victory never equals course completion.

## Encounter — Exchange Arrival Perimeter

Full version:

A host institution receives an exchange cohort while an unrelated tactical incident affects the arrival zone.

Rich dependencies can include staged movement, escort, protected areas and semantic playback.

Reduced version: READY.

Arrival paperwork, luggage custody and student safety are resolved or paused outside BattleSpec. A static battle determines whether the perimeter becomes usable.

Victory does not create enrollment, record recognition or dormitory assignment.
