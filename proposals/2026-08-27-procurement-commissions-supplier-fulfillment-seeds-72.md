# Procurement, commissions & supplier fulfillment seeds — Pass 72

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes an Ouros institution, law, supplier, price, settlement, procurement procedure or mechanical item. Names are working labels for reusable story structures.

## Design intent

These candidates use procurement as a source of persistent world consequences rather than a purchasing menu. The interesting questions are usually:

- what is actually needed;
- who can plausibly provide it;
- what each provider really offered;
- what dependencies sit behind that promise;
- whether a substitute meets the approved need;
- what physically arrived;
- what passed acceptance;
- what changed afterward.

They should reuse established workshops, routes, facilities, institutions and NPCs once canon supplies them.

## The Sample Passed, the Batch Did Not

Premise:

An institution previously approved a sample from a supplier. The production batch arrives with visible variation or a different provenance trail.

Playable structure:

1. Recover the original sample and inspection notes.
2. Compare the received batch against the same approved specification.
3. Determine whether the difference is cosmetic, functional, mechanical, provenance-related or still uncertain.
4. Ask the supplier for an explanation before assigning intent.
5. Decide whether to accept, partially accept, request rework, or escalate the discrepancy.

Persistent outputs:

- acceptance history;
- supplier response;
- batch provenance;
- possible rework;
- future specification revision.

Guardrail:

A sample does not generate a hidden quality guarantee. Mechanical equivalence must come from actual PTU/Caelo/implementation evidence.

## A Substitute Nobody Approved Yet

Premise:

The originally ordered material or component becomes unavailable. The supplier can provide something else immediately, but nobody with valid authority has decided whether the substitute is acceptable.

Choices:

- wait for the original;
- verify the substitute;
- approve limited use if an authored procedure permits it;
- redesign the downstream work;
- source elsewhere;
- pause the project.

Useful tension:

The substitute may be genuinely sensible while still requiring review. The supplier is not framed as deceptive merely for proposing it.

## Three Suppliers, Two Real Routes

Premise:

Three responses appear viable on paper. Current Travel/Courier state shows that one provider has no functioning route to the destination during the required window.

The decision becomes a grounded comparison among timing, route state, provenance, compatibility and current capacity rather than an abstract price ranking.

Callback:

When the blocked route reopens later, the previously infeasible provider can become viable for a different need without retconning the earlier decision.

## The Order Arrived Before the Room Was Ready

Premise:

A shipment arrives on schedule, but the receiving room is still under maintenance or lacks the required installation condition.

Consequences:

- custody and temporary storage must be explicit;
- acceptance may remain pending;
- the supplier should not be marked late or incomplete;
- Maintenance owns room readiness;
- Courier owns the completed delivery leg;
- procurement records the receiving-side blocker.

The episode can later matter if storage condition, access or a second delivery intersects the same space.

## The Supplier Who Can Deliver Half

Premise:

No current supplier can satisfy the entire need inside the desired window. One can fulfill half now.

Possible responses:

- partial fulfillment plus later balance;
- split sourcing;
- reduce project scope;
- wait;
- use a verified substitute for the remainder;
- reprioritize which locations receive scarce goods first.

No automatic moral framing is attached to allocation. If a public institution chooses priorities, Civic Governance or Crisis must own that decision.

## Same Workshop, Different Lead Time

Premise:

A familiar workshop can make the requested object again, but its current backlog, missing specialist, seasonal input or facility condition changes the completion window.

Purpose:

This creates continuity without turning supplier history into a guaranteed service level. The same NPC relationship can produce a different operational answer because world state changed.

## The Rejected Part Becomes Evidence

Premise:

A delivered component is rejected during acceptance. Later, its markings or provenance become relevant to a separate investigation.

Handoff:

Once the item becomes evidence, Case/Authority custody takes priority over ordinary return/rework movement.

Guardrail:

Rejection alone never implies counterfeiting, sabotage or crime. The case must have independent evidence and claims.

## A Public Project with a Private Dependency

Premise:

A publicly visible construction or restoration project has a supplier dependency whose commercial terms or technical details are private.

The public can legitimately know that the project is delayed without receiving every private quote, supplier constraint or internal budget record.

Story value:

Media, rumor and public-notice layers can carry different claims about the delay. Procurement retains the actual source records and privacy boundary.

## The Repair Crew’s Missing Material

Premise:

A maintenance crew is ready and authorized, but one input required for the work never reached the staging area.

Investigation path:

- verify whether it was ordered;
- verify supplier acknowledgement;
- check whether it entered production;
- check shipment/custody records;
- check whether it arrived at the wrong site;
- distinguish procurement failure from courier failure or receiving error.

The answer can be mundane and still change future workflow.

## “Local” Means Three Different Things

Premise:

Several stakeholders ask that an institution “use a local supplier,” but they mean different things: located nearby, owned by residents, using regional materials, employing a local workforce, or belonging to an established cooperative.

Gameplay:

The party cannot resolve the issue by finding a hidden TRUE/FALSE local flag. They must identify which criterion was actually approved, if any.

Guardrail:

This seed does not establish a local-preference law or imply that any definition is canonically correct.

## The Standing Order Nobody Reviewed

Premise:

A recurring supply arrangement has continued for a long time because it was once sensible. The service it supports has changed, so the old quantity, timing or specification may no longer fit current needs.

The story is a review problem rather than an accusation.

Possible results:

- continue unchanged;
- reduce or increase scope;
- change cadence;
- split the order;
- update specifications;
- end the arrangement cleanly.

## The Emergency Purchase That Must End

Premise:

During a crisis, an institution used an explicitly temporary sourcing route. The crisis is over, but the workaround became convenient and staff keep using it.

The question is whether the temporary authority has ended, whether a normal process now applies and what happens to relationships created during the emergency.

Guardrail:

No emergency authority exists unless canon establishes it first.

## Specification Review

Type: non-combat institutional scenario.

Premise:

A supplier proposes an alternate component that appears similar to the requested one. Different stakeholders focus on different aspects: physical fit, mechanical behavior, provenance, maintainability, appearance, delivery timing or compatibility with existing infrastructure.

Resolution tools:

- approved specification revisions;
- item/mechanical reference data;
- samples and observations;
- maintenance records;
- supplier clarification;
- authorized technical review;
- provenance evidence.

Possible outcome:

`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` is valid. The story does not need to force an answer before the world has enough evidence.

## Mystery — Four Orders, Three Deliveries

Premise:

Records show four procurement orders but only three recorded physical deliveries. Someone assumes one shipment vanished.

Investigation structure:

- reconstruct exact order IDs and revisions;
- inspect supplier fulfillment records;
- inspect shipment manifests and receiving logs;
- check whether two small orders were deliberately consolidated;
- check partial fulfillment and backordered remainder;
- check whether one order was cancelled or superseded after the supplier acknowledged it;
- distinguish “not delivered” from “never required to ship.”

Valid endings include:

- two orders were consolidated into one shipment;
- one delivery satisfied only a partial quantity and a remainder is still open;
- an amended order superseded an older one;
- a receiving record is missing;
- the evidence remains insufficient;
- an actual shipment discrepancy exists and is handed to Courier/Case as appropriate.

The mystery does not default to theft or fraud.

## Long arc — A Buyer Learns Its Suppliers

Purpose:

Let one institution accumulate operational history over multiple procurement episodes without generating a vendor reputation score.

Episode 1 — Baseline need:

The institution sources a routine but meaningful requirement. The player learns who can supply it and which routes/workshops support fulfillment.

Episode 2 — Constraint:

A known supplier remains competent but encounters a real blocker: route disruption, missing input, facility work or staff capacity.

Episode 3 — Alternative:

A second provider, partial fulfillment or reviewed substitution becomes relevant. The institution records why the choice changed.

Episode 4 — Correction:

A discrepancy occurs. The supplier may correct it, explain it, dispute it or request a specification clarification.

Episode 5 — New normal:

Later sourcing starts with more evidence. The world remembers known capabilities and constraints, but every new response still depends on current state.

Persistent payoff:

NPC dialogue, workshop schedules, route use, stored samples, public project status and future sourcing can all reference real prior episodes.

## Encounter — Supplier Yard Transfer Under Pressure

Premise:

A completed order is staged for transfer when an unrelated Pokémon disturbance makes part of the supplier yard unsafe.

FULL intended version:

Workers withdraw through several lanes while identified cargo can change position. Some routes become unsafe. The tactical objective may be to protect a clear transfer/withdrawal corridor rather than defeat every opponent. Wild behavior can prioritize territorial withdrawal or obstruction. Exact playback preserves whether cargo actually moved.

## Encounter — Installation Window Interrupted

Premise:

A supplier/specialist crew arrives for an installation tied to an accepted order. A local disturbance interrupts the scheduled work.

FULL intended version:

Technicians move toward protected exits, active equipment and installation zones must be protected, safe access changes during the encounter, and the opposing AI can have withdrawal/territorial objectives. Playback preserves the exact installation stage reached before interruption.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING when active site conditions matter
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

REDUCED executable premise:

Technicians evacuate and explicitly pause the installation before battle. Dynamic machinery and protected assets are outside AutoPTU. A conventional static battle resolves the local threat. Maintenance/technical state then decides if installation can resume; procurement tracks fulfillment; acceptance remains a later review.

## Canon questions intentionally left open

- Which Ouros organizations use external suppliers at all?
- Which needs are normally handled by internal workshops or community exchange instead?
- Which procurement records are public?
- Are there regions or institutions with formal bidding procedures?
- What qualifications are required for specialized suppliers?
- Are supplier samples/common demonstrations culturally normal anywhere?
- How are substitutions approved?
- What constitutes acceptance for different goods/work?
- Are recurring orders common?
- What emergency sourcing authority exists, if any?
- Which supplier relationships are already implied by approved settlement/institution canon?

Until those questions are reviewed, every candidate here remains optional scaffolding rather than world fact.
