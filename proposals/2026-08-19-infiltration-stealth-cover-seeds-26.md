# Ouros Infiltration, Stealth & Cover Seeds — Pass 26

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from abstract research patterns. No external characters, plots, dialogue or stealth rules are imported.

## Seed 1 — The Shift Change Nobody Updated

A service entrance still recognizes an old staff roster because the institution changed departments faster than its access records changed. The useful question is not “can the players fake a badge,” but who notices the discrepancy, who still believes the old roster and what evidence the outdated system creates.

Potential outputs: case hook, access review, institutional embarrassment, partial cover, audit trail.

## Seed 2 — The Wrong Uniform

A visitor arrives in clothing that was correct last season but the institution recently changed its public-facing presentation. Some staff barely notice; one veteran immediately does. The mismatch creates localized questioning rather than an instant global alarm.

## Seed 3 — Guest List, Two Copies

A festival venue has two legitimate guest lists produced at different times. One includes the players’ contact; the other does not. The conflict can be resolved through contacts, provenance or patient social access before anyone considers covert entry.

## Seed 4 — The Botanist Who Belongs There

An NPC genuinely works at a research facility while secretly investigating a separate issue. Their access is legitimate; their purpose is private. Players must determine whether unusual behavior indicates infiltration, harmless personal work or something else.

## Seed 5 — Recognition by Partner

A recurring rival does not recognize a disguised Trainer’s face but recognizes the Trainer’s distinctive Pokémon from earlier public battles. The rival gains a plausible suspicion, not proof.

## Seed 6 — A Cover Burned in One Port

An alias is exposed during a failed operation in a coastal settlement. Months later the same alias may still function inland because the information never traveled there. If media later publishes the alias, the situation changes again.

## Seed 7 — Public Observation Is Enough

Players initially assume they need to enter a restricted depot. Careful observation from a public road can answer the actual question with less risk. The quest rewards understanding the objective rather than choosing the most dramatic method.

## Seed 8 — The Maintenance Window

A public-works crew legitimately enters a facility during scheduled repairs. Players with a real reason to accompany them can gain limited access, but their movement remains constrained by the crew’s mandate. The challenge is using lawful access intelligently.

## Seed 9 — The Quiet Warehouse

A supposedly important warehouse has almost no security. That absence is itself information: the faction may have moved the asset, the intelligence may be stale, or the site may never have mattered. The system should not spawn guards merely because the location is tagged “infiltration.”

## Seed 10 — The Substitute Patrol

A familiar guard route changes because one patrol member is absent and a replacement follows a different routine. Players with old reconnaissance receive useful but imperfect information rather than a scripted betrayal.

## Seed 11 — The Alarm That Was Correct for the Wrong Reason

A facility raises an alert after an unrelated maintenance fault. Security actors become more attentive while the players are nearby. The players did not cause the alarm, but their cover may now face more scrutiny.

## Seed 12 — The Visitor Badge That Works Too Well

A legitimate temporary credential grants access to more areas than the issuer intended. The players can report the flaw, exploit the extra access, or continue only within the stated mandate. Any choice creates institutional memory.

## Seed 13 — The Courier with Two Seals

Two departments use different authentic seals on the same class of dispatch. A guard mistakes the less familiar one for a forgery. Provenance can resolve the dispute, but the incident may reveal deeper communication problems.

## Seed 14 — The Research Annex Visitor

A scientist from another region is invited to one laboratory but wanders into a neighboring project assuming the same invitation applies. The event looks suspicious from the outside but may be an honest access misunderstanding.

## Seed 15 — Backstage Without a Crime

A performer needs access behind a festival stage to recover equipment before a public show. The challenge uses venue schedules, staff contacts and temporary credentials. The same architecture tests social access without criminal framing.

## Seed 16 — The Old Service Passage

Renovation records reveal a service corridor no longer shown on public maps. Its existence does not mean it is unguarded, safe or legal to use. Depending on current world state it may be sealed, repurposed, inhabited by wild Pokémon or used by maintenance staff.

## Seed 17 — An Alias in the Newspaper

A reporter publishes the wrong conclusion that an alias belongs to a known public Trainer. That misinformation spreads farther than the real evidence. The players may benefit temporarily while the wrongly identified person suffers consequences.

## Seed 18 — The Guard Who Remembers the Favor

A security worker recognizes a player from an unrelated rescue months earlier. The prior relationship may make the guard more willing to listen, but it does not force trust or grant access beyond the guard’s authority.

## Seed 19 — The Distinctive Pokémon Problem

A Trainer can alter appearance, but bringing a rare or famous partner undermines the cover. The player must decide whether to leave the partner elsewhere, accept recognition risk or use a different approach. No mechanical bonus or penalty is invented until PTU/Caelo rules are checked.

## Seed 20 — Two Covers, One Contradiction

Two players separately create plausible cover stories that conflict when compared. One NPC notices the inconsistency. The group can repair the story, split roles or abandon the approach; one contradiction does not automatically expose every identity.

## Seed 21 — The Archive Copy

The objective is to preserve information before records are quietly altered. The best outcome may be a documented copy with provenance rather than stealing the original. Evidence custody matters after the operation.

## Seed 22 — The Whistleblower’s Chosen Exit

An insider decides to leave a faction and chooses the conditions under which they will meet the players. The adventure centers on protecting that voluntary decision. The insider remains an actor with agency and can abort if the plan becomes unacceptable.

## Seed 23 — Authorized Red-Team Day

A civic institution invites Trainers to test physical access procedures during a controlled exercise. The resulting detection logs become useful public-works evidence. This supports stealth-focused play without requiring illegal activity.

## Seed 24 — The Leak That Predates the Players

A faction begins blaming the party for information leaks. Evidence later shows the leak started months before they arrived. The players can investigate the existing penetration while dealing with suspicion caused by timing.

## Seed 25 — The Employee with Too Much Access

A worker accumulates access through years of role changes. No single permission is fraudulent, but the combined result lets them see information outside their current responsibilities. The situation can become an institutional review, a case or an antagonistic opportunity depending on evidence.

## Seed 26 — The Patrol Responded Too Soon

A supposedly private meeting is followed almost immediately by a security response in another district. Possibilities include a leak, a hidden communication channel, coincidence, an observer the players missed or a false assumption about how private the meeting was. The response time is a clue, not proof.

## Seed 27 — The False Counter-Infiltration Scare

A settlement becomes convinced an outside faction has infiltrated local services. Several suspicious facts have mundane explanations, while one genuine information problem points elsewhere. The arc tests evidence discipline and prevents paranoia from automatically becoming canon.

## Seed 28 — The Forgotten Cover

Years after an operation, an NPC addresses a retired Trainer by an old alias. The Chronicle shows that this NPC knew the cover but never learned the real identity. That small callback can reopen social history without inventing secret post-retirement activity.

## Seed 29 — A Cover Becomes a Real Career

A temporary role adopted for an operation unexpectedly fits the character. After the covert objective ends, the player may choose to pursue the profession legitimately. The system records the player-authored transition rather than deciding it automatically.

## Seed 30 — The Infiltrator Who Never Entered

A hostile organization acquires sensitive information through social contacts, public documents and observation without placing an agent inside the target building. Counter-infiltration work must therefore examine information flow, not just restricted doors.

## Long Arc A — The Quiet Department

A regional institution slowly shows evidence of internal information leakage. Early signs are ordinary: a rival arrives at locations too quickly, a confidential route change becomes public, and one staff member seems to know a decision before it is announced.

The investigation should support several hypotheses. A hostile infiltrator is possible, but so are compromised communications, careless publication, legitimate overlapping contacts or several independent leaks.

If an infiltrator exists, they must have a real actor record, access history, motives, knowledge and plan. Discovery can expose only part of the network. Removing one person does not automatically repair the institution’s procedures or public trust.

Potential connected systems: cases, communications, faction attention, public memory, governance, social bonds.

## Long Arc B — Three Names of One Trainer

A recurring NPC has used different names in three communities for legitimate, ambiguous or covert reasons. The players first encounter the identities independently. Evidence gradually links them.

The arc should resist the cheap reveal that every alias implies villainy. One identity may be professional, one private and one genuinely covert. Different witnesses know different portions of the history.

The eventual resolution depends on what the NPC actually did under each identity, not merely on the existence of aliases.

Potential connected systems: identity belief, case evidence, public memory, relationships, media corrections.

## Long Arc C — The Counterfeit Scare

A high-profile incident causes institutions across a region to distrust credentials. Legitimate visitors are questioned, old access records become liabilities and several communities adopt inconsistent responses.

Players can help separate real compromise from panic, improve procedures and investigate the original incident. The aftermath can permanently change how access works without producing a universal surveillance system.

Potential connected systems: civic governance, communications, cases, faction operations, travel services, institutions.
