# Ouros Air Quality, Aerosols & Atmospheric Exposure Seeds — Pass 77

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros concepts derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character or prose is transplanted.

## 1. The Valley With No Monitor

A mountain valley repeatedly reports morning haze, but the nearest official monitor sits on a ridge above the inversion-prone lowlands. Residents disagree over whether the readings are reassuring or simply irrelevant to where they live.

Possible outputs:

- temporary mobile sampling project;
- dispute about monitor placement;
- revised regional coverage map;
- later confirmation that ridge and valley can diverge strongly.

## 2. The Clean Factory, Dirty Wind

A newly upgraded factory becomes the obvious suspect when nearby air readings worsen. Maintenance records show its emission controls are operating correctly. A broader source-attribution investigation finds that the episode is arriving from outside the district.

The factory can still have other environmental impacts. Clearing it of this one claim does not certify every operation as harmless.

## 3. The Dirty Factory, Clean Day

A facility with a poor historical reputation receives public blame during a hazy day. The plant is in fact offline that week. The real story becomes why old institutional memory remains believable and what evidence would be needed to restore trust.

## 4. Smoke Without Fire Nearby

A settlement smells smoke and loses long-distance visibility. No local fire exists. Meteorology and Wildfire state eventually connect the plume to a distant region.

This creates a cross-regional consequence without teleporting knowledge about the source.

## 5. Fire Ended, Air Did Not

Fire crews declare a wildfire contained. Two days later, smoke continues to settle into a neighboring basin.

The crisis layer can close the active fire response while Air Quality remains active.

## 6. First Rain After the Haze

A long particulate episode ends with rain. The sky clears quickly, but scientists now want soil and pond samples to learn what was deposited.

The visual recovery and ecological investigation move on different clocks.

## 7. The Weezing District

A district has a long cultural association with Galarian Weezing populations. Historical records claim they helped the city survive an industrial era, but the exact ecological and institutional relationship is poorly documented.

Possible questions:

- were the Pokémon wild, partnered, or institutionally cared for;
- did their population change as air improved;
- which stories are public memory versus measured history;
- does the modern district still depend on them at all.

No numeric purification mechanic is assumed.

## 8. The Koffing Accusation

Several Koffing are seen near a service tunnel after workers report a strong odor. They become immediate suspects. Instrument readings later show the odor event began before their arrival.

The Koffing may have been attracted to the condition rather than caused it.

## 9. Three Odors, One Week

Residents report sweet, sulfurous and chemical odors on different days. Public discussion merges them into one incident. Monitoring eventually shows they have unrelated origins.

This tests whether Ouros can preserve separate observations instead of overfitting one master explanation.

## 10. The Moving Monitor

A monitor installed decades ago was once representative of an open field. Urban growth has surrounded it with roads, buildings and new heat/airflow patterns.

The question is whether to keep it for historical continuity, move it for representativeness, or run an overlap period with two stations.

## 11. The Missing Calibration Month

A historical air-quality trend appears dramatic until researchers discover that one monitor's calibration record is missing for a critical month.

The old data is not deleted. Its confidence and interpretation change.

## 12. The Observatory's Bad Summer

An astronomy facility loses multiple observation nights to haze. The astronomy team initially treats it as bad luck. A multi-year air-quality record suggests a recurring seasonal pattern.

This can create cooperation between Astronomy, Meteorology and Air Quality institutions.

## 13. Harbor Haze

A coastal port shows poor visibility during busy mornings. Candidate causes include ship traffic, industrial operations, humidity-related visibility conditions and transported regional particles.

The investigation needs actual observations rather than choosing the most narratively convenient culprit.

## 14. The Dust That Crossed the Mountains

An arid basin experiences severe surface disturbance. Days later a distant highland region observes an unusual dust signature.

The source and receptor never need to be loaded simultaneously in Minecraft.

## 15. Snow With a Layer

A late-season snowpack contains a visible deposited layer. Cryosphere researchers preserve it as a dated atmospheric record.

Later melt connects the same episode to Freshwater sampling.

## 16. The Orchard With Spotted Leaves

A particular orchard develops leaf injury. Growers suspect a local pest. Air-monitoring researchers propose an atmospheric explanation, while Soil and Flora teams have other candidates.

The story can remain unresolved for more than one season.

## 17. The Pond Downwind

A sensitive upland pond shows a slow chemical/ecological change even though no obvious local discharge exists. Long-term atmospheric deposition becomes one hypothesis among several.

This is a slow research arc rather than an acute pollution quest.

## 18. Festival Air Plan

A large recurring festival creates temporary transport and cooking emissions while also attracting vulnerable visitors and Pokémon. Organizers develop a response plan based on monitor readings and weather conditions.

The plan can improve across yearly editions.

## 19. The False Clear Day

A dramatic haze disappears overnight. Public messaging declares the problem solved. Long-term deposition and health investigations continue.

This seed is useful for teaching players that visibility is one indicator, not the entire atmospheric state.

## 20. Viewpoint Economy

A settlement built part of its tourism economy around a famous panoramic view. Increasing haze changes visitor patterns even before any direct ecological harm is confirmed.

This connects Air Quality to Tourism without turning air pollution into a combat mechanic.

## 21. The Old Filter House

A historical building once handled air for an underground facility. It is preserved as industrial heritage, but nobody is certain which parts still connect to active infrastructure.

An air incident forces Architecture, Technology and Archives to reconstruct the old network before changing anything.

## 22. The Biological Aerosol Mystery

A monitoring station begins collecting unusual biological material during a seasonal bloom. Flora researchers suspect pollen; fungal researchers suspect spores; local Pokémon activity may be correlated.

No allergy, disease or status is inferred without health/mechanical evidence.

## 23. The Route That Looks Closed

Heavy haze makes a familiar pass look inaccessible from a distance. The physical route remains open and stable.

Travel information must distinguish visibility concern from route condition.

## 24. The Route That Is Closed

A different episode causes authorities to close an aerial or mountain service because observation quality is inadequate for safe operation, even though the route itself is physically intact.

The closure is an operational decision, not a geometry change.

## 25. The Public Dashboard Lag

An institution's public display still shows yesterday's favorable reading while field monitors have already detected a new episode. Nothing was deliberately hidden; the publication pipeline simply updates slowly.

This links Air Quality to Media/Communications and institutional trust.

## 26. The Community Samplers

Residents establish a volunteer sampling network after believing official monitors miss neighborhood-scale episodes. Researchers must decide how to integrate different instruments and methods without dismissing the community data or treating every reading as equivalent.

## 27. The Cleaner Decade

A city's air measurements improve across ten years after technology and transport changes. Some ecosystems recover quickly, others slowly, and public memory still describes the city by its old reputation.

This gives positive long-term consequences without resetting history.

## 28. Ash or Dust?

A gray layer appears on roofs after a distant eruption and a regional dust event occur within the same week. Visual inspection alone cannot distinguish the source.

Samples, wind history and timing matter.

## 29. The Silent Monitor

A remote monitor goes offline at exactly the moment a plume is expected to cross its sector. The lack of data becomes meaningful uncertainty, not evidence of clean air.

A retrieval expedition may follow.

## 30. The Pokémon That Stayed

Most visible wild Pokémon activity decreases during repeated haze mornings, but one persistent individual continues using the same location.

The system records the observation without inferring immunity, bravery, sickness, attraction or special Ability.

# Long Arc A — The Air Over Meridian Basin

Year 1: a few haze episodes are treated as isolated events.

Year 2: a monitoring network expands and reveals strong valley/ridge differences.

Year 3: wildfire smoke, local dust and industrial sources overlap during the same season, exposing weaknesses in simple source attribution.

Year 4: public advisories improve, but historical deposition studies find effects in a sensitive pond.

Year 5: the region changes transport, monitoring and restoration priorities. Air quality improves in some dimensions while public memory and ecological recovery lag behind.

The arc has no required villain.

# Long Arc B — The Weezing Years

A district's identity is built around old stories that Galarian Weezing cleaned polluted air during industrial expansion.

Research can uncover:

- old photographs;
- inconsistent institutional records;
- changing Weezing populations;
- historical factory locations;
- oral histories;
- modern monitoring data.

The final understanding may be more nuanced than either "the stories were completely true" or "the stories were a myth." The species can remain culturally important even if the historical mechanism is only partly understood.

# Long Arc C — What Fell From the Sky

Several ecosystems across a region show subtle changes over multiple years.

Early investigations remain separate:

- a pond chemistry study;
- leaf injury in highland vegetation;
- residue on an archive monument;
- a snowpack layer;
- visibility records.

Only later does research test whether atmospheric deposition links some of these observations. The result may support one source, multiple sources or no common explanation.
