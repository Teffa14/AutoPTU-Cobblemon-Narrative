# Ouros Interregional Mobility & Recognition Seeds — Pass 32

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros concepts derived from high-level research patterns. No external character, dialogue or plot is imported.

## 1. The Visitor Who Is Famous Somewhere Else

A successful competitor arrives expecting recognition. Most locals do not follow that circuit.

The interesting state is not humiliation. It is the difference between public achievement and local awareness.

Possible outcomes:
- one battle institution recognizes the record;
- a local rival becomes curious;
- media notices only after verification;
- ordinary residents remain indifferent;
- the visitor learns that reputation travels unevenly.

## 2. Reciprocal Field Access

Two ecological institutes agree to recognize each other's basic field-safety orientation.

The agreement does not grant unrestricted access to sensitive sites.

A player can complete local orientation faster because previous evidence is recognized, while still needing site-specific approval.

## 3. The Missing Referral Packet

A Pokémon arrives for specialist care from another region, but part of the referral record never arrived.

The clinic knows:
- the patient is expected;
- a referral exists;
- some details are missing.

It does not invent diagnosis from the absence.

The adventure can involve restoring communication, contacting the origin clinic or working with incomplete but legitimate information.

## 4. Exchange Week

A host academy receives a small group of visiting learners.

The week creates:
- guest lodging;
- shared practica;
- club events;
- local tours;
- translation/terminology questions;
- new social connections;
- temporary staffing load.

No visiting student automatically transfers permanently.

## 5. Return Ferry, Different Region

A Trainer returns after several months and discovers that the ferry still runs but the arrival district has changed.

Callbacks may include:
- a former café replaced;
- host contact promoted;
- a route under repair;
- a research project completed;
- new public signage;
- an old rumor now corrected.

The region remembers the player without freezing around them.

## 6. Tournament Town Over Capacity

A settlement hosts a major interregional competition.

The conflict comes from ordinary capacity strain:
- no lodging;
- crowded transport;
- clinic queues;
- temporary vendors;
- lost visitors;
- overwhelmed volunteers.

No sabotage is required.

## 7. The Badge Nobody Knows What To Do With

A visitor presents a legitimate Badge from another regional circuit.

The host institution can verify that it is genuine but has no authored rule saying what it qualifies the visitor to do locally.

Possible resolutions:
- record accepted as background only;
- supervised demonstration;
- limited equivalency;
- no equivalency but respectful recognition.

Never invent automatic cross-region Badge power.

## 8. Two Maps, One Pass

Researchers from neighboring regions arrive with different maps of the same mountain transition.

Both maps were accurate when produced.

A landslide and seasonal route shift explain the difference.

The quest rewards comparing provenance instead of deciding one institution is incompetent.

## 9. The Local Guide Becomes the Expert

A high-level visiting Trainer knows battle systems better than their host but knows almost nothing about local migration timing.

A low-profile resident guide becomes operationally indispensable.

This creates expertise asymmetry without lowering either character's competence.

## 10. Research Sample Handoff

A sample must move from one institution to another for analysis.

Interesting state includes:
- custody;
- preservation conditions;
- route availability;
- documentation;
- receiving staff schedule.

Do not invent sample degradation rules or medical effects without explicit systems support.

## 11. The Regional Term Nobody Translated

Two institutions use different names for the same technical phenomenon.

A visiting researcher initially believes they are discussing different things.

The resolution is language/terminology mapping, not a hidden villain.

## 12. Visitor Information Is Outdated

A travel guide was correct last season but still lists a route as open.

Local media corrected it, but the correction never reached some outside channels.

The resulting content can connect travel, communications and public memory.

## 13. A Famous Festival's Foreign Delegation

A recurring festival invites performers, researchers or battlers from another region.

The visiting delegation creates new content around:
- rehearsal/practice differences;
- lodging;
- public reception;
- collaborative events;
- historical exchange.

Their presence should not erase local participants.

## 14. The Specialist on Temporary Assignment

A technician, doctor or researcher is assigned to a host region for several weeks.

Their temporary presence creates new services while also increasing workload elsewhere.

When the assignment ends, the host must decide whether to train local staff, request another rotation or accept reduced capacity.

## 15. Cross-Region Rivalry Without a Grudge

Two recurring competitors meet only at interregional events.

Their rivalry history persists across years, while their everyday lives remain in different places.

The relationship can evolve through public records and occasional encounters without requiring constant physical proximity.

## 16. Joint Conservation Review

Two stewardship programs manage habitats connected by migration.

One region protects a nesting site. The other manages a feeding corridor.

Neither program can solve the full problem alone.

The arc focuses on shared evidence, seasonal timing and reciprocal access rather than political conquest.

## 17. The Record Is Genuine but Expired

A visitor presents a genuine institutional record whose validity window ended.

The host does not accuse them of fraud.

Possible outcomes:
- renewal;
- supervised reassessment;
- temporary limited access;
- alternate evidence.

## 18. Different Rules, Same Profession

Two regions have institutions with similarly named roles but different responsibilities.

A visiting Ranger-like worker or field technician assumes the title means the same thing locally.

The story explores scope and institutional culture without importing legal powers.

## 19. The Competitor Who Stayed

A Trainer arrives for a tournament, then chooses to remain for a longer personal project.

The system should not automatically mark them as resident.

Their state can shift through:
competition visit -> extended guest stay -> temporary assignment -> residence only if canon/player intent establishes it.

## 20. Mutual-Aid Arrival

After a crisis, a neighboring region sends a small specialist team.

The visiting group has skills and equipment but lacks local route knowledge.

Local responders remain essential.

This prevents external experts from replacing the host community.

## 21. The Guest Pokémon

A visiting Trainer's Pokémon repeatedly interacts with a local wild collective.

The system records observed behavior only.

Do not infer that the Pokémon wants to stay, migrate permanently or join the local collective.

## 22. The Handoff Nobody Owns

A case crosses regional service boundaries and both institutions believe the other side now owns coordination.

The result is a stalled case, not proof of corruption.

Players may help establish:
- responsible contact;
- evidence transfer;
- next action;
- explicit ownership of the operational handoff.

## 23. Regional Archive Exchange

Two archives agree to digitize or copy selected public records for each other.

Unexpected result:
old regional myths, battle records and migration notes become comparable for the first time.

This can seed archaeology, science and public-memory arcs without moving the original artifacts.

## 24. The Port That Became Important

A small arrival town becomes a major transfer point after a new interregional connection opens.

Over several arcs it gains:
- lodging;
- food services;
- visitor information;
- expanded clinic load;
- media attention;
- new jobs;
- habitat pressure;
- public works debates.

The town changes because of connectivity, not a generic upgrade tree.

## 25. Return Champion, New Rules

A former champion returns years later to compete in a host region.

The competition format changed since their last visit.

Their historical achievement remains true while local challenge conditions are different.

No automatic grandfathered privilege is assumed.

## 26. The Temporary Market

An interregional event creates a temporary market of crafts, food and services.

Some visitors return in later years, allowing:
- recurring vendor relationships;
- changed menus;
- apprenticeships;
- supply links;
- public-memory callbacks.

The market vanishes after the event unless state supports a permanent business.

## Long Arc A — The Open Route

A new physical connection links two previously distant regions.

Phase 1: survey and transport planning.
Phase 2: first service and limited visitors.
Phase 3: unexpected service pressure and ecological effects.
Phase 4: institutions begin reciprocal agreements.
Phase 5: host settlements adapt unevenly.
Phase 6: a review decides what should remain, expand or change.

The arc has no required villain. Its conflict emerges from connectivity changing real world state.

## Long Arc B — The Recognition Problem

Several institutions discover that their records are not mutually understood.

A battler, researcher, field worker and student each encounter a different recognition problem.

Their cases expose:
- genuine records with unclear local meaning;
- outdated verification channels;
- privacy limits;
- different standards;
- missing equivalency rules.

Players can help build a reciprocal recognition framework without creating a universal global credential system.

## Long Arc C — Host Region Year

A region hosts several interregional events in one year: research meeting, performance festival, competition and conservation review.

Each event affects different layers, but the cumulative result is the real arc:
- overloaded transport;
- new guest relationships;
- businesses adapting;
- public opinion changing;
- institutions learning to coordinate;
- returning visitors becoming familiar faces.

By year's end, the region is more connected but also must decide how much infrastructure should remain after peak demand ends.

# Mechanical/canon guardrails

- No passport, visa, citizenship, immigration or customs rules are implied.
- No Badge automatically grants regional access.
- No visitor automatically becomes a resident.
- No host institution automatically accepts another institution's credential.
- No foreign Pokémon automatically counts as invasive or illegal.
- No cross-region case automatically implies extradition or detention power.
- No visitor event generates crowd mechanics inside AutoPTU until objective/crowd support exists.
- No regional reputation becomes globally known without an information path.
- No public record reveals private medical, academic, psychic, housing or case data.
- No regional travel resets or suppresses Pokémon levels unless Ouros deliberately adopts a verified rule.
