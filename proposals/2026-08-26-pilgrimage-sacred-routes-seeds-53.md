# Ouros Candidates — Pilgrimage & Sacred Routes — Pass 53

Status: PROPOSED. Explicitly non-canon.

All names, communities, route histories and traditions below are placeholders for review. No Legendary presence, supernatural efficacy, legal authority or PTU reward is implied.

## Candidate A — The Emberglass Way

The Emberglass Way is a proposed multi-settlement cultural route linking old waymarks, community rest houses, a ridge overlook and a weathered terminus structure. Modern residents disagree about how old the full route really is. Some segments are clearly historic; others may be later reconstructions that joined separate local traditions.

Narrative function:
- create repeated travel through several settlements;
- give local stewards recurring responsibilities;
- connect archaeology to ordinary civic life;
- make route openings, repairs and closures visible world events;
- support hospitality, conservation, public memory and research hooks;
- let players build history with a place through return visits.

World-truth boundary:
The route's traditional explanation remains a `mythic_claim`. Archaeological observations can support or challenge parts of its claimed antiquity without resolving metaphysical questions.

### Station pattern

Proposed stations:
- South Ledger House — a public register, maps, notices and route-maintenance board;
- Ashstep Marker — a damaged waymark beside an ordinary road junction;
- Reedwater Rest — a small service community where route traffic affects lodging and supplies;
- Bellglass Stair — a steep ridge segment with strong seasonal winds;
- Quiet Basin — a conservation-sensitive stop where access may be redirected during nesting periods;
- Split Cairn — two nearby marker traditions with conflicting claimed order;
- Emberglass Terminus — an old structure whose original function remains uncertain.

These names are placeholders and should not enter canon automatically.

## Persistent arc — Opening Season

Phase 1: Pre-season survey
Stewards ask for updated route observations before the busiest travel period. Players can document erosion, stale signs, service closures and ecological notices. This phase should be mostly exploration and social play.

Phase 2: Competing route orders
Two communities publish different customary sequences. A newly documented marker appears to support neither version cleanly. The goal is to gather provenance and understand why each order exists, not to declare one community fraudulent.

Phase 3: Visitor surge
Public attention increases after a widely shared report. Hospitality demand rises, trail wear becomes visible and a quiet station receives more traffic than its stewards expected. Players may help coordinate notices, alternate routes and local services.

Phase 4: Bellglass closure
Strong seasonal conditions and structural damage make one ridge segment unsuitable for ordinary travel. A cultural observance is approaching. Stewards must choose whether to suspend the segment, reroute the sequence, limit access or move part of the event.

Phase 5: Return after repair
The route reopens later. Changes made during the closure persist: repaired infrastructure, altered public expectations, ecological recovery, revised maps and perhaps a new historical interpretation.

No phase requires combat.

The story premise remains: regain safe access to the route and protect stranded travelers.

## Non-combat hook — The Missing Sequence

A restored archive record indicates that one station may have been added to the customary route much later than public signage claims.

Possible outcomes:
- stewards amend interpretation signage;
- a community keeps the current sequence for living cultural reasons while acknowledging the newer historical evidence;
- researchers revise a publication;
- tourism material lags behind the correction;
- players discover that historical accuracy and current tradition answer different questions.

No hidden canonical answer is generated unless authored evidence exists.

## Long-term callback hooks

A later season can reuse the same route with changed state:
- repaired markers show who funded or performed work;
- a rest house changes ownership or staffing;
- a previously quiet station becomes popular;
- a bypass becomes semi-permanent;
- ecological monitoring changes access dates;
- a disputed interpretation becomes more or less accepted;
- a player promise to return can create a relationship callback;
- public memory can remember whether the group respected a closure or ignored steward guidance.

None of these memories grant automatic mechanical bonuses.
