# Ouros Batch Traceability & Recall Seeds — Pass 73

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

These are original Ouros candidates derived from the research patterns in Pass 73. No institution, location, technology, law, item, medicine, supplier or policy below is canon unless separately reviewed.

## 1. The Shelf That Emptied Before Anyone Knew Why

A familiar storefront suddenly removes one ordinary product line from display.

What the player can observe:

- an empty section that was stocked on prior visits;
- staff answering with a cautious “held pending review” instead of a diagnosis;
- one older customer claiming the product was “always fine”;
- a notice that names a product family but not yet an exact affected batch.

Possible truths:

- a counterfeit report triggered a broad precautionary hold;
- a supplier found a packaging mismatch;
- one batch is suspect while older stock is safe;
- the storefront received a correction after the initial notice;
- no defect is ever confirmed, but the trace still matters.

Persistent consequences:

- shelf state changes;
- staff routines change;
- Procurement may seek alternatives;
- later correction or clearance updates the notice but preserves the old one in history.

## 2. Same Label, Different Batch

Two visually identical containers are found in different facilities. One is inside the affected scope and one is not.

The challenge is to compare:

- batch/provenance marks;
- receipt dates;
- shipment history;
- storage location;
- supplier response;
- any correction notice.

The player can solve the practical problem without proving root cause.

## 3. The Counterfeit That Copied a Real Serial

A suspect device carries a serial/mark that matches an authentic item elsewhere.

This prevents the system from treating “valid-looking serial” as proof of authenticity.

Useful scenes:

- compare wear, repair marks and custody history;
- find when the duplicate mark first appeared;
- identify which unit has continuous provenance;
- avoid accusing the current holder merely because they possess the suspect unit.

Potential escalation to Case/Authority occurs only if evidence and canon support it.

## 4. The Replacement Part Already Installed

A maintenance notice identifies a component family after several units have already been installed.

The facility is not automatically unsafe and not automatically safe.

The player may need to:

- identify which installation used which batch;
- determine whether a repaired machine received a different component later;
- schedule inspection windows;
- separate “affected component installed” from “failure observed”;
- help keep unaffected services operating.

The Maintenance layer owns technical outcome and reopening.

## 5. The Clinic Has Stock, But Not Usable Stock

A care facility appears fully supplied, yet part of its stock is held pending review.

Narrative tension comes from availability rather than invented medical effects.

Possible choices:

- seek replacement stock;
- transfer cleared stock from another facility if world state allows;
- prioritize service changes under Care authority;
- trace a batch already used earlier;
- contact travellers who may hold issued units.

The new layer never decides treatment, dosage or health effects.

## 6. The Notice Reached the Shop, Not the Expedition

A direct notice reaches the originating storefront after a field team has already departed with issued equipment from the same batch.

The players must work through existing Communications and Travel state:

- what channel can plausibly reach the team;
- whether the team has coverage;
- who knows their current route;
- whether a courier or rendezvous can intercept them;
- what the notice actually instructs.

The equipment remains mechanically ordinary unless exact rules say otherwise.

## 7. The Correction That Looks Like a Recall

A public notice says a device needs an updated instruction plate or minor replacement component.

Rumour turns this into “all units are dangerous”.

Useful conflict:

- storefront staff lose customers despite unaffected stock;
- an institution must clarify scope;
- old signage remains visible somewhere;
- a user who already received the correction still believes their unit is recalled;
- Public Memory can preserve how the misunderstanding spread.

## 8. The Batch That Was Never Distributed

An investigation initially covers four production lots. One lot appears in manufacturing records but never left the workshop.

The world can narrow the action without erasing the earlier broad notice.

This seed teaches players that production, release and distribution are separate events.

## 9. The Returned Unit That No Longer Matches the Records

A recovered unit has the correct outer case and maker mark, but its internal component was replaced during an older repair.

Questions:

- which identity level does the current action target;
- does the replacement component have its own provenance;
- was the repair documented elsewhere;
- is the current unit actually affected;
- does it need inspection rather than recovery.

This connects Material Culture and Maintenance without turning either into a hidden forensic engine.

## 10. The Supplier Corrected the Process, the World Has Not Caught Up

A supplier verifies a process correction and new production begins, but old affected units remain scattered through the distribution network.

The story therefore splits:

- upstream cause correction;
- downstream trace/recovery;
- replacement supply;
- public confidence;
- old notice cleanup.

Fixing the source does not close every downstream record.

## 11. Three Reports, One Original Incident

Three storefront workers report “the same defect”. Investigation shows that two reports were repeated from the first report rather than independent observations.

This uses the Rumor/Testimony layer to prevent false corroboration.

The practical hold can remain appropriate even while the evidence count changes.

## 12. The Product Everyone Keeps Using Anyway

A voluntary or advisory hold exists, but not every holder agrees with it.

This seed should only be used after canon establishes the relevant authority and agency boundaries.

The narrative value is social:

- one user trusts long personal experience;
- another stops using immediately;
- staff cannot simply confiscate property without authority;
- the notice may recommend review rather than forced surrender.

Do not create legal powers by convenience.

## 13. The Unlocated Pair

Records say ten units entered circulation. Eight are reconciled. Two remain unlocated.

Weeks later:

- one appears in Found Property;
- the other was forwarded with a household move;
- or the trace remains unresolved permanently.

The system should allow closure with documented unresolved units when the owning authority permits it rather than fabricating a perfect ending.

## 14. Event Week Substitute Pressure

A temporary event loses access to an affected batch just before opening.

Event Operations wants replacement supply quickly. Procurement finds a substitute. The substitute is plausible but not mechanically or culturally equivalent by default.

The player may:

- help validate non-mechanical compatibility;
- accept a reduced activity schedule;
- redirect cleared stock;
- communicate the change;
- leave the substitute unapproved.

Narrative pressure never authorizes invented item mechanics.

## 15. A Workshop Learns to Trace Its Work

Long-form recurring arc.

Visit 1:
A workshop produces ordinary goods with minimal provenance because no previous problem required more.

Visit 2:
A mismatch or component problem forces staff to reconstruct which outputs used which input batch.

Visit 3:
The workshop adds a simple inspection/marking practice.

Visit 4:
Another issue occurs, but the improved records narrow the affected scope quickly.

Visit 5:
A player revisits months later and can see the changed intake shelves, marking station and archived notices.

The arc creates institutional learning without a hidden “quality level”.

## 16. A Storefront Learns the Difference Between Alert and Withdrawal

Long-form social/service arc.

An initial concern causes staff to remove too much stock. Later clarification restores some items. A second episode years later is handled more precisely because staff remember the earlier confusion.

Memory should live in staff experience, procedures and physical world changes, not a universal recall bonus.

## 17. Mystery — Seven Units, Six Handoffs

Premise:

A maker records seven outputs. Procurement records seven accepted units. Courier has six downstream handoffs.

Candidate explanations:

- two units were consolidated into one package;
- one stayed at the receiving depot;
- one was installed directly and skipped a storefront record;
- one handoff is missing from the log;
- one unit never physically existed despite a duplicate administrative line;
- a real loss occurred.

Evidence sources:

- maker batch record;
- procurement receipt;
- courier manifest/handoffs;
- current custody;
- installation history;
- found-property record;
- witness statements.

No culprit is required.

## 18. Mystery — The Safe Batch With the Bad Reputation

A batch excluded from the final affected scope continues to be avoided because early public messaging named the entire product family.

The player can reconstruct:

- initial broad scope;
- later narrowed scope;
- who observed which notice version;
- what storefront signage remains;
- whether direct correction reached regular users.

This is a Public Memory/Communications problem after the technical scope is resolved.

## 19. Encounter — Warehouse Recovery Window

Full version:

Staff are recovering affected containers from several storage zones while a legitimate Pokémon threat disrupts the depot. The tactical version wants moving staff, protected routes, inaccessible zones, environmental hazards and objective-aware AI.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement/forced movement/interception — BLOCKING;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full damage — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Staff evacuate and place safely reachable stock into quarantine before combat. Remaining areas stay explicitly unresolved. AutoPTU receives a static conventional battle arena. After battle, recovery resumes from the exact pre-battle trace state.

## 20. Encounter — Restricted Shelf Breakout

Premise:

A shop has already blocked a section containing affected goods when a Pokémon incident occurs in the building.

Full version wants civilian withdrawal, protected fixtures, multiple exits, potentially changing access and AI that prefers escape/territory rather than pure KO.

Reduced version:

The shop closes, customers leave and affected stock is sealed in world state. The battle takes place in a cleared static area. Storefront state and recall state are reviewed afterward.

Battle victory does not clear stock.

## 21. Encounter — Component Retrieval at a Closed Facility

Premise:

Maintenance needs to identify and remove an affected component from a facility already shut down for technical reasons. A wild Pokémon group now occupies part of the inactive space.

Full version wants territory/withdrawal goals, narrow routing, environmental zones, protected equipment and tactical AI.

Reduced version:

Technicians do not enter during combat. The component remains outside the tactical grid. AutoPTU resolves a static encounter, then Maintenance performs inspection/removal afterward.

## 22. Non-combat encounter — Trace the Forwarded Unit

A unit was issued to a resident, who later moved, then lent it to another household member, then returned it through a temporary desk.

Use:

- Residential occupancy history;
- Shared Equipment custody if applicable;
- Public Notices/Communications;
- Found Property;
- timestamps and actor knowledge.

The solution is about custody, not guilt.

## 23. Non-combat encounter — Which Notice Did They Read?

Several NPCs act on different versions of the same market action.

The player reconstructs:

- publication time;
- physical board update time;
- direct-message delivery;
- travel absence;
- whether the actor ever revisited the notice location.

This can create believable disagreement without hidden irrationality.
