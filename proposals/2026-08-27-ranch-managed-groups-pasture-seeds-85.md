## Design intent

These candidates use one persistent rural site repeatedly. They prioritize continuity, observation, care, work history, route state and ecology over generic fetch quests or mandatory battles.

Every concept should be attached later to approved Ouros geography and institutions only after canon review.

## The Gate Was Open, the Group Wasn't Gone

Morning staff find an outer gate open. The first count appears low.

The obvious assumption is escape, but yesterday's records show several known individuals were moved legally to a secondary zone and one is still under a Care handoff. A direct recount also reveals that two background Pokémon were hidden from the first viewing angle.

Useful systems:
- managed-group count reconciliation;
- worksite observation;
- maintenance condition of the gate;
- Care handoff;
- testimony/provenance.

Possible outcomes include a fully reconciled count, a genuine unresolved discrepancy, a maintenance issue or a stale handoff record. The scenario does not require theft, negligence or combat.

## Pasture Three Is Resting

A pasture visible from a public route is unexpectedly empty. Visitors begin asking whether the ranch is failing.

The real operational state may be an authored rest window linked to habitat recovery, water maintenance, a seasonal nesting buffer or another documented condition. The managed group is using a different zone.

The content connects Public Notices, Seasonality, Conservation and rural continuity. The empty landscape becomes environmental storytelling rather than evidence of crisis.

## One Individual Off the Line

One persistent Pokémon stops joining a familiar production or service routine.

The ranch layer records the deviation and opens a Care referral. The downstream service may become LIMITED until the owner system decides what the individual can safely do.

The player's role could involve observation, transport to care, finding a substitute route or helping the site operate at reduced capacity.

No diagnosis, status effect or yield penalty is generated by the narrative layer.

## The Working Partner Calls the Old Boundary

A Pokémon with a long-standing assignment uses a signaling or guiding routine that used to match the paddock layout. Recent maintenance moved a gate and changed the safe route.

The managed group hesitates or drifts toward the former opening. The problem is not automatically disobedience or poor training. The task requirements and work-assignment evidence are now stale because the physical procedure changed.

A new supervised trial, changed marker, altered route or reassignment may solve the issue.

## The Route Crosses a New Nest

A routine seasonal movement corridor overlaps an area where wild Pokémon are repeatedly observed nesting or sheltering.

The ranch operator wants predictability. Conservation actors want more evidence before disturbance continues. Travel/public-route users may also depend on the same corridor.

The player can support monitoring, temporary rerouting, timing changes or a direct encounter if one genuinely occurs.

No side automatically owns the route merely because it used it first.

## The Morning Count Has Two Numbers

Two records disagree before anyone enters the paddock.

One count comes from a shift handoff. Another comes from a later service log. The discrepancy may involve an individual temporarily assigned off-site, a returned Pokémon that was not checked back in, an aggregate estimate copied forward or a duplicate identifier.

This is a provenance mystery before it is a missing-Pokémon mystery.

## Temporary Upper Meadow

A lower zone becomes unusable for a limited window because of maintenance, water problems or documented environmental pressure. A higher meadow is available, but moving the group changes staffing, route timing and supervision needs.

The interesting choice is operational: accept reduced capacity, move the group, split it, or temporarily relocate some members.

If exact traversal depends on Pokémon capability, Travel and Pokémon Work must validate it.

## The Milk Route Changed First

A rural service remains functional, but the usual road no longer is. Customers in the nearest settlement notice changed delivery timing before they learn what happened upstream.

Courier/Travel state changes first. Storefront availability may change later. The ranch itself can remain healthy and productive.

This seed demonstrates why production, transport and retail must not share one global `AVAILABLE` flag.

The product need not be milk in final canon.

## The Ranch Becomes a Refuge

A crisis elsewhere temporarily places additional Pokémon or people at a rural site because it has shelter, water, space or staff.

Temporary refuge records coexist with the managed group's ordinary membership. Guests do not become owned by the host site. A high temporary count does not redefine the ranch's normal capacity forever.

After the crisis, some physical traces can remain: changed fencing, a temporary shelter converted to storage, a new service contact or a revised emergency plan.

## Same Species, Different Zone History

Two recurring Pokémon of the same species are known individually because their site histories differ.

One has repeatedly used the public-edge paddock and is calm around visitors. Another has only been observed in a quieter interior zone. That history can affect authored handling plans without generating a hidden temperament score or guaranteed behavior.

The scenario reinforces individual continuity without requiring full mechanical stat blocks for every background member.

## The Broken Fence Is Not the Cause

Several managed Pokémon are found outside the expected zone near a visibly damaged fence.

The fence looks like the obvious explanation. Timeline evidence shows the damage occurred after the first boundary observation, or on a different section. The actual cause remains unresolved or is traced to a gate handoff, changed route or other event.

The mystery teaches the player to preserve chronology rather than accepting the most visible clue.

## A Paddock Becomes a Quiet Zone

A marginal paddock is used less often over several seasons. Wild observations gradually increase there. The ranch eventually faces a choice: resume intensive use, keep it as an occasional fallback, coordinate a seasonal quiet window, or hand the ecological question to a formal stewardship process.

No choice is mechanically superior by default.

## The Public Path and the Movement Window

A path used by residents crosses a managed movement corridor for only a short recurring window. A new event, tourism increase or schedule change creates crowd pressure exactly when the group normally moves.

Possible responses include notice timing, temporary path diversion, altered movement window, supervised crossing or a new infrastructure proposal.

The conflict can be solved entirely without battle.

## Five Tags, Four Exact Pokémon

Five physical identifiers appear in old records, but only four exact Pokémon can be tied confidently to the current managed group.

One identifier may have been replaced, reused incorrectly, attached to a temporary guest or copied from an older register. Wildlife Monitoring/Telemetry should be consulted if actual tracking devices are involved.

This mystery must not infer death, theft or replacement without evidence.

## Encounter — Paddock Withdrawal

Narrative premise:
A managed group must be kept away from a disturbed gate area while a separate wild encounter blocks the route.

Intended full version:
The managed group withdraws along an active corridor outside or at the edge of tactical space. Explicit combatants may use legal Intercept and forced movement. Wild AI can prefer territorial denial or withdrawal. Environment state can matter only through validated AutoPTU controllers. Minecraft playback must keep background managed Pokémon visibly distinct from combatants.

Reduced version:
Ouros stops the managed group outside the battle area, removes workers/noncombatants, creates an explicit combatant manifest and runs a static legal battle. Afterward the movement plan resumes, reroutes or remains paused. The game does not simulate stampede damage, escort HP or scripted displacement.

## Encounter — Grazing Route Wildlife Conflict

Narrative premise:
A scheduled managed-group route crosses a repeatedly observed wild-use area.

Intended full version:
The encounter can express CLEAR_ROUTE/WITHDRAW intents, territorial or retreat priorities, Intercept at chokepoints, forced movement and reviewed environmental state. The managed group itself remains distinct from battle participants unless Ouros explicitly selects an individual.

Reduced version:
The managed group stops before the contested segment. A static legal encounter occurs only if the world state actually produces combat. Conservation and Travel decide afterward whether the route continues, changes time, reroutes or suspends. Battle victory never erases the ecological observation or creates permanent access rights.

## Noncombat mystery — Morning Count Reconciliation

Expected group count and direct observation disagree.

The player can compare exact known member IDs, yesterday's group-movement event, Care referrals, work assignments, temporary refuge records and shift handoffs. A correct conclusion may be `DISCREPANCY_REMAINS`.

The system should reward an accurate unresolved result over a fabricated complete answer.

## Long arc — A Ranch Learns Its Range

Phase 1 establishes ordinary life: zones, group baseline, workers, routes, wild edges and products/services.

Phase 2 introduces a small discrepancy: one route or paddock no longer behaves as expected.

Phase 3 shows that the response changes another system. A temporary movement pattern affects deliveries, public paths, staffing or wild observations.

Phase 4 produces an evidence-backed adjustment: a new seasonal window, changed gate, different working-Pokémon assignment, monitoring program or partial zone retirement.

Phase 5 revisits the same site months later. The ranch is recognizable, but its movement routes, ecology, service routine and physical presentation preserve the history of earlier decisions.

There is no hidden ranch level and no requirement that the endpoint be greater production. A smaller, safer or more ecologically compatible operating pattern can be an equally valid outcome.
