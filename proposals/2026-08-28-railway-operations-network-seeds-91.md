# Ouros Railway Operations & Network Continuity Seeds — Pass 91

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-28

These candidates assume only the reusable architecture in `design/railway-operations-stations-network-continuity-extension.md`. They do not establish that Ouros has rail transport.

## The Red Signal That Was Right

A routine service stops at an unexpected signal. Several passengers assume a technical failure because no obstruction is visible from the platform. A later section inspection finds a legitimate reason the corridor had been protected, but the original control record is incomplete.

Playable structure:
- establish normal service first;
- compare public board, staff report and control indication;
- inspect or receive direct evidence from the protected section;
- correct the passenger-facing explanation without rewriting what people believed during the delay.

Useful outputs:
- a maintenance follow-up;
- revised operational documentation;
- a public notice correction;
- a recurring passenger callback who remembers the incident.

No sabotage is implied.

## The Train Everyone Saw, Nothing Was There

Several control indications show what appears to be the same train moving through a section, yet platform staff report no corresponding arrival.

Possible evidence families:
- duplicated run identity;
- stale telemetry;
- clocks out of synchronization;
- a test movement not represented on the public timetable;
- a train terminating before the relevant section;
- faulty control equipment;
- incomplete direct observations;
- genuinely unresolved discrepancy.

The title is local shorthand, not proof of a supernatural train.

## One Line, Two Timetables

A maintenance restriction forces services to terminate early for part of the day. One station displays the revised short-turn pattern while another still shows the superseded full route.

The player can encounter:
- a traveler acting on old but previously correct information;
- staff trying to reconcile boards;
- a missed appointment or delivery without anyone behaving irrationally;
- the need to preserve both timetable revisions in information history.

Public information is the problem surface; Travel/Rail state remains authoritative.

## The Platform That Lost Its Line

A network revision bypassed an old platform years ago. The building remained in everyday use and gradually acquired other functions.

Candidate current uses:
- café or workshop;
- public meeting room;
- local archive/display;
- shelter or staging point;
- market spillover;
- maintenance access;
- wildlife observation point.

A new project proposes restoring rail access. Reopening would improve transport but also displace or reshape current uses. The conflict is about accumulated history and practical tradeoffs rather than nostalgia versus progress as a moral binary.

## Last Service Before the Planned Closure

A section is scheduled to close for works after one final normal operating window.

Potential hooks:
- a courier needs a route-dependent handoff;
- a regular passenger is making a routine trip, not a dramatic farewell;
- a maintenance team discovers a finding that may advance the closure;
- a passenger-facing notice still describes the old end time;
- an alternative route exists but changes an existing commitment.

The closure itself is planned world state. No countdown mechanic is invented.

## The Working Pokémon Is Off Shift

A rail operation visibly uses a particular Pokémon partner for a narrowly defined task. That individual is unavailable today.

The operator may:
- use a human/manual fallback already documented;
- reduce service;
- assign another individually qualified partner;
- suspend the affected function;
- discover that an old procedure incorrectly described the requirement by species or Type.

The story tests Pokémon Work continuity. No Type produces automatic rail capability.

## The Switch Moved, the Record Did Not

A physical junction is found in an alignment that does not match the most recent logged command.

Investigation can compare:
- current direct observation;
- command history;
- maintenance access;
- manual override records;
- staff recollection;
- network revision maps;
- timestamps.

Possible outcomes include an unrecorded authorized action, logging fault, later manual restoration or insufficient evidence. Theft/sabotage is not the default answer.

## The Old Cutting Became a Habitat Route

A decommissioned alignment has been unused long enough that repeated observation now shows persistent wildlife use.

A transport restoration proposal creates overlapping interests:
- improved connectivity;
- historical restoration;
- current ecological use;
- nearby resident access;
- maintenance practicality;
- alternative alignments or operating patterns.

A battle, if one occurs, cannot decide the conservation or public-works question.

## Station Reopens Before the Line

A station building completes its repair and public-space reopening while the corridor it once served remains under testing.

The reopened building can host:
- information desk;
- community or commercial services already valid in world state;
- exhibitions about the route;
- temporary transport coordination;
- ordinary foot traffic.

This seed reinforces that facility readiness and transport-service commissioning are separate.

## The Test Train Nobody Was Expecting

A controlled technical run is valid within the operator’s internal plan but was not projected onto passenger-facing information because no public boarding was planned.

A witness later reports “an impossible train” passing during a closure.

The report may be entirely accurate. The interpretation arose from an information boundary, not deception or paranormal activity.

## The Stop That Became More Important After Bypass

A service stops calling at a minor station. Instead of dying immediately, the location becomes more important for a different reason: a trailhead, workshop, public-space use, field team staging point, heritage operation, or ecological monitoring site.

The same station can therefore lose transport importance while gaining local significance.

## The Power Is Back, the Trains Are Not

An upstream outage is resolved, but service remains suspended because control tests, station readiness or inspection are incomplete.

Residents who correctly heard “power restored” may reasonably expect service to return. The operator’s later explanation can expose the dependency chain without treating public confusion as ignorance.

This seed connects Infrastructure Outage, Technology, Maintenance, Rail and Notices.

## Mystery — Five Train Reports, Three Actual Runs

Five independent-looking observations describe rail movements during a disruption.

Investigation reveals possible relationships:
- two reports describe the same run at different sections;
- one report uses a public service name for a test movement;
- one timestamp is based on a station clock later found inaccurate;
- a control indication may have persisted after the physical train cleared;
- one claimed run remains unsupported.

The goal is source reconciliation, not discovering a hidden villain.

## Mystery — Four Signals, Two Physical Causes

Four signal-related service stops occurred during one month. Local rumor treats them as a single recurring fault.

Records may show:
- two stops caused by one technical asset;
- one was a planned protective restriction;
- one had a separate route obstruction;
- the common symptom created the false appearance of a single problem.

The repair layer receives confirmed faults individually.

## Long arc — A Line Learns Its Stops

Phase 1: baseline
A line’s ordinary stations, regular workers, public information and service pattern are established before a crisis.

Phase 2: small restriction
A station bypass, short-turn or single-section closure changes everyday routines. Players may notice impacts without being asked to solve them.

Phase 3: investigation or project
A technical, ecological, access or capacity question makes one section important. Several parent systems contribute evidence.

Phase 4: network revision
A stop reopens, closes, shifts function or becomes a turnback. The physical and public map changes.

Phase 5: consequences
Passenger patterns, businesses, courier routes, wildlife use or neighborhood access adjust. Some changes are beneficial, others inconvenient, and some remain ambiguous.

Phase 6: later callback
An old timetable, former staff member, heritage vehicle, decommissioned platform or recurring passenger makes the earlier state relevant again.

The line never receives a hidden “level.” Its history is the sequence of actual operational revisions and world effects.

## Encounter — Station Concourse Evacuation

Status: proposed, implementation-aware.

Narrative premise:
An encounter develops near a station circulation area while passenger service is active.

Full intended version:
- several evacuation exits;
- access gates/doors can affect routes;
- CLEAR_ROUTE / WITHDRAW / PROTECT intent;
- Intercept/reactions where legal;
- objective-aware AI;
- passengers remain world actors rather than expendable battle tokens.

Readiness:
Uses the same permanent capability map as Trackside Withdrawal. Terrain/zones/reactions, tactical AI and adapter playback remain blocking.

Reduced version:
Transit Hubs evacuates the station first. Service is suspended. Important property, shops and passengers stay outside battle targeting. AutoPTU receives a static concourse/perimeter arena and only explicit combatants.

## Encounter — Decommissioned Cutting Wildlife Conflict

Status: proposed, implementation-aware.

Narrative premise:
Surveyors assessing an old alignment encounter a wild subgroup at a site with documented ecological use.

Full intended version:
- withdrawal/route-control objective;
- constrained cutting geometry;
- territorial/escape AI;
- environment effects only when validated;
- background observed Pokémon remain separate from battle participants.

Reduced version:
Surveyors leave before battle. Ouros chooses the exact tactical subgroup. The encounter uses reviewed static geometry. The outcome may make the immediate area safe, but cannot approve reopening, invalidate ecological evidence or transfer stewardship authority.
