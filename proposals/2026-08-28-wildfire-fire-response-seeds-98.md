# Wildfire & Fire-Response Seeds — Pass 98

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These are original Ouros candidates derived from Pass 98 research. They do not establish locations, institutions, rules or historical events.

## Design intent

These seeds use fire as persistent world history rather than a one-scene damage field. They favor evidence, access, assignment, partial operations, delayed consequences and later return visits. Any tactical fire, smoke, heat, wind, collapse, rescue or forced-movement mechanic remains gated by the engine-readiness snapshot.

## The Smoke Everyone Saw From Somewhere Else

Several settlements report a smoke column. Each describes a different apparent direction because the observers are on different sides of the same ridge.

Useful play:

- reconcile timestamps and viewpoints;
- determine whether there is one incident or several;
- verify which routes require closure;
- record what each community believed before the operational picture became clear.

No deception is required.

## The Water Point Is Full, the Response Is Not Ready

A known water source is available, but the relevant transport, pump, access route, trained operator or other approved dependency is unavailable.

The point is to separate visible resource presence from actual operational readiness.

No universal water/suppression arithmetic is implied.

## The Fire Is Holding, the Road Stays Closed

Responders report that spread has stopped under current observation, but the road owner still requires inspection for damaged trees, barriers, slope condition, visibility or another authored concern.

Residents perceive this as contradictory until the different authorities are explained.

## The Trail Reopens Before the Picnic Ground

A through-route passes review first. A nearby public facility remains restricted because its inspection or service dependencies are incomplete.

The world visibly returns in pieces instead of one global reopening flag.

## The Firebreak Became a Shortcut

A temporary cleared strip created during response remains physically legible months later. Residents begin using it informally.

Possible later owners/questions:

- Travel: should it become a route?
- Conservation: does it cross a sensitive area?
- Forestry: does vegetation management continue?
- Public Works: does it need stabilization?
- Public Memory: what does the community call it?

The original emergency action acquires an unintended second life.

## The Pokémon Returned Before Visitors

Wild Pokémon are observed using a burned or adjacent sector while public access remains restricted.

This does not prove the area is safe for visitors, fully recovered, or ecologically improved. It creates observations for Conservation/Science and social pressure around re-entry.

## The Lookout Who Was Better at Looking Out

A Pokémon or human responder repeatedly performs well in observation/communications but is less suitable for another response role.

The arc supports competence without treating every participant as interchangeable. Any Pokémon assignment requires individual evidence through Pokémon Work.

## The Drill Found the Broken Gate

A preparedness exercise reveals that an access gate, communication point, cache or route marker is unusable before any real incident occurs.

The reward is visible preparedness work: maintenance, replacement, revised procedure or a new fallback route. No hidden readiness bonus is required.

## The First Crew Left a Better Map

An earlier incident created detailed field notes that remain useful years later even though the terrain, roads or vegetation have changed.

Players must distinguish stable landmarks from obsolete assumptions rather than treating the old map as either perfect or worthless.

## The Hot Spot Was a Different Report

A late smoke report is initially logged as possible reignition. Verification shows it concerns a different small source or another visible phenomenon.

The story makes uncertainty operational without punishing responders for checking.

## The Reignition Was Real

A separate version of the same premise confirms residual fire in a previously quiet sector.

The important consequence is procedural: reopen the sector's response record, update access information and create new operations. The event does not need a surprise villain.

## The Warehouse Was Saved, the Business Was Not Ready

A structure remains standing, but utilities, inventory custody, smoke/contamination review, staff access or another owner-system dependency prevents immediate reopening.

Fire-response success and economic/service recovery remain different.

## The Burned Sign Stayed Up

A public warning sign survives after restrictions change. Travelers begin using the physical sign as evidence even though Public Notices has superseded it.

The problem is information lineage rather than malicious misinformation.

## The Volunteer Who Keeps Getting Assigned Too Much

A person or Pokémon who performed one useful emergency action becomes socially expected to solve every similar problem afterward.

The arc can explore role boundaries, training, consent, workload and institutional learning without creating a mechanical penalty or moral score.

## The Stream Crossing Became the Staging Area

A temporary response camp occupies a familiar recreation or travel site. After the incident, traces of that use remain: hardened ground, signs, repaired access, discarded temporary infrastructure that must be accounted for, or a new community memory.

## The Burn Scar Revealed the Old Boundary

Vegetation loss exposes an old wall, route marker, foundation or survey feature.

The fire did not create ownership truth. It created visibility. Archaeology, Cartography, Property or Case systems must interpret the evidence.

## The Incident With No Agreed Cause

Operational response closes successfully, but cause remains unresolved despite investigation.

Later people tell different stories. Public Memory can preserve those accounts while Case Authority preserves the actual evidence status.

## Mystery — Four Smoke Reports, Two Fires

Four reports arrive over three hours from different places.

Evidence can show that two observers saw the same large plume, one saw residual smoke after the main front had moved or stopped, and one witnessed a separate small incident. Alternate authored solutions can remain possible if evidence is incomplete.

This mystery tests provenance, geography and timing. It requires no battle.

## Mystery — Three Patrol Notes, One Reignition

Three patrol records appear inconsistent:

- one says no active flame observed;
- one later records heat/smoke;
- one records a quiet sector again.

Possible reconciliation depends on exact times, locations and observation methods. One brief reignition may fit all three. Another authored case may remain unresolved.

## Encounter candidate — Ridge Fireline Withdrawal

Full premise:

A stable approach near an active incident becomes contested after workers and civilians have already begun withdrawing. The complete version would allow route-control objectives, reactions, forced movement, verified environmental fire/smoke zones and tactical AI.

Current reduced form:

- all workers/civilians leave first;
- Ouros closes the active sector before battle;
- the fight occurs on stable reviewed ground outside flame/smoke effects;
- combatants are explicitly selected;
- no Minecraft fire tick, particle, wind or water block has tactical authority;
- victory only secures the immediate approach.

## Encounter candidate — Evacuation Junction Perimeter

Full premise:

An already-used evacuation junction must remain available for authorized responders. A distinct threat creates a route-control problem.

Current reduced form:

The evacuation movement is completed before combat. The junction becomes a static arena. Fire and traffic remain outside the grid. The result can permit later operational work but cannot reopen the road or change incident state.

## Encounter candidate — Mop-Up Patrol Contact

Full premise:

A patrol during residual-fire operations meets an independently motivated wild group or other authored threat.

Current reduced form:

The patrol leaves the warm/residual-fire sector and meets the conflict on verified stable ground. The battle does not certify that the fire is out and does not change the residual-fire record.

## Long arc — A Ridge Learns Its Firebreaks

Early visits establish ordinary forest routes, local routines, response drills and woodland uses.

A later smoke report begins a real incident. Different sectors are verified at different times, some access closes, and temporary response infrastructure appears. Suppression and evacuation progress independently.

The dramatic phase ends before the story does. Patrol, inspection, re-entry, route restoration and post-fire ecological review occur over later visits. A temporary strip becomes familiar. A business adapts to a closure. Wildlife uses the landscape differently. Residents remember who warned them and what information changed.

Months or years later, another planning decision references the old incident. The old fire remains relevant through maps, access patterns, restoration work, institutional practice and memory rather than repeated catastrophe.

## Canon decisions required before promotion

Promotion of any seed requires decisions about regional fire ecology, response institutions, authority, warning terminology, available technology, Pokémon work roles, cause investigation, re-entry practice, public access and any cultural use of managed/prescribed fire.

No seed should be promoted merely because its mechanical reduced form is implementable.
