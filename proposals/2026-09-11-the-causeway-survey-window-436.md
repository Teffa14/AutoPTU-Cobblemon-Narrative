# The Causeway Survey Window — Pass 436

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Canon effect: NONE
Location assignment: UNRESOLVED

## Premise

A local coordinator discovers signs that a frequently used crossing may become unsafe. The coordinator asks a technically capable NPC for help. The specialist cannot come immediately and counterproposes a precise future window at the crossing with a narrow scope: survey and diagnosis only.

The requester explicitly accepts those terms.

The interesting story begins when the appointment arrives. The world has continued moving while both actors were elsewhere.

The crossing may be unchanged. Water may have risen. A temporary repair may have been attempted. A wild population may now be using the structure. The route may have closed. New evidence may show that the original diagnosis was wrong. Another obligation may compete with the promised visit.

The stored agreement answers only what was promised, by whom, where, during which window and for what scope. It does not pre-author the state found on arrival.

## Narrative uses

This incident can function as a small infrastructure quest, an ecology observation thread, a relationship test, a route-access complication or the first clue in a longer regional problem.

The responder gains characterization through behavior around commitments rather than scripted exposition. Arriving on time, renegotiating after a route closure, refusing work outside the agreed scope, volunteering extra help or missing the window can all create later consequences without requiring a binary morality label.

The requester also has agency. A survey-only agreement may be enough. The requester may seek another specialist for repair. The requester may decide the crossing should remain closed. New information may make the visit unnecessary before the appointment begins.

## Reduced implementation

The reduced version requires no battle resolution.

The world stores the request, counterproposal, delivered terms, requester acceptance and responder commitment. At the scheduled window the planner reevaluates current obligations, travel feasibility, required knowledge and permission state.

If the responder reaches the crossing, the survey can resolve through authored observation points and evidence claims. The result can be one of several ordinary world-state findings: safe for now, unsafe pending repair, access blocked, evidence insufficient, or problem no longer present.

No forced movement, combat status, damage, reaction, weather phase or tactical escort is required. Environmental change can be represented by discrete route/access state authored by the world simulation.

## Full encounter version

A mechanically rich version can make the crossing itself active during inspection. Rising water, unstable footing or wind may alter traversable cells. Wild Pokémon may defend a nest or react to the inspection team without automatically becoming a defeat-all battle. The objective remains surveying or safely withdrawing, not necessarily causing knockouts.

If water or debris can displace actors, the encounter depends on complete movement including forced movement. If environmental danger changes during rounds, it depends on full turn/round lifecycle plus terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions. If the encounter applies persistent conditions, it also depends on the status lifecycle. Any Move, Ability, Item or Trainer Feature used to modify terrain, interrupt movement, protect another actor or alter the hazard must be individually admitted.

An escort or protect objective additionally requires legal-action infrastructure and tactical policy for that exact objective. Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics must be able to present authoritative objective state and a non-KO completion result without inventing rules outside AutoPTU.

## Consequence hooks

A successful survey may produce evidence rather than an immediate repair. That evidence can support a maintenance request, route closure, ecological mitigation or a later specialist call.

A missed visit can create distrust only if downstream relationship logic decides that the missed obligation matters in context. This proposal does not hard-code relationship mutation.

A changed route can force renegotiation without erasing the original promise. A new proposal should become a new durable causal object rather than editing the historical terms in place.
