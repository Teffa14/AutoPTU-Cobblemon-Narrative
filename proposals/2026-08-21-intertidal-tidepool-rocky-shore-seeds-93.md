# Pass 93 — Intertidal, Tidepool & Rocky-Shore Seeds

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These concepts are candidates for later authored placement and mechanical review.

## Worldbuilding candidates

### 1. Pool Twelve Is Empty
A long-monitored tidepool contains none of the Pokémon usually recorded there during one low tide. The first question is whether the survey timing, tide height, visitor activity or pool condition changed. The absence becomes evidence only after repeated comparable observations.

### 2. The Rock With Two Histories
A large offshore rock is both a long-used scientific reference marker and the recurring substrate for a Binacle pair. A proposed replacement marker would make surveys easier but disturb the exact surface that gives decades of observations continuity.

### 3. The Low-Tide Shortcut
Residents have crossed a rock shelf for generations during favorable tides. New visitors discover the route through a popular map and begin treating it as an always-open trail. Travel state must preserve the difference between cultural knowledge, physical exposure and safe access.

### 4. Moonpool Point Visitor Surge
A photograph makes a normally quiet low-tide site famous. Visitor numbers rise faster than the local stewardship program can adapt. The conflict is about access, education and observed disturbance rather than a blanket ban.

### 5. Crossed Transects
Two research groups publish apparently incompatible occupancy results. Both are correct: one consistently surveyed on falling tides and the other near the lowest exposure window. Revising the combined dataset becomes the quest.

### 6. The Pool That Never Drains
A tidepool once isolated every low tide now remains connected to the sea. The cause may be a small physical shoreline change, storm movement or an old survey error. Coastal Geomorphology must establish the physical revision before ecology is rewritten.

### 7. Wimpod Scatter Year
A familiar Wimpod colony repeatedly disperses earlier than usual when visitors approach. Observers must separate normal alert behavior from a real change in site use. Capturing members is never the default research method.

### 8. Pyukumuku Above the Foam Line
The same Pyukumuku is observed unusually high on shore across several mornings. Its persistence creates a recognizable local individual without implying distress, ownership or a desire to join a Trainer.

### 9. Research Bolt Missing
A fixed survey marker disappears. Scientists initially worry that years of transect continuity are lost. The story is about reconstructing reference geometry from photographs, maps and neighboring markers, not about blaming wildlife.

### 10. The Cave Mouth Window
A shallow sea cave can be entered only during a recurring low-tide window. Archaeological traces are visible inside, but access, site stewardship and tidal timing must be solved before any deeper expedition begins.

### 11. Three Shores, One Species
The same species is common on an exposed rocky headland, sparse beside a sheltered village and seasonal on a mixed sand-rock shore. The project tests whether wave exposure, visitor use, prey availability or another factor best explains the difference.

### 12. The Collected Shell Rumor
Empty shells disappear from a monitored shore and residents accuse tourists of collecting them. Investigation finds several plausible causes, including ordinary redistribution by waves and Pokémon use. A custody case should begin only when evidence supports human removal.

### 13. Tidepool School Day
An academy runs a recurring field-practice day around observation, photography and careful movement through the shore. Student reports become long-term data only after instructors review method and provenance.

### 14. The Forgotten Access Stair
An old stairway reaches a rocky platform during moderate tides, but official maps stopped showing it decades ago. Its reappearance in community maps creates a debate about maintenance, access and whether the route should be restored.

### 15. The Shelf After the Storm
A major storm ends, and the coastline appears superficially similar. Low tide reveals that several pools, cracks and boulder positions changed. The story bridges Crisis recovery and a new Coastal Geomorphology revision.

### 16. The Temporary Nursery
A sheltered low intertidal pocket is repeatedly used by juvenile Pokémon during one seasonal window. The term “nursery” remains an ecological observation, not ownership or a breeding facility designation.

### 17. The Tidepool Photo Boom
A rare-looking photograph sends visitors to one pool. Later review suggests the image may show a common individual under unusual lighting. Public interest persists even after the classification changes.

### 18. Former Partner at Low Tide
A previously released Pokémon is photographed at the same shore several seasons later. The observation preserves identity if evidence supports the match, but it does not restore command authority or imply the Pokémon returned for its former Trainer.

### 19. The Wrong Closure
A sensitive area is temporarily closed after an apparent disturbance. Follow-up evidence shows the affected site lies one segment farther along the coast. The institution must correct the measure publicly without pretending the original decision was malicious.

### 20. The Tide Came Earlier Than the Sign
A public tide-information board shows an obsolete seasonal schedule. Visitors reach a shelf later than intended. The correction becomes a Communications, Cartography and Travel problem rather than evidence of supernatural tide behavior.

### 21. High-Tide Feeding Record
Researchers repeatedly observe Binacle activity around the same rocks during high tide. The result becomes useful regional natural history but grants no combat effect, capture modifier or guaranteed spawn.

### 22. The Visitor Trail Between Pools
Foot traffic gradually concentrates along one line between popular pools. The route is visible before measurable ecological effects are clear. Stewardship can redirect access before inventing damage that has not been observed.

### 23. The Pool With Two Names
Researchers, fishers and local families use different names for the same tidepool. The Cartography layer records aliases and provenance instead of forcing one community name to erase the others.

### 24. One Extreme Low Tide
An unusually low exposure window reveals an old structure, several rarely observed organisms and a route nobody remembers using. Everything exposed must still be classified separately: archaeology, ecology, property, access and public memory.

### 25. The Strandline Mystery
Material repeatedly appears above the normal water line. Some is natural wrack, some is lost cargo and some may be waste. The shore becomes a provenance investigation rather than a generic cleanup quest.

### 26. The Quiet Colony
A Wimpod colony remains present but stops reacting strongly to a commonly used path. Researchers debate habituation, changed disturbance timing and simple observer bias. The game records behavior, not human-style emotional conclusions.

### 27. Accessible Tidepool Route
A community redesigns one observation route so more visitors can reach a representative pool without concentrating pressure on the most sensitive shelf. Accessibility and conservation become compatible design constraints rather than opposing factions.

### 28. The Unmoved Stone
An old photograph seems to show that a prominent rock shifted after a storm. Re-survey reveals the rock is stable and the historical photograph used a different reference angle. The satisfying conclusion is correcting the record.

### 29. Archival Transect
A museum discovers decades of photographs from the same rocky shore. Modern researchers can reconstruct historical zone occupancy, visitor use and shoreline appearance if dates and viewpoints are recovered correctly.

### 30. The Tidepool Atlas
Players and NPC researchers gradually build a regional atlas that records pool identity, tide context, observed occupants, access windows and old survey editions. It becomes valuable precisely because it preserves uncertainty and historical change.

## Long arcs

### Five Years at Moonpool Point
Year one establishes baselines and recurring individual observations. Year two brings visitor growth after media attention. Year three a storm modifies several pools and invalidates part of the old map. Year four introduces a restoration/access project whose success must be measured rather than assumed. Year five compares the new ecology with the original baseline and may conclude that some changes were natural, some visitor-linked and some still unexplained.

### The Shelf Between Towns
Two coastal settlements share a low-tide shelf used for travel, research and seasonal gathering. Each maintains different maps, names and stewardship traditions. Repeated access conflicts, storm changes and visitor pressure gradually force shared records and agreements without requiring either town to surrender its identity.

### The Intertidal Atlas
A multi-region research program begins with scattered tidepool notes. Players help standardize time, tide context, photographs and pool identity while preserving old local records. Over years the atlas becomes useful for science, tourism, conservation and historical comparison, but new shoreline revisions ensure it is never truly finished.

## Encounter contracts

### Low-Tide Shelf Survey
Full version: a time-sensitive survey across an exposed shelf where the tide can alter accessible geometry and wild Pokémon may withdraw through different routes.

Primary full dependencies: complete movement including forced movement/interception if water alters routes; full turn/round lifecycle for timed phase changes; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for any dynamic exposure; AI tactical policy for REACH_EXIT/WITHDRAW/PROTECT_POOL; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version: the server freezes one safe low-tide geometry for the entire encounter. Tide state changes only after battle. Uninvolved wildlife and researchers stay outside the grid.

### Pool Reconnection
Full version: players protect or operate a physical reconnection point while water state changes and Pokémon can independently withdraw.

Primary full dependencies: interactable objective contract, complete movement, environmental zones/hazards, tactical AI, adapter/playback.

Reduced version: assess and alter the blockage in overworld state. If a battle occurs, it uses a fixed adjacent platform; reconnection is committed afterward.

### Visitor Surge at Moonpool Point
Full version: staff route visitors while a wild colony attempts to leave a crowded low-tide site.

Primary full dependencies: complete movement/interception, objective-aware AI, crowd/civilian projection, environmental mechanics only where separately verified, adapter/playback.

Reduced version: visitor routing is resolved before battle and a static perimeter is cleared. The wild collective's movement remains ecological state rather than tactical AI.
