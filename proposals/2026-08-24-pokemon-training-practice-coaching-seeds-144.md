# Pokémon training, practice and coaching seeds — pass 144

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from the research and systems layer for Pass 144. Nothing here becomes established lore until reviewed.

## Design intent

These candidates make training a persistent part of Trainer life without turning repetition into free PTU progression.

Each concept should preserve four boundaries:

- observed practice is separate from authoritative mechanics;
- coaching interpretation is separate from truth;
- participation is separate from ownership/obedience;
- successful rehearsal is separate from transfer to another context.

## 30 candidates

### 1. The Drill That Stopped Working

A positioning drill used by a local Gym for years suddenly produces poor results with a new cohort. The equipment is unchanged. Investigation may reveal that the old exercise assumed a battlefield layout the Gym no longer uses.

Potential connections: Battle Institutions, Architecture, Public Memory.

### 2. Two Coaches, One Problem

Two respected coaches review the same battle transcript and propose different explanations for repeated losses. One blames spacing; the other thinks the pair is reacting too late. Both create separate short drills. Later evidence may support one, both or neither.

### 3. The Wrong Simulation

A practice machine built to imitate a recurring opponent reproduces the visual shape of the threat but not its actual timing. The Trainer becomes good at the simulator and still struggles in the rematch.

The narrative lesson is simulation fidelity, not punishment.

### 4. The Old Practice Field

An overgrown field behind an old Gym still contains painted lanes, worn targets and repair marks from several previous Leaders. A local club wants to reopen it while historians want to document the older configurations first.

### 5. The Pokémon That Leaves After Two Attempts

A partner consistently participates in the opening part of a care or work drill and then walks away. No diagnosis is assumed. The useful world state is the repeated pattern, the setup and the conditions under which the Pokémon returns.

### 6. The Cue From a Former Trainer

A transferred Pokémon responds strongly to a gesture its current Trainer has never used. Records show the gesture may come from a former partnership. The old cue can be documented without granting the former Trainer current authority.

### 7. The Rematch Notebook

A Gym challenger keeps handwritten practice notes after every loss. Years later, the notebook becomes a useful record of how the Gym's battlefield and coaching culture changed, even though many of the original tactical conclusions were wrong.

### 8. The Practice Partner Retires

A veteran Pokémon that has served as a calm sparring partner for novices is retired from that role. The institution must redesign its sessions rather than replace the individual with a mechanically identical substitute.

### 9. The New Body After Evolution

After Evolution, an old route drill no longer fits the Pokémon's dimensions or movement pattern. The pair revisits familiar cues and equipment while keeping the same persistent identity and relationship history.

No mechanical change is invented beyond the authoritative post-Evolution state.

### 10. The Underwater Transfer Test

A Pokémon performs a cue reliably in a shallow training pool. The pair later tests the same routine at a real lakeside work site. The two observations are stored separately because controlled success does not prove field generalization.

### 11. The Target Board With Old Marks

A target board accumulates years of dents, patches, painted scoring zones and signatures. The physical object becomes a small Chronicle artifact. Its marks are history, not an accuracy stat.

### 12. The Empty Training Slot

A player books a famous practice room and nothing unusual happens. The session proceeds normally, produces no progression and becomes only a short Chronicle entry. This is intentional world texture, not failed content generation.

### 13. The Facility Upgrade Changed the Drill

A Battle Club replaces its old flooring and movable partitions. The same named exercise now has different distances and sightlines. Historical session records must preserve which setup revision was used.

### 14. The Session With No Progress

A Pokémon repeats an objective several times with no observable improvement. The coach concludes that more repetition is unlikely to help and changes the problem definition instead of demanding a harsher session.

### 15. The Coach Changes Their Mind

An NPC coach publicly advocated one training philosophy for years. After reviewing evidence from students and Pokémon, they revise part of their approach. Old lessons remain historically authentic rather than being retconned.

### 16. The Private Routine Goes Public

A Trainer demonstrates a private cue during a public exhibition. Rivals may now legitimately know that cue exists, but they do not gain access to the pair's entire private training history.

### 17. The Partner Who Anticipates the Cue

A Pokémon begins the target behavior before the familiar cue is given. Possible interpretations include anticipation, environmental prediction, repetition history or a different stimulus. The system records the observation and keeps the explanation open.

### 18. The Wild Pokémon That Joins Once

A persistent wild Pokémon approaches a practice field and informally participates in one exercise. It leaves afterward. The event creates an observation, not capture rights, employment or a permanent training service.

### 19. The Care Station Practice

A clinic works gradually with a recurring patient on voluntarily entering a scanning or transport station. The main measure is participation history. No combat progression is expected.

### 20. The Weather Window Training Plan

A rescue group wants to practice under conditions similar to a mountain route, but the relevant weather does not occur during the scheduled week. They postpone instead of fabricating the hazard mechanically.

### 21. The Broken Simulator

A long-used practice device fails halfway through a session. Staff improvise a lower-fidelity alternative. The session remains useful, but the two setup revisions stay distinct in the record.

### 22. The Drill Outlived the Gym Leader

Three successive Leaders keep the same named drill while changing its purpose each time. Older Trainers remember a very different exercise under the same title.

### 23. The Visiting Coach

A specialist spends one week at a regional club and gives advice that conflicts with local practice culture. Some Trainers adopt pieces of the method; others do not. No universal better-coach score is created.

### 24. The New Trainer Inherits No Cues

A Pokémon changes custody or partnership. The new Trainer receives authorized records about equipment and care, but private cue history is not automatically transferred. Familiarity must be built through new observations.

### 25. The Same Drill, Different Pokémon

An obstacle course works well as practice for one body plan and poorly for another. The course does not measure intelligence or effort; it may simply test a different physical affordance.

### 26. The Coordination Problem Was Actually Spacing

A Doubles pair is described publicly as "not getting along" after several losses. Transcript review instead shows that both repeatedly choose legal positions that cluster them into the same area threats. Training focuses on spacing without inventing a relationship conflict.

### 27. The Move Was Learned Elsewhere

A Pokémon returns from a legitimate Tutor transaction knowing a new Move. The local coach helps practice timing and positioning around it. The Chronicle preserves that the Move came from the authoritative Tutor event, not from the later drills.

### 28. The Successful Failure

A Pokémon repeatedly fails the planned criterion, but the attempts reveal that the cue is ambiguous and the setup is poorly designed. The session succeeds as diagnosis even though the target behavior never occurs.

### 29. The Routine Maintenance Year

A competitive pair spends an entire season maintaining established routines. No new Move, Edge or dramatic breakthrough appears. Their practice history still matters when a later coach compares how stable the routines remained through travel and schedule changes.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Morning Practice

The players visit a familiar training ground. The usual group is there. Equipment works. The session ends normally. This candidate exists to protect Ouros from turning every routine into an incident.

## Long arc 1 — Five Rematches at Meridian Gym

Year 1: a challenger loses a formal match. Transcript review identifies two possible problems. The player chooses one short practice program.

Year 2: the rematch shows improvement in the targeted behavior but exposes a different weakness. The original training program moves to maintenance rather than being declared "mastered".

Year 3: a new Gym staff member questions the old practice apparatus and introduces a simulation. The simulation helps some Trainers and misleads others.

Year 4: the Gym changes battlefield configuration. Historical drills must be revised because their distances and routes no longer match the arena.

Year 5: another novice studies the archived practice records. The original player can see how their own failed sessions became part of local coaching history.

Possible endpoint: the Gym keeps several methods and teaches coaches to choose based on evidence instead of canonizing one perfect drill.

No arc step grants mechanical progression unless a separate PTU transaction occurs.

## Long arc 2 — The Practice Field Through Three Generations

An old community practice field predates the current League circuit.

First generation: local Trainers use improvised targets and marked lanes.

Second generation: a formal club installs equipment, booking records and safety procedures.

Third generation: the original equipment is partly preserved, partly replaced and partly repurposed for novice or care-focused practice.

The field can accumulate:

- old target boards;
- setup revisions;
- photographs;
- coach notebooks;
- accessibility changes;
- repair history;
- memorial uses;
- public demonstrations;
- ordinary mornings where nothing happens.

The location becomes valuable because of continuity, not because it grants a training bonus.

## Long arc 3 — One Cue, Many Contexts

A Trainer and persistent partner develop a simple authored cue during routine practice.

Months later, observers see the same cue used in a work task.

Later, it appears during a public exhibition, making its existence public.

Years later, the partner is retired from competition but still responds to the cue in a familiar non-combat context.

If the Pokémon later changes custody, is released or enters another role, the old cue history remains part of the record while current response becomes uncertain until observed again.

The arc explores memory, communication, privacy and continuity without creating an obedience stat.

## Encounter contract — Rematch Preparation Circuit

### Narrative premise

A coach converts evidence from an official loss into a short circuit designed to test positioning and response to area pressure.

### FULL version

Sparring partners and objective markers move. The coach changes pressure between phases. Participants must leave threatened areas, protect routes, reposition and decide when to stop or reset.

### Capability dependencies

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING if the drill uses interception, live forced repositioning or moving objectives;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL when actual sparring damage is used;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL if exact statuses are part of the drill;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING as a complete family when real tactical zones/hazards or reaction drills are required;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for practice-objective behavior;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

### REDUCED version

Keep the apparatus, objective criteria and coach feedback in world state. Use one legal static AutoPTU spar. Afterward, inspect authoritative events for positioning and target-choice observations. No custom zone, combo, dodge or progression effect is created.

## Encounter contract — Pair Coordination Drill

### Narrative premise

Two Pokémon preparing for a Doubles event practice spacing after a transcript review shows repeated clustering.

### FULL version

Opposing pressure and objective markers change over time. The pair must spread, regroup, protect routes and react to area threats while preserving independent legal actions.

### Dependencies

FULL depends mainly on BLOCKING AI tactical policy and BLOCKING complete movement if objective movement/interception is required. The environmental family remains BLOCKING when live zones or reaction-heavy pressure is used. Exact Moves, Abilities, Items and Trainer Features remain PARTIAL unless individually verified.

### REDUCED version

Run a conventional legal Multi Battle on a static map. Record spacing, legal target choices and repeated patterns. Any statement about improved coordination remains a narrative assessment; no linked action or combo bonus is granted.

## Encounter contract — Cooperative Care Practice

### Narrative premise

A persistent Pokémon is gradually familiarized with a care station, carrier or piece of non-combat equipment.

### FULL version

The Pokémon can approach, pause, disengage, return and interact with several stations while staff respond to voluntary choices.

### Dependencies

The intended full version depends mainly on BLOCKING AI tactical policy for non-combat objectives and BLOCKING adapter/playback for a rich in-world interaction. It does not inherently require damage, statuses or battle action economy.

### REDUCED version

Resolve each step as world-state observation. If the Pokémon disengages, the session ends or changes. Do not open a battle because a criterion was not completed.

## Encounter contract — Route Transfer Test

### Narrative premise

A pair has practiced a route behavior in a controlled environment and now tests it under real field conditions.

### FULL version

The route contains moving actors and a changing tactical pressure that requires the practiced behavior while preserving withdrawal and safety options.

### Dependencies

- base movement legality — VERIFIED for supported movement modes;
- complete movement — BLOCKING when interception/forced movement/objective routing is required;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if the field condition itself has tactical effects;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for non-hostile transfer objectives;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

### REDUCED version

Resolve route conditions in overworld state first. If battle occurs, freeze a static safe arena and use ordinary legal combat. Record whether the practiced behavior appeared in the field before or after the battle without granting a bonus.

## Mechanical guardrails for all candidates

No proposal in this file should create any of the following automatically:

- XP or Levels from repetitions;
- Tutor Points from practice time;
- Accuracy Training from target drills;
- Capability Training from obstacle courses;
- Advanced Mobility from route running;
- Skill Improvement from repeated tasks;
- Underdog's Lessons or any learned Move from observation alone;
- Mentor qualification from being called a coach;
- Loyalty changes from success, failure or refusal;
- fatigue, HP loss or Injury from descriptive training;
- simultaneous combo actions;
- custom reaction entitlement;
- fake Terrain/Hazard/Weather from training props;
- private training knowledge for opponents;
- ownership or capture rights over practice partners.

When PTU authorizes a real progression transaction, the proposal should link to it and preserve provenance instead of recreating it.
