# Ouros Geology & Resource Frontier Seeds — Pass 33

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from abstract research patterns. No external characters, prose or plots are transplanted.

## Seed 1 — The Layer Beneath the Road

Road repairs expose a geological layer containing unusual fossils. The road is important to local transport, so leaving the exposure open creates a real cost.

Possible directions:
- scientists request a documentation window;
- transport operators want the road reopened quickly;
- local residents care about access more than the discovery;
- a museum offers storage/preparation help;
- wild Pokémon begin using the newly opened cut as shelter.

No side is automatically wrong.

## Seed 2 — The Mine Map That Was Correct

Players find two contradictory maps of an abandoned mine.

Both were accurate when drawn.

A collapse and later drainage project changed the route between them.

The mystery is temporal provenance, not forgery.

## Seed 3 — Fossil Without Context

A rare fossil appears on the market with no trustworthy recovery record.

The object may be genuine while its claimed origin is false.

The interesting questions are provenance, custody and whether scientific interpretation is possible without the original context.

## Seed 4 — The Quiet Quarry

A quarry closes for ordinary economic reasons.

Months later, wild Pokémon begin nesting in the exposed rock walls.

A proposal to reopen extraction now interacts with a habitat that did not exist when the site first operated.

## Seed 5 — The First Deep Find

A small survey crew finds evidence that a known shallow deposit continues much deeper than expected.

Publication could transform the town through investment and visitor pressure.

Keeping the result private also has consequences.

## Seed 6 — The Wrong Fossil Boom

A rumor claims a famous fossil species has been found.

Visitors arrive before verification.

The actual discovery is scientifically interesting but not what the rumor promised.

The arc becomes partly about managing expectations, access and misinformation.

## Seed 7 — Museum Preparation Backlog

A museum has recovered more specimens than its small preparation team can process.

The problem is not lack of fossils. It is staffing, storage and prioritization.

Players can help through logistics, field documentation, funding, transport or recruitment without becoming paleontologists themselves.

## Seed 8 — The Working Cave

A naturally formed cave is simultaneously a water source, wild habitat and active workplace.

An expansion project that is harmless to one function may disrupt another.

The site should never be reduced to a generic mine dungeon.

## Seed 9 — Water in the Lower Level

An old mine begins flooding.

The cause could be seasonal groundwater, damaged pumping, a newly opened natural channel or another verified factor.

The first job is to determine what changed, not to assume sabotage.

## Seed 10 — The Specimen Nobody Wants to Revive Yet

A nearly complete fossil is recovered.

Researchers want years of study before destructive preparation or revival.

A sponsor wants a dramatic public revival.

The conflict is about scientific priorities and institutional incentives rather than good versus evil.

## Seed 11 — Fossil Child

A revived fossil Pokémon becomes a persistent local individual associated with a museum or care facility.

Its provenance is famous, but its future ownership/care arrangement remains a separate authored decision.

Later arcs can involve its behavior, public attention, welfare and relationships without turning it into a quest reward.

## Seed 12 — The Quarry Exchange

Two towns rely on different geological resources and trade them through an old route.

A disruption forces both to reconsider which materials they actually need and which traditions developed only because the route was convenient.

## Seed 13 — Old Supports, New Standards

A historical mine remains structurally usable but its supports were built under older practices.

An inspector recommends limited access while experienced workers argue that specific sections remain safe.

The arc revolves around evidence and staged verification.

## Seed 14 — The False Rich Vein

A survey appears to show a valuable deposit.

Later work reveals the signal came from a small concentrated pocket rather than a large body.

No fraud is necessary.

This can create financial and political consequences from a reasonable but incorrect early interpretation.

## Seed 15 — Underground River Capture

Excavation intersects a subsurface stream.

The water begins taking a new path.

Effects may propagate into a spring, wetland, cave ecosystem or town water system only if the world graph supports those dependencies.

## Seed 16 — The Guide Who Knows the Air

A veteran cave guide notices recurring warning signs that instruments record poorly or too late.

The story explores practical expertise, formal measurements and how institutions combine both kinds of knowledge.

Do not turn intuition into supernatural certainty.

## Seed 17 — Fossil Preparation Apprenticeship

A museum technician takes on an apprentice.

The progression is about patience, documentation and responsibility rather than battle level.

A damaged specimen can become a meaningful failure without destroying a campaign.

## Seed 18 — The Displaced Colony

A digging project causes a known wild collective to shift to another chamber.

The relocation changes which surface entrance they use.

A town then experiences Pokémon activity that appears new even though the population itself did not increase.

## Seed 19 — The Mine That Became a Shortcut

An abandoned tunnel unintentionally becomes the fastest route between two settlements.

Reopening commercial extraction would interfere with travel.

Closing it for safety would create transport costs.

The site now has a function nobody planned.

## Seed 20 — The Fossil Donation Dispute

A discoverer publicly announces that a fossil will be donated to a museum.

Later, another institution produces an older agreement suggesting custody was already promised elsewhere.

The issue is records and authority, not whether the fossil is genuine.

## Seed 21 — Geothermal Workshop

A workshop uses naturally available geothermal heat.

When the thermal pattern shifts, the business experiences intermittent service rather than instant catastrophe.

The investigation can connect geology, technology and workplace continuity.

## Seed 22 — The Illegal-Looking Dig

Players encounter an excavation operating at night.

It looks suspicious.

The crew may actually have legitimate access but be working unusual hours because of heat, transport windows, Pokémon activity or another authored constraint.

Appearance alone should not generate a crime state.

## Seed 23 — Layered Ownership Claims

A mineral deposit lies beneath a site used by another institution.

Different actors claim rights to the surface facility, underground resource, archaeological material and access tunnel.

Ouros should allow those claims to differ rather than invent one universal ownership rule.

## Seed 24 — The Fossil That Changes the Map

A fossil assemblage suggests that a region once had a different environment.

The discovery does not rewrite world truth immediately.

It generates research questions about old waterways, climate, migration or geology.

## Seed 25 — Mine Rescue Without Villains

A collapse traps workers or explorers.

The event can support rescue, logistics and aftermath without requiring sabotage, a criminal faction or a boss.

The cause can remain natural, accidental or unresolved.

## Seed 26 — The Public Dig

A museum runs a supervised public excavation day.

Players may join as visitors, volunteers, researchers, safety staff or competitors in a non-mechanical social event.

A minor find can become memorable through public memory rather than monetary value.

## Seed 27 — The Fossil Replica Problem

A museum discovers that an old display is a replica but nobody knows when the original left the collection.

This creates a provenance/custody case without assuming theft.

The original may have been loaned, transferred, destroyed, misplaced or stolen.

## Seed 28 — Rare Stone, Ordinary Consequence

A newly publicized decorative stone becomes fashionable.

Demand changes quarry schedules, shipping and visitor behavior.

The material has no special PTU effect.

Its importance is social and economic.

## Seed 29 — Cave Species Miscount

Researchers estimate a cave population by counting one accessible chamber.

A new passage later reveals the population was distributed differently.

The correction should update research and management without treating the original team as incompetent.

## Seed 30 — The Last Cart

An old mine-cart network is about to be decommissioned.

Workers, residents and visitors each remember it differently.

The final operational day can be a public-memory event that also exposes forgotten branches, old equipment or habitat connections.

## Long Arc A — The Deep Basin

Phase 1:
A routine infrastructure survey reveals unusual strata beneath a growing settlement.

Phase 2:
Research teams identify fossils and a potentially valuable resource body.

Phase 3:
Publication creates investment, tourism and political pressure.

Phase 4:
Subsurface water and habitat connections make unrestricted extraction more complicated.

Phase 5:
Players participate through research, exploration, logistics, negotiation, rescue or battle encounters depending on their interests.

Possible endings:
- controlled extraction with protected contexts;
- research reserve with limited access;
- infrastructure project rerouted;
- mixed-use site with ongoing monitoring;
- project abandoned after evidence changes its feasibility.

No ending is automatically morally correct.

## Long Arc B — Museum of the Living Past

Phase 1:
A small museum begins receiving new fossil material faster than it can process it.

Phase 2:
Players help stabilize provenance, storage and field records.

Phase 3:
One fossil becomes eligible for authoritative revival.

Phase 4:
The revived Pokémon becomes a persistent individual requiring care, privacy/publicity decisions and long-term institutional responsibility.

Phase 5:
Years later, the museum itself becomes part of public memory and new players encounter the consequences of the original excavation decisions.

The arc should not assume the revived Pokémon becomes a player's property.

## Long Arc C — The Hollow Mountain

Phase 1:
Several mines, caves and water passages are treated as separate locations.

Phase 2:
Mapping gradually reveals that they are parts of one connected subsurface system.

Phase 3:
Actions in one section begin causing observable consequences elsewhere: water rerouting, airflow changes, Pokémon movement or access changes.

Phase 4:
Different institutions discover they have been managing fragments of the same system independently.

Phase 5:
The regional challenge is to create a shared understanding and management model while preserving work, travel, habitat and research needs.

The climax can be institutional/exploratory rather than a boss battle.
