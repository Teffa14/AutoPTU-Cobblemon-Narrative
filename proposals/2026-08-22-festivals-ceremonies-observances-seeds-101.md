# Pass 101 Proposals — Festivals, Ceremonies, Observances & Rites

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Nothing here becomes established Ouros lore without explicit review and promotion.

These seeds use `design/festivals-ceremonies-observances-layer.md` and preserve the boundary between cultural meaning, public memory and PTU mechanics.

## 1. The Festival That Moved a Week

A migration-linked observance has happened on the same calendar date for decades, but the Pokémon arrive earlier three years in a row. Organizers debate whether the celebration follows the date, the migration or both.

## 2. The Bell That Rings Only Once

A historic town bell is used for one ceremonial signal each year. The mechanism is now unreliable. Repairing it raises questions about replacement parts, provenance and whether changing the sound still preserves the tradition.

## 3. The Missing Procession Route

A rebuilt bridge makes the old procession route impossible. Older residents prefer a detour that preserves sequence; accessibility advocates propose a shorter route; merchants prefer the commercial square. The problem is governance and continuity, not a battle.

## 4. Masks for Visitors

A regional celebration has public masks for guests and separate locally significant designs used by stewards. Tourism material begins flattening the distinction. The players can help archives, designers and organizers document the difference without declaring one style “authentic” by fiat.

## 5. The Harvest Without the Old Partner

A Pokémon that participated in a harvest observance for many years is no longer present. The community must decide whether to invite another individual, alter the practice or let that element end. No Pokémon is automatically assigned the role.

## 6. The Year With No Contest

A festival historically includes a Contest, but the hall is under repair. The edition continues with food, stories, exhibitions and community activities, proving the observance is larger than its competitive attraction.

## 7. First Light at the Reservoir

A dawn ceremony commemorates the reservoir opening. New scientific evidence shows the project also flooded an older settlement. Future editions must decide whether and how that history is acknowledged.

## 8. The Volunteer Ledger

The same family names appear in preparation records for generations, but the current organizer pool is exhausted. The players can improve handoffs, recruit help or redesign workloads without turning volunteer burnout into villainy.

## 9. Two Founding Stories

Two neighborhoods celebrate the same city anniversary using different founding narratives. Both traditions are historically old; neither has complete evidence. The observances can coexist while archives investigate the underlying chronology.

## 10. The Lantern Route Goes Dark

A yearly lantern walk depends on an old lighting network. A power problem affects only part of the route. The reduced event can relocate safely while Infrastructure investigates the failure.

## 11. The Festival Photograph

A famous photograph made one small ritual globally recognizable. Decades later, visitors crowd the exact viewpoint and disrupt the rest of the event. Tourism and local meaning begin to diverge.

## 12. The Quiet Edition

After a major disaster, organizers hold only a small ceremony and communal meal. There is no tournament, market or spectacle. The skipped activities remain visible in edition history without being treated as failure.

## 13. The Open Shrine Day

A normally restricted archive room opens to the public during one observance. The objects are available for viewing, not looting or mechanical interaction. A catalog discrepancy creates a research hook.

## 14. The Wrong Date on Every Poster

A printing mistake puts last year's date on regional advertising. Transport operators, hotels and local organizers now have different expectations. The event itself remains correctly scheduled in the authoritative calendar.

## 15. The Returning Alumni Walk

An academy has a yearly walk for graduates who choose to return. Some alumni are now rivals, officials, researchers or ordinary workers. Attendance does not imply loyalty to the institution.

## 16. The First Journey Send-Off

A community offers an optional departure gathering for new Trainers. One player character declines. The world records the decision only if it was public; no social penalty or mechanical bonus is generated automatically.

## 17. The River Opens Again

A route-reopening ceremony is planned after years of restoration. A final survey shows one section is still unsafe. The ceremony can happen while the route remains partially closed.

## 18. Three Foods, One Tradition

Three districts serve different versions of the same festival dish and each has documentary support. The event becomes a living demonstration of regional variation rather than a quest to identify a single “true” recipe.

## 19. The Steward's Last Edition

A long-serving organizer retires after the current festival. Their replacement inherits records, relationships and unresolved tasks, not their personality or social standing.

## 20. The Pokémon That Comes Every Year

The same persistent wild Pokémon may have been observed near the festival for several editions. This year it does not appear. The absence becomes an observation problem, not a guaranteed rescue quest.

## 21. The Festival That Outgrew the Square

Attendance now exceeds the original public space. Options include timed access, distributed activities, a second venue or shrinking the program. Public Space, Tourism and Accessibility all matter.

## 22. The Ceremony With No Miracle

A ritual asks for favorable weather. The weather is ordinary and nothing supernatural happens. The ceremony still matters socially. This seed exists to protect the project from turning every belief into magic.

## 23. The Ceremony With an Actual Anomaly

During another edition, a genuinely unusual phenomenon occurs. Science, Myth and Public Memory must investigate it separately; the fact that it happened during a ritual does not prove the ritual caused it.

## 24. The Guest Delegation

A neighboring region sends performers, researchers and officials to a local observance. Visitors participate under guest rules without acquiring local authority or cultural membership.

## 25. The Missing Ceremonial Object

A historically important object cannot be located during preparation. Custody records show several legitimate handoffs. The case begins as inventory/custody reconciliation, not automatic theft.

## 26. The Edition After the Fire

A wildfire changed the landscape used by an annual route. The new edition includes a recovery exhibit and a revised path while old photographs preserve the pre-fire version.

## 27. The Children's Version

A simplified daytime version of a late-night observance has existed for years. It is not “fake”; it has become its own stable branch of the tradition with different participation rules.

## 28. The Sponsor Wants the Name

A sponsor offers significant funding if the event adopts a branded title. Funding, naming rights, stewardship and cultural identity become separate negotiation issues.

## 29. The Last Ferry Home

A coastal observance ends after the final regular ferry. Residents know this; tourists do not. Transport planning becomes part of festival design rather than a random travel punishment.

## 30. The Hundredth Edition Archive

A centennial edition triggers a project to collect posters, photographs, menus, route maps, volunteer records and oral histories. The archive reveals gaps, corrections and contradictory memories without collapsing them into one official story.

# Long Arc A — Five Editions of the Bloom Walk

Year 1: the walk follows a fixed date and the flowers peak on time.

Year 2: bloom arrives early; visitors see mostly late flowers.

Year 3: organizers split the ceremonial date from the ecological walk.

Year 4: tourism surges around the actual bloom and pressures a sensitive habitat.

Year 5: the community adopts a flexible observation window and a fixed civic gathering.

The arc connects Seasonality, Flora, Tourism, Public Memory and Conservation. No mechanical blessing or spawn bonus is required.

# Long Arc B — The Festival After the Bridge

A town's oldest annual observance depends on crossing a historic bridge. Over several years the bridge is restricted, repaired, replaced and partially preserved. Each edition uses a different route while debate continues over what makes the observance recognizably the same.

Architecture, Public Works, Accessibility, Archives and Material Culture all contribute. The arc can end with several legitimate practices coexisting.

# Long Arc C — The Candle Years

A recurring winter observance becomes a continuity anchor across a long campaign. Early editions show novice Trainers and young institutions. Later editions show changed careers, retired organizers, new residents, corrected public memory and altered routes. Returning to the event reveals change without requiring exposition dumps.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Procession Route Interruption

FULL:

- moving procession with protected lanes;
- civilians/noncombatants represented through validated objective rules;
- route alternatives available during combat;
- opponents or wild Pokémon can withdraw rather than fight to KO;
- AI understands PROTECT, WITHDRAW and CLEAR_ROUTE goals;
- temporary festival geometry appears in Minecraft playback.

REDUCED:

- procession pauses before combat;
- civilians leave the battle area;
- organizers choose a safe static square or street segment;
- AutoPTU resolves a normal battle with legal combatants only;
- procession resumes, reroutes or ends afterward based on world state.

Capability dependencies:

VERIFIED foundations: targeting/footprints/range/LoS; base movement legality; core calculations; action economy/initiative; AI legal-action infrastructure.

PARTIAL when exact combat content uses them: full turn/round lifecycle; full stateful damage pipeline; status lifecycle; move-specific behavior; abilities; items; Trainer Features/perks.

BLOCKING for FULL: complete movement including interception/forced movement; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if temporary route features matter tactically; AI tactical policy; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Festival Grounds Evacuation

FULL:

- multiple exits and live crowd flow;
- protected noncombatant zones;
- temporary stalls/objects with validated interaction rules;
- AI avoids civilians and supports evacuation goals;
- optional weather/hazard phase only if exact mechanics exist.

REDUCED:

- evacuation resolves in overworld state first;
- temporary objects are removed from tactical authority;
- the battle starts only after a safe perimeter is created;
- any remaining incident uses a static arena.

BLOCKING for FULL: complete movement; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions when relevant; AI tactical policy; adapter/playback.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Bell Tower Challenge

FULL:

- players must reach a landmark through a challenge route;
- alternate movement modes matter only when individually validated;
- other competitors use objective-aware AI;
- the final ceremonial act occurs only after the mechanical challenge resolves;
- no supernatural benefit is granted by ringing the bell unless canon + rules explicitly support one.

REDUCED:

- climbing/traversal resolves as overworld route state or validated Skill challenge;
- any battle takes place on a static checkpoint arena;
- the bell interaction is narrative world state after the encounter;
- success produces public memory/standing only, not a combat buff.

BLOCKING for FULL battle version: complete movement if the route requires climbing/forced movement/interception; AI tactical policy; adapter/playback. Exact Skills/Features remain pending PTU/Caelo validation.

# Non-canon boundaries

These seeds do not establish a religion, ethnic group, sacred law, universal calendar, supernatural ritual effect, legal holiday system, compulsory rite, divine entity, family structure or region-wide tradition.

No participation creates mechanical friendship, Loyalty, healing, luck, capture, evolution, Accuracy, Evasion, Initiative, Weather, Contest or Skill effects.
