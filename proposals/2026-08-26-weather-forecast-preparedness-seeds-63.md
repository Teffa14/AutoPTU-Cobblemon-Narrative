# Weather Forecast & Preparedness Seeds — Pass 63

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

These concepts are original Ouros candidates derived from the Pass-63 research structures. Names, institutions, locations, technologies and practices are placeholders until canon review.

## The Forecast That Changed at Noon

A route office posts a morning forecast that keeps an exposed path open. Several hours later, new observations narrow the safe window and a revised bulletin recommends an earlier closure.

The playable question is who received which version and what decisions were already in motion.

Possible continuity:

- a field team returns early;
- a courier batch is held;
- one traveler only saw the original board;
- the old forecast remains archived;
- later review finds no misconduct, only a legitimate revision based on new evidence.

Do not force the revised forecast to become perfectly correct.

## Two Stations, One Valley

Two observation nodes repeatedly report different conditions for what locals casually call the same valley.

The investigation compares:

- actual node locations;
- elevation and exposure if canonized;
- report times;
- equipment state;
- local testimony;
- data gaps;
- prior observations.

Possible conclusion states include a recurring local difference, a station problem, timing mismatch or insufficient evidence.

The scenario should not begin by assuming one station is faulty.

## The Warning That Outlived the Storm

Conditions improve sooner than expected. Residents ask why a bridge, trail or service remains closed.

The answer may be procedural and physical: the storm ended, but inspection has not confirmed that the asset is safe.

This seed connects Weather Forecast to Facility Maintenance, Travel and public information. A player can help perform or enable the relevant inspection without turning the forecast service into the owner of infrastructure.

## Rain Plan Already Half Built

A recurring public event has a documented poor-weather plan. The forecast changes while temporary infrastructure is already being installed.

Choices may include:

- finish the original layout;
- activate the alternate layout;
- split activities across spaces;
- delay one component;
- cancel only the part with the unresolved dependency.

The next edition remembers which workaround actually worked.

This seed extends the Temporary Public Events layer rather than creating another festival system.

## The Ferry That Waited

A passenger service delays departure because the forecast window overlaps an exposed leg.

No disaster occurs.

Some passengers later complain that the delay was unnecessary because conditions remained manageable. The operator can point to the information available at departure time, not to hindsight.

This supports a world where good preparedness can produce an apparently uneventful outcome and still matter.

No maritime technology, ferry company or formal weather authority is canonized by this seed.

## The Missing Morning Reading

A forecast arrives with lower confidence because one familiar observation node did not report.

The player can inspect the site.

Potential causes:

- staff absence;
- blocked access;
- power or communication problem if the technology exists;
- maintenance overdue;
- damaged instrument;
- a harmless administrative gap;
- cause unresolved.

Wild Pokémon near the site are not automatically responsible.

If a battle occurs, use the reduced Weather Station Blackout contract until tactical weather and adapter support exist.

## Field Team Before the Front

A research team wants one final observation window before worsening conditions reach a field site.

The players decide whether to:

- help finish early;
- bring back existing samples or notes;
- cancel the outing;
- relocate the observation to a safer site;
- accept incomplete data.

This can be played without combat. The loss condition can simply be a data gap rather than injury or catastrophe.

## The Delivery That Was Held on Purpose

A replacement part for a facility is ready to travel, but the courier service holds the dispatch because the final leg is under a weather advisory.

The delayed delivery keeps the facility LIMITED longer.

The important causal chain remains explicit:

forecast -> courier decision -> delayed physical handoff -> maintenance schedule consequence.

The forecast does not directly set facility state.

## The Shop That Prepared for the Wrong Day

A small service changes staffing and stock in preparation for a predicted weather-related demand pulse. The conditions arrive later or elsewhere.

The story examines ordinary operational cost without inventing dynamic price simulation.

Possible follow-up:

- some preparation remains useful;
- perishable or time-sensitive stock is handled by existing systems if canonized;
- workers revise future planning;
- regular customers remember the unusual service day.

## Three Warnings, One Noticeboard

A public board still shows an old advisory under a newer one. A third update exists in another channel.

The player reconstructs information dissemination and discovers that several actors made reasonable but different choices because they had different versions.

This can connect Communications, Rumor/Testimony and local service routines.

The resolution is about provenance and receipt, not choosing a liar.

## The Clear Patch Nobody Forecast

A localized break in poor weather creates an unexpectedly calm window around one site.

Players can document it, but one observation is not enough to establish a permanent microclimate.

If similar observations accumulate in later seasons, the world may create a provisional local pattern claim.

This seed is designed for long-term continuity rather than immediate revelation.

## The Storm That Missed the Town

A strong forecast causes preparation, but the worst observed conditions pass outside the settlement.

Potential consequences:

- an event loses attendance but remains safe;
- a ferry delay causes missed connections;
- a repair crew was staged and never used;
- a nearby rural route receives the conditions instead;
- residents debate whether the preparation was excessive.

The design should preserve the original forecast and its uncertainty. Hindsight does not rewrite what actors knew.

## A Forecast Is Not a Cause

An ecological event occurs during predicted rain. Several people begin saying the forecast “brought” or “caused” the event.

Players can separate:

- the forecast;
- the actual rain observation;
- the ecological event;
- prior evidence of correlation;
- any causal hypothesis.

This seed directly reinforces the Science and Rumor boundaries.

## Last Inspection After the Rain

A facility or temporary structure appears fine after weather clears, but reopening depends on a condition check.

The task can involve:

- photographing visible state;
- reviewing earlier defects;
- comparing water intrusion or movement signs if supported;
- deciding whether a specialist is needed;
- reopening only the unaffected part.

Weather provides the trigger. Facility Maintenance owns the conclusion.

## Weather Board Regular

A recurring NPC checks the same public forecast board each morning because their work depends on route conditions.

Over time the player can notice:

- routine check times;
- which products matter to that job;
- how the person reacts to uncertainty;
- changes after a prior bad season;
- the routine disappearing when the role or residence changes.

This is an everyday-life callback, not an automatic quest giver.

## Long arc — A Forecast Network Learns a Region

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

The arc reuses existing settlements and routes over many visits.

Early phase:

A small set of observation points and local reports establishes baseline behavior. Forecast products are broad because evidence coverage is sparse.

First stress:

A significant weather window exposes a blind spot. One route receives conditions earlier or more severely than expected. Services respond unevenly because distribution and observation coverage differ.

Review:

Players can help inspect the missing data chain. The result may justify repairs, a new observation routine or a proposal for another node. Any new infrastructure requires the relevant canon/public-works process.

Adaptation:

Future forecasts become more locally scoped where evidence supports it. An event changes its rain plan. A ferry adjusts its decision process. A conservation team alters fieldwork timing.

Second stress:

A later season produces a different miss. The network is better informed but still fallible. This prevents institutional progression from becoming omniscience.

Persistent payoff:

The world remembers prior forecasts, service decisions, warnings, false alarms, successful preparation, station repairs and unresolved local patterns. Familiar NPCs refer to specific past events rather than a generic “weather reputation”.

## Mystery — Four Forecasts, Two Windows

Four forecast documents appear to disagree about one storm.

Investigation reveals that they were issued at different times and cover two overlapping spatial windows. One product was superseded before its validity window. Another was copied without its update timestamp.

Possible resolution:

The documents are internally coherent once provenance is restored. The mystery was created by distribution and presentation, not false forecasting.

Alternative authored resolutions may include an actual data error, but that needs evidence.
