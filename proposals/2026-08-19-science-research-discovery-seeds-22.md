# Ouros Science, Research & Discovery Seeds — Pass 22

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external characters, dialogue, plots or bespoke mechanics are copied.

## 1. The Missing Dawn Count

A field station has recorded the same species at sunrise for years. This season the count drops sharply, but midday sightings remain normal.

Possible causes remain open:
- a route shift;
- observer disturbance;
- construction noise;
- a new feeding site;
- predation pressure;
- instrument failure;
- timekeeping error.

The first quest is not “fix the population.” It is “determine whether the pattern is real.”

## 2. Two Maps, One Migration

Two respected institutions publish different migration routes for the same Pokémon population. Both maps are based on real evidence, but their observation windows cover different years.

Players can:
- compare archives;
- revisit old observation sites;
- collect new reports;
- discover that the route itself may have shifted over time.

No institution needs to be dishonest.

## 3. The Null Expedition

A prestigious team predicts a rare behavior during a specific weather window. Players spend substantial effort reaching the site, but the behavior never occurs.

The outcome can still be valuable:
- the hypothesis weakens;
- the timing window is revised;
- another environmental variable becomes important;
- future expeditions stop wasting resources on the same assumption.

The system should reward useful negative knowledge without inventing mechanical XP.

## 4. The Research Assistant Who Disagrees

A junior researcher notices that a lab's public explanation overstates what the data actually supports.

The conflict centers on:
- interpretation;
- reputation;
- publication timing;
- institutional pressure;
- uncertainty communication.

The junior researcher is not automatically correct, and the lab director is not automatically corrupt.

## 5. Three Observers, Three Behaviors

Three players observe the same wild collective from different locations and report apparently incompatible behavior.

Later analysis reveals that each saw a different subgroup and time window.

Purpose:
- teach provenance;
- reinforce that player knowledge is partial;
- create a dataset larger than any one perspective.

## 6. The Closed Field Station

A remote station has stopped reporting. The problem might be mundane:
- damaged power;
- washed-out access;
- staff evacuation;
- broken communications;
- wildlife disturbance.

Research records stored inside are more valuable than the hardware.

### Encounter contract

FULL version may require:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- objective-aware AI;
- protect/escape behavior;
- complete movement if equipment lanes and interception matter;
- Minecraft playback.

REDUCED version:
The team determines station safety in the overworld. Any battle occurs on a static legal arena away from fragile equipment. Recovery of records happens after combat.

## 7. The Replication Challenge

A famous observation has been cited for years, but no independent team has reproduced it under the same conditions.

Players receive funding to attempt replication.

Interesting outcomes:
- consistent result;
- partial match;
- failure to reproduce;
- method turns out to be underspecified;
- location conditions changed since the original study.

## 8. The Unlabeled Box

An archive contains old samples with incomplete provenance.

The contents may be scientifically valuable, but using them immediately would contaminate future claims because nobody can prove where or when they were collected.

The adventure centers on reconstructing custody and field notes rather than opening the box for loot.

## 9. The Site Everyone Uses

A convenient research meadow has become so popular that researchers themselves are changing Pokémon behavior there.

The institution must decide whether to:
- reduce visits;
- rotate sites;
- build observation blinds;
- publish new protocols;
- accept that old datasets are no longer comparable.

## 10. Citizen Survey Week

A regional lab asks Trainers, farmers, couriers, Rangers, fishers and ordinary residents to submit sightings during one week.

The challenge is data quality, not contribution count.

Ouros can preserve:
- observer identity;
- time/place;
- confidence;
- duplicate reports;
- impossible sightings;
- corroborated patterns.

The final output may become a public migration map with uncertainty bands rather than a perfect truth layer.

## 11. The Sensitive Nesting Paper

A research team confirms an unusually important nesting area.

Publishing the exact coordinates would help other researchers but could also increase tourism, collection pressure or disturbance.

The dispute concerns access and stewardship, not whether the discovery is real.

Potential outputs:
- public summary with coordinates withheld;
- controlled access;
- local steward partnership;
- delayed publication;
- a conservation project.

## 12. The Lab Without a Professor

A respected professor retires, disappears from public work or leaves for a long expedition. The lab remains.

Possible staff responses:
- one assistant becomes acting lead;
- specialties split into separate projects;
- funding becomes uncertain;
- another institution offers partnership;
- former students return as collaborators.

This creates institutional succession without assuming every research center belongs to one permanent NPC.

## 13. Research Rivalry Without Villainy

Two field teams compete to explain the same ecological anomaly.

They can:
- share raw data but disagree on interpretation;
- use different methods;
- race to publish;
- collaborate after both methods fail;
- remain professional rivals for years.

Winning the argument should depend on evidence, not a battle result.

## 14. The Pokémon That Changes the Method

A study uses a standard observation procedure until one individual Pokémon repeatedly notices and reacts to the equipment.

The correct response may be to redesign the method rather than label the Pokémon unusually aggressive.

This seed supports persistent individual Pokémon identity without assigning human motives.

## 15. Urban Adaptation Project

A city redevelopment project coincides with a rise in several Pokémon populations.

Research programs split the question:
- which species increased;
- where they came from;
- which infrastructure they use;
- whether food supply changed;
- whether older habitats were displaced;
- whether reports increased because observation improved.

Outputs can feed civic planning, ecology, media and public works layers.

## 16. The Fossil Provenance Dispute

A fossil in a museum has been displayed for decades under one locality label. New archival evidence suggests the excavation site was recorded incorrectly.

The issue affects:
- scientific interpretation;
- regional pride;
- museum signage;
- possible re-survey of two old sites.

It does not automatically change the fossil's PTU species or restoration rules.

## 17. Field Team Division

A multiplayer expedition has several simultaneous responsibilities:
- observe a behavior window;
- maintain instruments;
- navigate a second route;
- interview local witnesses;
- protect the camp if danger emerges.

No single player needs every Skill or Pokémon capability.

Operational roles are world-state assignments only until mapped to validated PTU mechanics.

## 18. Remote Sensor Retrieval

A sensor contains the only complete record of an unusual night migration. A wild collective currently occupies the route.

## 19. Field Station Disturbance

Wild Pokémon begin interfering with a monitoring site shortly after a new instrument is installed.

Competing hypotheses:
- sound;
- light;
- scent;
- changed human traffic;
- unrelated seasonal behavior.

### FULL version dependencies

Potentially requires:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- complete movement if machinery creates chokepoints;
- AI tactical policy for avoid/protect behavior;
- full lifecycle/damage/status/move/ability/item support for selected combatants;
- Minecraft playback.

### REDUCED version

Instrument effects are tested outside battle. Any triggered fight uses a static arena and legal combat only. The research question survives unchanged.

## 20. Weather Platform Replication

Two labs disagree over whether a behavior correlates with a weather condition. Players run an independent observation window at a remote platform.

### FULL version dependencies

If battle occurs under mechanically meaningful weather:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full damage/status pipeline as required;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy if behavior depends on objectives;
- Minecraft playback.

### REDUCED version

Weather remains world-state context only. Any battle uses a static legal environment without claiming weather mechanics that Java has not verified. The research result comes from observations and evidence.

## 21. The Correct Result Nobody Likes

A study finds that a beloved local explanation for a recurring Pokémon phenomenon is poorly supported.

The discovery creates:
- media debate;
- museum revision;
- frustration among residents;
- new research questions;
- no requirement that the tradition itself disappear.

Scientific conclusion and cultural meaning remain separate.

## 22. The Popular Result That Fails Review

A spectacular claim spreads through news and social spaces before reviewers notice a methodological flaw.

The correction becomes a public-memory event.

Later players may encounter:
- people who only heard the first version;
- people who know the correction;
- institutions that changed policy too quickly;
- archived posters that still contain the old claim.

## 23. The Forgotten Dataset

A decades-long dataset ends because the original researcher retired and nobody maintained the project.

A new team can revive it, but the gap in years remains meaningful.

The player is not asked to magically “complete” missing history.

## 24. The Instrument Maker

A local artisan builds specialized field equipment with researchers.

This creates a bridge between:
- material culture;
- crafting;
- research methods;
- supply chains;
- apprenticeships.

Mechanical bonuses are forbidden until PTU/Caelo and engine validation exist.

## 25. The Research Festival

A town hosts a public week where labs, museums, clubs and field teams present discoveries.

Possible activities:
- public talks;
- exhibitions;
- observation walks;
- demonstration battles using ordinary legal rules;
- poster disputes;
- recruiting assistants;
- archive donations.

This uses the event/memory layer without turning science into a tournament.

## 26. Long Arc — The Changing River Study

Phase 1: players notice contradictory reports about Pokémon distribution along a river.

Phase 2: several institutions establish different monitoring methods.

Phase 3: a public-works project changes flow, access and settlement traffic.

Phase 4: old datasets become difficult to compare with new conditions.

Phase 5: players help separate actual ecological change from observation bias.

Possible ending states:
- shared monitoring standard;
- two accepted regional models;
- continuing disagreement;
- a new habitat-restoration project;
- a discovery that the original question was framed incorrectly.

No predetermined villain required.

## 27. Long Arc — The Professor Network

Several regional labs exchange observations about a phenomenon seen in different environments.

Each institution has:
- different specialties;
- different equipment;
- different local trust;
- incomplete data.

Players become the connective tissue through fieldwork, sample custody where legal, replication and communication.

The arc culminates in a regional synthesis, not necessarily a boss encounter.

## 28. Long Arc — Research Boom

A major discovery makes one small settlement scientifically important.

Consequences unfold gradually:
- temporary field camps;
- new visitors;
- housing pressure;
- workshops and instrument demand;
- conservation concerns;
- media attention;
- rival projects;
- employment;
- expanded transport;
- locals disagreeing about growth.

The research layer feeds settlement, civic, economy, media and ecology systems without assuming the discovery itself is magical.
