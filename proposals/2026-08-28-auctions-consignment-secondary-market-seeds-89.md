# Ouros Auctions, Consignment & Secondary-Market Seeds — Pass 89

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-28

Nothing in this file establishes a settlement, institution, property rule, market custom, price, fee or canonical NPC.

These candidates depend on `design/auctions-consignment-secondary-market-extension.md` and use the provenance conclusions in `research/2026-08-28-auctions-consignment-secondary-market-scan-89.md`.

## Design intent

The market should create stories because exact objects, claims and actors move through it, not because prices randomly fluctuate.

Strong candidates should reuse existing districts, institutions, workshops, archives, transit services and recurring NPCs. A secondary market is most useful when it makes earlier world history visible again.

## Candidate — The Lot That Has Been Here Before

Premise:
A distinctive persistent object appears in a new catalogue years or months after the party last saw it under another custodian.

Opening facts:
- item identity is strongly supported by repair marks or provenance;
- current listing is legitimate enough to be public;
- the catalogue includes only part of its known history;
- the former custodian may or may not know it is being offered.

Player directions:
- ignore the sale;
- attend only to observe who is interested;
- ask the venue how it arrived;
- contact a former holder;
- bid or represent another actor;
- research the gap in provenance;
- decide the gap is not important enough to stop the sale.

Possible consequences:
- the object enters an institution and becomes publicly accessible;
- it remains private;
- it receives a corrected catalogue description;
- the former holder explains a legitimate transfer never previously recorded;
- the lot sells while the historical question remains unresolved.

No theft assumption is generated.

## Candidate — Catalogue Correction Before Opening

Premise:
A catalogue writer discovers that an attribution copied from an older source is weaker than initially believed only hours before public viewing begins.

Conflict:
The venue can publish a correction, pause the lot or continue with explicit uncertainty depending on its authored procedure.

Useful actors:
- catalogue writer who found the discrepancy;
- consignor who believes the older attribution;
- researcher with a source nobody else had checked;
- prospective buyer who already traveled for the viewing;
- operator deciding whether uncertainty is acceptable for continued display.

Design lesson:
Corrections should have operational cost without turning truthfulness into punishment.

## Candidate — The Proxy Bidder

Premise:
A small institution wants an object connected to its own local history but cannot send its usual representative.

The player can receive a narrow purchasing mandate.

Meaningful constraints:
- exact lot scope;
- explicit funding ceiling/reference;
- public or private principal identity according to authored policy;
- conditions under which the player must stop;
- no authority over unrelated lots.

Potential tension:
A competing institution has a different but legitimate reason to want the object.

Possible ending:
The party loses the auction and later negotiates access, a loan, a copy, an exhibition or research permission instead. Failure to acquire does not end the thread.

## Candidate — Unsold, Still Important

Premise:
An object receives no acceptable offer.

The interesting story starts after the failed sale.

Possible follow-ups:
- consignor takes it home;
- an institution that could not afford it offers conservation help instead;
- public interest increases because of the catalogue entry;
- a researcher asks permission to document it;
- the object returns in a later market with revised provenance;
- the consignor withdraws from public exchange permanently.

No-sale is a durable event, not a failed content trigger.

## Candidate — Two Descriptions, One Object

Premise:
A repaired tool, artwork, relic candidate or institutional object appears in two old catalogues with descriptions that cannot both be completely correct.

Investigation inputs:
- photographs;
- repair records;
- maker marks;
- archive dates;
- former custodian interviews;
- catalogue revisions;
- transport history.

Possible resolution:
The two descriptions refer to different stages of the same object's life.

Alternative:
One description simply copied an earlier mistake.

The scene can end with uncertainty preserved.

## Candidate — The Institution Selling Its Duplicate

Premise:
An archive, museum, laboratory, workshop or other institution proposes releasing an object it considers duplicative or outside its current mandate.

Required handoff:
Archives/Collections must establish whether disposal/deaccession is actually authorized before the market layer does anything.

Stakeholder tension:
- internal staff see redundancy;
- a local group sees historical significance;
- a researcher values the object's provenance rather than its uniqueness;
- Finance sees a possible funding benefit;
- Public Memory may already associate the item with the institution.

No universal deaccession rule is invented.

## Candidate — One Lot, Three Principals

Premise:
Three visible bidders appear to be competing, but one is acting for a workshop and another for a community institution.

The social scene becomes about mandates and motives rather than wealth alone.

Potential reveals:
- two bidders actually seek different parts of a lot;
- one principal wants access rather than ownership;
- one bidder has a hard funding condition and stops early;
- post-auction negotiation creates a shared-use arrangement if existing agreement systems allow it.

## Candidate — The Item That Should Not Be in the Catalogue

Premise:
A party member recognizes an item as matching an open Found Property record, unresolved Courier shipment or collection-custody record.

Critical rule:
Recognition is a lead, not proof.

Workflow:
1. compare exact persistent identity/provenance;
2. check whether the old record is still current;
3. determine whether a legitimate transfer occurred;
4. pause/escalate only under the venue's authored authority;
5. hand off to Found Property, Courier, Collections or Case as appropriate.

Possible outcome:
Everything is legitimate and the old record was simply never closed.

## Candidate — The Famous Sale Nobody Recorded Correctly

Premise:
Public memory remembers a dramatic old sale, but surviving finance, provenance and catalogue records disagree about what actually changed hands.

Useful distinction:
The cultural memory of “the sale” can be true as a social event while details about price, buyer, lot composition or later ownership remain uncertain.

Potential locations reused:
- archive reading room;
- old market square;
- current successor venue;
- former buyer's workshop/residence;
- public memorial/display referencing the story.

## Candidate — Market Day After the Storm

Premise:
A recurring specialist market resumes after a disruption, but the expected catalogue is thinner and several consignors are absent.

Possible causes:
- transport still limited;
- objects remain in safe storage elsewhere;
- a consignor changed plans;
- venue inspection reduced capacity;
- unrelated coincidence.

The market state reflects recovery without manufacturing scarcity bonuses or random price inflation.

## Candidate — The Viewing That Changes the Question

Premise:
A potential buyer attends intending to verify authenticity, but direct observation instead reveals that the public description is probably describing the wrong period in the object's life.

The resulting question changes from “is it real?” to “when was this modification made?”

This is designed to push mysteries away from binary fake/real answers.

## Candidate — A Bidder Returns as a Seller

Premise:
A recurring NPC who previously competed unsuccessfully for a lot later consigns a different object.

Persistent callback opportunities:
- their financial position may or may not have changed;
- their interests may have evolved;
- they can remember the earlier public event;
- former rivals may now become information sources;
- losing the earlier lot did not freeze their character arc.

No relationship label is inferred automatically.

## Candidate — The Workshop Wants the Parts, the Historian Wants the Whole

Premise:
An old machine/tool assembly is offered as one lot.

Competing interpretations:
- workshop sees reusable components;
- historian values configuration/context;
- owner wants a clean sale;
- conservator thinks disassembly may erase information;
- another institution may only need documentation.

Possible outcomes:
- intact acquisition;
- negotiated documentation before disassembly;
- no sale;
- owner changes the lot composition before opening if permitted;
- a reproduction solves one stakeholder need.

Mechanical crafting benefits are never inferred from historical value.

## Mystery — Eight Catalogue Entries, Six Physical Lots

Opening:
An old market catalogue lists eight entries, but photographs show only six physical groupings on display.

Possible explanations:
- two entries were combined before opening;
- one was withdrawn;
- one remained off-site;
- catalogue numbering survived a revision;
- two descriptions reference components of the same lot;
- records are incomplete.

Required evidence:
- catalogue revisions;
- display photographs;
- intake records;
- custody handoffs;
- offer records;
- consignor statements;
- event schedule.

No forced crime reveal.

## Mystery — Four Offers, Three Actual Bidders

Opening:
A preserved offer ledger appears to show four competitors.

Investigation:
One actor may have submitted an early offer and later bid as representative for another principal, or one offer may supersede another under a changed mandate.

The mystery teaches that actor identity, principal identity and offer identity are distinct.

## Long arc — A Market Learns What It Is For

Visit 1 — Baseline
The player experiences an ordinary market cycle. A few recurring operators and spectators are established. Most activity compresses.

Visit 2 — Memorable lot
One item with strong local provenance draws competing interests. The important outcome may be acquisition, no-sale or a corrected catalogue.

Visit 3 — Procedure under pressure
A provenance discrepancy, failed close or transport interruption exposes a weak handoff between Market, Finance, Courier or Collections.

Visit 4 — Visible adaptation
The venue changes something concrete: clearer catalogue revisions, separate viewing windows, better custody staging, published uncertainty notes or a different transfer process.

Visit 5 — Callback
An old bidder, consignor or object returns in a different context. The earlier event is remembered accurately according to actor knowledge.

Visit 6 — Market consequence outside the market
An acquired object now appears in a museum, workshop, community hall, research project or private collection. The sale mattered because world state changed elsewhere.

Visit 7 — New equilibrium
The venue has a recognizable history and procedure without needing a hidden “market level.” Some older practices may remain because no event justified changing them.

## Recurring NPC archetype candidates

The Careful Catalogue Writer
Interested in wording, source lineage and correction history. Their arc can be about becoming better at expressing uncertainty rather than always being right.

The Institutional Buyer
Works under explicit mandates and funding limits. Can lose bids without becoming incompetent.

The Regular Spectator
Rarely buys anything but remembers public sales and bidder behavior. Useful as local-knowledge source with the same limits as any witness.

The Repair-Specialist Consignor
Objects they sell often carry visible modification histories. Their participation naturally links workshops to provenance.

The Community Representative
Sometimes opposes removal of locally meaningful objects, sometimes supports sale to fund preservation elsewhere. No universal ideology is assumed.

The Handler
Knows custody and staging better than historical attribution. Can notice that “the paperwork says lot 12 but I moved this crate under lot 14” without knowing why.

## Faction/institution dynamics

Competing collection mandates
Two institutions can value the same object for different reasons. Their conflict can remain procedural and finite.

Local retention versus wider access
One group may prefer an object remain nearby while another promises conservation or public access elsewhere. Neither position is automatically correct.

Workshop versus collection use
A scarce historical tool may still have practical value. Material Culture, Archives and Workplaces may all have legitimate interests.

Public fundraiser versus preservation risk
A sale may support a public goal while creating pressure around important objects. Finance and Collections must remain separate authorities.

## Encounter — Auction Hall Evacuation

Full version intent:
Visitors withdraw through several routes while a hostile event is resolved. Intercept, forced displacement, access zones and tactical AI can matter. Persistent lots remain world objects rather than improvised combatants or destructible HP bars.

Capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions when route state is tactical;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced version:
The hall evacuates before battle composition. Lots are secured or remain outside the grid. Ouros selects exact participants. AutoPTU resolves a fixed legal arena. Market/Event/Facility state resumes afterward.

## Encounter — Consignment Transfer Interruption

Full version intent:
A transfer party attempts withdrawal or route completion while opponents pursue or deny access. Intercept/forced movement, terrain and objective-aware AI may matter.

Reduced version:
The transfer pauses and handlers/cargo move outside the tactical grid before battle. The fight resolves on a static arena. Courier and Market determine the next transfer step after AutoPTU returns a result.

The object cannot be awarded, stolen, delivered or authenticated merely because one side won.

## Noncombat encounter — Provenance Viewing

A catalogue claim conflicts with an old photograph or repair record.

Playable now through:
- source comparison;
- actor interviews;
- catalogue revision history;
- Material Culture provenance;
- Archive/Photography evidence;
- explicit uncertainty.

No special auction mechanic is necessary.
