# Pass 153 — Ritual, tradition, pilgrimage, and site-memory seeds

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Canon effect: NONE

These seeds exercise the Pass 153 architecture. Names, sites, communities, histories, Pokémon associations, and outcomes are placeholders until approved through Ouros canon review.

## Design target

Create regional stories where tradition matters because people keep doing, remembering, changing, arguing about, or travelling for something. Do not require supernatural confirmation. Do not reduce every tradition to a deception reveal. Do not assume the oldest surviving version is the only legitimate one.

## Seed 1 — The Road Everyone Walks Differently

Three settlements use the same mountain road during the same season.

One group treats the journey as remembrance of an evacuation. Another remembers it as gratitude for safe passage. A third says the route predates both stories and is simply where families used to meet before winter.

The road is physically shared. The interpretation is not.

Adventure uses:

- interview participants before the journey;
- compare household records with roadside markers;
- discover that one settlement changed the route after a landslide two generations ago;
- decide how to represent all three interpretations in a new public wayfinding project;
- protect an immediate route segment without allowing battle victory to decide which story is true.

## Seed 2 — The Shrine With Three Founding Stories

A small hilltop shelter has three incompatible founding dates.

Evidence suggests:

- the current building is relatively recent;
- an older marker existed nearby;
- the practice of stopping at the hill may predate both structures.

Local groups disagree because each is answering a different question when they say "the shrine began here."

Possible resolution: the Chronicle records three distinct milestones rather than selecting a fake single birthday.

## Seed 3 — The Festival That Changed After the Flood

A river settlement maintains an annual festival. Older records show the procession once crossed a bridge that no longer exists. After a historic flood, the route moved inland and a rescue remembrance became central to the observance.

A tourism office now markets the modern form as unchanged ancient tradition.

The conflict is not "tradition versus fake tradition." It is about whether public presentation can acknowledge transformation without diminishing present-day meaning.

## Seed 4 — The Route Closed but the Pilgrimage Continued

Habitat protection closes the traditional final approach to a mountain lake.

Some participants want access restored. Others already adopted a new stopping point years ago. Rangers can prove the ecological sensitivity of the old route but cannot decide cultural legitimacy.

Potential outcomes include:

- alternate route recognized;
- seasonal limited access under separate authority;
- symbolic final stop moved;
- community split into variants;
- accepted disagreement.

No route decision proves anything about the old origin story.

## Seed 5 — The Pokémon Seen Once Every Generation

A household tradition claims that an extraordinary Pokémon appears near a specific ridge roughly once in a generation.

Known evidence:

- several written sightings exist;
- descriptions are not identical;
- some generations have no surviving record;
- modern environmental observations establish unusual conditions at the ridge but do not identify a Pokémon;
- one photograph is ambiguous.

The campaign can investigate the tradition for years without ever guaranteeing an encounter.

Canon guardrail: until a direct Ouros fact approves presence, this remains `HISTORICALLY_ASSOCIATED_BY_CLAIM` or another evidenced lower state.

## Seed 6 — The Stone Everyone Touches for Different Reasons

A worn stone stands beside a market road.

Travelers touch it for luck. Local couriers say it marks the halfway point. Older residents remember leaving messages beneath it. A museum placard claims it is part of an older boundary marker, but the physical evidence is uncertain.

The same material anchor supports several living meanings without needing one universal explanation.

## Seed 7 — The Revival Committee and the Old Families

A civic committee wants to revive a dormant winter observance based on archive material. Several families say the proposed version combines practices that were never performed together.

The archive is genuine. The reconstruction is still interpretive.

Potential tensions:

- public funding;
- naming;
- route access;
- which variant is taught in school;
- whether the event should be described as revival, reconstruction, or new festival inspired by older material.

## Seed 8 — The Guide Who Rejects the Official Route

A regional brochure marks a single "historic pilgrimage path." A veteran guide says that route was standardized recently for visitors and that older families used multiple approaches depending on weather, village, and season.

The guide may be correct about local practice and still lack documentary evidence for every segment.

This seed connects Travel, oral testimony, mapping, public memory, and tradition continuity without making oral evidence automatically inferior or automatically decisive.

## Seed 9 — The Bell That Isn't Ancient

A famous bell is said by visitors to have "called pilgrims for centuries."

Material provenance proves the current bell is only decades old. Records then show that an earlier bell occupied the same ritual role, and before that a wooden clapper was used.

The correction changes object history but does not erase continuity of the signal practice.

## Seed 10 — The Ceremony That Survived Without Its Meaning

A town performs a short sequence of gestures before opening a seasonal market. Nobody can confidently explain the oldest meaning.

Several modern interpretations coexist:

- gratitude for safe harvest;
- remembrance of a fire;
- acknowledgement of local Pokémon habitats;
- simply "what we do before opening."

The story tests whether Ouros can preserve meaningful practice while origin remains `UNKNOWN`.

## Seed 11 — The Site Named After an Event Nobody Can Date

A ravine bears a name referring to an alleged historic rescue by a powerful Pokémon.

The name is old enough to be historically significant. The rescue date cannot be established. Later monuments add details absent from the earliest records.

The campaign can resolve when particular details entered the tradition without resolving whether the original event occurred exactly as told.

## Seed 12 — The Legend That Became a Tourism Brand

A local story appears on station posters, food packaging, souvenirs, and a mascot costume.

Some residents enjoy the visibility. Others think commercial use has flattened important differences among local versions. A third group sees the new forms as the latest stage of a living tradition.

There is no required moral answer. Record who authorizes each use, who objects, and how the public story changes.

## Seed 13 — The Missing Verse

Three communities sing related versions of a processional song. Two contain a verse absent in the third. A recently catalogued notebook contains a fourth wording.

The mystery asks:

- which versions are independently attested;
- whether the missing verse was removed, added elsewhere, translated differently, or simply never shared;
- what each community currently considers its own version.

Finding the notebook does not grant an "original lyrics" flag.

## Seed 14 — The Procession That Split in Two

An organizational dispute caused one observance to become two separate processions using different dates and routes.

Decades later, younger participants treat both as ordinary parts of regional life. Older records still frame the split as a crisis.

Organization Lineage owns the institutional split. Pass 153 records the resulting practice variants.

## Seed 15 — The Shrine Keeper Who Says "I Don't Know"

A recurring caretaker knows the building, visitors, maintenance history, current practices, and local stories in exceptional detail.

When asked whether the extraordinary Pokémon in the oldest story literally appeared there, the caretaker answers that the evidence does not settle it.

The NPC archetype rewards expertise without omniscience and gives the setting permission to preserve uncertainty.

## Seed 16 — The Oldest Building Is Not the Oldest Tradition

Researchers discover that the building advertised as the tradition's origin was constructed after the earliest written description of the practice.

Further work suggests participants previously gathered outdoors nearby.

This produces a clean recontextualization:

- the building's advertised age is corrected;
- the practice may still be older;
- exact origin remains unknown;
- the current building remains culturally important.

## Mystery package — Five Dates for the First Pilgrimage

Five sources supply five dates:

1. earliest surviving written mention;
2. date on a later monument;
3. a household genealogy estimate;
4. date a formal association standardized the route;
5. date the current annual event began after a revival.

Every date can be accurate within its own proposition. The player solves the mystery by asking what each date actually dates.

## Mystery package — Three Songs, One Missing Verse

The investigation starts as an authenticity dispute and becomes a transmission-lineage problem. A schoolbook copied one local version, a radio performance popularized another, and an older household recording preserves a different order.

Possible end state: `RELATIONSHIP_PARTIALLY_RECONSTRUCTED`, not `ORIGINAL_VERSION_FOUND`.

## Mystery package — The Statue That Is Younger Than the Story

A statue is assumed to be the origin of a local Pokémon legend. Conservation records prove it was commissioned much later. Older newspapers then reveal that the sculptor was illustrating an already familiar story.

Object provenance changes. Tradition provenance becomes more interesting.

## Mystery package — The Route That Appears on No Old Map

Modern participants insist a forest segment is traditional, yet surviving maps omit it.

Possible explanations include:

- maps were too coarse;
- the route used an informal path;
- the segment was adopted later;
- an older road shifted location;
- participants use "traditional" to mean inherited from grandparents rather than ancient.

Do not preselect the answer before evidence exists.

## Mystery package — The Festival Everyone Calls Ancient

The festival itself is demonstrably recent, but several practices within it have older independent histories.

The correct historical description may be "new container, older elements." That can still be meaningful living tradition.

## Long arc — A Region Learns Its Oldest Story Has Always Had Variants

### Stage 1 — ordinary continuity

Players encounter the tradition through ordinary life: route markers, food, songs, a seasonal journey, school material, a caretaker, and casual disagreement over details.

No urgent mystery is announced.

### Stage 2 — a practical conflict

A route closure, restoration project, commercialization proposal, archive discovery, or organizational split creates a need to describe the tradition more precisely.

### Stage 3 — divergent evidence

Different settlements preserve different versions. Material evidence resolves some dates while making the lineage more complex. The party learns that "which story is true?" is several separate questions.

### Stage 4 — extraordinary association remains bounded

A historic claim involving an extraordinary Pokémon receives stronger or weaker support, but the campaign does not force direct appearance. A Legendary encounter is not the reward for finishing the investigation.

### Stage 5 — public decision

Communities decide how they want routes, plaques, festivals, archives, schools, or visitor materials to represent what is known and disputed. Different communities can make different choices.

### Stage 6 — durable aftermath

Months later, players can see updated signs, changed routes, preserved older markers, multiple versions presented side by side, or continued disagreement. Chronicle keeps previous representations rather than overwriting them.

Possible resolutions:

- coexistence of variants;
- shared observance with separate interpretations;
- formal acknowledgment of uncertain origin;
- revival with explicit discontinuity;
- one branch ending locally;
- tourism copy corrected;
- no consensus;
- `ACCEPTED_AMBIGUITY`.

## Encounter concept — Procession Route Evacuation Corridor

Full version: participants are withdrawing while hostile actors contest a route. Movement, Intercept/forced movement, reaction windows, objective-aware AI, and potentially hazards matter.

Readiness: BLOCKED by incomplete movement family, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions when used, tactical AI, and adapter/playback.

Reduced version: READY at narrative-contract level. Pause the observance. Remove participants from BattleSpec. Freeze route geometry. AutoPTU resolves explicit combatants only. Allowed outcome: `IMMEDIATE_PROCESSION_ROUTE_CLEAR`.

Narrative premise preserved: danger interrupted a living observance and someone made the immediate route safe.

## Encounter concept — Venerated Site Threshold Perimeter

Full version: differentiated goals around withdrawal, blocking access, protecting participants, and possibly environmental/site hazards.

Readiness: BLOCKED for rich behavior.

Reduced version: READY. The site remains static. Significant objects are outside targetable battle state unless separately contracted. Participants withdraw before initiative. Allowed outcome: `IMMEDIATE_SITE_THRESHOLD_PERIMETER_CLEAR`.

Victory cannot establish sanctity, authenticity, ownership, historical truth, or supernatural favor.

## Encounter concept — Pilgrim Pass Chokepoint

Full version: escort, weather, displacement, narrow terrain, rescue, and objective-aware enemy behavior.

Readiness: BLOCKED where those families are required.

Reduced version: READY. Travelers remain outside BattleSpec. Static geometry represents the pass. Explicit combatants resolve the immediate threat. Allowed outcome: `IMMEDIATE_PASS_APPROACH_CLEAR`. Travel decides continuation afterward.

## Encounter concept — Processional Standard Handoff Perimeter

Full version: contested carrying/custody, movement, interception, handoff timing, and objective-aware AI.

Readiness: BLOCKED because generic object-carry/handoff tactical semantics are not globally verified, alongside movement/policy gaps.

Reduced version: READY. Custody freezes outside BattleSpec. The standard cannot be targeted or transferred tactically. Allowed outcome: `IMMEDIATE_HANDOFF_APPROACH_CLEAR`. The actual handoff is a later Ouros world-state event.

## NPC/faction archetypes

### The careful caretaker

Knows current practice deeply, distinguishes memory from evidence, and is comfortable saying an origin is unknown.

### The revival organizer

Genuinely wants a dormant practice to live again but may overstate continuity because a clean story helps recruitment.

### The route historian

Tracks path changes, closures, bridges, ferries, and stops. Their expertise is spatial history, not metaphysical truth.

### The household transmitter

Preserves one version through family practice. Their version can be old and sincere without being universal.

### The commercial adapter

Turns stories into visitor experiences. Can support preservation, simplify complexity, distort claims, or do all three at once.

### The skeptical participant

Does not believe every origin claim but still values participation, family continuity, food, music, journey, or community.

### The literalist claimant

Treats inherited narrative as exact history. This position can create conflict without making the character foolish or automatically wrong.

## Hard guardrails for all seeds

- No tradition grants a buff unless a separately verified PTU mechanic explicitly does so.
- No pilgrimage guarantees an encounter, capture, blessing, prophecy fulfillment, or moral status.
- No battle result establishes authenticity or cosmology.
- No shrine label creates terrain mechanics.
- No Minecraft animation decides ritual completion.
- No Cobblemon BattleState decides combatants or world consequences.
- Any Legendary/Mythical presence must be an explicit Ouros world fact with provenance/canon status.
- Any PTU occult Feature, Move, Ability, Item, or Trainer Feature must be individually source- and engine-verified before use.
