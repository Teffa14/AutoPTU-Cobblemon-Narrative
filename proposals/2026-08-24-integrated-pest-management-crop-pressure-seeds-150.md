## 30 candidates

1. The Orchard That Looks Worse — visible fruit damage rises, but measured harvest loss does not. The mystery is whether monitoring changed rather than pressure.
2. One Greedent, Ten Stories — the same individual is repeatedly photographed near berry rows and becomes locally famous before anyone proves it caused the losses.
3. The Missing Wild Fruit Year — orchard pressure rises after a poor wild-fruit season outside the farm boundary.
4. The Diglett Came From the Hill — a farm blames burrowing Pokémon until investigation shows a new disturbance displaced them from another site.
5. Three Rows, Three Causes — similar-looking crop damage comes from wildlife, irrigation failure and a harvest-record error.
6. The Beneficial Edge — a field margin scheduled for clearing turns out to support organisms associated with lower pressure elsewhere in the plot.
7. The Broken Screen Week — a greenhouse exclusion failure allows several species inside; only one is linked to actual propagation damage.
8. The Threshold Was Written for Another Crop Stage — a rule copied from early growth triggers unnecessary intervention during harvest.
9. The Worker Who Notices First — a long-term orchard worker detects a change before formal scouting does; later review checks whether the pattern is real.
10. The Camera Trap Faces the Wrong Row — a no-detection period reflects poor coverage rather than absence.
11. Applin in the Storage Apple — the fruit is visibly occupied, but its condition does not match the simple spoilage story everyone expected.
12. The Field With More Bugs and Less Damage — organism abundance rises while crop pressure falls, forcing the team to stop treating counts as outcome.
13. The Spray Ledger — old intervention records reveal that the farm repeatedly treated symptoms before it began recording cause hypotheses.
14. The Clean Orchard Myth — a historically prestigious orchard marketed itself as “pest free” even though archived scouting shows routine low-level pressure.
15. The Harvest Helper Misclassified — a recurring Pokémon behavior removes post-harvest roots and is useful in that phase despite appearing destructive at another time.
16. The Diversion Plot — planting or leaving an alternative resource reduces pressure on the managed crop, but monitoring must determine whether pressure moved elsewhere.
17. The Trap That Became an Attractant — a monitoring device changes local behavior and contaminates its own dataset.
18. The Wrong Predator — growers ask to protect a presumed biological-control species before anyone demonstrates it interacts with the target organism.
19. The Introduced Solution Proposal — a committee proposes bringing in a species from another region; Biosecurity blocks execution until risk and agency questions are resolved.
20. The Wild Pokémon Refuses the Job — an institution wants a known individual to patrol fields, but recurring presence never becomes a work assignment without agency and participation.
21. The Chemical Proposal Never Leaves Paper — review finds that physical exclusion and sanitation are sufficient, so the more hazardous intervention is never authorized.
22. The Rain After Treatment — pressure changes after an intervention and a major weather event, leaving causal attribution open.
23. The Orchard Across the Road — two neighboring sites share a species but only one has meaningful crop pressure because management and habitat differ.
24. The Non-Target Year — the primary target falls while another ecological change appears; follow-up determines whether the intervention contributed.
25. The Resistant Population Rumor — an intervention seems less effective, but alternate explanations include application failure, immigration and crop-stage mismatch.
26. The Festival Harvest Panic — a public event magnifies reports of crop loss even though standardized scouting remains within the established threshold.
27. The Former Partner at the Field Edge — a released persistent Pokémon is observed near a managed site; the system records identity evidence without assigning intent or management responsibility.
28. The Threshold That Goes Up — better records show the site tolerates more low-level feeding than operators assumed, reducing unnecessary intervention.
29. The Threshold That Goes Down — a sensitive research crop justifies action at lower pressure than the neighboring commercial field.
30. Nothing Happened This Season — scouting, records and harvest all remain ordinary. The quiet year becomes the baseline that makes a later change interpretable.

## Long arcs

### Five Seasons at Cedar Orchard

Year one: fruit loss triggers reactive removal attempts.

Year two: standardized scouting reveals that visible Pokémon counts and actual damage do not track cleanly.

Year three: a wild-resource failure outside the orchard produces a temporary surge in foraging pressure.

Year four: operators change field-edge management and revise the action threshold.

Year five: a similar-looking problem appears, but records prevent the institution from assuming the old cause. The payoff is institutional learning rather than a stronger boss.

### The Spray Ledger

A decades-old farm archive contains intervention logs but poor causal records. Players, researchers and operators reconstruct which treatments coincided with crop changes, non-target observations, worker reports and weather. Some past actions were reasonable with the evidence available at the time. Others were simply routine. The arc can end with better recordkeeping rather than assigning a villain.

### The Beneficial Edge Years

A narrow strip beside several fields begins as “unused ground.” Over years it becomes relevant to pollination, wildlife movement, crop-pressure observations and erosion control. Different operators want different outcomes. The story tracks how a small landscape feature acquires value through repeated evidence, not through a magic ecosystem bonus.

## Encounter contracts

### Orchard Threshold Survey

FULL: scouts move through several orchard lanes while wild Pokémon cross or withdraw. Required families: complete movement; AI tactical policy; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback. Add terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions only if an exact environmental mechanic is authored and verified.

REDUCED: scouting and wildlife movement resolve outside AutoPTU. A static confrontation occurs only if one remains. Battle result does not decide the survey conclusion.

### Greenhouse Exclusion Breach

FULL: staff secure compartments and samples while multiple organisms move toward exits or shelter. Required: complete movement, AI tactical policy, adapter/playback. Any broken-glass, chemical or environmental hazard also requires the environmental family and exact rules.

REDUCED: staff close unaffected compartments first. AutoPTU receives one dry static compartment with actual combatants and no invented crop/glass/toxin mechanics.

### Beneficial Edge Conflict

FULL: the objective is to preserve a monitoring point and allow non-hostile actors to withdraw while a separate confrontation is resolved. Required: complete movement, AI tactical policy, adapter/playback.

REDUCED: ecological actors withdraw in world state. If combat remains, it occurs in a static arena. The ecological relation stays unresolved until later monitoring.

### Threshold Review Meeting

No battle is required. The correct result can be `NO_ACTION_MONITOR_ONLY`, a revised scouting plan, or a narrower hypothesis.

## Cross-layer hooks

Food/Agriculture supplies crop stage and resource objective. Interspecies Ecology supplies ecological relation hypotheses. Flora and Soil supply vegetation/land condition. Biosecurity controls introduced biological-control proposals. Toxicology and environmental layers control hazardous-agent consequences. Food Safety receives only downstream post-harvest evidence. Pokémon Agency and Working Pokémon control any individual Pokémon participation.

## Non-inferences

These seeds never establish that Bug-types are pests, that a wild Pokémon seen near a field caused damage, that capture/KO improves harvest, that biological control is harmless, that chemicals create PTU Poisoned, or that loaded Minecraft entities measure infestation.
