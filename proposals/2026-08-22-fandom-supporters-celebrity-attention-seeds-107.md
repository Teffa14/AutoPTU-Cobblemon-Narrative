# Pass 107 Proposals — Fandom, Supporters, Celebrity & Public Attention

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Promotion requires canon/mechanics review.

1. The Empty Supporter Section — a historically loud section is unexpectedly half-empty for one match. Possible causes include transport disruption, ticketing error, boycott, competing event or simple attention drift.

2. The Fan Club That Outlived the Champion — a club still meets years after its original subject retired and now functions partly as an archive.

3. Two Audiences, One Gym Leader — local students admire a Leader as a teacher while regional fans follow only battle results. The two groups react differently to the same decision.

4. The Wrong Signature — a famous autograph on an old poster may be genuine, copied, misremembered or added later. Archives and material provenance matter.

5. The Unofficial Stat Book — supporter volunteers maintain excellent public battle statistics, but several assumptions are undocumented.

6. The Pokémon With Its Own Supporters — a persistent partner Pokémon becomes famous independently of its Trainer after repeated public appearances.

7. Former Partner, New Banner — supporters continue using imagery of a Pokémon after it has been released or transferred. Public nostalgia does not create command authority.

8. The Watch Party District — a neighborhood becomes known for public viewing nights even though the main venue is elsewhere.

9. The Critic Who Never Misses a Match — a regular critic has attended for years and maintains a detailed archive. Opposition does not make them an antagonist.

10. Three Posters, Three Personas — official, sponsor-produced and fan-made posters present the same public figure differently.

11. The Missed Appearance — a public figure cancels without public explanation. Rumors proliferate, but the system does not infer illness, scandal or hostility.

12. The Supporter Train — rail schedules temporarily add capacity for a major final. Transport history becomes part of supporter culture.

13. The Quiet Clubhouse — membership has declined, but the remaining members maintain decades of physical records.

14. The Banner From the Old Arena — a supporter banner survives a venue demolition and later becomes a museum loan.

15. Local Hero, Unknown Elsewhere — a rescue worker is famous in one valley and almost anonymous in the capital.

16. The Broadcast Made Them Famous — an ordinary research assistant becomes unexpectedly recognizable after one widely shared field report.

17. The Wrong Pokémon on the Merchandise — an old commercial product associates the public figure with a Pokémon that was never actually their partner.

18. The Rival Supporters Share a Shelter — two supporter groups cooperate during a weather emergency while continuing to support different competitors afterward.

19. The Fan Song Became Tradition — an unofficial song outlives the original group that wrote it and becomes part of pre-event ritual.

20. The Archive Correction — decades later, supporters correct a famous match date without changing the cultural importance of the story.

21. The Public Figure Who Stops Competing — attention shifts toward coaching, civic work or performance rather than disappearing instantly.

22. The Sponsor Assumes the Crowd — a sponsor expects a supporter group to endorse a campaign because they support the same Trainer. They do not.

23. The Photograph Queue Problem — a popular public appearance creates crowding in a plaza and blocks a route used by residents and wild Pokémon.

24. The Unpopular Winner — a competitor wins formally while the audience remains divided. Official result and reception remain separate.

25. The Loved Loser — repeated losses do not prevent a competitor from gaining supporters because their style, persistence or local identity resonates.

26. The Supporter Who Became Staff — a long-term fan later takes a legitimate job at the institution. Employment authority derives from the role, not fandom history.

27. The Club Splits Peacefully — one supporter organization becomes two because members prefer different activities or public figures. No feud is required.

28. The Fan-Made Map — supporters publish a guide to away venues and transport. It is useful but slowly becomes outdated.

29. The Old Rivalry Without the Rivals — supporters continue a playful tradition after the original competing Trainers have reconciled or retired.

30. The Attention Spike That Fades — one spectacular incident creates a month of heavy attention, then most casual followers move on while a small persistent group remains.

## Long arcs

### A. Five Seasons in the North Stand

Year 1: a small supporter section forms around a newly rising competitor.
Year 2: travel traditions and handmade banners appear.
Year 3: media attention grows faster than the actual organized group.
Year 4: the competitor changes role or venue, splitting expectations.
Year 5: the supporter culture survives in a different form, proving that fandom can become a community history rather than a simple fame meter.

### B. The Public Pokémon Years

A Pokémon becomes recognizable through repeated battles or public-service appearances. Over several years, fan artifacts, false stories, photographs, retirement rumors and changing custody test whether Ouros can preserve individual Pokémon agency while public attention grows around it.

### C. The Archive of Applause

A supporter archive begins as scrapbooks and match programs, later adds recordings, interviews, corrected statistics and objects from demolished venues. Decades of public memory eventually make it useful to historians investigating how a competitive institution changed.
