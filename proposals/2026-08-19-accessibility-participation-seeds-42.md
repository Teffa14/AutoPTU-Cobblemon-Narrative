# Accessibility & Participation Seeds — Pass 42

Status: NON-CANON Ouros candidates. Original structures derived from research themes. No external plot, character or dialogue is copied.

## Seed 1 — The Lift That Fails Before Finals

A major battle venue has a functioning step-free route during most of the season, but its lift fails the morning of a public qualifier. The important problem is event access and operations, not a character's medical state.

Possible consequences:
- temporary venue reroute;
- delayed match schedule;
- maintenance investigation;
- public communication correction;
- later capital project.

No battle rule changes automatically.

## Seed 2 — Symbols on the Survey Map

A conservation survey uses color-coded markers that several participants cannot distinguish reliably. The research question is still valid; the presentation is the barrier.

The team redesigns the map with shapes, labels and high-contrast markers. The scientific task remains unchanged.

## Seed 3 — Quiet Hour at the Commons

A busy settlement Commons introduces a recurring quiet period with reduced announcements, fewer performances and a calmer layout. Different residents use it for different reasons.

This can alter NPC schedules and social opportunities without implying anyone has a diagnosis.

## Seed 4 — The Route List

An expedition map is beautiful but difficult to navigate for some players. A local cartographer publishes a structured text/list version of landmarks, connections, services and closures.

The list becomes useful to everyone during a fog event when the visual map is harder to read.

## Seed 5 — Caption Relay Failure

A public event relies on a caption display for announcements. The display fails while the audio system continues.

The incident becomes a staffing/infrastructure problem. Nobody is required to disclose why captions matter to them.

## Seed 6 — The Alternate Entrance Nobody Updates

A museum has a step-free entrance, but its public map still points visitors toward an old route that was closed during renovation.

The physical access exists. The information layer failed.

This can connect archives, communications and public works without inventing malicious intent.

## Seed 7 — Field Practicum, Equivalent Evidence

An academy field course previously required every student to climb an observation tower. Faculty revises the assessment after realizing the actual learning goal is identifying migration evidence, not climbing.

Students may now use tower observation, remote optics, a lower observation station or verified sensor data.

PTU Skills remain whatever the character sheet says.

## Seed 8 — The Wheelchair Gym Leader

A proposed Gym Leader uses a mobility device and is known primarily for a distinctive tactical philosophy, mentorship style and civic role.

Their device is part of ordinary life. It does not lower Speed, change initiative or create a mandatory medical subplot.

## Seed 9 — Ferry Boarding Window

A ferry usually supports assisted boarding, but high tide temporarily makes one boarding mechanism unavailable. The operator publishes a narrower safe boarding window until repairs are complete.

This creates travel-state decisions without creating a random encounter.

## Seed 10 — The Colorblind Warehouse Puzzle

A warehouse safety puzzle originally uses three colored valve indicators. The revised version adds distinct shapes and labels while keeping the same logical sequence.

The puzzle remains a puzzle.

## Seed 11 — Voice-Free Raid Coordination

A multiplayer group prepares structured pings for retreat, focus target, regroup and hold position. Voice chat remains optional.

These signals do not alter AutoPTU actions. They help players communicate intent around the battle interface.

## Seed 12 — The Rest Bench Project

A long historic route gains a small network of rest points after repeated community feedback. The project also helps older travelers, families, injured workers, tourists and anyone carrying heavy equipment.

The change is ordinary infrastructure, not a quest reward buff.

## Seed 13 — A Guide Who Signs

A regional guide uses a signed language and works with visitors through signing, writing, maps and an interpreter when needed.

The guide's role is exploration expertise. Their communication method is not a mystery to solve.

Ouros should only define a fictional signed language if canon authors deliberately build one; otherwise use high-level representation without copying a real-world language incorrectly.

## Seed 14 — The Too-Loud Festival

A popular festival creates a temporary low-stimulation area away from the main stage. The festival remains lively elsewhere.

Players can meet NPCs there, but the space should not become a special quest zone merely because it exists.

## Seed 15 — Archive Reader Tools

An archive installs magnification, adjustable lighting and alternate-format finding aids after researchers report repeated difficulty reading fragile originals.

The archival source remains unchanged; the interface improves.

## Seed 16 — The Broken Ramp Rumor

A rumor says a clinic has become inaccessible. The actual issue is narrower: one route is closed, while a temporary entrance remains open.

Correct information matters more than dramatic framing.

## Seed 17 — No Timed Dialogue

A negotiation sequence was planned with rapidly disappearing dialogue choices. The institution redesigns it so players can read options at their own pace while the fictional urgency is represented through world clocks outside the menu.

The story remains urgent. Reading speed is not turned into a character stat.

## Seed 18 — Tournament Accommodation Record

A formal competition publishes an accommodation process before registration. An approved presentation or input change is recorded separately from the battle contract.

No hidden stat adjustment occurs.

## Seed 19 — The Accessible Ranger Post

A remote Ranger/outpost-equivalent facility is rebuilt with clearer signage, lower service counters, alternate communication methods and improved route information after a recovery project.

The facility becomes more useful to many visitors, not only one named character.

## Seed 20 — Tactile Ruin Model

A museum creates a tactile model of a ruin so visitors can study its spatial structure without entering the fragile original site.

Researchers later discover that the model also helps compare two reconstruction hypotheses.

The model does not replace physical fieldwork when the question actually requires field evidence.

## Seed 21 — Subtitles Reveal the Pattern

A series of environmental sound captions reveals that a machine's rhythm changes before each failure. The accessibility channel becomes useful evidence for all players.

The world event must emit a semantic sound change first; captions do not invent evidence.

## Seed 22 — Co-Pilot Expedition

Two players voluntarily divide controls or responsibilities during an exploration sequence. One handles navigation input; the other makes route and research decisions.

The support arrangement is explicit and reversible. It does not transfer character ownership.

## Seed 23 — A School's Old Assumption

An academy's legacy assessment assumes every student can complete the same physical route. A review separates the actual competency from the historical presentation method.

The revision creates debate about standards without making either side a caricature.

## Seed 24 — The Accessible Home Workshop

A craftsperson redesigns their workshop after their own needs change over time. The new layout later improves apprentice training, tool organization and evacuation safety.

No cure arc is required.

## Seed 25 — Navigation by More Than Color

A mine uses colored route lamps. After smoke and lighting failures make the colors unreliable, crews add symbols, numbering and directional shapes.

Accessibility and emergency robustness reinforce each other.

## Seed 26 — Public Works Feedback Loop

A town collects repeated access complaints about one plaza. Rather than generating a single heroic quest, the issue becomes a multi-stage civic project: observation, temporary fixes, consultation, construction, testing and follow-up.

## Seed 27 — The NPC Who Declines Help

An NPC using an assistive device declines unsolicited help during a routine interaction.

The system records the refusal only if relevant to the scene. It does not label the NPC rude, proud or secretly struggling.

## Seed 28 — The NPC Who Requests Help

Another NPC explicitly asks for assistance moving equipment through a difficult entrance.

Accepting or declining the request can affect the immediate task, but the character remains more than the request.

## Seed 29 — Access Information as Travel Planning

Before an expedition, players can inspect route gradients, ferry boarding options, rest points, weather exposure and current service outages.

This information supports planning without adding new PTU movement values.

## Seed 30 — The Old Caption Archive

A local broadcaster preserved caption transcripts of decades of public events. Researchers later use those transcripts to reconstruct announcements whose audio recordings degraded.

Accessibility infrastructure becomes historical provenance.

# Longer arc A — The Open Circuit

A regional competition circuit gradually audits its venues over several seasons. Problems differ by town: one venue has poor signage, another lacks a reliable alternate entrance, another has inaccessible registration UI, and another has excellent facilities but outdated public information.

Players may participate as competitors, staff, civic representatives, journalists or researchers. The arc ends with a more resilient circuit, not a single villain.

# Longer arc B — The Route for Everyone

A famous mountain route has accumulated informal workarounds for decades. Different groups use it: hikers, couriers, researchers, workers, families, Trainers and local Pokémon.

A public-works project considers rest points, alternate grades, transport assistance, habitat protection and emergency access. Every improvement creates tradeoffs with history, ecology, cost and maintenance.

The arc connects travel, conservation, public works, finance and accessibility without defining one universal 'correct' route.

# Longer arc C — Signals Across Ouros

Over time, players help standardize how important world information is represented through audio, captions, symbols, text logs, map markers and public notices.

The project begins after unrelated incidents reveal that many systems rely on one fragile communication channel. Later crises show the value of redundancy.

The endpoint is an interoperability standard used by institutions, not a magical device.
