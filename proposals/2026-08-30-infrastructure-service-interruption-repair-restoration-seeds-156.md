# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Infrastructure, Interruption, Repair & Restoration — Pass 156

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. No named site, institution, route, NPC, rule or historical event in this file is established canon.

These seeds exercise the continuity grammar in `design/infrastructure-service-interruption-repair-restoration-continuity-extension.md` while keeping technical and tactical authority bounded.

## The City That Keeps Running Around the Broken Line

A buried service line failed months ago. Instead of one cinematic blackout, the district accumulated workarounds: a clinic uses an alternate feed, market refrigeration runs from rented generation, one tram platform closes early, and several workshops coordinate around reduced service hours.

The adventure begins after the emergency is already old.

Players can discover:

- which dependencies were actually affected;
- which businesses improvised successfully;
- which temporary systems became socially important;
- why permanent work remains incomplete;
- whether the original failure cause is even known.

Possible payoff: restoration removes the original problem but creates a new civic question because the temporary route changed foot traffic and local commerce.

No corruption explanation is implied.

## Five Times They Said “The Power Was Back”

Five records use nearly identical language:

1. a feeder was energized for testing;
2. emergency generation reached a clinic;
3. the central station resumed reduced operation;
4. most residential service returned;
5. the final temporary generator was removed.

The records are compatible once restoration scope is reconstructed.

Mystery lesson: ordinary language compresses staged restoration into a false contradiction.

## The Pump That Works but the District Still Floods

A repaired pumping station passes its local operational check, yet low streets still flood during heavy rain.

Possible explanations remain open until evidence exists:

- another dependency is constrained;
- drainage routes changed;
- capacity assumptions are stale;
- the repaired pump is operating below historical design conditions;
- the observed flooding has a separate cause.

The repair is a real fact. It does not prove the wider system is restored.

## The Bridge That Was Repaired Twice

Public memory says a mountain bridge “was repaired twice.” Records later show the first intervention stabilized one damaged section and reopened pedestrian access. The second replaced the load-bearing span years later.

A current dispute arises because old signs, maps and oral histories use different reopening dates.

No source needs to be fraudulent.

## The Map Still Shows a Line That Was Abandoned

A maintenance map, tourism map and old engineering plan disagree about a service corridor crossing a hillside.

Investigation can establish staged decommissioning:

- one branch stopped carrying service;
- another remained active for monitoring;
- the surface access road continued to be maintained;
- the old label survived on public maps.

The physical corridor becomes an exploration site whose history is more interesting than a hidden villain base.

## The Temporary Generator Everyone Forgot Was Temporary

A generator installed after an emergency remains in place long after the incident. Residents treat its noise and fuel deliveries as normal. A new maintenance problem forces the community to ask why permanent restoration never replaced it.

Potential causes include changed priorities, missing components, an abandoned redesign, unclear ownership, a dependency nobody documented correctly or a deliberate local preference. Do not choose the cause until authored evidence supports it.

## The Old Substation Became a Field Station

A decommissioned electrical site is repurposed as a field-research station because the building, access road and surviving communications equipment remain useful.

Old control-room labels and conduits still exist. Visitors repeatedly assume the station can still energize the former line.

Hard boundary:

`PHYSICAL_EQUIPMENT_REMAINS != SERVICE_CAPABILITY_REMAINS`

The site supports environmental storytelling without requiring a supernatural secret.

## The Replacement Part From the Wrong Generation

A crew receives a component that appears compatible but belongs to a later revision of the system. Nobody needs to be incompetent: records changed after an earlier rebuild, labels were retained, and the procurement request referenced an old drawing.

The adventure can involve document reconciliation, travel to locate a compatible component, or redesign authorization.

A tactical encounter is optional.

## The Utility Crew That Knows the Site Better Than the Map

A veteran maintenance crew can navigate a facility using institutional memory that never reached current drawings. A junior engineer has accurate recent records for modified areas the veterans rarely visit.

Neither source is universally superior.

The player gains the best reconstruction by combining both knowledge slices.

## The Station With One Working Platform

A landslide or structural incident closes most of a small regional station. One safe platform remains available under reduced operations.

The station continues to matter socially because passengers adapt around a narrow service window. A later repair can restore capacity in phases.

This seed should link to the existing rail/transit authority rather than invent a duplicate timetable system.

## The Town That Learned to Live Around a Missing Bridge

A bridge closure turns a ten-minute local connection into a long detour. Months later, the detour has created new businesses, altered school/work travel and changed which settlements interact most often.

When replacement finally becomes possible, restoration has consequences because the world did not freeze during the outage.

The bridge repair is not automatically the morally correct endpoint for every resident.

## The Cable Marked Active in Three Records

Three records all say “active” but refer to different states: electrically energized, administratively retained, and physically maintained.

The mystery is terminology and scope.

## The Repair That Changed the Route

A temporary path around a collapsed structure proves safer for pedestrian travel than the original path. A permanent rebuild proposal would restore freight access but remove the temporary pedestrian connection.

This can create faction and civic negotiation hooks without turning infrastructure into a binary quest marker.

## The Quiet Restoration

No boss, sabotage or catastrophe occurs. A long-running repair reaches acceptance, service is restored in stages, temporary equipment is removed, and the Chronicle records the change.

The payoff comes from seeing ordinary life normalize: lights return, shop hours change, a route reopens, technicians leave, and NPC schedules update.

This seed is a deliberate test that the system can support meaningful non-combat world evolution.

## Recurring NPC candidates

All are NON-CANON roles, not named canon characters.

A veteran line inspector remembers three obsolete layouts and openly marks where memory is uncertain.

A junior engineer trusts current drawings but learns that historical modifications were incompletely migrated.

A service dispatcher has excellent outage reports but little direct field knowledge.

A caretaker remains responsible for a decommissioned facility because other equipment at the site still needs inspection.

A local shopkeeper understands the practical effect of reduced service better than its technical cause.

A craft specialist can repair one specific class of component but has no authority to decide broader service restoration.

A field technician is an excellent observer of symptoms and a poor source for organizational motive.

## Long arc — The Network Nobody Sees as a Network

Phase one establishes ordinary infrastructure through small interactions: a pump inspection, bridge closure notice, substation maintenance, temporary generator and old station map.

Phase two introduces unrelated-looking interruptions. Each has a local explanation or unresolved question. The campaign does not assume a single mastermind.

Phase three reveals real dependencies. Some events connect because one service depends on another. Others remain genuinely independent.

Phase four produces a regional stress event. Players understand enough of the network to anticipate consequences that NPCs with narrower knowledge slices may miss.

Phase five is restoration rather than a mandatory final battle. Different assets return at different times. Temporary solutions remain in some locations. One old facility is retired instead of repaired. Another is repurposed.

The arc pays off accumulated world knowledge without requiring that every outage share one cause.

## Mystery — Four Closures, One Road

A mountain road has four closure dates in public memory. Research reveals four distinct events: hazard inspection, temporary closure, partial reopening and full freight restriction.

The final answer is a timeline, not a culprit.

## Encounter contract — Substation Isolation Perimeter

Narrative premise: an already-authored incident requires technicians to reach a safe approach and perform isolation/assessment work.

Full intended version:

- dynamic electrical or environmental hazard zones;
- reaction windows around unstable equipment;
- tactical withdrawal and route denial;
- complete movement interactions if displacement near hazards matters;
- full lifecycle for delayed changes;
- AI tactical policy capable of understanding access denial rather than only legal attacks;
- adapter/playback capable of representing authoritative hazard and equipment state.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline as selected content requires;
- status lifecycle as selected content requires;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- move-specific behavior, abilities, items and Trainer Features/perks by individual audit;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY at narrative-contract level if individually audited battle content is used.

Before initiative, Ouros establishes that the relevant equipment is isolated from tactical interaction, technicians and bystanders remain outside BattleSpec, and the fight occurs on static safe-approach geometry.

Permitted tactical output:

`IMMEDIATE_SUBSTATION_APPROACH_CLEAR`

Forbidden inference:

`APPROACH_CLEAR != ASSET_ISOLATED`

`BATTLE_WON != POWER_RESTORED`

## Encounter contract — Flood Pump Access Corridor

Narrative premise: a pump facility must be reached during an authored water-management problem.

Full intended version needs changing water depth, dynamic terrain/hazards, forced movement if current matters, reaction windows, lifecycle and tactical AI that understands holding/clearing access.

Full status: BLOCKED, especially on terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions and AI tactical policy.

Reduced version: READY.

Ouros freezes water level and environmental condition for the tactical slice. The pump, controls, technicians and repair objective remain outside BattleSpec. Static geometry is used.

Permitted output:

`IMMEDIATE_PUMP_ACCESS_CORRIDOR_CLEAR`

After the battle, Ouros separately resolves assessment and repair from authored facts.

`CORRIDOR_CLEAR != PUMP_WORKING`

## Encounter contract — Bridge Inspection Chokepoint

Narrative premise: inspectors need access to a safe staging side of a closed bridge.

Full intended version would need structural failure/collapse semantics, destructible or changing terrain, environmental forced movement, hazards/reactions and authoritative playback.

Those are not globally verified.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

The unsafe span is non-traversable and non-targetable scenery. The encounter occurs only on verified static staging geometry. Inspectors remain outside BattleSpec.

Permitted output:

`IMMEDIATE_INSPECTION_STAGING_AREA_CLEAR`

`STAGING_AREA_CLEAR != BRIDGE_SAFE`

`BATTLE_WON != BRIDGE_REOPENED`

## Encounter contract — Repair Convoy Staging Perimeter

Narrative premise: repair materials and crew need an approach area cleared before a non-tactical work episode.

Full intended version would require escort semantics, vehicles or object carrying, complete movement, tactical retreat/protect policy and authoritative moving-object playback.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

Vehicles remain stationary. Crew, cargo and semantic work objectives remain outside BattleSpec. Explicit combatants fight in fixed geometry.

Permitted output:

`IMMEDIATE_REPAIR_CONVOY_APPROACH_CLEAR`

Whether the convoy departs, arrives, unloads or repairs anything is decided later by Travel/Logistics/Infrastructure world state.
