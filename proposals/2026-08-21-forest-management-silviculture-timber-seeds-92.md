# Pass 92 Proposals — Forest Management, Silviculture & Timber

Status: NON-CANON WORLD-BUILDING CANDIDATES.

These concepts are derived from the Pass 92 research but are original Ouros material. None become canon automatically.

## 30 candidate concepts

1. The Veteran Tree Left Standing — every nearby tree is removed except one deliberately retained landmark. Years later it becomes a navigation point, roost and dispute over whether the original reason for retention still applies.

2. The Road That Outlived the Harvest — a temporary extraction road remains after the project and gradually becomes a worker route, wildlife corridor and emergency access road.

3. The Missing Mark — field crews find one tree physically marked for removal even though the current plan says it belongs to a retention patch. The mark may be old, mistaken or evidence of an outdated revision.

4. The Order From the Rebuild — a town recovering from disaster creates sudden demand for timber. The forest project is only one link in a larger Public Works and Finance chain.

5. Two Good Plans — two forestry teams propose different treatments for the same stand. Both are defensible because they prioritize different objectives.

6. The Quiet Landing — a landing closes after one season but becomes a regular place where wild Pokémon are observed because the ground remains open.

7. Trevenant at Compartment Twelve — repeated Trevenant observations occur near one treatment zone. Players must distinguish territory, disturbance, coincidence and incomplete survey coverage.

8. The Buffer on the Wrong Map — a stream buffer was correctly marked on an older course of the stream. Freshwater revisions later reveal that the current channel has shifted.

9. Timber With a Story — a batch from a documented project is later used to repair a historic building. Material Culture can preserve the whole provenance chain.

10. The Closed Skid Trail — a rehabilitated trail is reopened informally by hikers and eventually creates a new Road Ecology question.

11. The Plot That Did Not Regenerate — planting was completed correctly, but follow-up surveys show patchy survival. No villain or worker negligence is assumed.

12. The Stand That Regenerated Too Well — dense regeneration creates new management problems and changes where Pokémon are observed.

13. Windthrow After Retention — a tree intentionally retained for habitat falls years later. It becomes deadwood rather than a failed decision by default.

14. The Mill Without a Forest — a local mill remains economically important even after its historical nearby harvest area is no longer used.

15. The Forest Without a Mill — a forest-management institution loses its processing partner and must change transport, storage or project timing.

16. The Old Landing Archive — photographs reveal that a current meadow was once a log landing, explaining its unusual shape and soil history.

17. The Worker Who Knows the Shortcut — a forestry worker knows an access route missing from official maps because it was never intended to remain permanent.

18. Salvage Is Not Automatic — after a storm or wildfire, different actors disagree over whether damaged trees should be removed, retained or monitored first.

19. The Retained Snag — a standing dead tree is deliberately kept, becomes habitat, then later creates a safety review when public access expands nearby.

20. The Stream That Changed First — downstream measurements shift before visible regeneration problems appear, sending investigators back to roads and drainage rather than tree removal alone.

21. The Former Partner in the Regeneration Plot — an individually known released Pokémon is photographed in a young stand. The system records the observation without inferring motive or ownership.

22. The Contract With No Trees Named — a timber agreement specifies quantity and timing but not source. The management institution must decide which legal project, if any, can satisfy it.

23. The Forgotten Nursery — a temporary seedling nursery established for one project remains in use by local residents years after the project ends.

24. The Second Harvest Map — a planned future treatment conflicts with habitat information discovered only after the first harvest.

25. The Bridge Made From Compartment Seven — a public structure later becomes historically important because its timber provenance is unusually complete.

26. The Fire Access Road — a road created for forest work later becomes decisive during a wildfire response, validating an old maintenance decision.

27. The Road That Became the Problem — the same access route later produces recurring soil and drainage issues, forcing a closure or redesign.

28. The Forest With Three Owners in the Rumors — residents use three different names for who “owns” the forest, while actual management, land claim and operational authority belong to separate entities.

29. The Harvest That Became a Habitat Mosaic — a once-controversial treatment produces a patchwork of open and closed structure that different species use differently over time.

30. The Forest Ledger — a regional archive gradually records projects, retained trees, road closures, timber batches, regeneration surveys and corrections across decades.

## Long arc 1 — Five Cycles at Alder Reach

Year 1: a selective harvest is proposed because a nearby rebuilding program needs material.

Year 2: work proceeds in several treatment zones while one stream buffer and several veteran trees are retained.

Year 3: post-harvest monitoring finds adequate regeneration in most areas but recurring disturbance near one landing.

Year 4: a storm drops one retained tree and damages a closed access road. The fallen tree enters the Decomposition layer while the road becomes an emergency-access debate.

Year 5: a second management plan uses five years of evidence. The correct outcome may be more harvest, less harvest, a different road layout, monitoring only or a mixed plan.

No single year determines whether the first project was “good” or “bad.”

## Long arc 2 — The Timber Trail

A single documented timber batch is followed from forest inventory to harvest, landing, transport, workshop and construction project.

Years later the building is repaired, and some original wood is reused elsewhere.

This arc tests whether Ouros can maintain material identity and provenance without simulating every log as an entity.

Possible linked systems:

- Forest Management;
- Workplaces;
- Transport;
- Finance;
- Material Culture;
- Architecture;
- Public Memory.

## Long arc 3 — The Forest Road Years

A temporary access road is built for one project.

It is then successively:

- closed;
- informally reused;
- mapped by players;
- adopted by wildlife;
- damaged by heavy rain;
- partially rehabilitated;
- reopened during an emergency;
- debated as a permanent public trail.

The road itself becomes a persistent historical object linking Road Ecology, Soil, Freshwater, Crisis and Cartography.

## Encounter contract — Timber Landing Interruption

Full version:

Wild Pokémon move through an active landing while workers, equipment and stacked timber create a changing tactical space. Success may mean clearing a route, allowing withdrawal or protecting an evacuation rather than KO.

Required families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- action economy/initiative;
- full lifecycle where relevant;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Workers evacuate and equipment shuts down before combat. The server freezes one safe static layout. AutoPTU resolves a standard battle using only actual combatants. Work delay, wildlife observations and timber custody are updated afterward.

## Encounter contract — Riparian Buffer Survey

Full version:

A survey team needs to reach several locations while wildlife and changing stream-edge conditions affect safe routes.

Dependencies:

- complete movement;
- environment family;
- objective-aware tactical AI;
- adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Survey and stream-state work occur in overworld. Any battle uses one fixed shoreline/forest arena and applies no water-quality or terrain penalties from narrative state.

## Encounter contract — Regeneration Plot Night Watch

Full version:

Several wild actors enter and leave a regeneration area while players try to observe, protect or redirect rather than defeat them.

Dependencies:

- complete movement;
- AI tactical policy;
- environment family if supported plot zones matter;
- adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Observation/camera events happen outside combat. If a confrontation occurs, only the involved Pokémon enter a standard encounter. Regeneration state changes only from validated observations and project data.

## Mechanical non-inferences

These proposals do not establish:

- falling-tree damage;
- logging-machine hazards;
- automatic Rough Terrain from logs or stumps;
- forest-wide Trevenant control;
- Grass-type work bonuses;
- Fire-type logging utility;
- Naturewalk immunity to forestry hazards;
- Groundshaper road-building rights;
- Cut-like Moves as legal timber extraction;
- spawn bonuses from restoration;
- ecological penalties merely because a harvest happened.
