# Food-Service Safety Assessment, Correction & Reopening Seeds — Pass 131

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-29

These concepts use the continuity architecture from Pass 131. None establishes food law, inspection institutions, disease mechanics, professional titles or regional canon.

## Design target

Food-service safety stories should create useful consequences even when no one becomes ill and no deliberate wrongdoing occurs.

The strongest situations come from ordinary operations under incomplete information:

- a service period differs from the one remembered later;
- a repair changes one station but not another;
- two public notices describe different scopes;
- a product problem and a venue problem overlap without sharing one cause;
- a venue resumes limited service before every related investigation closes;
- public reputation lags behind operational recovery.

## Seed — The Counter Stayed Open

A familiar market stall has its hot-food board covered, but customers can still buy sealed goods from the front counter.

People describe the stall as both “closed” and “open.” Both descriptions are locally reasonable.

Investigation paths:

- compare service-scope notices;
- inspect dated menu boards;
- ask adjacent vendors which part of the operation changed;
- trace the equipment fault to Maintenance;
- determine whether any batch action exists separately.

Possible outcome:

The food-preparation station remains paused while packaged retail continues. The stall later keeps the split-counter design permanently because customers prefer it.

## Seed — The Refrigerator Was Repaired Yesterday

A kitchen cooling unit has a fresh maintenance tag. A complaint refers to food served two days earlier.

The obvious temptation is to treat the repair as proof of the complaint's cause.

Useful evidence:

- fault-detection time;
- repair start/completion;
- actual service timeline;
- which ingredients used that unit;
- Cold Chain references, if any;
- batch provenance;
- Community Health evidence if a health signal exists.

Possible conclusions:

- the fault predates service and matters;
- the fault began later and is unrelated;
- affected ingredients were moved before service;
- the complaint concerns another menu item;
- evidence remains insufficient.

## Seed — Two Kitchens, One Address

A large community hall contains a public café kitchen and a separate event-preparation kitchen behind the same street address.

A notice says “kitchen assessment underway.” Rumor treats the entire building as affected.

The player can reconstruct which room, service period and equipment were actually in scope.

Long-term consequence:

The building adopts clearer internal location names, and those labels later help during an unrelated evacuation or maintenance event.

## Seed — The Menu Changed Before the Notice

Regular customers insist a certain dish disappeared a day before any official operational notice.

Possible explanations:

- ingredient shortage;
- voluntary precaution;
- chef choice;
- supplier delay;
- routine seasonal menu change;
- a separate assessment already underway but not yet public.

The puzzle rewards chronology instead of assuming concealment.

## Seed — The Pokémon Everyone Blamed

A wild or community-known Pokémon was repeatedly seen behind a restaurant before a safety concern became public.

Residents connect the two events immediately.

Evidence can show:

- the Pokémon was outside the food-storage area;
- the observed condition predated its appearances;
- it used the alley because waste collection times changed;
- the animal was present but causal evidence remains absent;
- a separate waste-management problem attracted it.

The conclusion must stay individual and local. No species-wide “pest” classification is created.

## Seed — The Sink Works, the Kitchen Does Not

Maintenance completes a sink repair. The public assumes reopening should be immediate.

The food-service episode still has another unresolved scope:

- another station not yet assessed;
- follow-up evidence pending;
- a separate water-quality handoff;
- limited staffing;
- an operational decision not yet made.

This seed teaches `REPAIR_COMPLETE` and `SERVICE_RESUMED` as different facts without making anyone incompetent.

## Seed — The Kitchen Reopened, the Clinic Did Not Close Its File

A venue resumes service after its operational correction is verified. Community Health continues investigating a cluster because the broader cause remains unresolved.

Public discussion treats the two institutions as contradicting each other.

The story can resolve by explaining scope:

- venue correction addressed a verified operational condition;
- the cluster investigation asks a wider causal question;
- reopening does not prove the venue caused or did not cause past illness;
- cluster closure does not control restaurant operations unless canon says otherwise.

## Seed — The Same Batch Went Somewhere Else

A batch served at one venue is placed under trace by Batch Traceability. The same batch reached several other sites.

At the first venue, an equipment fault also occurred.

The player must avoid merging two evidence trees prematurely.

Potential resolution:

The batch issue explains one subset of records while the equipment fault had no effect on food. Or the opposite. Or both matter independently.

## Seed — The Temporary Serving Window

During corrective work, a café serves through a side window using only items that do not depend on the affected preparation room.

Customers like the faster service. After full reopening, the window stays.

Months later:

- a street vendor relationship forms nearby;
- Pokémon begin waiting in the shaded side area;
- the old main queue becomes shorter;
- the venue's public identity changes because of a temporary workaround.

## Seed — The Complaint Arrived After Closing Time

A customer report is logged at 22:40, but the meal was purchased at lunch.

An operations log says the relevant station was already closed by 22:40, leading someone to dismiss the report incorrectly.

The mystery turns on distinguishing:

- report time;
- purchase time;
- preparation time;
- consumption time;
- symptom/observation time if health records legitimately expose it.

No deception is required.

## Seed — The Owner Fixed the Wrong Problem First

A visible broken tile receives immediate attention because everyone can see it. The assessment later identifies a different operational condition as more relevant.

The first repair remains worthwhile; it simply addressed another issue.

This supports an NPC who acts responsibly with incomplete information rather than a caricatured negligent owner.

## Seed — The Regulars Came Back Slowly

The venue reopens completely. Technical operations recover faster than reputation.

World-state consequences can include:

- reduced customer volume;
- familiar regulars returning on different days;
- a neighboring stall taking permanent market share;
- staff changing menu focus;
- a community event deliberately hosted there months later;
- rumors persisting after the formal episode closes.

No universal reputation score is required. Existing relationship/public-memory systems can consume the event.

## Seed — The Festival Kitchen Has No Yesterday

A temporary festival kitchen exists for only three days. An operational concern arises on day two.

This creates a different continuity problem from a permanent restaurant:

- temporary equipment;
- borrowed space;
- rapidly changing staff;
- short service window;
- event owner, venue owner and food-service operator may differ;
- closure may occur naturally when the event ends before the investigation finishes.

The episode can remain open in Chronicle after the physical kitchen disappears.

## Seed — The Old Café Record Becomes Useful

Years later, a new tenant uses the same kitchen footprint under a different venue identity.

An old correction record helps explain:

- why a drain was rerouted;
- why one wall has an access panel;
- why storage shelves use unusual numbering;
- why the public entrance moved.

The old establishment's incident does not transfer blame to the new operator. Physical history persists without moral inheritance.

## Mystery — Five Times the Restaurant “Reopened”

Five legitimate dates circulate in local records:

1. dining room access restored;
2. packaged-goods counter resumed;
3. one preparation station verified;
4. full menu returned;
5. formal episode closed.

NPCs use “reopened” for different milestones.

Resolution:

Build a timeline by scope. No date needs to be fake.

Reward:

- corrected Chronicle understanding;
- a historical notice becomes interpretable;
- an NPC's apparently contradictory testimony becomes credible.

## Mystery — Three Cleanings, Two Questions

Records show three cleaning events during one week. People assume repeated cleaning means repeated contamination.

Actual purposes may differ:

- routine closing clean;
- cleanup after a plumbing repair;
- targeted correction after an assessment observation.

The investigation asks which record answered which question.

The player should not infer that “more cleaning” equals “more dangerous.”

## Mystery — The Receipt From the Wrong Kitchen

A receipt identifies the venue but not the preparation room. On that day, two internal kitchens served different menus through the same front counter.

Evidence routes:

- menu item;
- service time;
- prep logs;
- room assignment;
- ingredient batch references;
- worker recollection.

The solution is spatial provenance.

## Mystery — The Sample Arrived After the Correction

A sample was collected before corrective work but its result became available after reopening.

The later timestamp causes public confusion.

The system must preserve:

- sample collection time;
- custody/result time;
- what physical state existed at collection;
- what correction changed afterward;
- which owner can interpret the result.

The new result may reopen one question without erasing the valid earlier decision based on then-available evidence.

## Exploration — The Kitchen Behind the New Wall

A restaurant renovated years ago after an operational episode. The current kitchen uses a different layout.

The player investigates a harmless historical puzzle:

Why does a tiled service hatch open into a storage room?

Evidence:

- old floor plans;
- photographs;
- maintenance tags;
- archived menus;
- wall anchors;
- route markings;
- former staff testimony.

Resolution:

The room once served as a temporary clean preparation area during correction. The workaround later shaped the renovation.

Implementation:

READY with current capabilities. No battle or food-safety mechanics required.

## Exploration — The Market Counter With Two Numbers

A public market stall displays a current stall number, but old documents use another.

The apparent discrepancy comes from a post-incident reorganization that split one large counter into two smaller service scopes.

The exploration connects:

- market history;
- commercial services;
- food-service continuity;
- cartography/address history;
- public memory.

Implementation:

READY. Uses static geometry and evidence records.

## Social quest — The Owner Wants the Story Straight

A venue owner asks the player to help assemble a public timeline after reopening. The task is not public relations spin by default.

Player choices concern accuracy and privacy:

- include verified dates;
- avoid naming affected individuals;
- distinguish confirmed operational issues from rumors;
- mention unresolved questions honestly;
- link a supplier/batch issue only if provenance supports it.

Possible consequences:

- increased trust from some actors;
- criticism from those who wanted stronger certainty;
- a durable public record useful in later seasons.

## NPC archetype — The Scope Keeper

A worker or administrator who remembers exactly which counter, room or shift a record refers to.

Strength:

Prevents institutional memory from flattening everything into “the restaurant incident.”

Weakness:

May know scope perfectly while knowing little about cause.

Use:

Excellent recurring supporting NPC for markets, hospitals, League venues or public halls without making them an investigator-general.

## NPC archetype — The Repair Witness

A staff member who was present during physical corrective work and can describe what changed.

They can establish:

- chronology;
- physical layout;
- equipment replacement;
- who was present.

They cannot automatically establish:

- contamination;
- health cause;
- regulatory compliance;
- batch disposition.

## NPC archetype — The Regular Who Notices Routine

A customer with strong memory for ordinary patterns:

- which entrance staff usually use;
- when deliveries tend to arrive;
- what menu board changed;
- where a familiar Pokémon usually waits.

Their evidence is valuable but scoped to observation. Familiarity does not become omniscience.

## Faction / institution dynamic — Operator, Property Owner, Service Owner

A venue may involve three different responsible actors:

- operator runs food service;
- property owner controls building infrastructure;
- another institution owns the market/event/public facility.

All three may want safe reopening while disagreeing about sequence, cost, access or evidence responsibility.

This creates negotiation without requiring villainy.

## Faction / institution dynamic — Health Investigator and Venue Assessor

If canon eventually creates distinct institutions, their questions can overlap without sharing authority.

Community Health asks whether reports form a meaningful health pattern and what exposures are supported.

The venue assessment asks what happened operationally at the site and whether corrections are verified for its scope.

The same evidence can be relevant to both while conclusions remain separate.

## Encounter — Kitchen Service Withdrawal

Full version:

A hostile independent event reaches the venue during service. Staff/customer withdrawal occurs across several exits while combatants protect routes.

Full dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — PARTIAL;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING.

Reduced version — READY:

Ouros pauses service first. Customers, workers, Pokémon not selected as combatants, food, samples, private records and controlled equipment leave BattleSpec. AutoPTU receives static safe geometry and explicit combatants. Victory secures immediate access only. The operational episode resumes later under its own owner.

## Encounter — Receiving Dock Diversion

Full version:

A separate conflict interrupts delivery handling. Rich version would protect a narrow route, withdraw staff and represent controlled-object custody while the encounter unfolds.

Dependencies:

Same broad status as Kitchen Service Withdrawal, with complete movement PARTIAL, lifecycle PARTIAL, zones/reactions BLOCKING, tactical policy BLOCKING and adapter/playback BLOCKING.

Reduced version — READY:

Receiving stops first. Shipment custody remains in world state outside BattleSpec. Workers withdraw. Combat occurs on static dock geometry. Afterward, Food/Batch/Cold Chain owners determine whether receiving can continue and what the shipment status is.

## Encounter — Follow-Up Visit Perimeter

Full version:

An assessment team arrives after corrections but an independent threat blocks the perimeter. Rich implementation may need withdrawal, protected zones, Intercept/reactions and objective-aware AI.

Reduced version — READY:

The assessment is suspended before battle. Assessors, evidence and equipment leave the tactical scene. A conventional battle clears the perimeter. The authorized owner later decides whether the visit resumes. Tactical victory never creates a verification result.

## Long arc — A Street Learns What “Open” Means

Phase 1: ordinary life

Several food venues establish normal routines, known staff, delivery windows, Pokémon visitors and regular customers. Nothing is wrong.

Phase 2: a limited concern

One venue changes a service scope after an observation. The reason initially remains uncertain. Nearby businesses respond socially before evidence settles.

Phase 3: parallel owners

Maintenance investigates equipment. Batch Traceability checks one product. Community Health may examine a small health signal. Food-service assessment reconstructs the venue's operation. The branches can converge or separate.

Phase 4: correction

Physical changes become visible. One counter reopens before another. A side window or alternate service pattern appears.

Phase 5: recovery

Formal operational recovery occurs by scope. Customer behavior recovers on another timeline. A neighboring business may retain customers gained during the interruption.

Phase 6: persistence

Months later, the street still contains:

- changed queue geometry;
- a retained temporary window;
- updated room names;
- a supplier relationship that changed;
- an old public notice in an archive;
- a regular Pokémon using a new waiting place;
- staff who remember the event differently by role.

Phase 7: later callback

A later maintenance fault, festival or historical investigation reuses the old records. Prior continuity reduces ambiguity without dictating the new cause.

## Canon questions attached to these proposals

Before any seed becomes canon, authors must decide only what that story requires:

- which venue exists;
- who operates it;
- whether a formal assessment institution exists;
- what mandate applies;
- what records exist;
- which corrective action actually occurred;
- who can decide service state;
- what information can become public;
- what relationship, if any, a Pokémon has to the operation.

Everything else should remain unknown rather than generated automatically.

## Mechanical guardrail

No proposal above authorizes:

- invented contamination statuses;
- sickness from food;
- slippery/heat/steam/fire kitchen hazards;
- food spoilage timers;
- Chef inspection checks;
- species-derived sanitation or detection;
- automatic effects from Fire/Ice/Water/Poison typing;
- battle victory as verification, reopening or causal proof.

Mechanically rich versions remain future-facing. Reduced versions preserve the story now.
