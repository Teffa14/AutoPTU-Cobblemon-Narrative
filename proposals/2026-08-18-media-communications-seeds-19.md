# Ouros Media & Communications Seeds — Pass 19

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external names, plots or prose are transplanted.

## 1. The Late Correction

A regional notice reports that a mountain road is open. A later field team discovers a fresh collapse, but the correction reaches only some settlements. Different travelers now hold different versions of the same route state.

Use: travel, public memory, crisis, communication coverage.

No battle required.

## 2. Last Bulletin Before the Storm

A small settlement's public board contains the final verified transport schedule before communication lines fail. Players can use it to reconstruct who plausibly left town before the outage.

Use: investigation and timeline reconstruction.

## 3. The Reporter Who Missed the Battle

A journalist arrives after a major battle and must build the story from witnesses, official logs and the players' optional interview. Conflicting accounts create several plausible public narratives.

Hard rule: the system cannot invent player quotes.

## 4. Relay Hill

A remote relay station loses power during a period of worsening weather. Restoring it reconnects clinics, transport offices and field teams.

Battle contract: use the Relay Tower Defense contract in `design/media-communications-information-layer.md`. FULL version depends on lifecycle, hazards/zones/reactions, objective-aware AI and adapter/playback. REDUCED version uses one static legal encounter followed by overworld repair.

## 5. Courier Through the Dead Zone

A research institute needs a physical report delivered because normal channels are unavailable.

The courier is not automatically an escort unit inside battle.

FULL battle dependency: complete movement/interception, AI tactical policy, adapter/playback.

REDUCED version: the courier remains outside tactical combat while players clear a chokepoint.

## 6. Two Papers, One Festival

Two local publications cover the same festival differently. One emphasizes economic success; the other emphasizes crowding, habitat pressure and service disruption.

Both can cite true facts while framing them differently.

## 7. The Anonymous Tip

A case receives an unsigned message containing one verifiable detail and several unsupported claims.

The message creates a lead, not truth.

## 8. Radio Silence at the Reserve

A wildlife reserve normally sends regular field reports. The reports stop.

Possible causes remain open until investigated: equipment failure, staffing issue, weather, route failure, deliberate shutdown or something occurring in the reserve.

The absence of information is the hook.

## 9. Correction Never Reached the Harbor

A ferry operator follows an outdated closure notice because the latest revision failed to propagate.

This creates a service problem without requiring sabotage.

## 10. The Popular Wrong Map

A widely shared travel guide still marks an old shortcut that has become ecologically sensitive.

Players can help update the guide, redirect traffic or investigate why the route changed.

## 11. Emergency Frequency

During a large crisis, several institutions share one emergency broadcast channel. Conflicting priorities create disputes about what must be transmitted first.

This is a resource-allocation/social scenario unless an implementation later models communications capacity numerically.

## 12. Interview After Defeat

A recurring rival loses a public match. A reporter asks both competitors for comment.

The rival's response is authored from their own knowledge and personality. The player's response requires player input or may be declined.

The published story can affect public standing without changing battle results.

## 13. Community Bulletin Keeper

A small town has one NPC responsible for updating notices from transport, clinics, jobs, markets and local events.

The NPC becomes a useful information hub but can only post what they have received.

## 14. Archive of Corrections

A newspaper keeps every correction beside the original story. Years later, the correction history reveals how a public myth formed from a series of reasonable early mistakes.

Use: archaeology/public memory crossover without ancient history.

## 15. The Broadcast That Saved Nothing

An evacuation alert was correctly issued, but poor coverage meant many residents never received it.

The story focuses on infrastructure and response design rather than assigning automatic blame.

## 16. Field Photographer

A photographer documents ecological changes over multiple seasons. Images become evidence only for what is actually visible and dated.

The system must not infer invisible causes from photographs.

## 17. Rumor Desk

A local broadcaster runs a clearly labeled segment that collects community rumors and asks listeners for corroboration.

Rumors can spawn investigations while remaining tagged as unverified.

## 18. Rival News Networks

Two institutions compete for audience and access. One is fast but error-prone; the other is slow and heavily verified.

Neither must be villainous.

## 19. The Missing Dispatch

A rescue team expected updated route conditions but never received them. Players trace the failure through sender, relay, transport desk and recipient rather than assuming someone lied.

## 20. Pokémon as Messenger

A trained Pokémon may carry a physical message only if the individual creature's authoritative capabilities, training and world-state context support the task.

No species is automatically granted messenger capability by flavor.

## 21. The Overheard Broadcast

Players hear only the last half of a public report. The partial message is stored as partial knowledge rather than the complete information packet.

## 22. Broadcast Studio Evacuation

A damaged studio is still serving as a regional information hub during a crisis.

FULL encounter dependency: hazards/zones/reactions, lifecycle, AI tactical policy, adapter/playback if evacuation and hazard timing occur inside battle.

REDUCED version: evacuation is handled in overworld state, with one static encounter at a blocked exit.

## 23. The Wrong Champion Headline

An unofficial outlet posts a winner before a tournament ruling is finalized. Later correction does not fully erase the first version from public memory.

No tournament mechanics are modified.

## 24. Research Embargo

A research team has verified a discovery but temporarily restricts publication to protect a fragile site.

Players may disagree with the decision, but the system must distinguish access restriction from falsifying the discovery.

## 25. The Town Without Notifications

A settlement deliberately keeps digital/broadcast infrastructure minimal and relies on in-person boards and scheduled announcements.

This is cultural variation, not technological inferiority by default.

## 26. Relay Cooperative

Several isolated communities jointly maintain a regional communications chain. Every relay belongs to a different local group, so repairs require cooperation rather than one central corporation.

## 27. Faction Press Office

A faction publishes statements explaining its actions.

Statements are stored as faction claims. They can be sincere, selective, mistaken or propagandistic, but narrative generation cannot decide which without evidence.

## 28. Public Correction Questline

Players discover that a widely repeated story about a past event is wrong. The challenge is not merely proving the truth but getting a correction into multiple channels with different audiences.

This ties evidence, media, public memory and institutions together.

## 29. Signal Shadow

A geographic area has unreliable coverage. Residents develop local practices: scheduled check-ins, physical markers, courier stops and fallback meeting places.

The communications weakness becomes regional culture and expedition planning.

## 30. World News Digest

A player-facing digest periodically summarizes high-salience changes from regions the player plausibly follows.

It should prioritize direct consequences, corrections, active cases, travel disruptions, crisis alerts and known institutions.

It must never expose private or undiscovered state.

# Longer arc candidates

## Arc A — The Region Goes Quiet

A cascading infrastructure failure disconnects several settlements. The arc begins as a communications problem, then reveals transport and power dependencies. Players restore partial links, choose which communities receive temporary service first and watch local alternatives emerge. No single villain is required.

## Arc B — The Story Everyone Knows

A famous old incident has one dominant public version. Newly recovered records do not prove a secret conspiracy; instead they reveal that several small publication errors accumulated over years. Players can investigate, interview surviving witnesses and decide how to present the corrected account without rewriting canonical facts.

## Arc C — The Public Eye

A recurring group of reporters follows major League, rescue, ecological and faction developments over a long period. Their relationship with players changes based on access, corrections, cooperation and refusals, but they never gain omniscient knowledge. Their archive becomes a historical resource for future characters.

# Mechanical guardrail

Most seeds in this file should remain overworld/world-state content. Any battle version must use the permanent capability categories and the current readiness snapshot. No communication failure may be simulated as invented PTU Accuracy, Initiative, damage, status, Move denial, Feature interruption or other tactical modifier unless the exact rule exists in PTU/Caelo and is verified in the engine.
