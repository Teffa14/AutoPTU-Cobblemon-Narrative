# Shared Public Space, Parks & Commons Seeds — Pass 67

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. No name, place, institution, rule or event below is established Ouros lore.

These candidates are designed to plug into existing settlements and locations after canon review. They should usually reuse a known park, square, courtyard, waterfront edge, greenway or similar shared place rather than inventing a new settlement.

## The Bench Everyone Uses at Different Times

A single sheltered seating area appears in several unrelated NPC routines. An early-morning worker stops there before shift, a midday regular eats there, an evening hobby group gathers nearby and a nocturnal observation record notes Pokémon activity after everyone leaves.

The hook begins when two actors describe the bench as if it belongs to different social worlds. Both are correct because their schedules never overlap.

Useful outputs:

- recurring routine callbacks;
- low-cost actor introductions;
- a future missing-object or testimony investigation;
- evidence that a place has several clocks instead of one dominant use.

No routine creates ownership or reservation rights by itself.

## East Gate Closed, West Gate Open

One entrance to a familiar shared place becomes unavailable because of an authored maintenance, event, ecology or infrastructure state. The rest of the location remains usable through another entrance.

NPC routines adapt differently. A commuter takes a longer route. A vendor receives fewer passersby. A research observer loses their normal sight line. A visitor incorrectly assumes the whole place is closed.

The content remains small unless another system turns the changed flow into a larger problem.

The exact cause is selected from current world state, not generated merely to create inconvenience.

## The Practice Group That Arrives Before Dawn

An existing club, team or informal group has a recurring early use pattern in a shared lawn or plaza zone. Their routine becomes relevant because another observation, maintenance task or temporary restriction uses the same time window.

Possible resolutions include schedule separation, a temporary alternate zone, consultation or simply discovering that the supposed conflict was based on stale information.

This seed does not establish permits, priority rights or sport rules.

## The Path People Stopped Taking

A once-normal route through a park or square shows declining use. Nothing physically blocks it.

Players can investigate several grounded possibilities:

- a different entrance became more convenient;
- lighting or a fixture is under repair;
- a temporary event changed habit and the old pattern never fully returned;
- a recurring Pokémon presence changed how people route around the area;
- a guide or notice is stale;
- a downstream service moved.

The generator must derive the explanation from existing state or leave it unresolved. It should not invent a hidden threat just because the path is quiet.

## Market Day Leaves a Different Park

A temporary market or public event completes successfully. Event Operations closes its program, but the shared space does not instantly reset.

After teardown:

- one lawn remains worn;
- a seating layout changed;
- waste or maintenance work remains;
- regular users return at different speeds;
- wildlife may reappear according to ecology state;
- one successful temporary routing choice may become a candidate for future civic review.

The key scene happens after the event rather than during it.

## The Courtyard After Graduation

An institutionally managed courtyard has an intense one-day use, followed by an abrupt return to ordinary routines. Decorations disappear, guests leave and the resident community reoccupies the place.

A small callback can reveal what remains: a moved bench, a forgotten notice, an altered meeting spot, a new photograph in circulation or a damaged fixture referred to Maintenance.

No school, ceremony or graduation custom is established until matched to canon.

## A Quiet Corner Becomes Famous

A low-use corner of a shared destination receives unexpected attention after a publication, image, battle story or other existing Media/Public Memory event.

Tourism owns the new visitor pressure. This seed focuses on the local consequences inside the place:

- regular users shift elsewhere;
- a narrow path becomes worn;
- a quiet observation window becomes harder to maintain;
- signage or steward presence changes;
- nearby service patterns react.

The popularity driver must exist in world state. The system cannot spontaneously declare a location viral.

## The Greenway That Became a Shortcut

Players discover that a path built or maintained for one documented purpose is now regularly used as a shortcut by another cohort.

The shortcut may be harmless, beneficial or problematic depending on evidence. If it causes wear, habitat conflict or access problems, the relevant owning system receives the issue.

The interesting decision is whether the informal route should be accommodated, redirected or simply observed longer. Any permanent redesign requires Civic/Public Works authority.

## The Park Rule Nobody Remembers Changing

Several actors confidently remember different versions of an access or use rule. A current sign, old notice, guide edition, meeting record or staff statement does not line up cleanly.

The investigation reconstructs provenance:

- when each version was active;
- which zone it applied to;
- whether a temporary restriction was mistaken for a permanent rule;
- whether an old guide remained in circulation after revision.

A good ending may be clarification rather than discovering wrongdoing.

## Watering Day

A normal stewardship or maintenance routine temporarily changes how one garden or lawn zone can be used. The same predictable routine later becomes useful evidence in a different story because it establishes who was present, which gate was open and when the space was already wet or restricted.

This seed exists to make maintenance schedules part of world memory instead of invisible background simulation.

No Pokémon performs technical work unless an authoritative individual capability and setting rule supports it.

## The Return Before Teardown

Regular users come back to a plaza or park before a temporary event has fully cleared its equipment. No emergency is required. The scene is about competing expectations and incomplete handoff.

Possible outcomes:

- teardown accelerates;
- one zone reopens while another remains closed;
- regulars use an alternate route;
- a fixture or access problem is discovered only because ordinary use resumes;
- Event Operations revises its next teardown plan.

## Four Regulars, Three Time Windows

Mystery seed.

Four people give apparently conflicting accounts of an ordinary shared area. Each describes who was present, which entrance was active and what they normally see there. The statements appear mutually incompatible until the player reconstructs recurring time windows.

One statement may be outdated. Two actors may rely on the same original observation. A maintenance or event record may explain a temporary schedule shift.

Playable evidence sources:

- routine records;
- timestamps;
- photographs where provenance exists;
- access notices;
- maintenance logs;
- event setup/teardown state;
- Rumor/Testimony lineage;
- direct observation on a later day.

No combat is required. The resolution can remain probabilistic or incomplete when evidence is insufficient.

## Pondside Withdrawal

Mechanically rich encounter candidate.

Premise:

A shared route passes near a zone where wild Pokémon are acting defensively. Ordinary users have already reported enough current evidence for a temporary closure, but the immediate area still needs to be made safe for ecological review.

Full intended version:

Civilians withdraw through changing safe lanes while Trainers manage a territorial encounter. Protection and withdrawal matter more than KO. Interception or forced displacement may matter. Exact terrain, weather or hazard effects apply only if PTU/Caelo mapping and engine support exist. Opponent AI may prefer defense or retreat.

- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL

- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL

Reduced version:

Evacuate ordinary users in world state first. Close the affected zone. Run a static reviewed battle at a safe edge using only supported rules. After the authoritative result, hand the site to Ecology/Conservation and public-space state for review.

A win does not prove that the same Pokémon caused earlier incidents, does not create a permanent closure and does not establish ownership of the area.

## Plaza Access Break

Mechanically rich encounter candidate.

Premise:

One entrance to a familiar plaza is blocked by an immediate threat while another route remains available. The location matters because it is part of daily circulation rather than because it is a dungeon.

Full intended version:

The scene uses multiple exits, withdrawing noncombatants, protected fixtures and CLEAR_ROUTE/ESCAPE/PROTECT objectives. Dynamic barriers, interception or forced movement can matter only when their capability families are ready. Tactical AI must understand access and withdrawal.

## A Commons Learns Its Users

Long-form recurring arc candidate.

Visit 1 establishes baseline. The players learn several recurring users, time windows, entrances and ordinary ecological signals without a crisis.

Visit 2 introduces overlap. A new recurring activity, route change or visitor driver puts two uses into the same zone.

Visit 3 creates pressure. A restriction, temporary event or maintenance state makes the overlap visible. The players gather evidence and help with a reversible mitigation.

Visit 4 produces review. Civic, maintenance, conservation, accessibility or event systems handle whichever part they actually own. The shared-space layer records the updated ordinary-use pattern.

Visit 5 is a callback weeks later. The place is familiar but not reset. One group has moved, an entrance changed, a repaired feature remains visible, or an old quiet area has become active again.

The arc should work even if no combat ever occurs.

## Canon review questions

Before any seed is instantiated, identify:

- an existing or approved Ouros location;
- its actual responsibility/authority relationships;
- the groups that genuinely use it;
- current access and maintenance state;
- any ecological sensitivity;
- whether the location already appears in another proposal;
- which names and routines are approved;
- which temporary rules are culturally/institutionally plausible.

Nothing in this file answers those questions automatically.
