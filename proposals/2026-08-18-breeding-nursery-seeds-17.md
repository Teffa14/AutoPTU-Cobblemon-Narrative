# Breeding, Egg & Nursery Seeds — Pass 17

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros concepts derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot or character is copied.

## Seed 01 — The First-Partner Nursery

A regional nursery raises young Pokémon intended for first-time Trainers.

The interesting conflict is institutional rather than villainous. Demand has grown faster than the nursery's capacity. Staff disagree over whether to increase throughput, expand the facility, partner with smaller local caretakers or reduce placements until quality-of-care standards recover.

Possible play:
- observe daily operation;
- interview staff and applicants;
- help inspect potential partner facilities;
- improve transport or supplies;
- assist with a placement ceremony;
- discover that public expectations do not match actual capacity.

No mechanical criteria for “starter readiness” are invented.

## Seed 02 — The Egg With Three Custodians

An Egg has moved through a rescue team, a clinic and a nursery.

All three institutions have legitimate records explaining why they held it. None of those records automatically resolves who has the legal claim to decide its future.

The quest is a custody/provenance case, not a DNA mystery.

Useful intersections:
- case-authority layer;
- care records;
- public memory;
- institutional trust.

## Seed 03 — Hatch Day Without a Quest

A player has carried an Egg through several unrelated adventures.

When the PTU/Caelo hatch clock resolves, the hatch occurs in whatever place the player actually reaches at that time. The world records who is present and where it happened.

The significance comes from accumulated history, not from forcing a dramatic event around the hatch.

## Seed 04 — The Nursery Night Shift

A busy nursery has enough staff during the day but insufficient coverage at night.

Players can become involved because:
- a club member works there;
- a caretaker asks for temporary logistical help;
- transport arrives late;
- a weather closure creates unexpected boarding demand.

The story can remain social and operational unless another world-state problem intersects it.

## Seed 05 — The Empty Incubator Row

A nursery normally has several Eggs in care. One week the incubator row is nearly empty.

Possible causes must be investigated from actual state:
- fewer breeding requests;
- a transport delay;
- a policy change;
- population decline in a partner conservation program;
- another facility taking over intake;
- a rumor falsely claiming the nursery is unsafe.

Absence becomes a clue rather than an automatic crisis.

## Seed 06 — The Old Hatching Book

A nursery keeps decades of handwritten hatch records.

The records are valuable because they preserve:
- dates;
- locations;
- caretakers;
- parentage claims;
- transfers;
- first placements.

Some entries conflict with modern digital records. The conflict can reveal clerical mistakes, institutional changes or a genuine historical question without the generator deciding which version is true.

## Seed 07 — A Lineage Everyone Misremembers

A locally famous Pokémon family is said to descend from one celebrated partner.

The surviving records do not fully support the public story.

Possible outcomes:
- the story is basically correct but simplified;
- several different lines were merged by public memory;
- the famous Pokémon was a mentor/caretaker rather than a parent;
- the institution intentionally emphasized symbolism over biology.

The point is to separate lineage, public memory and cultural legacy.

## Seed 08 — Nursery Expansion Vote

A settlement must decide whether a nursery may expand into adjacent land.

Stakeholders include:
- nursery staff;
- nearby residents;
- transport operators;
- ecologists;
- local wild-Pokémon advocates;
- businesses expecting more visitors.

No faction is automatically correct. The resulting world state changes building footprint, capacity, traffic and possibly habitat pressure.

## Seed 09 — The Caregiver's Apprentice

A young caretaker wants to qualify for greater responsibility.

The player can help through:
- supervised field observation;
- supply preparation;
- record audits;
- transport handoffs;
- assisting a senior mentor.

Narrative progress never awards PTU Hatcher Features directly.

## Seed 10 — The Egg That Crossed Ouros

An Egg has a long travel history because its intended recipient relocated repeatedly.

The Egg itself becomes a chronicle object linking:
- ferry records;
- temporary nurseries;
- a mobile clinic;
- player escorts;
- several settlements.

When it eventually hatches, those locations become provenance, not stat bonuses.

## Seed 11 — Conservation Release Cohort

A nursery is raising several juveniles for an eventual release program.

The main questions are:
- whether the habitat is ready;
- whether food and shelter are sufficient;
- whether transport is safe;
- whether the cohort should be released together or in stages.

The actual species, breeding legality and readiness criteria require authored canon and PTU review.

## Seed 12 — The Abandoned Egg Report

Someone reports finding an unattended Egg near a route.

The case begins with observation, not assumptions.

Possible truths include:
- temporary loss during transport;
- deliberate abandonment;
- a wild nesting context misunderstood by a traveler;
- a staged false report;
- an institution already searching for it.

The system must not infer ownership or parentage merely from location.

## Seed 13 — Nursery Open Day

A nursery holds a public event with:
- guided tours;
- care demonstrations;
- Trainer education;
- staff Q&A;
- a small exhibition battle for already battle-ready Pokémon;
- adoption/placement information.

The event can intersect public memory, clubs, performance culture and settlement commerce.

## Seed 14 — The Pairing Request

Two Trainers want to use an authorized breeding process involving Pokémon they each control.

The important gameplay is procedural and social:
- both players explicitly consent;
- the institution verifies eligibility through PTU rules;
- custody of a resulting Egg is decided before the process;
- the system records the agreement.

No relationship label is assigned to the parent Pokémon.

## Seed 15 — The Breeder Rival Who Never Battles You

A recurring NPC competes with the player through:

- placement success;
- research publications;

The rivalry can remain entirely outside formal battle.

## Seed 16 — Emergency Intake

Players may help with:

- temporary shelters;
- transport coordination;
- locating specialist care.

No injury or medical effect is invented.

## Seed 18 — The Nesting-Ground Detour

A seasonal nesting area appears beside a normal travel route.

Players can:
- reroute;
- observe from a distance;
- help establish temporary signage;
- report the change to transport services;
- assist researchers.

If a territorial battle occurs, it should arise from actual behavior and proximity, not mandatory encounter generation.

## Seed 19 — The Juvenile Who Keeps Returning

A young wild Pokémon repeatedly returns to the edge of a nursery.

Observable facts can accumulate:
- time of arrival;
- food-seeking behavior;
- reactions to staff;
- route used;
- injuries only if mechanically/medically established.

Do not conclude that it “wants to be adopted” until behavior or an authored scene supports that interpretation.

The Chronicle can link:
- hatch event;

- first Trainer;
- first battle result;
- public or private setting.

The battle grants no special bonus from being “home raised.”

A Hatcher-oriented Trainer runs classes about Egg care and young-Pokémon handling.

## Seed 22 — The Wrong Record, Right Pokémon

A juvenile's digital file lists the wrong source nursery.

The Pokémon is healthy and safe. The problem is provenance.

Players trace:
- transport logs;
- old labels;
- caretaker notes;
- hatch records;
- transfer dates.

The resolution matters for public records, institutional accountability and future lineage history.

## Seed 23 — The Local Egg Festival

A settlement has a cultural event celebrating new Pokémon and new Trainers.

Possible activities:
- storytelling;
- nursery tours;
- first-partner ceremonies;
- craft stalls;
- noncompetitive exhibitions;
- public education.

The event must not mechanically accelerate hatching unless PTU/Caelo says so.

## Seed 24 — The Rare Lineage Rumor

A rumor claims that an Egg belongs to a “rare bloodline.”

The system treats this as a public claim, not fact.

Possible consequences:
- unwanted attention;
- price speculation;
- research requests;
- attempted theft;
- institutional pressure;
- misinformation.

The story explicitly challenges the idea that lineage automatically equals value.

## Seed 25 — The Nursery Supply Route

A nursery depends on a predictable flow of food, bedding, medicine and equipment.

A route disruption causes service pressure without directly harming Pokémon.

Players can solve the problem through logistics, alternate suppliers or route restoration.

This connects the nursery to the existing material-culture and travel layers.

## Seed 26 — The Hatching Window Expedition

A research team expects an Egg to hatch during a remote field expedition based on an authoritative PTU/Caelo clock.

The party must decide whether to:
- continue the expedition;
- return to a facility;
- establish a temporary care site;
- transfer custody to another team.

The hatch itself still occurs only when rules say it does.

## Seed 27 — The Nursery Without Breeding

A well-known facility refuses to perform breeding services.

It specializes in:
- boarding;
- juvenile socialization;
- first-partner preparation;
- recovery;
- Trainer education.

This prevents the world from treating “nursery” and “breeding center” as synonyms.

## Seed 28 — The Breeding Center Without Boarding

The inverse institution handles only authorized breeding consultation and Egg generation, then immediately transfers Eggs to separate nurseries or owners.

This can create logistical links and jurisdiction/custody questions between institutions.

## Seed 29 — The Family Archive

A retired Trainer has kept records of several generations of Pokémon they personally raised.

The archive may include:
- hatch dates;
- photos;
- battle records;
- caretakers;
- moves learned;
- retirement locations.

It becomes a public-memory and historical source, but every biological claim retains provenance confidence.

## Seed 30 — The Nursery After the Founder

A beloved nursery founder retires or dies.

The institution survives.

Questions emerge around:
- succession;
- care philosophy;
- staffing;
- archived records;
- relationships with nearby settlements;
- long-running placement programs.

This creates legacy without requiring the founder to remain an active quest giver.

## Longer arc A — From Egg to Partner

A player receives or takes custody of an Egg through an authored/legal process.

The arc is not one quest. It spans:
- custody;
- ordinary travel;
- care history;
- hatch event;
- juvenile observation;
- socialization;
- training;
- eventual first battle or noncombat role.

The Pokémon's mechanics always come from PTU/Caelo.

## Longer arc B — The Nursery Network

Several small care facilities begin sharing records, specialists and emergency capacity.

Player actions can affect:
- which towns join;
- how transport works;
- who pays for expansion;
- how records are standardized;
- where specialist services exist;
- how crises are handled.

Over time, a decentralized network becomes a durable regional institution.

## Longer arc C — The Vanishing Hatch Records

Historical nursery records across several settlements have unexplained gaps.

The mystery may connect to:
- institutional reforms;
- lost archives;
- a disaster;
- changes in ownership law;
- a conservation program;
- intentional privacy practices.

The arc is about reconstructing evidence. It does not fabricate biological relationships to make the mystery dramatic.

## Implementation notes

Pass 17 seeds intentionally favor noncombat and standard-combat structures because the current Java engine still blocks complete forced movement/interception, battlefield terrain/hazard/reaction families, Trainer Feature execution, tactical AI and Minecraft adapter/playback.

Recent Java work upgrades items from BLOCKING to PARTIAL for narrative readiness because held-item state and one parity-backed item hook now exist. Abilities remain PARTIAL with a parity-backed Mega Launcher slice. Lifecycle and damage remain PARTIAL despite new generic hook seams.

No seed may use Egg damage, hatch acceleration, inherited Moves, Hatcher bonuses, nursery combat buffs or breeding outcomes without PTU/Caelo validation.
