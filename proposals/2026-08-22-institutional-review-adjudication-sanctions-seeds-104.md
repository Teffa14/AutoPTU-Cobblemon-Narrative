# Pass 104 Proposals — Institutional Review, Adjudication, Sanctions & Appeals

Status: NON-CANON CANDIDATES. Requires authored institutional mandates and PTU/Caelo mechanical review where relevant.

These concepts use the Pass 104 review layer. None establishes a universal justice system for Ouros.

## 30 narrative candidates

### 1. The Inspection That Was Already Scheduled

A Gym or Dojo receives a routine inspection weeks before a public incident occurs. Rumors later claim the inspection was punishment. The records show otherwise. Players can uncover how ordinary administration became political folklore.

### 2. Two Rulebooks on the Same Desk

An event official and a competitor each have a genuine rules document, but one is an older version. The conflict is about effective dates, not dishonesty.

### 3. The Protest Filed Too Late

A competitor has strong evidence of a procedural problem but submits it after the authored event's result-review window. The institution must decide what can still be corrected without pretending the past match never happened.

### 4. The Inspector Who Recuses

An assigned reviewer discloses a prior professional relationship with one party and steps aside. Public rumor treats the recusal as proof of corruption even though the record shows the opposite: the conflict was identified and handled.

### 5. The Result That Stands for the Wrong Reason

A protest alleges illegal equipment. The evidence does not establish that allegation, but review finds a different documented administrative error. The official result remains while the event process receives a corrective order.

### 6. A Warning Without a Villain

A research institute receives a formal warning because its public notice omitted a required access restriction. Nobody acted maliciously. The story is about fixing a real procedural weakness before it causes harm.

### 7. Reinspection Season

A Gym passes battle-quality review but fails a separate facilities/operations inspection. It keeps operating under limited conditions while repairs and a later reinspection are scheduled.

### 8. The Suspended Permit During Nesting Week

A field-access permission is temporarily suspended because the underlying conservation zone changed state. The review is about whether the permit conditions still fit the current world, not whether the researcher did something wrong.

### 9. The Appeal With Better Evidence

A camera trap, archived log or corrected timestamp appears after a decision. A new review changes one finding while leaving the rest intact. Chronicle preserves both decisions and why they differ.

### 10. The Decision Everyone Misquotes

A public summary says an institution was “cleared.” The actual decision says one allegation was not established and several operational corrections are still required. Media/rumor state diverges from the decision record.

### 11. The Badge-Giving Authority Question

A battle institution has strong Trainers but inconsistent administration. Review focuses specifically on whether it may continue granting formal progression records, not whether its staff are “good people.”

### 12. The Contest Device That Is Actually Allowed

Spectators accuse a performer of cheating because they use unfamiliar equipment. The current rule version explicitly permits it. The interesting consequence becomes a public correction and a later rule debate, not punishment.

### 13. The Contest Device That Was Allowed Last Year

The inverse case: the equipment was legal under a previous edition. The participant relied on outdated material distributed by a third party. Review must separate violation, intent and remedy.

### 14. Temporary Access, Permanent Rumor

A responder receives emergency access to a restricted site. Months later people claim the access proved permanent authorization. Credential records show the grant expired exactly as intended.

### 15. The Forgotten Reinstatement

A credential suspension expired, but one downstream access system never received the reinstatement event. The player encounters an institutional data problem rather than a new sanction.

### 16. The Wrong Person With the Same Name

A decision is attached to the wrong identity because two records share a common name. Fixing it requires identity/provenance work, public correction and restoration of access without deleting the erroneous historical record.

### 17. The Clean Battle, Dirty Paperwork

A Gym challenge itself is mechanically valid and fairly resolved. Later review finds that the event's required registration/recording step was mishandled. Battle result and institutional compliance remain separate.

### 18. The Paperwork Was Fine, the Battle Was Not

The inverse. Registration is correct, but the authoritative transcript shows a battle-state inconsistency in a subsystem whose rules are actually implemented and reviewable. The institution can address that narrow question without reconstructing intent.

### 19. The Funding Review After a Good Project

A grant-funded project produces excellent results but spends restricted resources outside the approved purpose. Scientific merit and grant compliance are different findings.

### 20. The Bad Project With Perfect Compliance

A restoration or public-works pilot follows every approved step and still performs poorly. The correct response is program review and redesign, not discipline for failure itself.

### 21. The Reviewer Who Changes Their Mind

During the same proceeding, a technical reviewer revises their interpretation after better evidence arrives. The system preserves the correction rather than treating consistency as more important than accuracy.

### 22. The Hearing Nobody Needs to Attend

A small records dispute is resolved through document review. Players can submit evidence asynchronously. This seed prevents every institutional issue from becoming a theatrical courtroom scene.

### 23. The Hearing Everyone Wants to Watch

A high-profile Gym review has intense public interest, but private medical/custody information remains redacted. Public access to the proceeding does not imply public access to every evidence item.

### 24. The Sanction That Ends on Time

An activity suspension reaches its authored expiry date. The institution restores access without demanding a redemption arc. The world remembers the sanction but does not make it permanent by inertia.

### 25. The Rule Everyone Followed and Nobody Likes

A controversial result is upheld because the rule was applied correctly. The story then moves to governance: should the rule change before next season?

### 26. The Rule Changed, the Old Result Did Not

After public debate, an institution changes its policy prospectively. Old decisions remain valid under the earlier rule unless an explicit review route says otherwise.

### 27. The Evidence That Cannot Be Published

A decision depends partly on sensitive ecological coordinates, medical information or private custody records. The public summary explains the disposition without exposing the underlying protected data.

### 28. The Voluntary Repair Before Decision

A party fixes damage and reaches an agreement with affected actors before formal review ends. The institution records that repair when deciding whether any separate institutional consequence remains necessary. Repair is not automatic forgiveness or dismissal.

### 29. The Emergency Hero Still Needs Reinspection

A suspended specialist performs exceptionally under a separately authorized emergency role. Their actions become positive evidence, but do not automatically erase the original reason for review.

### 30. The Archive of Overturned Decisions

An archive preserves decisions later amended or overturned. Researchers discover that institutional standards evolved through mistakes, corrections and dissent rather than through a perfectly linear history.

## Long arc A — Five Inspections of Stonebridge Gym

Year 1: a routine review documents small operational issues but leaves authorization intact.

Year 2: a crisis forces the Gym to serve as an emergency hub, revealing strengths and new weaknesses.

Year 3: leadership changes. Old inspection findings remain relevant but are not inherited as personal blame.

Year 4: a disputed challenge result triggers a separate competition review. The battle transcript and institutional procedure are examined independently.

Year 5: the Gym passes a new inspection under a revised rulebook partly shaped by lessons from earlier reviews.

The arc lets one institution accumulate administrative history without becoming either corrupt or flawless.

## Long arc B — The Rulebook Years

A regional competition changes one rule over several seasons.

Early decisions expose ambiguity. A controversial case is upheld under the existing wording. Participants organize a governance review. A later edition clarifies the rule. Another case reveals an unintended consequence. The final form is better but still imperfect.

The important persistent objects are rule versions, interpretations, decision history and player/NPC memories of previous editions.

## Long arc C — The Reinstatement Ledger

Several unrelated actors across years receive temporary suspensions or access restrictions from different institutions.

Some expire normally. One is overturned. One requires reinspection. One record fails to propagate to a downstream system. One person never requests reinstatement because their career changed.

Eventually an archivist or systems auditor discovers that the region's real institutional history is visible in the reinstatement ledger, not just the sanctions.

## Mechanically explicit encounter concepts

### Inspection Day Interruption

Narrative premise:

A scheduled institutional inspection is interrupted by a real emergency. The players can help secure the site. Later review must distinguish pre-existing compliance evidence from actions taken under emergency conditions.

FULL implementation needs:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- complete movement/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING for live evacuation and corridor control;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if the emergency creates tactical hazards;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT/EVACUATE/WITHDRAW priorities;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED implementation:

World state evacuates civilians and freezes a safe arena first. AutoPTU resolves only the conventional confrontation. The inspection later consumes separate pre-incident observations, emergency records and authoritative battle events. Helping during the crisis does not automatically pass the inspection.

### Protested Exhibition Result

Narrative premise:

A formal exhibition finishes, but a participant protests an eligibility, roster, equipment or rule-version issue.

FULL implementation:

Primarily institutional/world state. Exact battle dependencies are only those actually implicated by the protest. If the dispute concerns an Ability, Item, Move or Trainer Feature, that exact family and mechanic must have current parity evidence.

REDUCED implementation:

Use the existing authoritative result plus available registration/evidence. Do not replay or regenerate the match simply because a protest exists. The decision may uphold, amend, invalidate, request a rerun where authored rules permit it, or take no action.

### Credential Review Under Evacuation

Narrative premise:

A specialist is under scheduled credential review when a separate emergency occurs. They may receive a narrow temporary emergency permission because they are useful, while the original review remains unresolved.

FULL implementation needs:

- complete movement — BLOCKING for dynamic rescue routes;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if hazard mechanics are tactical;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for rescue/withdrawal behavior;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED implementation:

Pause the review in world state, issue an explicit temporary permission through Credentials/Authority, resolve rescue logistics outside the grid, and use conventional static combat only where necessary. Resume the original review afterward using the emergency performance as evidence, not as automatic reinstatement.

## Canon protections

These seeds do not establish:

- a regional court system;
- police powers;
- criminal offenses;
- incarceration;
- universal fines;
- universal appeal rights;
- mandatory public hearings;
- psychic testimony;
- lie detection;
- confession mechanics;
- social-roll control over PC beliefs;
- automatic Pokémon obedience or testimony;
- universal League authority over unrelated institutions.

Any such element requires separate authored canon and mechanical review.
