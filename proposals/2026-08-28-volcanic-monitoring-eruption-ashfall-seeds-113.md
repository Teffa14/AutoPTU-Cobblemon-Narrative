# Ouros Volcanic Monitoring, Eruption & Ashfall Seeds — Pass 113

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These are reusable candidates, not established events, locations, factions or mechanics.

## Design intent

These seeds use the proposed volcanic-continuity layer without declaring where volcanoes exist in Ouros. They are designed to produce persistent relationships, conflicting evidence, return visits and owner-system consequences. Full tactical versions must declare implementation dependencies; reduced versions keep active volcanic processes outside BattleSpec.

## Situation — The Station Saw It, the Town Did Not

A monitoring node records an unusual observation during a period when the nearest settlement notices nothing unusual.

Possible developments:

- the observation is valid but localized;
- another node was offline during the same window;
- a public rumor turns “unusual reading” into “eruption predicted”;
- the monitoring institution issues a cautious revision rather than an alarm;
- an older resident remembers a similar false interpretation from years earlier.

Persistent consequences:

- the node gains a local reputation;
- public trust can rise or fall depending on how uncertainty was communicated;
- later events can reuse the old observation as comparative evidence.

## Situation — Ash North, None at the Farm

Several settlements report visible residue after a volcanic episode. A farm closer to the mountain reports none.

The reports can all be genuine because location, wind/weather and timing differ. Weather owns atmospheric conditions; the volcanic layer stores ash observations; Agriculture decides crop consequences.

Useful investigation:

- compare observation times;
- distinguish airborne plume sightings from ground deposition;
- recover photographs or samples with provenance;
- identify whether one report was made before another part of the event.

No procedural dispersion model is required.

## Situation — The Pokémon Left Before the Notice

A familiar Pokémon disappears from its usual ridge before an activity assessment changes.

Possible interpretations:

- coincidence;
- habitat disturbance unrelated to volcanism;
- an individually established sensory capability;
- reaction to another Pokémon or human activity;
- a real environmental observation that science has not yet connected;
- unresolved.

The community may build folklore around the event. The system records the behavior without granting the species eruption prediction.

## Situation — The Cable Route Reopened First

A volcanic episode ends or decreases. A lower transport segment is inspected and reopened while the upper observation sector remains restricted.

Narrative value:

- tourists interpret reopening as “everything is safe”;
- researchers continue working under stricter access;
- local businesses recover at different rates;
- route staff become recurring witnesses to the transition.

Travel and access owner systems keep authority over reopening.

## Situation — The Old Notice Is Still on the Wall

A superseded volcanic notice remains posted at a rural shelter, trail entrance or shop.

The current assessment is less restrictive, but the old paper becomes evidence of what residents knew at that time.

Potential uses:

- resolve a dispute about whether a group departed before or after an update;
- show information lag without villainizing anyone;
- create a local custom of dating and annotating notices;
- reveal that the building stopped receiving communications during the critical window.

## Situation — The Monitoring Gap Was Maintenance

An observation series has a conspicuous blank interval.

Rumor claims records were hidden. The actual provenance may show ordinary maintenance, a communications outage or access blockage.

Possible outcomes must remain evidence-driven. The gap itself can matter even when no misconduct occurred, because later reviewers cannot reconstruct that interval with certainty.

## Situation — The Hot Spring Changed First

A hot-spring operator notices an authored change in their service before a monitoring assessment changes.

The observation is handed to Geology/Science for interpretation. Tourism/Hospitality owns business consequences. Water/Hot Springs owns the service state.

The volcanic layer stores only the relationship between observation, assessment and later evidence.

No automatic rule connects every hot-spring change to eruption risk.

## Situation — The Temporary Observation Post Stayed

During an episode, a temporary monitoring or information point is established near a community route.

After the episode:

- the equipment may leave;
- the shelter may remain;
- locals may keep using the location as a meeting point;
- a small archive or notice board may persist;
- a recurring Pokémon may adopt the quieter space.

A crisis workaround becomes durable social geography.

## Situation — The Farm Remembers Two Ashfalls

A family or cooperative possesses records from two different historical episodes.

The older record uses different place names and observation practices. The comparison can reveal:

- changed field boundaries;
- a relocated road;
- altered settlement vocabulary;
- different cleanup practices;
- an old habitat no longer present.

This supports family, agriculture, cartography, archives and public-memory layers without declaring mechanical crop damage.

## Situation — The Observatory Recorded Nothing

Residents report unusual sounds or visible activity while a nearby monitoring node reports no corresponding observation.

Possible reasons:

- the node does not measure that phenomenon;
- the event was outside its effective coverage;
- the report refers to a different time;
- the observation was real but non-volcanic;
- the node was degraded;
- the cause remains uncertain.

This seed teaches that “no instrument record” and “nothing happened” are separate claims.

## Situation — The Guide Knows the Old Flow Road

A local guide remembers a path around an old hardened flow field that disappeared from modern public maps after a newer road opened.

The path may matter for:

- historical research;
- habitat surveys;
- emergency access planning;
- pilgrimage or local memory;
- a missing-person search;
- an old utility route.

The route must be inspected before becoming mechanically usable. Memory does not override current access state.

## Situation — The Air Route Changed, the Village Did Not

An airborne-material observation affects an aviation decision while nearby ground communities experience little or no direct consequence.

This creates a useful world lesson: different audiences can react to different dimensions of the same episode.

Aviation owns flight decisions. Volcanic monitoring preserves the evidence and handoff.

## Mystery — Five Reports, One Afternoon

Five records appear to disagree:

- a guide reports no ash at 13:10;
- a monitoring post reports airborne material at 13:18;
- a village shop records ash cleanup at 14:05;
- a ferry crew reports clear conditions at 14:12;
- a farm log reports first residue at 15:00.

Resolution method:

- normalize location IDs;
- compare observation type;
- compare timestamps;
- distinguish plume from deposition;
- preserve source provenance;
- avoid forcing a single “correct” report when scopes differ.

## Mystery — Three Warnings, Two Audiences

Archive research finds three notice products with different wording. One addressed ground access, another transport operators, and a later revision reduced one concern while maintaining another.

The mystery resolves through audience and revision history, not a secret liar.

## Exploration — The Observatory Below the New Road

Premise:

A modern route bypassed an older observation station and its access path. The old building still contains site maps, maintenance logs and annotated photographs from a historical episode.

Immediately runnable version:

- current route and old footpath are static overworld geography;
- entry requires already-authored access/permission;
- exploration uses documents, location IDs, dated photographs, maintenance provenance and stable rooms;
- any combat occurs in a reviewed inert space.

Future mechanically rich version may include unstable volcanic terrain, active vents or environmental conditions only after exact PTU/Caelo rules and engine families exist.

## Long arc — A Town Learns the Mountain Again

Phase 1: ordinary continuity

Establish residents, guides, shops, routes, researchers, farms, hot-spring or tourism actors only where canon supports them. Show monitoring as background work rather than exposition.

Phase 2: conflicting evidence

A set of observations changes. Some institutions act. Others wait. Pokémon behavior or local folklore creates additional testimony but not automatic truth.

Phase 3: episode and footprint

If an authored episode occurs, observations arrive unevenly. Different owner systems make their own decisions. The playable story focuses on relationships, information, access and consequences rather than simulating eruption physics.

Phase 4: uneven recovery

Routes, businesses, habitat surveys, utilities and public spaces recover on separate timelines. Monitoring continues after the dramatic event ends.

Phase 5: historical reuse

Months or years later, another minor observation or unrelated investigation makes the archived episode relevant. Old actors return with changed jobs, opinions and relationships.

The arc has no single `volcano_recovery_level`.

Full version:

Actors move toward an already-established shelter/service point during or soon after an ashfall episode. A separate threat creates a protection problem.

Additional full-version requirements appear if ash changes LoS, movement, status, damage or zones. Those require the environmental family plus the corresponding PARTIAL damage/status/movement/lifecycle families and exact PTU/Caelo rules.
