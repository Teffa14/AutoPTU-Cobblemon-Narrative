# Ouros World Agency Seeds — Pass 03

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. Names, locations, factions and exact mechanics are placeholders until canon and PTU review.

## Seed S — The Civic Gym

### Core idea

A Gym is also embedded in the life of its settlement. Its Leader may have responsibilities outside badge battles: emergency response, training local volunteers, sponsoring public events, protecting a route, advising local government or maintaining a specialized facility.

### Adventure use

A player who never intends to challenge the Gym can still become entangled with it through:
- a missing trainee;
- public safety work;
- a damaged training facility;
- a dispute with another civic institution;
- a local environmental crisis;
- a faction attempting to buy influence through sponsorship.

### Design value

This lets Gyms participate in world stories without making every arc a badge quest.

### Guardrail

Each Gym should have its own civic relationship. Do not standardize every Leader into a mayor, police chief or regional ruler.

## Seed T — Two Factions, One Road

### Core idea

A major route is being improved by one organization and protected by another. Both provide real benefits. Their methods conflict.

Faction A wants to widen the road, build rest stations and increase commerce.
Faction B wants to restrict traffic to protect a migrating Pokémon population.

Neither side begins as a villain.

### Player choices

Players may:
- gather ecological evidence;
- escort surveyors;
- negotiate seasonal restrictions;
- expose false claims from either side;
- propose an alternate route;
- protect workers during a wild disturbance;
- support one faction fully;
- refuse involvement.

### Persistent outcomes

Possible world-state changes include:
- different NPC traffic;
- altered encounter populations;
- new shops or services;
- restricted travel windows;
- faction influence shifts;
- a future dispute if the compromise fails.

Exact travel, encounter and environmental effects require later implementation review.

## Seed U — The Three Trails Mystery

### Core idea

A valuable research specimen disappears from a field station. The investigation has three independent lead families from the beginning.

Possible lead families:
- physical traces near the storage area;
- conflicting witness accounts;
- unusual behavior from nearby Pokémon.

Each lead points toward different nodes. No single Skill Check is required to keep the investigation alive.

### Twist architecture

The system decides the actual truth before clues are generated. NPC claims may be sincere but wrong.

Possible truth categories:
- theft;
- accident;
- Pokémon interference;
- internal procedural error;
- faction operation;
- deliberate relocation for safety.

### Ouros requirement

Store evidence, claims and actor knowledge separately. The player should be able to accuse the wrong party, remain uncertain, or solve only part of the case.

## Seed V — The Weathered Arena

### Core idea

A battle occurs in a location whose environmental state can change during the encounter.

The arena might contain:
- damaged flood controls;
- geothermal vents;
- unstable crystal formations;
- industrial machinery;
- movable cover;
- vegetation that can spread or be cleared;
- a failing power system.

### Encounter identity

The objective is partly tactical and partly environmental. Players can fight normally, interact with the location, protect an object, disable a hazard or create a safer battlefield state.

### Guardrail

No exact field transition is canonical until the responsible Move, Ability, Capability, object interaction and AutoPTU implementation are validated against PTU/Caelo.

## Seed W — The Pokémon Who Keeps Returning

### Core idea

A wild Pokémon repeatedly appears near the same settlement after a disaster displaced its group.

It is not immediately a capture encounter.

The Pokémon may:
- steal food;
- watch trainers from a distance;
- sleep near a familiar structure;
- react badly to specific sounds or equipment;
- slowly tolerate certain NPCs;
- lead players toward another displaced Pokémon.

### Bond arc

Player behavior creates milestones rather than filling an exposed meter.

Possible milestones:
- accepts aid;
- allows treatment;
- follows at a distance;
- initiates contact;
- defends someone;
- returns after disappearing;
- chooses a new habitat;
- voluntarily joins a Trainer if that outcome fits the story.

### World consequence

The arc can reveal a wider ecological or infrastructure problem without turning the Pokémon into a quest object.

## Seed X — The Front That Outlives Its Leader

### Core idea

Players remove, expose, defeat or persuade the visible leader of a harmful faction project. The project does not automatically disappear because other people, contracts, money and infrastructure still support it.

### Follow-up states

The front may:
- collapse;
- fragment;
- become more extreme;
- moderate under a replacement;
- be absorbed by another faction;
- continue bureaucratically with no charismatic leader;
- become a legitimate service after reform.

### Design purpose

This prevents faction arcs from reducing to one boss battle.

## Seed Y — The Rival Who Learned the Wrong Lesson

### Core idea

A recurring rival studies a previous battle but only knows what they actually witnessed.

If the player won through one surprising strategy, the rival prepares specifically against that strategy. The player may arrive with a completely different team or approach.

### Narrative value

The rival feels adaptive without gaining perfect information.

The rival's beliefs can also be wrong:
- overestimates one Pokémon;
- misreads a sacrifice play as a preferred tactic;
- assumes the player fears a certain matchup;
- prepares for a Move they saw once but the Pokémon no longer knows.

### Guardrail

AI planning can use observed battle history and public information only. Hidden party information, private boxes, future Moves and non-public Chronicle facts remain inaccessible.

## Seed Z — Hold the Signal

### Core idea

A battle's victory condition is maintaining access to a communications or navigation system long enough for a rescue message to leave the area.

Enemies do not need to be defeated completely.

Possible player approaches:
- protect the operator;
- keep opponents away from controls;
- repair interrupted equipment;
- create a safe route to the transmitter;
- force attackers to retreat;
- abandon the equipment and escape with partial information.

### Persistent outcomes

Full success can accelerate rescue.
Partial success may transmit only location data.
Failure may force another group to search manually.

Exact round counts, object HP, actions and combat rules are intentionally undefined pending AutoPTU/PTU design.

## Seed AA — The Quiet Schism

### Core idea

A faction develops two internal groups that disagree about methods rather than ultimate goals.

Example structural conflict:
Both want to protect a rare habitat.
One favors strict exclusion.
The other favors controlled public access, research and education.

### Player interaction

Players may discover the split through inconsistent orders, different NPC attitudes, competing jobs and leaked documents.

They can:
- strengthen one wing;
- broker a compromise;
- expose corruption;
- accidentally cause a split;
- ignore the dispute;
- exploit the division for another goal.

### Long-term use

Faction identity evolves without requiring a wholly new organization every time the world changes.

## Seed AB — The Blackout of Facts

### Core idea

A crisis creates several simultaneous rumor networks. The physical event is real, but nobody initially understands the cause.

Sources may include:
- frightened civilians;
- faction statements;
- field researchers;
- competitors spreading blame;
- partial recordings;
- Pokémon behavior;
- travelers from neighboring areas.

### System requirement

Each rumor stores its source and claim separately from world truth.

Players can solve the crisis operationally without ever learning the full explanation, or discover the cause later through another arc.

### Multiplayer value

Different players may legitimately know different versions of the event.

## Seed AC — The Regional Pulse Arc

### Core idea

Several faction fronts advance at different speeds across a region while players pursue unrelated goals.

Example sequence:
- a conservation group expands field stations;
- a transport company opens a new route;
- smugglers redirect activity away from the increased patrols;
- a Gym begins training volunteers after several route incidents;
- migrating Pokémon begin using a different corridor.

None of these is automatically the main story.

### Arc formation

If player actions or world state connect enough of these fronts, the system may promote the intersection into an authored regional arc.

This lets larger stories emerge from accumulated state instead of assuming every minor event was secretly planned from the beginning.

## Seed AD — The Shared Aftermath

### Core idea

One major world event creates different content for different participation lanes.

After a damaged dam floods part of a valley:
- battlers protect evacuation routes from aggressive displaced Pokémon;
- researchers survey habitat changes;
- rescue-oriented players search isolated structures;
- social players coordinate shelter disputes;
- faction-focused players investigate responsibility;
- explorers discover a newly exposed cavern;
- crafters or economy-focused players help restore services.

### Design purpose

Multiple players can engage with the same event without everyone receiving the same quest.

The world-state outputs from each lane can affect the others.

## New implementation questions

- How frequently should World Pulses execute relative to Minecraft time and player activity?
- Which faction state changes should be visible immediately in the overworld versus surfaced through rumors first?
- How should AutoPTU represent battle objectives involving zones, objects, protection or escape?
- Which PTU/Caelo environmental interactions are already supported by the current AutoPTU engine?
- How should persistent wild Pokémon entities coexist with Cobblemon spawning/despawning?
- Which relationship consequences can remain narrative-only and which need mechanical modifiers?
- How should player-specific knowledge be synchronized in multiplayer without leaking private discoveries through shared UI?
