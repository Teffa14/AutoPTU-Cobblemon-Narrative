# Fisheries, Angling & Aquaculture Seeds — Pass 70

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros material derived from high-level research patterns. No source plot, character or exact mechanic is transplanted.

## 30 candidate seeds

### 1. The Empty Morning

Local fishers report almost no bites for three mornings. The stock may have moved, the water temperature may have changed, a new predator may be present, or fishing effort may simply be concentrated in the wrong reach. The quest begins with effort records rather than an assumed population crash.

### 2. The Tagged Return

A Pokémon tagged during a research programme years ago is observed again near its original release site. The sighting becomes valuable because it links individual identity, release provenance and long-term monitoring.

### 3. Two Kinds of Magikarp

A hatchery and a naturally recruiting river population look similar at first glance. Researchers need a reliable provenance method before claiming the stocking programme rebuilt the fishery.

### 4. Last Week of the Season

A popular angling window closes soon because of an authored spawning period. Tourism pressure rises as visitors rush to arrive before the closure, forcing transport, lodging and conservation systems to interact.

### 5. The Net Maker

An old workshop repairs locally distinctive fishing gear. The craft has material provenance and cultural value, but no mechanical bonus is assumed until a PTU item contract exists.

### 6. The Famous Whiscash

A single large Whiscash has appeared in local stories for years. Different fishers claim to have seen it in different reaches. The investigation tests whether all sightings belong to one persistent individual.

### 7. Festival Without a Catch

A fishing festival produces unusually poor catches but excellent visitor turnout. Organizers must decide whether the event succeeded socially while scientists investigate what the catch data actually means.

### 8. The Wrong Closure Sign

A shoreline sign says an area is closed for spawning, but the current institutional record says the closure ended last week. The case becomes a communications and public-record problem before it becomes an enforcement problem.

### 9. Hatchery Water Failure

A hatchery intake loses capacity during a planned release window. The issue connects Freshwater, Infrastructure, Staffing and Care; moving the release date may be safer than forcing the schedule.

### 10. Released but Not Restored

A large cohort was released three years ago. Recent surveys show fewer returning adults than expected. The programme needs review without turning the institution into a villain.

### 11. The Unexpected Bycatch

Fishers targeting one common species repeatedly encounter another population. The result may indicate distribution change, gear selectivity or a real ecological shift. Bycatch contacts remain observations until assessed.

### 12. Veluza Market Morning

A market receives biological material shed by Veluza through a locally authored collection practice. Provenance must distinguish resource harvesting from capturing or harming the Pokémon.

### 13. The Broken Measurement Board

A town's famous angling record board mixes weight, length and unreliable historical estimates. Public memory, sports records and scientific data need separate correction paths.

### 14. Reef Nursery Rumor

Visitors claim a protected cove is full of juvenile Pokémon. Conservation staff cannot publish exact coordinates until observations confirm the claim and sensitivity is reviewed.

### 15. The Fisher Who Never Keeps Anything

A respected angler practices release-only fishing and has decades of notebooks. Those records become useful for Science even though the Trainer owns none of the Pokémon described.

### 16. Night Net Lights

A harbor fishing method uses lights after dark. Researchers notice that the practice may overlap with a known nocturnal activity corridor. Light, fishery effort and ecology become one investigation.

### 17. One Bad Boat

Catch rates from one vessel collapse while neighboring boats remain normal. The cause may be gear condition, crew practice, route choice or instrumentation rather than the stock itself.

### 18. School at the Breakwater

A Wishiwashi aggregation appears near a breakwater during a public event. The visible school must not be interpreted automatically as the whole regional population or as an invitation to capture it.

### 19. The Old Hatchery Pond

A decommissioned hatchery pond has become an independent wetland habitat. Reopening the site for production would trade one public benefit for another.

### 20. The Missing Release Records

A box of old stocking records lacks clear release locations. Later population claims cannot be resolved until Archive, Cartography and Science evidence are reconciled.

### 21. Fishing Line Cleanup Week

Residents organize a shoreline cleanup after repeated entanglement reports. The event can remain entirely non-combat and still change conservation state, public memory and tourism.

### 22. A Tournament Changes Its Rules

A long-running competition moves from retain-to-score to observation-and-release because the local management objective changed. Old records remain valid for their historical ruleset.

### 23. The Spring Run Arrives Late

A known seasonal movement begins later than expected. Meteorology, Freshwater, Seasonality and Fisheries each hold part of the evidence; no single system gets to declare the cause.

### 24. The Hatchery Graduate

A persistent Pokémon raised by an institution is released and later becomes associated with a wild collective. Its individual provenance remains intact after it leaves institutional custody.

### 25. The Gear Restriction Nobody Understands

A rule prohibiting one fishing method is widely seen as arbitrary because the original management report is no longer public-facing. The quest is about reconstructing the evidence and deciding whether the measure still serves its purpose.

### 26. The Market Wants Bigger Fish

A buyer's preference shifts effort toward larger individuals. Scientists raise a concern about changing selection pressure, but the world does not assume the concern is already proven.

### 27. The Quiet Pier

An old pier stops attracting anglers after a new transport terminal changes pedestrian flow. The fish may be unchanged even while the fishery culture around them disappears.

### 28. The Wrong Species in the Pond

A hatchery worker reports an unexpected juvenile. Possible explanations include record error, accidental transfer, natural entry or a genuine biosecurity problem.

### 29. Research Boat Open Day

A fishery research vessel becomes a temporary public venue. Players can learn sampling, mapping and monitoring through observation without receiving automatic Researcher Features or mechanical bonuses.

### 30. The Catch That Wasn't Rare

A photograph of an unusual catch spreads online and creates a tourism surge. Later evidence suggests the species was always present but rarely targeted by the gear most local fishers used.

## Three longer arcs

### The Estuary Ledger

Year 1 begins with conflicting reports about declining catches. Year 2 establishes effort-normalized surveys across several zones. Year 3 adds a spawning closure and habitat-restoration project. Year 4 tests whether recruitment changed. Year 5 revisits the same fishery after new transport and tourism pressure. The arc can conclude with a more accurate understanding rather than a simple victory state.

### The Hatchery Question

A regional hatchery has operated for decades and is publicly credited with supporting a valued population. Players uncover incomplete release records, changing water conditions and uncertain survival estimates. The institution can adapt its programme, reduce releases, change monitoring or close one facility without the story requiring corruption or sabotage.

### The Lake Festival Years

A fishing festival returns annually. Each edition accumulates records, rivalries, vendors, ecological observations and rule changes. A famous persistent aquatic Pokémon may appear in some years and not others. The event becomes a public-memory anchor while the lake's fishery and ecology continue changing independently.

## Encounter contract 1 — Spawning Reach Closure

Full version requires dynamic withdrawal/protection, complete movement/interception, terrain or zone semantics if the protected reach matters mechanically, tactical AI and Minecraft playback.

Reduced version resolves closure compliance, aquatic movement and visitors before battle. Any battle uses a static shoreline arena. The spawning reach does not apply invented tactical effects.

## Encounter contract 2 — Hatchery Intake Failure

Full version requires interactable objectives, water-state projection, possible moving wildlife, tactical AI and adapter playback.

Reduced version resolves intake operation through Infrastructure/Freshwater state first. If conflict remains, AutoPTU receives a fixed legal arena with only actual combatants.

## Encounter contract 3 — Fishing Festival Overflow

Full version could support boats, competing fishers, live fishing objectives and noncombatants moving between zones. That depends on complete movement, objective-aware tactical AI and adapter/playback.

Reduced version keeps competition scoring and fishing effort in the event system. AutoPTU opens only for discrete battles. Official fishing results are not derived from battle damage.

## Mechanical boundary

No seed assigns Fishing DCs, rod modifiers, bait bonuses, net attacks, hooked statuses, food yields, hatchery growth rates, stock formulas or capture bonuses. Those remain blocked until exact PTU/Caelo mechanics and implementation evidence are available.
