# Ouros Social Bonds, Mentorship & Clubs Seeds — Pass 09

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research themes.

## 1. The Empty Clubroom
A once-active exploration club has lost nearly all its members after several expeditions ended badly. The room still contains maps, damaged equipment, old notices and unfinished projects. Players can revive it, repurpose it, merge it with another group or leave it dormant.

Potential consequences:
- old members return for different reasons;
- unresolved maps become new expedition hooks;
- the club's reputation changes depending on the new direction;
- physical renovations appear in Minecraft.

## 2. The Mentor Who Is Wrong
A respected field expert teaches a method that worked for decades but no longer matches current ecological conditions. The mentor is competent, not fraudulent. A learner may discover the mismatch first.

The interesting question is whether the player challenges, updates or quietly works around the mentor's method.

## 3. Reciprocal Apprenticeship
Two Trainers each know something the other lacks. One is excellent at field navigation; the other understands Pokémon care and recovery. A shared project makes each person mentor and learner in different contexts.

## 4. The Returning Alumni
A former club member returns after years away. Public memory remembers them as a success, while people who knew them personally remember a more complicated departure. Their return can create new projects, old tensions and different accounts of the club's history.

## 5. Mixed-Specialty Field Practicum
A regional institution creates a practical field assignment requiring several roles: observation, navigation, battle readiness, first aid, negotiation and documentation.

No single specialty can complete everything efficiently. The content is designed to create peer dependence without forcing one solution.

## 6. A Rival Worth Asking
A recurring battle rival has expertise relevant to a crisis. Asking for help creates a social choice separate from the rivalry's competitive history. Cooperation does not automatically end the rivalry.

## 7. The Club Funding Vote
A shared institution has enough resources for only one major upgrade this season. Competing proposals might include a training arena, research equipment, rescue supplies or public outreach space.

The choice changes facilities and future activity availability rather than producing a simple good/bad outcome.

## 8. The Quiet Student Network
Several novice Trainers learn more from each other than from formal instructors. They exchange field notes, warnings and small practical discoveries. Players can join, mentor, ignore or accidentally disrupt this peer network.

## 9. The Specialist Everyone Avoids
An expert is extremely useful but difficult to work with. Their social reputation has reduced access to knowledge that the region genuinely needs. The system should allow collaboration without requiring the player to become their friend.

## 10. First Time Teaching
A veteran player is asked to supervise beginners during a safe expedition or practice event. The challenge is preparation, communication and responsibility rather than defeating stronger enemies.

Possible callbacks:
- trainees later appear as competent NPCs;
- a trainee remembers specific advice;
- a failed lesson becomes a future improvement goal.

## 11. The Club Rivalry That Is Mostly Healthy
Two institutions compete for members, prestige and public attention. Most participants enjoy the competition; a few take it too far. The story can distinguish institutional rivalry from personal hostility.

## 12. The Shared Workshop Night
Crafting, research, cooking, repair and Pokémon care specialists gather periodically in one public work session. Players can help another person's project, ask for advice or contribute materials.

This creates social scenes with productive outcomes rather than dialogue-only interaction.

## 13. The Exchange Program
Visitors from another settlement or region temporarily join a local institution. Their expectations about training, Pokémon care or public behavior differ from local norms.

No culture is automatically declared correct. The content focuses on negotiation, curiosity and practical friction.

## 14. The Dormant Promise
An NPC and player made a concrete promise many sessions earlier. The system notices that circumstances now make the promise relevant again and offers a callback without assuming either side still feels the same way.

## 15. Mentor Succession
A respected instructor retires, disappears from public life or changes careers. Several possible successors embody different philosophies. The institution must decide whether to preserve, reform or split the old program.

## 16. The Pokémon Care Circle
Trainers with Pokémon recovering from stressful events meet informally to share non-mechanical care routines and observations. A Pokémon may become comfortable with certain people or locations over time.

No therapy claims or mechanical recovery bonuses are generated automatically.

## 17. The Club Project That Fails
A community project misses its goal. The failure leaves useful information, partial infrastructure and changed relationships. Instead of deleting the quest, Ouros records what was learned and creates later recovery opportunities.

## 18. An Invitation With Context
An NPC sends a message because a new event intersects with something the player actually cares about: a species they studied, a former rival, a location they helped, or a skill they demonstrated.

This is preferable to generic 'come talk to me' notifications.

## 19. Competing Mentors
Two experts offer different approaches to the same field. Both methods have strengths and blind spots. A player may study with one, both or neither.

The relationship system records what was learned and experienced without assigning a morality score to the choice.

## 20. The B-Team Expedition
Trusted supporting NPCs undertake a bounded secondary operation while the main player group handles another crisis. Their outcome changes a later location or provides new information.

The operation must use known resources and must not resolve the player's central arc off-screen.

## 21. Community Knowledge Night
A settlement periodically hosts short public talks where researchers, Rangers, artisans, battlers and ordinary residents share discoveries. Conflicting observations can surface naturally.

This connects the observation, myth, crafting, case and social layers without requiring a single quest.

## 22. The Former Student
A player meets someone once taught by an NPC they know. The former student's methods reveal what that mentor was actually like in practice, which may differ from public reputation.

## 23. The Club Without a Building
A new player-created group begins with no formal headquarters. Its first progression is social: recurring meeting places, borrowed storage, favors and recognition. A physical clubroom only becomes possible later if the group creates enough support.

## 24. A Pokémon Chooses the Lesson
A Trainer arrives intending to practice one thing, but their Pokémon repeatedly engages with a different activity or environment. The scene can become a bond/observation opportunity without granting an unsupported mechanical bonus.

## 25. The Difficult Reconciliation
Two NPCs with a long shared history need to work together again. The player can create conditions for cooperation but cannot force forgiveness. Success may mean functional collaboration while tension remains.

## 26. Social Map of a Settlement
Instead of a single reputation number, an important town contains overlapping circles: researchers, shopkeepers, students, battlers, heritage stewards, responders and local families. Helping one group can introduce the player to others while also exposing disagreements inside the settlement.

## 27. The Guest Instructor
A well-known Trainer visits temporarily. Access is not merely a reward for fame; different players may care about different lessons, stories or contacts. The visit can produce public demonstrations, small-group work and private conversations.

## 28. Graduation Without Ending
Completing a formal program changes the player's relationship to the institution. They may become alumni, mentors, donors, visiting specialists or critics. Graduation opens a new social role instead of deleting the hub.

## 29. The Club Archive
Old meeting notes, project reports and photographs create a small institutional memory. A new generation may interpret old decisions differently or rediscover unfinished work.

## 30. The Player Who Starts a School
Very late progression may allow players to establish a training, research, craft or exploration program. The important decisions are who it serves, what it teaches, who joins, what standards it adopts and how it changes the surrounding settlement.

Mechanical progression rewards remain undefined until PTU/Caelo and AutoPTU support are reviewed.

## Cross-system hooks

These seeds can connect to existing Ouros layers:
- Chronicle: shared social events and promises;
- World Agency: clubs and institutions as actors;
- Observation: peer research and field reports;
- Public Memory: alumni, public rivalries and institutional reputation;
- Cases: trainees or specialists as bounded case roles;
- Material Culture: workshops, apprenticeships and commissions;
- Myth/Archaeology: tradition keepers and research societies;
- Mission Grammar: field practicums, club projects and teaching assignments.

## Safety / authorship boundary

Generated content must never decide a PC's romance, private emotion, forgiveness, hatred or interpersonal identity. It may remember observable shared events and offer opportunities. Player-authored relationship labels remain authoritative.
