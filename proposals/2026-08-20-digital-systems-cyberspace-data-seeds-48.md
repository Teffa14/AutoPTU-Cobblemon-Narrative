# Ouros Digital Systems, Cyberspace & Data Seeds — Pass 48

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, dialogue or distinctive character is imported.

## Design intent

These seeds use software, data, virtual spaces and digital Pokémon as parts of a persistent world. They should create consequences through existing systems: institutions, routes, research, public memory, cases, infrastructure, Pokémon agency and player history.

No seed grants hacking powers, Porygon administrator access, Rotom network control or digital battle rules by narrative fiat.

## Seed 01 — The Cached Road

A popular public route service still displays a mountain crossing as open because one regional cache has not received the latest closure update.

The physical road state, official route record, cached copy and visitor knowledge remain separate. The story can resolve through verification and correction without any sabotage.

## Seed 02 — Three Branches of the Same Dataset

Three research institutions began from the same ecological dataset, then corrected different records locally during years of poor connectivity.

All three branches contain useful work. Reconciliation requires tracing versions and provenance rather than choosing a winner by institutional prestige.

## Seed 03 — Yesterday's Backup

A clinic scheduling system is restored after failure. The service works again, but appointments and annotations entered after the snapshot no longer appear in the current view.

Those events still happened. Staff must reconstruct the missing operational state from receipts, messages and people rather than treating restore as time travel.

## Seed 04 — Porygon in the Water Network

A Porygon repeatedly appears in monitoring systems during pressure anomalies. Operators suspect interference.

Investigation may show that it was following the same anomaly the staff were trying to diagnose. Presence remains evidence, not guilt.

## Seed 05 — Unexpected Porygon2

A Porygon2 assigned to repetitive archival work begins spending time comparing records outside its task queue.

The institution must distinguish malfunction, adaptive behavior and independent initiative without having access to the Pokémon's private thoughts.

## Seed 06 — The Rollback Worked

A transit-control update causes intermittent display errors. Rolling back restores service immediately.

Later review shows the previous release also contained a quieter long-standing problem. The easy recovery creates a second decision about when and how to attempt the upgrade again.

## Seed 07 — The Server Nobody Can Retire

A settlement wants to replace an old local server. Each migration test reveals another small service that quietly depends on it.

The campaign becomes a map of institutional memory, undocumented interfaces and staged replacement rather than a single “fix the computer” quest.

## Seed 08 — Authentic Log, Wrong Conclusion

A system log accurately records an account accessing a restricted file. Public discussion identifies the account owner as responsible.

The log proves account activity, not who physically controlled the session. The Case layer must keep attribution as a hypothesis until corroborated.

## Seed 09 — The Museum Has Two Labels

The physical exhibit still uses an old interpretation while the digital catalogue contains a corrected description.

Visitors photograph both and accuse the museum of hiding history. The actual cause is an incomplete exhibit-update project.

## Seed 10 — The Missing Battle Packets

A tournament archive has a gap during a controversial match. One side calls the missing entries proof of cheating; another calls them proof of a cover-up.

AutoPTU's authoritative result remains intact. The missing public archive affects perception and investigation, not the historical battle outcome.

## Seed 11 — Retired Account

A staff member left an institution months ago, but an old service account tied to their role remains active.

The account's continued existence exposes a governance problem. It does not establish that the former staff member used it.

## Seed 12 — Format Migration

Two regions have exchanged research for decades but use incompatible record formats.

A conversion project reveals fields that have no exact equivalent. The players must decide what information needs preservation, annotation or manual review.

## Seed 13 — Clock Drift

A network of wildlife sensors reports apparently impossible movement by the same collective.

The observations are real, but several station clocks have drifted. Correcting timestamps changes interpretation without deleting the original measurements.

## Seed 14 — Orphaned Node

A Porygon's repeated route through abandoned network nodes suggests a hidden destination.

The final node may contain an obsolete service, historical archive or isolated simulation. The system does not assume the Pokémon is lost, trapped or asking to be captured.

## Seed 15 — The Training Simulation

A battle institution maintains a simulated tactical space used to rehearse positioning and decisions.

Results are training records, not formal wins, EXP or League progression unless future governing rules explicitly authorize those effects.

## Seed 16 — The Digital Memorial

A community maintains an online memorial whose dates and participant list differ from a later archival discovery.

Updating the memorial becomes a public-memory decision. The older version remains historically relevant because people saw and shared it for years.

## Seed 17 — Virtual Reconstruction

A museum builds a navigable reconstruction of an ancient district from incomplete maps and excavation records.

Visitors begin treating the simulation as a photograph of the past. Curators must expose which elements are evidence-backed, inferred or deliberately illustrative.

## Seed 18 — The Route App Creates the Crowd

A travel application recommends a recently restored trail. A popular update suddenly routes far more visitors there than local facilities expected.

The digital recommendation becomes one causal input to tourism pressure, transport use and conservation response.

## Seed 19 — Signed by an Old Office

Copies of an official digital notice continue circulating after the office that issued it has changed policy.

The document was authentic when published. Authenticity and current validity are different questions.

## Seed 20 — Deletion Without Disappearance

An institution removes an incorrect entry from its current database after review.

Screenshots, archived editions, publications and actor memories still exist. The correction creates a new state rather than erasing the old claim from history.

## Seed 21 — The Public Terminal Is Fine

Residents report that a public terminal is “broken” because one service fails. Other services on the same terminal continue working.

The incident teaches the world-state system to distinguish interface, host asset, network path and individual service health.

## Seed 22 — The Rotom Was in the Screen

A Rotom visibly inhabits a display at the same time private records are leaked.

The leak investigation cannot assume that device possession gave the Pokémon database access. Physical device interaction and digital authorization remain separate.

## Seed 23 — The Old Simulation Still Runs

A decommissioned educational server still hosts a small simulation populated by scripted characters and years of accumulated state.

Players initially call the inhabitants artificial intelligence. Investigation must determine what is actually autonomous before the system adopts that label.

## Seed 24 — Private Notes on a Shared Kiosk

Two club members use the same public workstation. One later sees a reference to a document they did not know existed.

The story revolves around access configuration and privacy boundaries. It never reveals the private document's contents unless access is legitimately established.

## Seed 25 — Emergency Map Restore

During a storm, an emergency service restores an older map configuration because the current server is damaged.

The fallback restores critical coverage but temporarily reintroduces an obsolete evacuation route. Field reports and map editions must reconcile quickly.

## Seed 26 — The Version That Won the Argument

A civic proposal cites a cost table from a public dataset. Another group produces a different official version with different numbers.

Both are genuine snapshots from different dates. The dispute shifts from “which file is fake?” to “which conditions still apply?”

## Seed 27 — Research Embargo Leak Claim

A sensitive ecological study appears to have leaked coordinates of a nesting site.

A public derivative contains enough context for visitors to approximate the area even though the protected coordinates themselves were never published. The incident becomes a lesson in derived information, not just file permissions.

## Seed 28 — The Scoreboard Disagrees with the Match

A synchronization error shows the wrong winner on public displays for several minutes.

AutoPTU's transcript remains authoritative. The error can still create celebration, confusion, media clips and later correction in public memory.

## Seed 29 — The Virtual Quarter

A regional public-service network gradually grows a shared virtual area containing help desks, clubs, research rooms and public exhibitions.

The project must decide what persists, who moderates each space, how identities link to physical actors and which services remain accessible during outages.

## Seed 30 — The Last Clean Snapshot

A long-running research system becomes unstable. The newest verified backup is months old, while newer records exist across individual devices and exports.

Recovery becomes a reconstruction project with provenance, uncertainty and partial success rather than a single restore button.

## Long arc A — The Legacy Network

A region decides to retire a decades-old digital network. Early work looks simple: migrate public records, update terminals and replace old hosts.

Each stage reveals dependencies created by years of ordinary institutional practice. A small ferry schedule reads one legacy table. A clinic maintains a local exception list. A museum's catalogue links to identifiers no longer used anywhere else. Several Porygon have worked around the network long enough that staff confuse observed habits with formal system requirements.

Players can help institutions inventory dependencies, create fallbacks, test migrations, compare versions and communicate outages. The network's retirement changes workplaces, archives, transport and public services over several arcs.

The conclusion should not be “old technology bad” or “new technology bad.” A successful ending leaves a region with better understood systems, preserved history and deliberate choices about which legacy functions remain.

## Long arc B — Three Copies of the Truth

Three research groups publish contradictory conclusions about a migrating Pokémon population.

The apparent scientific feud begins as a data problem. One institution has the oldest baseline, another has the best recent coverage, and the third corrected a known identification error but never transmitted the change to the others.

Players trace record lineage, field-check disputed sites and discover that no dataset is simply false. Reconciliation produces a stronger model while preserving the older versions as evidence of what each institution reasonably believed at the time.

The arc can later feed Science, Public Memory, Conservation and Media systems.

## Long arc C — The Virtual Quarter

A utilitarian regional portal starts as a place to access public notices and institutional services. Clubs request meeting spaces. Researchers create visualization rooms. Museums add remote exhibits. A battle institution adds non-authoritative training simulations.

As the Quarter becomes culturally important, new problems appear: identity verification, moderation, archived versions, accessibility, privacy, service outages, persistence after host migration and disagreements over whether old spaces should be preserved.

A Porygon population or several individually known Porygon may interact with parts of the system only where canon and rules support it. Their presence does not make them administrators.

The long-term arc asks what kind of public place the Quarter becomes and how it connects to physical communities. It does not require revealing that physical reality is itself a simulation.

## Promotion questions

Before any seed becomes canon, resolve:

- regional technology level;
- whether persistent virtual spaces exist at all;
- physical hosting and outage behavior;
- identity and privacy policy;
- Porygon/Rotom PTU and Caelo rules;
- Pokémon storage metaphysics;
- what digital records are authoritative for each scope;
- what Minecraft can represent reliably;
- which battle dependencies have moved from PARTIAL/BLOCKING to verified implementation.
