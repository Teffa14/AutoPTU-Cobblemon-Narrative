# Ouros Technology, Energy & Infrastructure Seeds — Pass 29

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns only.

## 1. The Brownout District

A growing district experiences short power interruptions at predictable peak hours. Residents blame the old substation, while operators suspect a new commercial load and conservation workers point to unusual Pokémon activity around the distribution corridor.

The first useful outcome is not a culprit. It is a measured load profile, a map of affected services and a temporary operating plan.

## 2. The Rotom Who Likes One Pump

A Rotom repeatedly inhabits one municipal pump controller but ignores identical units nearby. Operators are divided between removing it, accommodating it and studying the behavior.

The system records only observed device-specific behavior. It does not assume the Rotom can operate every machine in the network.

## 3. Night Shift Apprentices

A utility operator starts a supervised night apprenticeship program after several veteran technicians retire. Players may train alongside apprentices, investigate recurring faults or help update old documentation.

Technical job titles do not create Technology Education ranks automatically.

## 4. The Backup Generator Nobody Tested

A clinic has a backup generator recorded as operational. During a routine capacity test, it fails to carry the full load.

Because the failure happens during testing rather than disaster, players can solve a preparedness problem before lives depend on it.

## 5. The Abandoned Switching House

A decommissioned electrical switching house sits beside a modern route. Wild Pokémon now use the warm, sheltered structure as habitat.

A proposal to demolish it creates a three-way problem involving safety, industrial heritage and ecology.

## 6. The Cold Chain Break

A ferry delay and intermittent refrigeration threaten a shipment used by clinics and researchers.

The problem can be addressed through rerouting, priority power, temporary storage, alternate suppliers or accepting that some material will be lost.

No item spoilage timer is invented until rules/canon defines one.

## 7. The Misleading Alarm

A factory alarm repeatedly triggers without any confirmed dangerous event. Staff begin ignoring it.

Players must separate sensor fault, environmental interference, Pokémon behavior and actual risk before the next alarm becomes socially meaningless.

## 8. Windworks Migration Week

A wind generation site lies on a seasonal flight corridor. Most of the year the facility runs normally, but one migration window requires temporary operational changes.

The story is about coexistence planning and monitoring, not defeating the migrating Pokémon.

## 9. The Manual Valve

A modern water-control system fails during maintenance, but an older manual bypass still exists behind a sealed service corridor.

The old system is slower and labor-intensive, yet it prevents complete shutdown.

## 10. The Relay on Borrowed Parts

A remote communications relay has stayed alive through years of improvised repairs using compatible parts from different generations of equipment.

A modernization team must decide whether to replace everything, preserve some components or first document why the strange system still works.

## 11. Factory Line Three

Only one line in a Poké Ball component factory produces inconsistent output. The raw materials, staff and power supply appear normal.

Potential leads include calibration drift, machine wear, a logging error and Pokémon interference. Sabotage remains only one hypothesis.

## 12. The Technician Who Knows the Sound

An experienced operator can identify a failing machine by a change in vibration and sound, but the automated monitoring system reports normal values.

Players may compare embodied expertise with instrument data instead of treating either source as automatically superior.

## 13. Power for the Festival

A settlement festival will exceed normal evening electrical capacity if every planned attraction runs simultaneously.

Organizers can stagger events, rent temporary generation, reduce decorative loads or change venue layout.

The crisis is optional because good planning can prevent it.

## 14. The Research Freezer Priority

A regional outage leaves limited backup power. A research freezer, a clinic refrigeration unit and an emergency communications room all request priority.

The technical layer records capacities and fallback limits. The civic/institutional layer resolves who is authorized to prioritize loads.

## 15. The Old Grid Map

Official diagrams show a feeder line that no longer exists physically. Years of undocumented repairs created a different network than the archive suggests.

Players can reconstruct the actual topology through field inspection, maintenance records and operator memory.

## 16. Porygon in the Legacy System

An institution uses a long-running Porygon-associated computer service whose documentation is incomplete.

The system must distinguish the Pokémon's observed role, its consent/behavior, actual software state and what operators merely assume it understands.

No universal Porygon hacking ability is granted.

## 17. The Broken Lift to a Healthy Mine

A mine itself is structurally sound, but its cargo lift is offline. Production stops even though the resource deposit remains available.

The quest shows how one technical bottleneck can change economy and transport without a region-wide disaster.

## 18. Heat in the Greenhouse

An agricultural greenhouse receives too much waste heat from nearby infrastructure during an unusual weather period.

Farmers, utility operators and researchers may choose insulation, altered operating schedules, heat recovery or temporary crop relocation.

## 19. Streetlights and Nocturnal Pokémon

A town replaces old streetlights with brighter fixtures. Residents appreciate improved visibility, but observation records show changes in nocturnal Pokémon behavior.

The lighting project may be adjusted instead of simply cancelled.

## 20. The Pump Station Archive

A renovation uncovers decades of handwritten pump logs. They reveal recurring seasonal problems that modern dashboards never captured because the digital system is newer than the pattern.

Historical operational data becomes a science/phenology hook.

## 21. Two Operators, Two Philosophies

Two skilled maintenance teams disagree about whether a heavily modified machine should be repaired again or replaced entirely.

Neither side is corrupt or incompetent. One values reliability through known behavior; the other values standardization and future support.

## 22. The Temporary Microgrid

A remote district creates a temporary local power network after a route failure isolates it from the main grid.

If it works well, residents later debate whether to keep some local independence after the main connection returns.

## 23. The Quiet Factory

An old factory has been closed for years, but its ventilation fans occasionally start on their own.

Possible explanations include residual automation, an overlooked backup system, Pokémon interaction or an unauthorized occupant.

The investigation should not assume a haunting or antagonist.

## 24. Water Pressure at Dawn

Only homes on higher ground lose water pressure during early morning hours.

The problem becomes a small infrastructure mystery involving demand patterns, elevation, pump timing and an aging valve.

## 25. The Spare-Part Cooperative

Several small settlements cannot justify stocking rare replacement parts individually, so they create a shared regional cooperative.

A missing part shipment can become a travel/logistics story without requiring scarcity to be permanent.

## 26. The Device Recall

A manufacturer issues a voluntary recall after a fault is discovered in one model of field device.

Some users ignore the notice, some never receive it and others rely on the device too heavily to surrender it immediately.

This connects media delivery, technical risk and personal equipment provenance.

## 27. The Habitat Controller

A constructed urban habitat uses water circulation, shade systems and environmental controls. One subsystem starts failing intermittently.

The story asks whether operators can keep the habitat stable while repairs occur and whether wildlife behavior provides early warning.

## 28. The Unauthorized Improvement

A talented technician quietly modifies a public machine and genuinely improves performance, but bypasses documentation and approval.

The technical result may be good while the institutional process remains problematic.

## 29. The Last Coal Torkoal

An old industrial site historically used Torkoal-associated heat as part of its operations. The facility is transitioning to a new system, leaving questions about workers, Pokémon care, heritage and future use of the site.

Do not assume any mechanical energy output for Torkoal without PTU validation.

## 30. The Line That Became a Trail

A decommissioned utility corridor is now used informally by hikers and wild Pokémon. A new infrastructure proposal wants to reactivate it.

The route layer, conservation layer and civic layer all become relevant.

# Longer arc A — The Grid Remembers

A region modernizes its electrical network after years of patchwork repairs.

Act I: surveys reveal that official network maps are incomplete.

Act II: operators discover undocumented bypasses installed during older crises, including one created because of a past player or historical event.

Act III: modernization forces choices about redundancy, heritage, ecology and service priorities.

Act IV: the new network enters testing and exposes a different weakness than the one everyone expected.

Resolution does not require a villain. The arc can end with a more resilient but still imperfect system whose design reflects player decisions.

# Longer arc B — Machines and Habitat

A city expands technology-assisted habitats for wild Pokémon.

Early content focuses on observation, maintenance and coexistence.

Later, repeated failures reveal that the habitat systems were designed around incomplete seasonal data rather than malicious intent.

Players can help revise operations, relocate equipment, expand monitoring and negotiate service priorities with other city needs.

The final state may be a hybrid human/Pokémon urban landscape rather than a perfect technological solution.

# Longer arc C — The Factory Generations

A factory has operated for decades under several owners and technologies.

Players initially enter because of a small repair or production problem.

They gradually uncover maintenance records, retired workers, replaced machines, old environmental impacts and current economic dependence on the facility.

Different generations of workers disagree about what the factory means to the settlement.

The arc may conclude in modernization, conversion, partial closure, worker cooperative control, heritage preservation or another canon-approved outcome. No option is pre-labelled morally correct.

# Encounter contracts

FULL: dynamic power zones, technical controls, protected technicians and objective-aware enemies.

Dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING where machinery or interception is used
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — BLOCKING
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

REDUCED: technicians isolate the equipment before battle; AutoPTU resolves a conventional static encounter and the infrastructure result is written back afterward.

## Pump Hall Emergency

FULL: water-level or machinery state changes during battle, interactable controls and a REACH/ACTIVATE objective.

Primary blockers: terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions; AI tactical policy; adapter/playback; complete movement if water or machinery forces displacement.

REDUCED: secure the access route in a static battle, then resolve pump operations in the overworld.

## Factory Safe Shutdown

FULL: ordered machine-control objectives, changing facility state and tactical opposition that understands those objectives.

Primary blockers: objective semantics, AI tactical policy, interactable-object support, adapter/playback and any hazards/reactions used.

REDUCED: the shutdown is a recoverable overworld machine puzzle. Combat, if triggered, is a separate legal static battle.
