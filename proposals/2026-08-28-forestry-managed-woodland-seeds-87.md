# Ouros Forestry & Managed Woodland Seeds — Pass 87

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes a canonical Ouros settlement, institution, forest, law, species use, craft tradition or battle mechanic.

These candidates are designed to plug into `design/forestry-managed-woodland-harvest-restoration-extension.md` and existing Conservation, Workplaces, Material Culture, Safety, Travel, Weather/Crisis, Public Memory and Wild Ecology layers.

## The Path Cleared Twice

A forest path is blocked after two separate events close together. The first clearance record is complete. The second visible blockage looks almost identical.

The hook is not “clear another log.” Players reconstruct which timber belongs to which event, whether the route was fully reopened the first time, and whether a different route now makes more sense.

Possible consequences:

- the old route reopens again;
- a short reroute becomes permanent;
- some timber is deliberately retained;
- the path remains limited pending a safety review;
- the incident reveals that public signage still references an obsolete route.

## The Charcoal Batch Has the Right Maker, Wrong Patch

A workshop receives material associated with a known charcoal maker or equivalent craft operator, but provenance points to a woodland patch different from the one expected.

Nothing proves wrongdoing.

Possible explanations:

- the operator used an approved alternate source;
- the record references an old boundary name;
- material was consolidated with another batch;
- a supplier handoff was mislabeled;
- the claim remains unresolved.

This candidate exists to connect craft identity with exact source provenance without inventing a quality bonus or legal accusation.

## Fallen Timber, Standing Restriction

A storm produces abundant fallen wood in a zone where ordinary material collection is restricted or disputed.

Different actors see different priorities:

- route crews want limited clearance;
- a workshop sees urgently needed material;
- stewardship actors want some deadwood retained;
- residents want safe public access restored;
- researchers want the disturbance documented before removal.

The story is about sequencing and provenance. A successful route clearance does not automatically authorize material removal from the whole zone.

## The Grove People Call Empty

Visitors describe a woodland patch as empty because few Pokémon are visible during daytime visits.

Workers and monitoring records describe repeated activity at other times or in other micro-sites.

The player can compare:

- observation windows;
- trail-camera/photography evidence where available;
- direct sightings;
- work schedules;
- seasonal state;
- soundscape observations;
- recent access changes.

The outcome may simply be that the public label “empty grove” was based on a narrow observation window.

## The Sap Harvest Shifted the Problem

A woodland product collection area appears normal, while another nearby zone starts seeing repeated wild-Pokémon incursions into a work route or settlement edge.

The player investigates whether the two facts are connected.

No causal relationship is presumed.

Possible outcomes:

- evidence supports a connection and the intervention changes;
- a seasonal movement pattern explains the overlap instead;
- a third pressure is responsible;
- the evidence remains mixed.

This candidate is inspired only by the high-level displacement structure researched in Pass 87. It does not copy any external plot.

## The Trail That Became a Wildlife Route

A path historically used by workers is now repeatedly used by a wild collective after another part of the woodland changed.

The route itself is physically intact.

Possible responses:

- alter work hours;
- create a parallel footpath;
- use supervised windows;
- move a staging point;
- monitor before deciding;
- keep the route unchanged if evidence does not support intervention.

No battle is required.

## Old Cut Mark, New Damage

A tree or stump bears an old human work mark, but recent physical damage has appeared around it.

Residents assume the current crew caused it because the old mark is obvious.

Players can compare:

- dated photographs;
- work assignments;
- storm history;
- tool/equipment records;
- material batches;
- fresh versus weathered surfaces;
- witness observations.

The mystery can resolve as two separate events occupying the same physical object.

## The Restoration Patch Blocks the Shortcut

A restoration project closes an informal shortcut that residents have used for years.

The conflict is operational rather than moral.

Questions include:

- is the shortcut formally recognized anywhere;
- who actually depends on it;
- is the closure temporary;
- can another path absorb the traffic;
- is the restoration objective compatible with limited access;
- what happens to a nearby wild route if human traffic shifts elsewhere.

The same path can return later under a revised arrangement.

## The Work Crew Leaves One Log Deliberately

Players arrive after a route-clearance operation and find one large fallen log still beside or partly across the corridor.

People assume the job was unfinished.

The work record shows the remaining timber was deliberately retained for an authored ecological, erosion-control, boundary or staging reason.

The story teaches that visible “unfinished work” may be an intended state. The exact reason remains scenario-specific and must be backed by a real management record.

## The Storm Made More Wood Than the Workshop Can Use

A storm creates a sudden supply of recoverable material, but the local workshop has limited storage, labor or processing capacity.

The meaningful choices happen after the disturbance:

- what is left in place;
- what must be moved for safety/access;
- what can be stored;
- whether another institution wants material;
- whether hurried collection would damage provenance or restoration goals.

There is no assumption that “free material” has zero cost or no ecological consequence.

## The Species Count Changed After the Opening

A woodland intervention creates a new opening. Later observations report a different mix of Pokémon around it.

Players can help determine whether the change is:

- repeatable across observation windows;
- seasonal;
- related to the opening;
- related to a nearby route or event;
- too weak to interpret yet.

The extension records observations. Science/Conservation owns the conclusion.

## The Apprentice Learns the Boundary Map

A new worker or trainee repeatedly uses older local names for patches that no longer match current management maps.

The mismatch creates small operational confusion rather than incompetence.

A short arc can involve:

- learning historical names;
- reconciling old and current maps;
- updating handoff language;
- preserving older terminology in archives/public memory;
- avoiding a future intervention in the wrong zone.

No Skill bonus or progression reward is assumed.

## Mystery — Six Stumps, Four Recorded Cuts

Six apparently recent stumps are visible near a managed-use boundary, while current work records contain only four removal events.

Potential evidence:

- weathering differences;
- earlier superseded work records;
- storm-break evidence;
- material-batch provenance;
- route-clearance records;
- historical photographs;
- current boundary revisions.

Possible resolutions:

- two stumps predate the current records;
- two derive from a different approved project;
- the mapped boundary moved;
- documentation is incomplete;
- evidence remains insufficient.

The mystery must not force an illegal-cutting reveal.

## Encounter — Restoration Crew Boundary Conflict

Narrative premise:

A crew enters a restoration patch during a time when wild Pokémon have also begun using it as part of a route.

Full version:

- crew withdrawal;
- PROTECT/WITHDRAW or CLEAR_ROUTE intent;
- movement/interception pressure;
- wild tactical policy that values route/withdrawal where rules support it;
- reviewed zone effects only after terrain/hazard implementation;
- semantic Minecraft playback.

Reduced version:

All crew, tools, seedlings and noncombatants leave the grid before battle creation. The encounter uses fixed geometry or becomes a noncombat observation scene. A win may remove an immediate threat but does not establish ecological cause, authorize clearing or mark restoration complete.

## Noncombat encounter — The Missing Cut Mark

A work note and the visible forest disagree about where an intervention took place.

Players reconstruct provenance through:

- work records;
- maps and boundary revisions;
- photographs;
- material batches;
- storm observations;
- witness claims.

The scenario works entirely through world-state evidence and can finish with uncertainty.

## Long arc — A Working Forest Learns Its Boundaries

First visit:

The woodland is ordinary. Work, public travel and wild use already overlap in stable patterns.

Second visit:

A small discrepancy appears: a blocked trail, altered wildlife route, disputed material source or changed opening.

Third visit:

Players discover that several actors hold accurate but differently scoped information.

Fourth visit:

A route, harvest window, work sequence or restoration practice changes.

Later return:

The changed practice has both intended and unintended effects.

Later disturbance:

A storm, fire recovery phase, seasonal event or visitor-pressure change tests the new arrangement.

Long-term state:

The woodland acquires a new baseline. Old boundaries, paths and decisions remain in history rather than being erased.

## Potential actor archetypes

These are role templates only, not canonical NPCs or institutions:

- woodland steward who knows management history but does not automatically possess scientific authority;
- trail crew lead focused on keeping access workable;
- craft household/operator whose products carry source provenance;
- field ecologist tracking change over time;
- apprentice learning multiple generations of boundary terminology;
- material buyer who understands quality claims but not necessarily ecological context;
- local resident whose route knowledge predates current management maps;
- wild-Pokémon monitor whose evidence complicates a work schedule.

No archetype grants authority, Feature, Skill or legal power until canon/rules establish it.
