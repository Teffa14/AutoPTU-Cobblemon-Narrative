# Passive Acoustic Monitoring / Bioacoustics — Ouros Seeds 167

## 30 worldbuilding and quest candidates

### 1. The Recorder That Heard Nothing

A recorder returns with a complete, technically valid file and no target calls. The surrounding site was occupied during the same week according to another survey method. The useful mystery is detectability, not disappearance.

### 2. The Rare Call Was Real

A classifier repeatedly identifies a rare-call pattern. Human review agrees that the sound matches the reference. Later evidence suggests the source may have been a mimic rather than the presumed species. The detection remains authentic while source attribution changes.

### 3. The Marsh Interior Gap

A monitoring network has excellent coverage along boardwalks and roads but almost none in the center of a wetland because deployment is difficult. A new method finally samples the interior and changes the apparent regional pattern.

### 4. Three Recorders, One Chorus

Three devices log the same loud chorus within seconds. Early summaries count three events. A later linkage review concludes they probably captured one event from different positions.

### 5. The Clock That Drifted Overnight

Two stations appear to document a call moving impossibly fast across a valley. Timekeeping review finds one recorder clock drifting. The biological interpretation changes while every audio file remains untouched.

### 6. The Upgrade Broke the Trend

A new recorder detects high-frequency calls the old units rarely captured. The monitoring program improves, but the annual series can no longer be compared using raw detection totals.

### 7. Noibat Above the Human Band

Researchers hear almost nothing at an old cave listening site. Extended-frequency equipment records repeated patterns. The investigation asks what the signals represent without granting the party echolocation or revealing exact source locations.

### 8. The Loud Factory Year

A new industrial cycle overlaps with a long-running dawn monitoring window. Target calls decline in recordings. The open question is whether animals changed behavior, calls became masked, devices became less effective, or several factors interacted.

### 9. The Detector Loved Rain

A classifier starts producing many target hits during heavy rain. Review shows that a recurring rain-on-metal pattern shares acoustic features with the target profile. The model revision becomes part of the archive.

### 10. The Call Library Was Too Small

A local population uses a recurring variant that was absent from the original reference library. Old recordings contain the pattern but were never flagged. Reprocessing creates new candidate detections without inventing new historical audio.

### 11. The Silent Breeding Season

A monitored population produces fewer expected calls during one season. Visual and field-sign surveys still find evidence of site use. The program records a behavioral anomaly instead of declaring population collapse.

### 12. The Recorder Moved

A storm rotates or displaces a device without destroying it. The file continues recording. Later comparison needs a deployment revision because its acoustic context changed halfway through the season.

### 13. The Battery Died at the Worst Time

A recorder stops shortly before the expected migration window. The absence of data becomes an institutional problem rather than an ecological conclusion.

### 14. The Old Recording Under the New Model

A new classifier processes a decade-old archive and flags a previously unnoticed pattern. Experts can revisit the source audio, but the system retains both the old and new interpretations.

### 15. Chatot at the Rail Yard

A persistent Chatot population incorporates station sounds into local vocal repertoires. Automated monitoring of both wildlife and infrastructure begins producing cross-domain false positives.

### 16. One Call, Two Species Hypotheses

Two specialists agree on the sound structure but disagree about the source. Both interpretations remain linked to the same recording until field evidence resolves or narrows the question.

### 17. The Hydrophone With a Perfect File

An underwater station stops transmitting, but after physical recovery its local storage is intact. Communications failure and scientific data loss turn out to be separate issues.

### 18. The Missing Night Window

A firmware revision accidentally records only daytime hours for several weeks. The program does not backfill or fabricate the missing nocturnal survey effort.

### 19. The Quiet Zone Worked Differently

A quiet-zone policy reduces human noise, but the target calls do not increase. Other acoustic profiles become easier to detect. The intervention can still alter monitoring quality without producing the expected ecological response.

### 20. The Festival Detection Spike

A festival week creates dozens of target-like detections from instruments, loudspeakers, costumes and visiting Pokémon. The resulting review becomes a test of detector robustness rather than a wildlife emergency.

### 21. The Accessible Site Bias

A region’s “best monitored habitat” turns out to mean “habitat closest to roads.” A later network expansion reveals how much institutional knowledge had been shaped by access logistics.

### 22. The Recorder in the Nesting Buffer

A previously acceptable listening site becomes sensitive after a nesting area shifts. Research Ethics and Conservation require a relocation even though moving the device reduces comparability with earlier years.

### 23. The Device Became a Landmark

A long-running recorder station becomes locally recognizable and appears in photographs, school trips and trail directions. Its scientific role and its public-memory role accumulate separately.

### 24. The Volunteer Review Disagreement

Community reviewers classify a large batch of calls differently from an automated model. Expert review finds that both approaches perform well under different acoustic conditions.

### 25. The Same Pokémon, Maybe

A distinctive call recurs over several seasons. Researchers suspect one persistent individual but lack sufficient evidence for a `pokemon_entity_id` linkage. Chronicle stores the hypothesis without creating identity.

### 26. The Detector Version Nobody Wrote Down

A historical dataset cannot be fully reproduced because the exact classifier revision used on one year of recordings is missing. The raw files survive, allowing reprocessing but not perfect reconstruction of the original workflow.

### 27. The Morning Shift Moved

Calls that historically peaked near dawn appear progressively later. Diel Activity, Climate and Soundscapes can investigate timing together. The acoustic program records the pattern without deciding the cause.

### 28. The Recorder Array Outlived the Project

Funding or institutional priorities end, but several devices and a valuable archive remain. A museum, university, community group or successor program may inherit stewardship without inheriting the original research question.

### 29. The Famous Silence

A widely discussed recording is famous because almost nothing happens in it during a historically noisy place and time. Decades later it becomes a baseline for comparing infrastructure and ecological change.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Station Nine

The recorder worked. The schedule was correct. No unusual call, failure, theft, storm or revelation occurred. The result is retained because ordinary years make later anomalies interpretable.

## Long arc A — Five Years at Chorus Marsh

Year one establishes a small recorder network and a provisional call library. Year two exposes access bias because most devices sit along the boardwalk. Year three introduces a classifier revision and a cluster of false positives. Year four expands coverage into difficult wetland interior. Year five finally allows the institution to reinterpret the entire series with explicit effort and method vintages.

The arc can connect Soundscapes, Community Science, Diel Activity, Migration, Wetlands, Research Ethics and Public Memory. It does not require a villain. Success means better knowledge with preserved uncertainty.

## Long arc B — The Call That Changed Species Three Times

A distinctive archived call is initially assigned to one known species. Later observations suggest a second source. A documented mimic then becomes plausible. Years afterward, new field evidence may resolve the source or leave it uncertain.

Every scientific revision changes the label attached to the recording. The original waveform, timestamp and location remain unchanged. The arc is useful for Taxonomy, Archives and Science because it demonstrates how interpretation can evolve without retconning history.

## Long arc C — The Listening Network Becomes Infrastructure

A handful of temporary recorders gradually become a regional monitoring network. Stations acquire maintenance schedules, technicians, access agreements, public dashboards, privacy rules and archival value. Some sites close; others move; one becomes a community landmark. A later generation inherits the network but changes its research priorities.

The arc produces institutional history, jobs, maintenance hooks and long-term environmental evidence without turning the network into magical surveillance.

## Encounter concept — Recorder Array Retrieval After Storm

FULL: technicians and PCs retrieve several devices across storm-altered terrain while wildlife can withdraw and paths can change. Required families include complete movement, environment/hazards when the storm modifies the arena, AI tactical policy for non-hostile objectives and Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback. Standard battle targeting, base movement, calculations, initiative and legal-action generation can use VERIFIED infrastructure; lifecycle, damage, statuses, Move behavior, Abilities, Items and Trainer Features remain PARTIAL whenever invoked.

REDUCED: world state resolves the storm route, technicians, wildlife and recorder positions before combat. Any confrontation uses a stable static arena. Scientific validity resolves after the fight.

## Encounter concept — Ultrasonic Cave Survey

FULL: an extended-frequency recorder logs patterns people cannot hear directly. Researchers move between stations and wildlife may avoid contact. If actual combat uses a Sonic Move or Ability, that exact effect must be validated separately. Complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback remain BLOCKING. Environment is BLOCKING when cave conditions have tactical effects.

REDUCED: survey readings and wildlife movement resolve outside AutoPTU. A later battle uses ordinary static geometry and only legal combat effects possessed by actual combatants.

## Encounter concept — Hydrophone Mooring Recovery

FULL: a submerged listening station must be physically recovered. Dynamic current, underwater traversal, debris and withdrawal would depend on complete movement plus terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, with tactical AI and adapter/playback also BLOCKING.

REDUCED: Freshwater or Maritime world state handles access and recovery. A separate battle may occur on a validated static arena. The missing signal remains an evidence problem, not a combat mechanic.

## Non-combat investigation — Rare Call Alert

An automated detector repeatedly flags a rare-call pattern. Players inspect raw recordings, deployment history, environmental context and possible mimicry. The outcome can be `VALID_PATTERN_SOURCE_UNRESOLVED`. No battle is required and no capture/spawn reward follows automatically.
