# Pass 94 Proposals — Kelp Forests, Seagrass Meadows & Submerged Vegetation

Status: NON-CANON WORLD-BUILDING CANDIDATES.

These ideas may be promoted only after regional canon, PTU/Caelo rules and implementation dependencies are reviewed.

## 1. The Transect That Went Quiet

A monitoring line that reported every month stops transmitting after a storm. The first expedition expects destroyed equipment and instead finds that some markers remain intact while the kelp canopy around them has changed unevenly.

## 2. The Meadow That Looks Recovered

A seagrass restoration site appears lush from shore. Underwater surveys find strong adult cover but almost no young recruitment, leaving the institution unsure whether the project has truly recovered.

## 3. Skrelp After the Storm

Several Skrelp appear far from their usual survey area after severe weather. Researchers must determine whether this is one displacement event, a new route, or a previously missed population.

## 4. The Wreck Under Green Water

A wreck that was easy to map years ago is now buried inside thick vegetation. Dhelmise sightings occur nearby, but nobody has enough evidence to say whether the same individual is involved in every report.

## 5. The Barren Nobody Dated

A diver reports a wide grazer-dominated patch and blames the last storm. An archive photograph reveals that the barren was already present years earlier.

## 6. The Juvenile Year

A fishery suddenly reports unusually high juvenile observations near a seagrass meadow. The increase may reflect habitat use, survey effort, movement from another area or a genuine recruitment event.

## 7. The Floating Forest Fragment

A large detached kelp mat leaves the coast and becomes temporary open-ocean habitat. Different Pokémon use it during its drift, turning one storm event into a moving ecological story.

## 8. The Harbor Meadow

A small seagrass bed survives inside a working harbor. Expansion proposals can improve vessel flow but may alter light, sediment and access around the bed.

## 9. The Old Anchor Line

Boat anchors repeatedly scar the same shallow meadow. A mooring project solves one pressure but changes where visitors concentrate instead.

## 10. The Wrong Grazer

Residents blame one visible Pokémon species for kelp decline. Long-term observations show that grazing pressure increased before that species became common.

## 11. The Missing Canopy Shadow

A coastal photographer notices that a familiar dark patch at the surface no longer appears in morning images. The cause remains open until underwater surveys are completed.

## 12. The Clearer Water Problem

Water clarity improves after a major project, but vegetation recovery remains slow. The missing factor may be substrate, grazing, temperature, seed supply or something not yet measured.

## 13. The Seagrass Map From Before the Marina

An old survey shows a bed extending beneath what is now a marina. The map is historically correct and becomes evidence in a debate about what changed and when.

## 14. The Kelp School Route

A recurring group of juvenile Pokémon uses the edge of a kelp forest during one season. The route is observed repeatedly but remains ecological behavior, not a guaranteed spawn lane.

## 15. The Restoration Tags

Storm debris moves several restoration markers. The plants may still be alive, but provenance between original cohorts and surviving patches becomes uncertain.

## 16. The Understory Nobody Counts

Surface canopy looks sparse, so locals assume the forest is collapsing. Divers find a dense understory and strong recruitment below the visible surface.

## 17. The Canopy That Came Back Too Fast

Kelp returns rapidly after a disturbance. Researchers debate whether this is full recovery, short-lived regrowth or a temporary response before another shift.

## 18. The Dhelmise Photograph

A famous underwater photograph appears to show the same Dhelmise seen at another wreck years earlier. The visual similarity becomes an identity hypothesis, not an automatic entity merge.

## 19. The Meadow Beside the Outfall

A seagrass bed near an outfall changes gradually. Sampling must distinguish water-quality effects from sediment, temperature, vessel disturbance and natural variability.

## 20. The Storm-Wrack Nursery

Detached vegetation accumulates in a sheltered cove and temporarily attracts many small Pokémon. The habitat disappears naturally weeks later, but the event remains in Chronicle.

## 21. The Researcher Who Counts From Shore

One institution maps surface canopy from boats while another performs diver transects. Their reports disagree because they are measuring different habitat layers.

## 22. The Kelp Road

A narrow channel through dense vegetation becomes a routine route for local swimmers and research teams. After canopy expansion, the same route remains known but no longer easy to use.

## 23. The Seagrass Seed Cache

A restoration group discovers stored seed material with incomplete provenance. The material may still be usable, but it cannot be treated as equivalent to a fully documented cohort.

## 24. The Quiet Holdfast Zone

Midwater fish observations remain normal while seafloor invertebrate surveys decline. The forest can look stable at one layer while changing at another.

## 25. The Visitor Buoy Experiment

A popular dive site adds fixed mooring buoys to reduce anchoring damage. The project succeeds physically but redirects crowds toward another fragile patch.

## 26. The Former Partner in the Kelp

A previously released Pokémon is observed months later inside a kelp forest. The record proves presence, not why it came, whether it lives there permanently, or whether it wants renewed partnership.

## 27. The Reef-Kelp Boundary

A rocky reef and kelp forest overlap spatially. Separate monitoring programs initially describe the same area differently because one tracks reef condition and the other canopy structure.

## 28. The Meadow After the Bloom

A major algal bloom ends, but seagrass does not immediately return. The recovery story spans water quality, light, sediment and restoration rather than ending with the bloom.

## 29. The Fisher Who Noticed First

A fisher reports changed juvenile distribution months before formal surveys detect a canopy shift. The observation becomes evidence with provenance, not automatic proof of cause.

## 30. The Submerged Vegetation Atlas

A regional program compiles decades of canopy maps, seagrass extents, storm records, restoration cohorts, Pokémon observations and photographs. Every edition preserves what was known at that time.

# Long Arc A — Five Storms at Greenwater Bay

Year 1: baseline kelp and seagrass maps are assembled from old surveys.

Year 2: a storm removes canopy in exposed sectors but leaves sheltered beds mostly intact.

Year 3: fisheries and ecology teams report different changes in juvenile presence.

Year 4: a small restoration trial begins while grazer and predator observations continue.

Year 5: another storm tests whether earlier recovery was resilient or temporary.

The arc has no guaranteed villain or final boss. The value comes from cumulative evidence, competing interpretations and visible environmental history.

# Long Arc B — The Harbor Meadow Years

A working harbor contains a surviving seagrass meadow. Over several years the settlement adds moorings, changes ferry schedules, improves stormwater treatment and redesigns a waterfront. Each change may affect the meadow differently.

Players can contribute surveys, public consultation, route planning, restoration, fisheries data or visitor management. The story remains unresolved enough that future generations can inherit the same site.

# Long Arc C — From Barren to Forest

A grazer-dominated rocky area becomes the focus of a long restoration experiment. Some interventions fail, others produce temporary canopy, and one nearby untreated area recovers unexpectedly.

The important mystery is not “which monster caused the barren?” It is which combination of predators, grazers, temperature, storms, recruitment and human activity explains the different trajectories.

# Promotion guardrails

Do not promote any seed to canon until the region, habitat identity, responsible institutions and species associations are authored.

Do not add:

- automatic Stealth or cover from kelp;
- Stuck or entanglement from seaweed;
- current or wave forced movement;
- Poisoned from decaying vegetation;
- healing from seagrass;
- Water/Grass Terrain from visuals;
- nursery spawn bonuses;
- rare-spawn rewards for restoration;
- Dhelmise spawning from wreck debris;
- grazer morality;
- automatic restoration success.
