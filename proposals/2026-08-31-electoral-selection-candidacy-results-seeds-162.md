# Electoral Selection, Candidacy & Results Seeds — Pass 162

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. None of these places, offices, candidates, procedures or outcomes exist in Ouros unless separately promoted.
Date: 2026-08-31

These seeds assume only that a future canon location may have a locally authored electoral selection rule. They never establish a universal political model.

## The Candidate Who Won Before the Last Ferry Arrived

A remote island reports later than the mainland. Public boards show a candidate leading strongly before the final district result is available. Residents later remember that the candidate “had already won.”

The mystery is temporal, not conspiratorial. Players can reconstruct which totals were available at each hour and when the result actually reached its confirmation threshold.

Useful systems: Electoral Selection, Maritime Ferry, Communications Network, Media, Public Memory, Archives.

## The Poster Still Says Candidate

A candidate withdraws after thousands of posters have already been printed and some were distributed to remote settlements. Months later an old poster becomes supposed evidence that the candidacy remained active.

The paper is authentic. Its implied current state is obsolete.

Useful systems: Electoral Selection, Material Culture, Public Notices, Media, Archives.

## Five Times Someone Said “Everyone Voted for Her”

Five witnesses mean five different things:

- everyone at a campaign meeting applauded;
- a supporter club endorsed her;
- a local poll strongly favored her;
- one small district returned a unanimous tally under its local rule;
- the final aggregate result selected her by a clear margin.

The investigation resolves scope and provenance rather than deciding who lied.

## The Election With Two Correct Turnout Numbers

Two official-looking reports list different turnout. One uses the eligibility snapshot at registration close. Another uses a later canon-approved updated eligibility pool. Both calculations are internally correct.

The quest is to identify the denominator before accusing anyone of falsification.

## The Endorsement Nobody Controlled

A respected retired Trainer endorses a candidate. Supporters immediately describe an entire old fan community as backing the same person, but the group has no rule that lets one figure speak for every member.

The endorsement remains real. Group support remains unresolved.

## The Debate Nobody Broadcast

A scheduled debate occurs after a communications outage prevents the expected live feed. The candidates, audience and local reporters experience the event. Most of the region receives only later summaries, clips and conflicting quotations.

Years later people disagree over who “won” the debate while the electoral result itself remains fully documented.

Useful systems: Performance, Broadcast, Communications Network, Media, Personal Records, Public Memory.

## The Recount That Changed Nothing

A local rule triggers a recount after a narrow preliminary result. The final totals remain the same.

The narrative value is procedural memory. Some supporters later retell the recount as proof that the process was invalid; others retell it as proof that the original count was perfect. The Chronicle preserves the narrower fact: a recount occurred and produced the recorded outcome.

## The Acting Holder During the Election

An office is temporarily covered by an acting holder while a scheduled selection proceeds. Public signs display the acting holder's name. Campaign posters display candidates. Older correspondence still carries the prior permanent holder's signature.

All three identities are valid in their own contexts.

Civic Office owns the acting mandate. Electoral Selection owns the campaign. Archives preserve older records.

## The Candidate Who Opposed the Project They Later Inherited

During the campaign, a candidate argues against a public project. By the time the confirmed result becomes effective, contracts or physical work already exist under prior valid decisions.

The new holder does not automatically cancel the project. Civic Governance and the project owner determine whether a valid review route exists.

This seed is useful for showing institutional continuity without making political disagreement meaningless.

## The Single-Candidate Vote

A small institution has one confirmed candidate under its local rule, but participation still occurs because the governing procedure requires a valid selection event rather than automatic appointment.

Do not assume what “yes,” abstention, threshold or failure means. Those details must exist in the local rule before the seed can be promoted.

## The Ballot With the Old Place Name

Ballot materials use an address or settlement label that was correct when the process opened. A place-name update becomes effective before voting closes.

The old label can remain procedurally valid if the governing rule says so. Place-Name/Address owns location identity lineage; the election layer records which reference the ballot actually used.

## The Candidate Who Changes Affiliation

A candidate's public association changes during the campaign. Older endorsements and media reports remain historically correct. New ballot labeling follows only the rules actually in force.

The system must not rewrite prior appearances to match the later affiliation.

## The Result Announced in One Settlement First

A communications failure means the confirming authority posts the final result locally before another settlement receives the message. People in both places can honestly remember different times when “the result became known.”

`RESULT_CONFIRMED_AT_AUTHORITY != RESULT_RECEIVED_EVERYWHERE`.

## The Quiet Election

No scandal, violence or conspiracy occurs. Candidates campaign on ordinary local priorities. Voting happens. The result is confirmed. The outgoing holder completes routine handoff.

The purpose is to establish a normal baseline that future disputed or emergency transitions can meaningfully contrast against.

## The Former Candidate Who Stayed Useful

A losing candidate remains a conservation volunteer, coach, shop owner, researcher or committee participant. Their civic relevance changes without disappearing.

`NOT_SELECTED != NPC_REMOVED_FROM_WORLD`.

## The Campaign Office Becomes Something Else

A temporary campaign storefront closes after the process. Months later the same room is rented by a clinic outreach desk, hobby club, repair cooperative or ordinary merchant.

Environmental storytelling can preserve faded mounting holes, an old notice in a storage box or archived photographs without freezing the building into permanent political scenery.

## Long arc — Three Elections, One Bridge

A bridge project begins as a technical proposal before the first selection. One holder approves studies. A later campaign debates cost, route and ecological impact. Construction begins under another holder. A third selection happens during implementation.

Each campaign can describe the bridge differently, but the actual proposal, contracts, surveys, construction phases and inspections remain owned by their specialist systems.

The payoff arrives years later when residents attribute the bridge to different leaders depending on which stage they remember. The Chronicle can answer who proposed, approved, funded, built, inspected and opened it without forcing one “true owner” of the achievement.

## Long arc — The First Election Nobody Remembers the Same Way

A settlement preserves posters, newspaper summaries, personal diaries, one incomplete broadcast archive and formal result records from an early electoral process.

Later generations compress nomination, campaign, preliminary count and confirmation into one remembered night. Players investigating the anniversary discover that several famous stories happened on different dates.

No recovered document changes the official result unless the governing review route says it can.

## Long arc — The Office Changes, the Town Continues

The campaign follows ordinary workers through three office-holder episodes. A transit clerk, librarian, Gym staffer, courier and conservation ranger keep doing their jobs while different holders enter and leave office.

Some policies change. Many routines continue. Pending projects cross transitions. Former candidates remain neighbors.

The arc makes political change visible without turning every institution into a personal extension of one leader.

## NPC archetypes

Returning Officer / Process Clerk: remembers deadlines, versions and procedural provenance. Their competence does not make them omniscient about campaign motives.

Campaign Organizer: coordinates appearances, volunteers and messaging. They cannot manufacture actual votes through narrative fiat.

Local Reporter: tracks claims and preliminary results while distinguishing official and unofficial sources.

Forum Host: creates a place where candidates answer questions. Hosting does not grant authority to decide who performed best.

Archive Specialist: reconstructs old procedure records without treating archives as perfect or complete.

Former Candidate: loses one selection but retains profession, relationships and possible future relevance.

All archetypes are NON-CANON until attached to approved locations and institutions.

## Encounter concept — Polling Place Access Perimeter

Full intent: an unrelated wild or hostile incident blocks safe approach while the process is active. Rich play could include protected access, staged evacuation, Intercept/forced movement and tactical route-control AI.

Full dependency profile: targeting/LoS VERIFIED; base movement VERIFIED; complete movement PARTIAL; calculations VERIFIED; action economy VERIFIED; lifecycle PARTIAL; damage/status/content families PARTIAL; hazards/zones/reactions BLOCKING if active; legal-action infrastructure VERIFIED; tactical policy BLOCKING; adapter/playback BLOCKING.

Reduced version: pause voting before initiative, secure ballots and staff outside BattleSpec, use static geometry and explicit combatants. AutoPTU may return only `IMMEDIATE_POLLING_PLACE_APPROACH_CLEAR`. Ouros separately decides whether and when voting resumes.

## Encounter concept — Ballot Transport Chokepoint

Full intent: an authorized container travels between sites while an incident threatens the route. Rich semantics require escort/object carrying, complete movement, lifecycle, tactical protect/withdraw policy and playback.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: freeze custody and container location before initiative. Keep the container outside tactical ownership. Resolve a static route battle. The only permitted output is `IMMEDIATE_BALLOT_ROUTE_CLEAR`. Courier and Electoral layers handle later transport.

## Encounter concept — Count Center Evacuation Perimeter

Full intent: staff must leave or secure a count site while a current threat develops. Rich semantics require civilian withdrawal, protected areas, lifecycle, possibly hazards/reactions and objective-aware AI.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: pause counting and secure people/material outside BattleSpec. Run an ordinary audited encounter at a static perimeter. The battle cannot add, remove, validate or recount votes.

## Encounter concept — Campaign Event Incident Separation

Full intent: a public appearance is interrupted by a current-world tactical problem. Rich crowd routing and protect/withdraw behavior remain blocked.

Reduced version: adjourn the event before initiative. Remove crowd, candidates and noncombatants unless Ouros explicitly selected a legal combatant for independent reasons. A standard battle resolves the immediate safety problem only.

Hard consequence boundaries:

`BATTLE_WON != ELECTORAL_SUPPORT`

`BATTLE_LOST != CANDIDACY_ENDED`

`CANDIDATE_PRESENT != COMBATANT`

`CROWD_VISIBLE != ELECTORATE`

## Noncombat scenario — Result Reconciliation Session

This can run now without battle implementation.

Players compare:

- election-process version;
- authorized option list;
- voting-window timestamps;
- aggregate intake/count records;
- preliminary reports;
- discrepancy notes;
- confirmation records;
- communications delivery times;
- archived public notices.

The solution may be that two apparently conflicting public reports were valid at different stages. The generator should prefer provenance reconstruction over surprise fraud unless evidence actually supports misconduct.
