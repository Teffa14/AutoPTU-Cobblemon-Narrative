# Ouros Road Vehicle / Fleet Narrative Seeds — Pass 142

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Nothing in this file establishes Ouros geography, institutions, technologies, laws, vehicle prevalence or named characters.
Date: 2026-08-30

These seeds assume only that a future canon location may contain one or more road vehicles or fleet-like shared assets. Replace all institutions, technologies and roles with canon-approved equivalents before use.

## The Bus With Yesterday's Fleet Number

A replacement vehicle arrives bearing a fleet number that local riders associate with another bus retired years ago. Older route photos, workshop sheets and current service records appear to conflict.

The resolution can be mundane: the institution reused the number after retirement, while historical records continued to refer correctly to the earlier vehicle.

Persistent outputs can include a corrected archive index, an old photograph newly identified, and recurring locals who distinguish the vehicles by paint details rather than number.

## The Replacement Vehicle Arrived Before the Notice

A regular service vehicle develops a condition concern before morning departure. Operations assign a spare immediately, while the public information system updates later.

For twelve minutes, witnesses can truthfully report that the replacement was already at the stop while the board still showed the original vehicle.

The scenario trains the world to preserve assignment time, observation time and publication time separately.

## The Repair Was Finished, the Release Was Not

Workshop staff finish the requested physical work late in the day. The vehicle remains unavailable because the required verification/release step has not occurred.

A passenger who sees the vehicle driving slowly inside the yard assumes service has resumed. It has not.

No corruption is required. Physical movement inside a controlled site and authorization for public operation answer different questions.

## One Van, Three Operators

A durable service van has passed between three institutions over decades. Each repainted it and assigned a different local identifier.

A current investigation into an old photograph initially appears to show three separate vehicles. Small persistent features—body repair, window configuration, an old mounting point—support a linkage to one physical asset.

Ownership history remains separate from who operated the vehicle on any specific day.

## The Taxi Livery Changed, the Vehicle Did Not

A local transport operator rebrands after a merger, contract change or community redesign. Several vehicles keep old colors for months.

Travelers begin using paint color as a misleading shorthand for operator identity.

The useful consequence is social memory: locals may call the same vehicle “the blue one” years after it becomes green.

## The Vehicle Retired From Service but Still Runs

A former passenger vehicle is removed from public service but remains mechanically capable of low-risk private movement. It later becomes a community room, club shelter, mobile stall, field classroom or preserved historical object if canon supports the use.

Seeing it move later does not mean retirement records were false.

`RETIRED_FROM_ROLE != PHYSICALLY_IMMOBILE`.

## The Spare Was Already Assigned Elsewhere

A service loses its primary vehicle and requests the usual spare. Dispatch discovers that the spare is already supporting an event, field team or neighboring institution.

The problem becomes a resource-allocation story rather than an automatic cancellation.

Possible outcomes include a delayed run, another substitute, a revised route pattern or no service that day. No choice is predetermined by this seed.

## The Inspection Record Is Current; the Defect Is New

A vehicle passed its last applicable inspection. A new symptom appears the following morning.

A character insists that “it was inspected” while another insists that “something is wrong now.” Both can be correct.

The mystery resolves through temporal scope rather than blame.

## The Wrong Vehicle Got the Route Card

Two visually similar vehicles sit beside each other in a depot. A current route card is placed on the vehicle that is not assigned to the run.

The display is wrong while the service roster is right.

This can create a small passenger confusion scene, a courier mistake, or an archive photograph whose caption becomes misleading years later.

## The Pokémon Waits Beside the Old Maintenance Bay

A recurring Pokémon associated with a mechanic, driver or depot routine continues waiting beside the bay where a familiar vehicle used to park.

The vehicle has moved to another depot or retired. The Pokémon’s routine persists for a while before adapting.

This is environmental storytelling only. The behavior does not prove ownership, employment, consent or mechanical capability.

## The Temporary Replacement Became the Familiar One

A substitute vehicle stays on a route for several weeks because the original repair takes longer than expected. Regular passengers start recognizing the substitute’s driver, noises, seating quirks and nearby Pokémon routines.

When the original returns, the temporary asset has already accumulated local social history.

The service continues, but its community associations have changed.

## The Old Number Is Under the Paint

Maintenance removes a damaged panel or old paint layer and reveals a former fleet identifier.

The discovery can link a present vehicle to an earlier operator or depot without implying theft or concealment. Repainting may have been completely routine.

The old identifier becomes a provenance clue for Archives or Material Culture.

## The Workshop Closed Before the Vehicle Did

A long-lived vehicle outlasts the maintenance facility that once supported it. Later repairs occur at another location, while old records remain split between institutions.

Future investigators can encounter two histories that overlap imperfectly.

This seed can connect Facility Maintenance, Place Reference, Archives and transport operations without merging their authority.

## The Vehicle Was Available, the Driver Was Not

A serviceable road asset sits ready in the depot, but the assigned qualified person is unavailable.

Public observers see the vehicle and assume the service should run.

The incident demonstrates a dependency boundary:

`VEHICLE_SERVICEABLE != CREW_AVAILABLE`.

## The Driver Was Ready, the Vehicle Was Not

The inverse occurs on another day. Staff are present and the road is open, but the vehicle is restricted pending assessment.

The repeated contrast helps players learn that visible staff, visible vehicle and operational service are three different facts.

## The Road Reopened Before the Fleet Recovered

A storm, landslide or infrastructure problem closes a road and damages or strands several service vehicles. Road owners reopen the connection first.

Passenger service remains limited because fleet availability, maintenance and positioning recover on a different timeline.

`ROUTE_OPEN != SERVICE_NORMAL`.

## The Service Returned Before the Original Vehicle

A substitute restores the route while the original remains under repair for weeks.

Public notices correctly say service is restored even though depot staff correctly say the original vehicle is still unavailable.

This can become a long-term callback when the original finally returns.

## The Vehicle Has Two Home Depots

Older records list one home depot. Current operations list another. A temporary relocation period between them appears in neither simplified directory.

The complete chronology reveals three valid operational states at different times.

This seed works especially well after a facility closure or regional restructuring.

## The Defect Was Reported Twice

A driver and a passenger independently report the same symptom using different language. Operations create two initial records before recognizing the relationship.

Merging the reports should preserve both provenance chains instead of deleting one as a duplicate.

The first reporter does not automatically have the more accurate diagnosis.

## The Vehicle With No Public Number

A support vehicle works regularly around a depot but carries no number the public recognizes. Staff refer to it by workshop nickname, asset record and parking bay.

A player trying to identify it must use context rather than assuming every road vehicle participates in one universal registry.

## Mystery: Five Times the Vehicle Was “In Service”

Five records use the phrase “in service” differently:

- admitted to the fleet;
- technically serviceable;
- assigned to a route;
- physically operating that morning;
- carrying passengers at the observation time.

The mystery asks players to recover the intended meaning and timestamp of each record.

No document needs to be false.

## Mystery: Three Fleet Numbers, Two Vehicles

A depot history contains fleet numbers 12, 27 and 12 again.

The first 12 belongs to an older vehicle. Number 27 belongs to another. Years later the number 12 is reused after the first vehicle is gone.

A photograph without date information remains ambiguous until other evidence narrows the period.

Possible final state: `ACCEPTED_AMBIGUITY` if the evidence never becomes sufficient.

## Mystery: The Bus That Was “Repaired Twice”

Two maintenance records appear to describe duplicate repair of the same defect.

One repaired the immediate symptom. A later episode addressed the underlying cause after recurrence, or the second record may belong to another vehicle under a reused identifier.

The seed should not predetermine which explanation is true.

## Exploration: The Old Depot Yard

A former depot can be explored after its transport role ends.

Environmental evidence may include faded bay numbers, route-card brackets, repair pits, paint layers, charging/fueling infrastructure if canon supports it, workshop markings, abandoned waiting areas and new occupants using the space differently.

The exploration uses Place Reference, Material Culture, Archives and static world geometry. It does not require vehicle-combat mechanics.

## Exploration: The Roadside Lay-By With Four Eras

One roadside stopping place preserves traces of several transport eras: an old shelter foundation, newer signs, a relocated pull-in area and a current temporary stop.

Players can reconstruct how vehicles and services changed while the road itself remained recognizable.

## Long arc: A Town Learns Which Vehicle Is Coming

Phase one establishes ordinary service. Residents recognize individual vehicles through sound, paint, drivers, recurring Pokémon and routine parking positions.

Phase two introduces small substitutions: one inspection, one maintenance delay, one event deployment. Each change is recorded independently and has a narrow public consequence.

Phase three creates overlapping pressure. The primary vehicle is restricted, the spare is already assigned elsewhere, and a workshop dependency delays release. The service owner must choose among limited patterns, borrowing an asset, delaying runs or suspending some trips according to canon-authorized options.

Phase four resolves the immediate pressure without resetting the world. A borrowed vehicle may remain socially memorable. A fleet number may change. A depot bay may acquire a new routine. The original vehicle can return later under a revised role.

Months afterward, the town remembers vehicles as characters in local history without anthropomorphizing them or replacing their human/Pokémon crews.

## Encounter: Roadside Inspection Withdrawal

Full intended version:

An inspection is interrupted by a threat. Rich play could require protecting withdrawing personnel, managing roadside zones and resolving Intercept or displacement near the road edge.

Dependency profile: same permanent families as Depot Exit Perimeter; rich semantics remain blocked by complete-movement incompleteness, terrain/zones/reactions, tactical policy and adapter support.

Reduced version: READY.

Traffic/service is stopped before BattleSpec. Inspector, driver, records and tools withdraw. The vehicle is static scenery and receives no invented combat HP. Victory yields `IMMEDIATE_ROADSIDE_WORK_AREA_CLEAR`; inspection continues afterward.

`TACTICAL_VICTORY != INSPECTION_PASSED`.

## Encounter: Substitute Vehicle Loading Bay Chokepoint

Full intended version:

A substitute is already selected, but a tactical threat blocks the loading bay. Rich implementation could include shifting boarding zones, coordinated withdrawal, moving vehicle geometry and objective-aware AI.

Dependency profile: full rich version remains blocked by complete movement, terrain/zones/reactions, tactical policy and semantic adapter/playback.

Reduced version: READY.

Passengers remain outside BattleSpec. Original and substitute states freeze. No boarding occurs during combat. AutoPTU resolves a conventional static fight. Victory yields `IMMEDIATE_LOADING_BAY_CLEAR`; Road Passenger Transport resumes or revises the run afterward.

`LOADING_BAY_CLEAR != BOARDING_AUTHORIZED`.

`SUBSTITUTE_PRESENT != SUBSTITUTE_ASSIGNED`.

## Encounter: Retired Vehicle Yard Perimeter

Narrative premise:

A physically surviving retired vehicle sits in a yard or adaptive-reuse site where an independent threat creates a temporary access problem.

Full version could tempt designers to give the old vehicle collision, cover or destructible-object mechanics. Do not do this without exact contracts.

Reduced version: READY.

Treat the vehicle as protected static environmental geometry if reviewed geometry supports it, or keep it entirely outside the combat footprint. Victory clears the immediate perimeter only.

`TACTICAL_DAMAGE != VEHICLE_DAMAGE` unless a verified future mechanic explicitly bridges them.
