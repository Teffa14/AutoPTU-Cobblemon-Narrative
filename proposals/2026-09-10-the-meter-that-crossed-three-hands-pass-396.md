# The Meter That Crossed Three Hands — Pass 396

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON

## Premise

A small group of field candidates performs a routine certification survey using one shared instrument. The kit is checked out to the first candidate, handed to a second at a field station, transferred again during the return leg, and eventually returned to storage.

Later, an environmental change makes one old reading unexpectedly important. The result conflicts with a newer observation. Investigators need to know which physical kit produced the old reading, who held it at that semantic time, whether the handoff happened before or after the measurement, and which actors actually know those facts.

The discrepancy does not establish sabotage, incompetence or deception. Calibration drift, environmental change, transcription error, wrong timing, an incomplete handoff record or a genuinely meaningful anomaly all remain hypotheses until supported.

## Character and faction pressure

The certification evaluator cares about procedure, documentation and stewardship as well as technical outcome. A candidate who finished the route successfully can still have made a poor handoff. Another candidate can be blamed publicly despite never holding the instrument at the disputed time. A depot worker may know the return state but not the field sequence. A researcher may know why the reading matters but have no direct knowledge of custody.

This creates rivalries and institutional pressure without requiring a villain. The same event can affect certification, trust, future access to shared equipment and the credibility of the environmental investigation.

All institution names, candidate identities, locations and regional consequences remain unresolved.

## Reduced version

The full narrative premise can run without AutoPTU.

Use semantic time, `WorldResource`, reservations, explicit holder transitions, handoff history, private knowledge, communication, observations, travel and ordinary world replanning. The environmental change occurs between scenes. Players reconstruct the sequence, interview holders, inspect records and decide whether a repeat survey is necessary.

No battle Item effect is assigned to the instrument. No tactical weather, forced movement, reaction, status or Trainer Feature is required.

## Mechanically rich version

A repeat survey occurs while conditions are deteriorating. The team must reach measurement points, protect the instrument, choose whether to finish data collection or assist another field party, then withdraw safely.

Potential full-version elements and exact dependencies:

- line-of-sight measurement positions or ranged protection: targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- ordinary navigation around blocked cells: base movement legality;
- dragging a hurt teammate, push/pull, knockback, interception or forced reposition: complete movement;
- ordinary damage/stat resolution: core calculations;
- timed order of trainer/Pokémon actions: action economy/initiative;
- multi-phase storm or countdown windows: full turn/round lifecycle;
- persistent damage consequences: full stateful damage pipeline;
- complex exposure, injury or delayed conditions: status lifecycle;
- rain, flooding, wind zones, unstable footing, visibility changes, hazards or reactions: terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- any named Move used for environmental interaction: move-specific behavior;
- Ability-triggered weather/terrain or entry effects: abilities;
- any mechanical use of the survey kit as battle gear: items;
- interrupts or specialist bonuses from a Trainer Feature: Trainer Features/perks;
- choosing only legal combat actions: AI legal-action infrastructure;
- prioritizing rescue, protecting the instrument, finishing measurements, abandoning a point or withdrawing after the objective: AI tactical policy;
- stable objective markers, pickup/handoff acknowledgement, environmental playback and authoritative non-KO completion: Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

## Long-term arc hooks

If later canon supports it, the disputed reading can connect to a broader environmental arc without making the original candidates central heroes. A series of mundane records from different dates can reveal a changing habitat, infrastructure problem or migration pressure.

The certification rivalry can also recur independently. One candidate may become unusually strict about documentation. Another may resent being blamed by people who had incomplete records. Their relationship can evolve after the truth becomes clearer without forcing reconciliation.
