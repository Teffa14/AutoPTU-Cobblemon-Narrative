# Pass 130 — Libraries, Lending, and Public Knowledge Seeds

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-08-23

These concepts derive from the Pass 130 research scan. They are candidates, not established Ouros lore. No institution, settlement, rule, event, Pokémon relationship, or supernatural claim below becomes canon by inclusion here.

## 30 candidate concepts

### 1. Two Editions, One Valley

A field guide used by older Rangers shows a path and species names that no longer match the newest edition. Both books are legitimate. The older copy becomes valuable because it explains how the valley was understood before a bridge, taxonomy revision, and migration shift.

### 2. The Book That Was Never Missing

A locally famous history is reported stolen. Inventory review shows it was mis-shelved during a building renovation and later cataloged under a translated title. No theft occurred. The incident exposes weaknesses in catalog reconciliation rather than a criminal network.

### 3. Last Stop Before Snow

A mobile library makes its final mountain visit before a seasonal road closure. Several residents have holds waiting, while one requested atlas is delayed at a rail junction. The story is about access timing and route dependency, with no requirement for conflict.

### 4. Notes in Copy Seventeen

One ordinary field manual contains decades of handwritten route observations from several unknown readers. The printed work is common; this copy is not. Researchers must separate the publisher's text from copy-specific annotations before treating any note as evidence.

### 5. The Pressed Specimen

A dried plant is found between the pages of a returned botany guide. The object may be scientifically interesting, but its location in a library copy does not establish where or when it was collected. The sample is handed to Science/Material Culture with its uncertain provenance intact.

### 6. The Branch After the Flood

A neighborhood branch reopens gradually after water damage. Some books are replaced, some copies are stabilized, some services move upstairs, and a temporary reading room remains elsewhere for months. The building's recovery and the collection's recovery proceed at different speeds.

### 7. The Return Slot of the Old Station

A station library closed years ago after rail redevelopment, but an old exterior return box remained. A book is found inside decades later. The question is not “who stole it?” but which library system still owns the copy and how its long absence should enter Chronicle.

### 8. Courtyard Regular

The same wild Pokémon is observed repeatedly in a quiet library courtyard during certain hours. Staff and patrons know the individual informally. Its presence does not make it a library Pokémon, and its eventual absence should not automatically become a missing-Pokémon quest.

### 9. The Chatot Phrase

A Chatot repeatedly imitates a phrase commonly heard in a reading room. Later another Chatot in the area begins using a similar phrase. Social Learning may eventually study transmission, but the library does not infer comprehension, authorship, or a hidden message.

### 10. The Catalog Kiosk Rumor

A Rotom begins appearing around one public catalog terminal. Intermittent catalog failures predate the sightings. The Pokémon may interact with the device, but Digital Systems must establish whether it caused any outage; species flavor does not make Rotom a network administrator.

### 11. Donation Without Provenance

A donor leaves a box of old regional maps. Several are ordinary reprints, one is a reproduction, and one may be an original survey copy. The library pauses that item's circulation and requests provenance assessment rather than silently accessioning everything as rare material.

### 12. The Reference Answer That Prevented a Quest

A rumor claims an old quarry road has reopened and leads to a newly exposed ruin. A reference worker finds that the report reused a historical place name and actually describes a different route. The correct outcome is a corrected notice and no expedition.

### 13. The Corner Reading Circle

An informal weekly reading group gradually gains regular participants, a noticeboard, and a shared list of requested books. It may eventually become a club, remain informal for years, or dissolve naturally. Library hosting never implies the institution owns or endorses the group.

### 14. The Redacted Nesting Atlas

A conservation atlas is publicly available but omits precise coordinates for a sensitive nesting site. A full version exists under scoped research access. The redaction is ordinary stewardship, not evidence that the institution is hiding a supernatural location.

### 15. The Accessible Edition Arrived First

A newly translated, accessible-format edition reaches a remote branch before the standard print copies because it travels through a different sharing network. The event exposes how “availability” depends on format, route, language, and service agreements rather than one global inventory.

### 16. Three Libraries, Three Names

Three institutions catalog the same historical site under different legitimate names from different periods. Identity/Language layers resolve that the records refer to one location. None of the catalogs needs to be erased; aliases and historical labels remain searchable.

### 17. The Binder's Repair

A common field guide becomes locally distinctive after a repair workshop uses a recognizable binding technique. Years later, that physical repair helps identify the same copy in a photograph. No mechanical bonus comes from better binding.

### 18. Both Theories on the Shelf

A library retains an older ecological monograph alongside a newer study that disputes it. A public argument claims the older book should have disappeared once disproven. The institution instead preserves access while catalog metadata clearly records dates and later criticism.

### 19. The Atlas in Transit

A research team needs a specific oversized atlas available only through resource sharing. The request is approved, but the copy is physically delayed by a route closure. Research can continue with partial alternatives rather than freezing the story until a parcel arrives.

### 20. The Route That Became a Library Route

A mobile service begins as a temporary response to a branch closure. Years later the route persists because small settlements value the service even after the building reopens. Travel, Rail, Postal, and Library schedules slowly co-evolve.

### 21. One Surviving Copy

A fire destroys most copies of a locally printed booklet. One circulation copy survives because it was checked out. The work remains bibliographically ordinary, but that individual copy gains historical importance and may eventually leave normal circulation for preservation.

### 22. The Library of Things

A branch experiments with lending non-book resources such as basic field kits or educational instruments. Supply Chains and Metrology remain authoritative for inventory and calibration. Borrowing an instrument does not grant the skill to use it correctly.

### 23. Reference Only

A rare atlas is available to anyone in a supervised reading room but cannot leave the building. A visitor publicly calls this “secret access.” The library can demonstrate that access is broad while physical circulation is restricted for preservation reasons.

### 24. Returned After Thirty Years

A person returns a book borrowed decades earlier. The institution accepts it without turning the event into a moral trial. The copy carries old stamps, repair marks, and an edition no longer used for current field advice, making its history more interesting than any penalty.

### 25. Stacks Under Renovation

A building renovation moves entire subject areas into temporary storage. The catalog says the library holds the material, but access is slower and some items require staff retrieval. “Owned” and “available right now” remain distinct.

### 26. Catalog Down, Collection Present

A digital catalog outage prevents normal search and hold processing. The shelves and books still exist. Experienced staff use local knowledge and printed finding aids for limited service while Digital Systems resolves the outage.

### 27. The Taxonomy Shelf Split

A Pokémon species was reclassified after several influential field guides were published. Some shelves group older works under the historical name while the catalog links them to the current concept. Taxonomy owns the scientific revision; Library owns discoverability across editions.

### 28. The Children's Map With Old Names

A beloved children's geography book remains widely read despite using district and station names that changed years ago. Rather than withdrawing every copy, a new edition adds modern names while the old one becomes a useful record of public geography.

### 29. The Reading Room During the Storm

A branch becomes an informal information/shelter point during a major weather event. Emergency Services owns dispatch and response. Library staff provide public information and a known indoor place without becoming emergency responders by profession.

### 30. The Quiet Year

Nothing dramatic happens at a regional library for an entire year. Books circulate. A mobile route runs. A few translations arrive. Two programs end. One new reading group starts. Chronicle records normal institutional continuity instead of forcing a crisis because the system needs content.

## Long-form arc A — Five Editions of the Meridian Field Guide

Year 1: players encounter a widely used field guide. Its maps and species names match current institutional knowledge.

Year 2: a river channel changes and one route entry becomes outdated. The guide remains common in circulation.

Year 3: Taxonomy changes the accepted classification of one population. A corrected edition appears, while the old edition remains on shelves and in homes.

Year 4: a translation is updated using the new terminology, but one remote branch still has only the earlier translated edition. A mobile library carries comparison copies between settlements.

Year 5: researchers discover that a handwritten note in one old circulation copy documented the changed population earlier than the published literature. The copy becomes significant without making every other old edition rare.

Possible outcomes:

- copy moves to a special collection while digital access is created;
- annotation remains uncertain and is published as a research question;
- library mounts a small exhibit through Museums/Public Memory;
- the new edition cites the older observational history;
- no supernatural revelation is required.

## Long-form arc B — The Library Road

Phase 1: several remote settlements depend on a single mobile route.

Phase 2: a rail line opens. Some stops become easier to serve, while others become less direct because population and schedules shift.

Phase 3: a small branch opens near one station. Mobile service shrinks but continues beyond the rail corridor.

Phase 4: wireless catalogs make holdings easier to discover, increasing resource-sharing demand rather than eliminating physical delivery.

Phase 5: severe weather interrupts both road and rail. Earlier investments in distributed deposits, local reading rooms, and cached catalogs determine how much access remains.

The arc is about service adaptation, not technological replacement. New infrastructure changes which layer does the work.

## Long-form arc C — One Copy, Many Hands

A specific field manual begins as an ordinary circulation copy.

Over years it accumulates:

- a repair;
- a route annotation;
- a pressed specimen with unknown collector;
- water damage from a known flood;
- an interlibrary-loan trip;
- a corrected place-name note;
- a photograph showing it in an expedition camp;
- eventual promotion out of ordinary circulation.

Its `copy_id` never changes. The work and edition remain ordinary. The individual object becomes historically meaningful because Chronicle can prove where this copy was and how people used it.

## Encounter contract A — Flooded Reading Annex Evacuation

FULL intention:

A lower annex is threatened by rising water while staff, patrons, and some wild Pokémon need routes out. Collection carts are obstacles or objectives only where their placement is explicitly authored.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED for conventional attacks;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING for mobile civilians, route protection, displacement;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL if phase/delayed effects are used;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for rising-water tactical state;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for `EVACUATE`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_ROUTE`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED:

Resolve flood stage and evacuation in world state. Remove civilians and unique collection items from the grid. Freeze a dry legal battle area. Resolve combat normally. Apply collection-condition and reopening consequences afterward.

## Encounter contract B — Mobile Library Chokepoint

FULL intention:

A mobile library unit is blocked on a route while service staff and perhaps waiting patrons are nearby. The purpose is restoring passage, not assigning HP to the vehicle or books.

Required capability families:

- complete movement/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING for escort/route-clearing actors;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_VEHICLE`;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING for semantic route objectives;
- environment category only when a validated hazard is actually authored.

REDUCED:

Secure the vehicle and staff outside the grid. If confrontation remains, run a static chokepoint battle. Travel/Road/Rail and Library state determine when service resumes afterward.

## Encounter contract C — Reading Courtyard Wildlife Intrusion

FULL intention:

A recurring wild group enters a crowded courtyard. The best outcome may be to create a withdrawal route rather than defeat every Pokémon.

Required capability families:

- complete movement — BLOCKING;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for `WITHDRAW`, `REACH_EXIT`, `AVOID_CIVILIANS`;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED:

Evacuate patrons in world state and give wildlife a non-combat withdrawal resolution. Only open AutoPTU if an independent confrontation remains.

## Non-combat contract D — Reference Desk Misinformation

No battle dependencies.

A circulating warning claims a newly dangerous ruin has appeared. Reference comparison finds an old map edition and obsolete place name. The warning describes an event already documented and resolved years earlier. The player can correct the public notice without receiving a mandatory exploration quest.

## Implementation gates

Do not implement library scenes by giving books combat stats, scanning Minecraft shelves as the authoritative catalog, or encoding factual truth inside item flavor text.

Before any mechanically rich library encounter reaches production:

1. choose whether the premise actually needs battle;
2. if yes, freeze the world-state snapshot first;
3. keep patrons, librarians, holdings, circulation, and institutional decisions outside AutoPTU unless they are genuine combatants;
4. list exact capability dependencies;
5. use the reduced version while complete movement, tactical AI, dynamic environment, or adapter playback remain blocking.

## Canon decisions required before promotion

- Which library systems exist at campaign start?
- Is ordinary borrowing available to visitors or only registered local patrons?
- Which resources circulate?
- What privacy applies to circulation history?
- Are mobile libraries common or region-specific?
- What publishing/printing technology exists?
- Which languages and accessible formats are available?
- Can player-founded settlements establish public libraries?
- Which resources are sensitive enough for supervised/restricted access?
- Which copies already have historical significance before player arrival?
- How is digital access represented without duplicating physical unique items?

Until answered, all concepts above remain proposals.
