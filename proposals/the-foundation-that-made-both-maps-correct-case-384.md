# The Foundation That Made Both Maps Correct — case 384

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Canon effect: NONE

## Premise

Two field maps of the same old structure disagree.

The older map shows one clean rectangular room. The newer map shows a wall line that breaks the rectangle and seems to place part of the room outside its own foundation.

At first this looks like a bad survey, a falsified record or a continuity error.

A later site revision exposes a lower foundation. The upper room was built across an earlier alignment. Both maps were accurate for the physical state visible when each survey was made.

The mystery changes from “which map is wrong?” to “what changed, which construction phase is each map describing, and who knew that before the new exposure?”

## Evidence structure

Revision one records the site before the lower foundation is visible.

Observation A records the upper room alignment under revision one.

Interpretation A says the visible room appears to be one construction phase. This is a reasonable actor claim, not world truth.

Revision two records an explicit physical change that exposes the lower foundation.

Observation B records the newly visible misalignment under revision two.

Interpretation B can refine the earlier theory: the visible room likely reuses an older foundation or occupies a later construction phase.

The earlier observation remains unchanged.

## Reduced playable form

The reduced version can run without AutoPTU.

The player or an NPC reaches the site through ordinary world travel, inspects several fixed evidence refs, accesses one historical map through an explicit archive/communication path and records a later interpretation.

Progress comes from linking records. The player does not need to defeat anything to solve the central question.

A useful consequence is social rather than tactical: an NPC previously blamed for a bad survey can be partially vindicated, while a second question opens about whether anyone intentionally concealed the lower structure.

## Rich encounter form

A mechanically richer version can place documentation under time pressure while access conditions worsen. Objectives can include marking the exposed alignment, protecting a fragile evidence point, escorting a specialist, recovering survey equipment and withdrawing after enough evidence is recorded.

Combat or wild-Pokémon pressure may occur, but success is based on the evidence objective rather than a mandatory KO sweep.
