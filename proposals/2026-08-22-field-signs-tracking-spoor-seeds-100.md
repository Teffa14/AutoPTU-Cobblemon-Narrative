# Pass 100 Proposals — Field Signs, Tracking & Spoor

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Nothing in this file becomes established Ouros lore without explicit promotion.

These seeds are designed to use the systems in `design/field-signs-tracking-spoor-layer.md` while preserving uncertainty, Pokémon agency and PTU/Caelo mechanical boundaries.

## 1. The Last Tracks at Cedar Ford

A service Pokémon fails to arrive at its normal destination after a storm. Tracks near a ford look recent, but one branch may predate the weather event. The useful question is not “which monster attacked it?” but which evidence still has a trustworthy time relationship.

## 2. The False Rare Trail

A conservation volunteer reports tracks from a species not documented in the valley for years. Measurements fit imperfectly. The players can compare substrate, camera coverage, archived prints and local common species before anyone announces a rediscovery.

## 3. Scent Lost at the Market

A Pokémon with a mechanically verified Tracker capability follows a known reference scent toward a busy market. The trail becomes ambiguous among deliveries, crowds and multiple exits. The next step may be interviews, camera records or parcel logs rather than a chase.

## 4. Three Prints, One Night

Three separate trackways cross the same wetland trail overnight. One is a resident individual, one is a group route and one is a distorted older print enlarged by water. The research value lies in disentangling them.

## 5. The Feather on the Fence

A distinctive feather caught on a fence is initially used to claim a rare species crossed the road. Later analysis shows the feather could have been transported by wind or another Pokémon. The crossing claim remains open.

## 6. The Scratched Orchard Trees

Several orchard trees develop new rub/scratch marks. Farmers suspect crop damage. Ecologists suspect repeated route use. A former partner Pokémon is rumored to be responsible, but no identity can be assigned from the marks alone.

## 7. Old Prints Under New Snow

A brief thaw exposes an older layer of tracks beneath fresh snow. The route is historically interesting but irrelevant to the current missing-Pokémon search unless timestamps can be separated.

## 8. The Trail That Ends at Rail

A trackway reaches a station service yard and stops where hard surfaces erase footprints. Rail schedules, cameras and custody records become the next evidence sources. Tracking hands off cleanly to other Ouros systems.

## 9. Rub Tree Archive

Researchers discover that the same group of trees has carried fur, scratches and scent marks for many years. The site becomes a long-term ecological record rather than a spawn point.

## 10. The Empty Burrow Entrance

Fresh soil surrounds a burrow entrance, but no Pokémon is present. The sign supports recent activity, not current occupancy, ownership or aggression.

## 11. Wrong Foot, Right Route

A novice misidentifies a track species but correctly reconstructs the direction of travel. The mistake is preserved in the research history and later corrected without invalidating the route observation.

## 12. The Camera That Disagrees

Tracks suggest two individuals passed through. The camera shows one. Possible explanations include camera blind spots, old sign, overlapping routes or misclassification. The system accepts uncertainty instead of forcing a hidden second Pokémon.

## 13. After the Festival

A park contains hundreds of mixed human and Pokémon traces after a festival. A rare-looking sign is nearly impossible to interpret because sampling conditions are poor. Public excitement outruns evidence.

## 14. The Drag Mark Case

A long disturbed line through vegetation opens a case because witnesses call it evidence of an attack. Field examination finds multiple plausible causes. No victim or offender is inferred until corroborating evidence exists.

## 15. No Tracks at the Waterhole

A species expected at a drought refuge leaves no detectable sign during a survey. The ground is hard and wind-scoured. The correct record is nondetection under low sign-preservation conditions, not absence.

## 16. The Repeated Bed Site

Flattened vegetation appears in the same sheltered place over several weeks. The site can be recorded as repeated rest use without inferring a den, nest, ownership or emotional attachment.

## 17. The Released Partner's Trail

A former partner Pokémon was released years ago. A trackway resembles the species near a place it once visited. Players may investigate, but the system does not turn nostalgia into identity confirmation.

## 18. The Hair Sample Mix-Up

Two hair samples collected from neighboring signs were mislabeled during a rushed survey. The chain-of-custody error becomes visible when later results conflict. The correction preserves both the original mistake and the revised interpretation.

## 19. The Trail Through the Burn Scar

Post-wildfire surveys find a new repeated route through an unburned pocket. This supports habitat-use research and recovery planning, but does not imply the same individuals use it every time.

## 20. Footprints in the Construction Zone

Workers find tracks inside a future road corridor. The prints may support a crossing assessment. They do not automatically stop construction or prove a protected population; governance and conservation systems decide what follows.

## 21. The Scent Reference Problem

A handler has an old blanket believed to belong to a missing Pokémon. The custody history is uncertain. Before using it as a reference sample, the group must establish whether it can reasonably support a tracking attempt.

## 22. The Trail That Crosses Itself

A group route loops back over itself and creates the impression of multiple Pokémon. Trackway interpretation must account for sequence and direction instead of counting prints as individuals.

## 23. The Night Shift Tracks

Workers repeatedly find signs near a facility only during night shifts. Diel Activity and Workplaces can explain the pattern without assuming sabotage or secret ownership.

## 24. The Tide-Erased Route

Intertidal signs are visible only during a narrow low-tide window and vanish after the next tide. The observation remains in Chronicle after physical evidence is erased.

## 25. The Sample That Identifies Nobody

A preserved biological trace confirms species but cannot distinguish individuals. The result resolves one question while leaving the identity question open.

## 26. The Wrong Animal on the Poster

A visitor center publishes an illustrated track guide based on an old classification. New research revises one sign. Archives and Public Memory preserve both the old guide and the correction.

## 27. The Trail Behind the Clinic

Repeated prints behind a clinic generate rumors about a wild Pokémon seeking treatment. The only established fact is repeated route use near the building. Care and Pokémon Agency require more evidence before inferring intent.

## 28. The Crossing Used Once

A new wildlife underpass records one clear trackway. The project team celebrates, but Road Ecology correctly treats this as evidence of one use, not restored connectivity.

## 29. The Lost Surveyor's Markers

A field team disappears from schedule during a long survey. Their own bootprints, survey flags and Pokémon signs overlap. Tracking becomes a mixed human/Pokémon evidence problem and a rescue route rather than a battle hunt.

## 30. The Field-Sign Atlas

An institution builds a regional archive of footprints, feathers, rub marks, burrows, photographs and substrate notes. Years later, the value comes from comparing old and new distributions, not from turning the atlas into exact spawn coordinates.

# Long Arc A — Five Years on the Same Trail

Year 1: a repeated trackway is documented along an informal forest route.

Year 2: road works disturb part of the substrate and detections decline.

Year 3: camera coverage suggests the route shifted, but sign evidence remains ambiguous.

Year 4: a restoration crossing opens and only occasional tracks appear.

Year 5: repeated evidence supports a changed route pattern, while the original trackway remains preserved as historical state.

The arc connects Road Ecology, Cartography, Conservation, Photography and Wild Collectives without requiring the same Pokémon to remain alive or visible for five years.

# Long Arc B — The Missing Partner Case

A known Pokémon fails to return from an institutional task. The case begins with a last direct sighting, then a verified reference scent, an uncertain trackway, a transport disruption and later a biological trace. Several clues point in different directions because they were created at different times.

Possible endings include recovery, voluntary relocation, transfer to another caretaker, an unresolved trail or evidence that the Pokémon left the expected route before the incident everyone initially blamed.

No ending requires combat.

# Long Arc C — The Spoor Archive

A small field station begins preserving sign observations from ordinary surveys. Decades of in-world data gradually reveal changes in route use, seasonality, introduced populations, restoration outcomes and former habitats. The archive also contains old mistakes, poor sampling and superseded classifications.

Players can improve the archive without ever turning it into omniscient world truth.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Last Tracks at Cedar Ford

FULL:

- degradable overworld track segments;
- verified scent mechanic when available;
- target may continue moving;
- optional rescue/withdraw objective;
- water/ford state can matter tactically only through verified environment mechanics;
- AI understands escape/protection goals.

REDUCED:

- tracking resolves before battle;
- trackway is an overworld evidence object;
- the destination site is selected from evidence, not a live chase;
- AutoPTU receives a static ford map if combat occurs;
- recovery occurs after the battle or without battle.

Permanent capability dependencies:

VERIFIED foundations: targeting/footprints/range/LoS; base movement legality; core calculations; action economy/initiative; AI legal-action infrastructure.

PARTIAL when exact content is invoked: full lifecycle; full stateful damage; status lifecycle; move-specific behavior; abilities; items; Trainer Features/perks.

BLOCKING for FULL: complete movement/interception/forced movement; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if the ford changes tactics; AI tactical policy; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback; overworld sign/scent system.

# Encounter Contract 2 — The False Trail

FULL:

- track classification;
- sample/camera corroboration;
- road-crossing state;
- possible autonomous wildlife withdrawal.

REDUCED:

- entirely noncombat investigation is valid;
- sign observations remain evidence;
- later camera/sample results may return INCONCLUSIVE;
- no Pokémon is spawned just because the players followed a track.

BLOCKING for FULL tactical wildlife withdrawal: complete movement and AI tactical policy.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Scent Lost at the Market

FULL:

- exact PTU/Caelo Tracker implementation;
- known scent reference;
- overworld scent ambiguity/degradation;
- crowd/access graph;
- autonomous target route;
- optional pursuit transition.

REDUCED:

- if Tracker is mechanically unavailable, scent exists only as an authored clue;
- the trail reaches the market and becomes inconclusive;
- witnesses, cameras, postal records and transport logs become the next sources;
- any later battle is separate from the tracking resolution.

BLOCKING for FULL: exact Tracker contract; overworld scent state; complete movement/interception for chase behavior; AI tactical policy; Minecraft playback.

# Non-canon boundaries

No tracking institution, ranger guild, rare-species trail, famous tracker, missing Pokémon or regional field-sign tradition is established here.

No Pokémon receives Tracker, better Perception, scent abilities or tracking instinct because of real-world animal analogies.

No spoor grants Accuracy, initiative, capture chance, surprise, damage, movement or spawn bonuses.

No sample creates ownership, cloning rights, breeding rights, diagnosis or individual identity without another validated system.
