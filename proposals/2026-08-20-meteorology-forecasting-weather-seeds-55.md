# Ouros Meteorology, Forecasting & Weather Seeds — Pass 55

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros material derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character, institution or protected prose is reproduced.

## Design intent

These candidates use weather as persistent world state, information and planning pressure. Most are designed to remain interesting before weather becomes a crisis and without requiring a battle.

## Candidate 01 — The Station That Always Reads High

A mountain station has reported warmer conditions than nearby stations for several seasons. Nobody knows whether the difference reflects a real microclimate, sensor placement, calibration drift or changes around the building.

The useful content is comparison, maintenance, historical records and site inspection. There is no default saboteur.

## Candidate 02 — Forecast Revision at the Ferry Office

A ferry operator delays departure because of an early storm forecast. A later update moves the storm track away from the route, creating pressure to reverse the decision before passengers and cargo are redistributed elsewhere.

The story is about acting under uncertainty and the cost of changing plans twice.

## Candidate 03 — Three Valleys, One Rain Band

A moving rain system crosses three connected valleys at different times. Players can observe the same system through farming, travel, ecology and local reporting rather than receiving three unrelated weather rolls.

## Candidate 04 — The Missing Sensor Window

One remote station stops transmitting during the most important six hours of an approaching weather system. Nearby observations still exist, but confidence drops.

The repair mission matters because it improves future knowledge, not because the sensor is secretly a superweapon.

## Candidate 05 — Fog on Market Morning

A recurring coastal fog reduces transport confidence during an important weekly market. Residents disagree about whether the current year has become worse or people merely remember old fog differently.

Historical observations, harbor logs and local memory can be compared.

## Candidate 06 — Castform Field Notes

A research team has years of observations from one individual Castform. Its reactions correlate with some local weather transitions but fail during several unusual events.

The team studies the pattern without assuming Forecast is perfect prediction.

## Candidate 07 — The Festival Rain Plan

A major outdoor festival has a fully developed wet-weather alternative that has never been used. A borderline forecast forces organizers to decide when to activate it.

Either venue can succeed. The narrative consequence comes from logistics, public communication and institutional memory.

## Candidate 08 — The Forecast Everyone Misquoted

A forecast states that heavy rain is possible in the western district. Public retellings gradually convert that into “the western district will flood.”

The event connects meteorology with media provenance and rumor propagation.

## Candidate 09 — Lightning Without a Battle Effect

A dramatic electrical storm crosses an exposed plateau. Lightning is visible and audible, but no authored tactical lightning mechanic exists.

The environment can remain visually intense while AutoPTU receives ordinary legal battle state unless a future rules contract explicitly supports more.

## Candidate 10 — A Farmer's Better Forecast

A farmer repeatedly predicts short local showers more accurately than the regional bulletin because of detailed knowledge of one hillside.

The institution investigates that local pattern rather than declaring folk observation superior or inferior by default.

## Candidate 11 — The Empty Gauge

A rain gauge is found empty after a night when everyone nearby remembers heavy rain. Possible explanations include blocked collection, wrong timestamp, localized conditions or memory error.

The gauge does not automatically prove the public wrong.

## Candidate 12 — Two Forecast Models

Two institutions use different methods and produce different five-day outlooks. Both have legitimate evidence and known weaknesses.

Players can choose planning strategies without the system secretly designating one group as incompetent.

## Candidate 13 — The Route That Dries First

After storms, one upland path consistently becomes usable earlier than nearby roads. That repeated pattern becomes part of transport planning and later cartographic knowledge.

## Candidate 14 — Harbor Wind Shift

A normal wind shift changes which side of a harbor can safely handle loading. The event affects schedules and labor without becoming a region-scale emergency.

## Candidate 15 — Forecast Archive Gap

A weather institution has meticulous records except for one historical season. Later public claims about that year's famous storm rely heavily on memory and newspapers.

The gap connects meteorology, archives and public memory.

## Candidate 16 — The False Calm

A dangerous system is forecast to arrive, but the morning is clear. Some actors begin cancelling preparations because they interpret the calm as evidence that the forecast failed.

The plot concerns timing uncertainty and decision discipline.

## Candidate 17 — Mountain Visibility Window

A research expedition needs a short visibility window to survey a distant ridge. The forecast offers a plausible window but not a guarantee.

Success can come from preparation and observation rather than combat.

## Candidate 18 — A Station Inside a Growing Town

A station that once stood in open terrain is now surrounded by buildings. Its long record remains valuable, but the site itself has changed.

The institution must decide whether to relocate it, preserve continuity or operate a second station.

## Candidate 19 — Weather-Sensitive Wildlife Shift

Observers notice that one wild collective changes its activity several hours before some rain systems. The pattern is interesting but inconsistent.

Ouros records a biological observation, not a supernatural forecast mechanic.

## Candidate 20 — The Broken Public Board

The regional forecast is accurate, but one settlement's public board has not updated for two days because communications failed.

The information problem is downstream of the meteorology problem.

## Candidate 21 — Storm Photography Rush

A rare cloud formation draws photographers to an exposed overlook. Conservation staff and route managers become concerned about crowding before the weather itself becomes dangerous.

## Candidate 22 — Calibration Day

A station is taken offline for scheduled calibration during a period of unusual conditions. Public criticism follows because people assume the outage means neglect.

Players can help compare backup observations and explain the operational tradeoff.

## Candidate 23 — Rainfall Contest

A science club runs a seasonal forecast challenge using transparent rules and later compares predictions with observed totals.

The reward should be social/research recognition unless PTU/Caelo explicitly provides mechanical progression.

## Candidate 24 — The Storm That Split

A forecast treats one system as a single front. It later divides, producing moderate impacts in two different areas rather than one severe impact.

The archived forecast remains useful evidence about what forecasters knew before the split.

## Candidate 25 — Weather-Control Rumor

Repeated unusual weather near an old facility produces rumors that the site controls the climate. The system preserves the rumor while investigators separately assess natural, technical, Pokémon and observational explanations.

## Candidate 26 — The Unreliable Ridge Relay

A mountain relay transmits weather observations only intermittently. Engineers, meteorologists and Rangers all care for different reasons.

Repairing it improves communication coverage and forecast confidence but does not change the weather.

## Candidate 27 — Dry Season Without Drought

A region enters its normal dry phase. New visitors interpret the brown landscape as an emergency because they do not know the local cycle.

This creates tourism, education and public-information content without a crisis object.

## Candidate 28 — The Weather Window Contract

A construction crew has a narrow forecast-dependent window to complete exposed work. A delay would be expensive but safe; rushing could create new problems.

No weather damage is generated unless the actual mechanics support it.

## Candidate 29 — After the Famous Miss

Years ago, the regional forecast badly missed a storm. The institution changed procedures afterward, but public jokes still reference the failure.

A new uncertain forecast tests whether institutional learning has actually improved decision-making.

## Candidate 30 — The New Station Network

Several settlements cooperate to build a shared observation network. Each station solves a different coverage gap, and the project gradually improves route planning, agricultural timing, crisis preparation and scientific understanding.

The arc can take multiple world years without requiring a villain.

# Longer Arc A — The Year of Bad Forecasts

A region experiences a sequence of forecast misses. The first appears to be ordinary uncertainty. The second exposes a station outage. The third reveals that several microclimates have changed relative to the older model baseline.

The arc can connect:

- instrument maintenance;
- urban development;
- ecological observations;
- archived climate records;
- public trust;
- media corrections;
- seasonal anomalies;
- eventual model revision.

The final answer does not need to be sabotage or Legendary interference.

# Longer Arc B — Weather Across the Archipelago

Several islands depend on ferries and small stations. A regional observation network is incomplete, so forecasts are strong near major ports and weak in remote channels.

Over multiple expeditions, players can help establish temporary stations, improve communications, map local wind/fog patterns and build mutual-aid procedures. Later storms use that accumulated state.

# Longer Arc C — The Castform Project

A research institution begins a multi-year study of weather-sensitive Pokémon. Different individuals show different observable patterns. Some correlate with local weather, some with temperature, some with human routines and some with no obvious variable.

The arc is about careful observation and avoiding premature conclusions. It must never convert Castform into a perfect weather oracle or weather-control device without authored canon.
