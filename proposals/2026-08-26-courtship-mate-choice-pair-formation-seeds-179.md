# Pass 179 Proposals — Courtship, Mate Choice & Pair Formation

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-08-26

These are candidates for future Ouros canon. They must not be treated as established populations, institutions, species behaviors, relationships or PTU mechanics until separately approved.

## Design objective

Use reproductive-social behavior to create recurring wild individuals, seasonal callbacks, public traditions, scientific uncertainty and ecological consequences without turning the world into a breeding simulator.

Every concept below preserves these boundaries:

- display does not prove mating;
- pair association does not prove parentage;
- a missed season does not prove pair dissolution;
- PTU Infatuation remains separate;
- Trainer ownership never extends to a wild partner or potential offspring;
- Minecraft behavior is presentation, not authority.

## Thirty worldbuilding candidates

### 1. The Display Court Outlived the Pair

A small clearing has been used for elaborate evening displays for decades. The two persistent Pokémon first associated with the site disappear from the record, but later individuals continue using the same physical court.

Use: long-term site history, Social Learning question, vegetation management conflict.

### 2. One Display, Three Explanations

Researchers, local residents and a visiting behavior specialist disagree whether a recurring dawn display is courtship, pair maintenance or territorial signaling. All three interpretations remain plausible after the first season.

Use: scientific disagreement without villainy.

### 3. The Pair Was Never Exclusive

Two individuals were repeatedly observed together for three seasons, leading the public to narrate them as a permanent pair. A later season shows one participant repeatedly associating with another individual.

Use: public memory versus bounded behavioral evidence.

### 4. The Interrupted Approach

A recurring approach sequence stops halfway whenever a new freight line passes nearby. The public interprets this as “rejection,” while observers notice that both Pokémon leave immediately after the vibration and noise begin.

Use: Soundscapes, Rail, behavior interpretation.

### 5. Illumise Night Without a Pattern

A local Volbeat/Illumise-like population is authored to perform visible nocturnal displays, but one year the usual coordinated pattern does not appear. Individuals are still present and active.

Use: festival uncertainty without automatically implying population collapse or breeding failure.

### 6. The Display Everyone Photographs

A rare seasonal display becomes a photography attraction. Image volume increases dramatically while actual observation coverage becomes more biased toward one accessible viewing area.

Use: Photography, Community Science, Conservation.

### 7. The Wrong Audience

A Pokémon repeatedly performs a conspicuous display when another species is nearby. Initial observers assume cross-species courtship; later evidence supports social signaling or resource competition instead.

Use: anti-anthropomorphic mystery.

### 8. The Quiet Partner

One member of a recurring pair-like association performs elaborate displays. The other shows little obvious reciprocal behavior but repeatedly remains nearby and returns across seasons.

Use: prevent “no display = no association” assumptions.

### 9. The Former Partner at the Display Site

A released former Trainer partner establishes a wild home range that overlaps a traditional display site. It participates in a local display season years later.

Use: Pokémon Agency, Release, Spatial Ecology; never restore Trainer authority automatically.

### 10. The Festival Is Early, the Pokémon Are Not

A civic festival remains fixed to an old calendar date while the observed display window gradually shifts with local phenology.

Use: Seasonality, Climate, Public Memory, Tourism.

### 11. The Display Site Becomes a Road Verge

Urban expansion reaches a long-used display site. The behavior continues for several years in a narrowing strip before shifting to another place.

Use: Architecture, Road Ecology, Spatial Ecology.

### 12. The Object Presentation Mystery

An individual repeatedly places visually distinctive objects in the same display area. The behavior could be courtship, site decoration, play or tool-associated behavior.

Use: Material Culture, Cognition, Play, unresolved function.

### 13. The Pair That Only Meets in Winter

Two persistent Pokémon with different annual ranges repeatedly associate during one narrow seasonal window, then separate normally for the rest of the year.

Use: site fidelity and seasonal pair association without permanent co-residence.

### 14. The New Signal After the Blackout

A population’s nighttime display changes after years of artificial lighting are removed from a district. The change is real; whether it represents restored historical behavior remains uncertain.

Use: Lightscapes, urban restoration, historical evidence.

### 15. The Display Became Shorter

A long-term acoustic/visual series shows the same display beginning at the same time but ending sooner over several years. Population counts appear stable.

Use: research hook that avoids defaulting to abundance.

### 16. The Pair Association Ends Quietly

Two individuals previously assessed as a pair-like association stop co-occurring. Both remain healthy, locally present and behaviorally ordinary.

Use: normalize dissolution without injury, death or conflict.

### 17. The Public Wants a Love Story

A local tourism office markets two famous wild Pokémon as a “lifelong couple.” Researchers only have evidence for two seasons of repeated association.

Use: Media, Tourism, Public Memory, scientific caution.

### 18. The Signal Is Real, the Receiver Is Wrong

A detector correctly identifies a species-specific call pattern, but the call came from a mimic or recording rather than the assumed individual.

Use: Passive Acoustics, Social Learning, source attribution.

### 19. The Site Is Perfect and Nobody Uses It

A restoration project recreates every visible feature of a historical display site, yet the local population continues using a less “ideal” location.

Use: management humility and emergent site preference.

### 20. The Familiar Rival Is Part of the Display

Two individuals repeatedly compete at the same display site, but direct aggression is rare and both continue returning. The relationship could include competition without ordinary territorial exclusion.

Use: rivalry without forcing combat.

### 21. The First Observed Display Was Not the First

An archive photograph reveals that a behavior thought to have emerged five years ago was visible decades earlier.

Use: Photography, Archives, Public Memory.

### 22. The Research Playback Changes the Result

A study using recorded calls produces stronger responses than passive observation. The research team must separate natural behavior from intervention-driven behavior.

Use: Research Ethics, Passive Acoustics.

### 23. The Display Court Floods

A river shift or flood season makes a long-used court inaccessible. The population experiments with several nearby sites over successive seasons.

Use: Freshwater, Fluvial Geomorphology, behavior adaptation.

### 24. The Pair That Reforms

Two persistent individuals cease associating for a season, then later resume repeated reciprocal behavior. Records correctly preserve interruption and later re-association rather than pretending continuity.

Use: longitudinal identity and uncertainty.

### 25. The One-Sided Display

An individual repeatedly displays toward another that consistently withdraws. The behavior is worth documenting but never escalates into a hidden “affection meter.”

Use: agency and refusal.

### 26. The Famous Pattern Is Actually Several Patterns

A public-facing “traditional dance” turns out to combine multiple distinct sequences performed under different wind, light or social conditions.

Use: repertoire revision, scientific reclassification.

### 27. The Display Population Moves but the Festival Stays

A well-known display shifts to a different lake edge. The settlement must decide whether to move viewing infrastructure, leave the old festival site symbolic, or reduce public access.

Use: Tourism, Conservation, Public Space.

### 28. The Nest Appears Elsewhere

A strongly supported pair-like association repeatedly uses one display site, but a later nesting episode occurs somewhere else and does not prove which individuals are genetic parents.

Use: explicit handoff to Wild Nesting without lineage inference.

### 29. The Display That Prevented a Battle

Players recognize a repeated visual sequence from prior observation and realize that two wild Pokémon are engaged in a display rather than preparing an attack.

Use: knowledge payoff without mechanical bonus.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Cedar Court

A well-monitored season records ordinary attendance, ordinary displays, no disturbance and no major changes.

Use: baseline year that becomes valuable later.

## Long-form arc candidates

### Five Seasons at Cedar Court

Year one establishes a recurring display site and several persistent individuals. Year two adds public viewing pressure. Year three introduces a road or lighting change. Year four records a shift in participants and repertoire. Year five shows whether the site, behavior or public tradition persists.

The arc works even if no Eggs, nesting or battle ever occur.

Primary systems: Courtship, Spatial Ecology, Light/Sound, Public Space, Community Science, Conservation.

### The Pair With Three Histories

Two recognizable Pokémon are first described by residents as a pair, later assessed scientifically as a repeated association, and eventually linked to a nesting episode whose parentage remains unresolved. Archives preserve all three versions of the story.

The value comes from keeping public narrative, behavioral evidence and reproductive outcome distinct.

Primary systems: Courtship, Public Memory, Wild Nesting, Archives, Pokémon Agency.

### The Night-Sky Repertoire

A local luminous display population is documented over twenty years. Researchers discover individual variability, seasonal patterns and several stable motifs. A tourism festival popularizes one motif as “the traditional pattern,” while the scientific archive preserves a much richer repertoire.

Later generations may adopt or abandon motifs, creating a handoff to Social Learning only if transmission evidence supports it.

Primary systems: Courtship, Lightscapes, Photography, Social Learning, Tourism, Archives.

## Encounter contracts

### Display-Site Crowd Evacuation

Narrative premise:

A public viewing night becomes unsafe because visitors move beyond the managed viewing area while wild Pokémon are actively displaying nearby. The ecological problem is crowd pressure and route management, not hostile Pokémon.

FULL version:

World state establishes display site, viewing buffers, visitors, staff, persistent wild individuals and exits. If an independent battle threat appears, civilians and wild noncombatants can move dynamically through the tactical space.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED
- base movement legality: VERIFIED
- complete movement including interception/forced movement: BLOCKING for dynamic evacuation and non-hostile crossing
- core calculations: VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL only if exact battle effects are invoked
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING if viewing buffers, darkness, unstable shoreline or environmental effects become tactical
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL
- abilities: PARTIAL
- items: PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for `EVACUATE`, `WITHDRAW`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `AVOID_WILDLIFE`, `PROTECT_VISITOR`
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING

REDUCED version:

Public Space and Conservation close the viewing area, move civilians out and let the displaying Pokémon withdraw through world state. If a separate combat threat remains, AutoPTU receives a static arena containing only legal combatants. The display episode remains marked `INTERRUPTED_BY_CROWD_EVENT`.

Narrative premise preserved: the public event was disrupted and the ecological record remains incomplete.

### Courtship-Site Restoration Survey

Narrative premise:

A former display site has been restored after vegetation or hydrology changed. Researchers want to observe whether wild Pokémon return without forcing them to do so.

FULL version:

Researchers and wildlife can move between observation points and withdraw from disturbance. A separate encounter can occur while maintaining non-hostile ecological objectives.

Capability dependencies:

Complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback are BLOCKING for mobile observers and wild withdrawal. Environment is BLOCKING if water, mud, vegetation or protected cells have tactical effects. The standard verified/partial categories remain as above.

REDUCED version:

Research activity pauses before combat. Researchers leave, wildlife movement resolves outside the grid and the site configuration is frozen. AutoPTU receives a conventional battle only if needed. Afterwards the courtship layer records whether the survey window was lost or partially completed.

No victory can cause the Pokémon to adopt the restored site.

### Pair-Separation During Migration Stopover

Narrative premise:

Two persistent individuals previously assessed as a pair-like association arrive at a stopover at different times after a storm or route disruption. Observers want to avoid turning a temporary separation into a rescue or capture assumption.

FULL version:

The Pokémon may independently `CROSS`, `WITHDRAW`, `REJOIN`, remain apart or leave the area while other tactical actors pursue unrelated objectives.

Capability dependencies:

- complete movement: BLOCKING
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING if storm, water or terrain remains tactically active
- status/move/ability/item/Trainer Feature categories remain PARTIAL when exact mechanics are invoked
- targeting, base movement legality, core calculations, action economy/initiative and AI legal-action infrastructure remain VERIFIED

REDUCED version:

Migration resolves each individual’s arrival/departure outside combat. Courtship records `TEMPORARY_SEPARATION_OBSERVED` or `NOT_REOBSERVED_TOGETHER` without concluding dissolution. Any unrelated battle occurs in a separate static snapshot.

### What Was the Display For?

Non-combat investigation.

Players compare photography, acoustic recordings, territorial observations, pair histories and disturbance context from several seasons. Valid outcomes include:

- courtship supported;
- pair-maintenance supported;
- territorial function supported;
- multiple functions supported;
- evidence changed by researcher intervention;
- unresolved.

No Skill check should automatically reveal “the true function.” Skills may structure what evidence a character can notice or interpret only when PTU/Caelo rules support that use.

## Engine guardrails for all Pass 179 concepts

Never implement these shortcuts:

- courtship display as `Attract`;
- pair formation as `Infatuated`;
- failed display as a Loyalty penalty;
- courtship site as an aura/zone;
- signal light as Accuracy modification;
- scent display as automatic lure mechanics;
- same gender/opposite gender assumptions beyond authoritative mechanical data;
- crowd cheering as morale buff;
- pair bond as shared initiative or Pack Mon;
- mating-related behavior as capture vulnerability;
- Minecraft hearts as lineage state;
- KO/capture of a competitor as successful mate choice.

## Canon questions raised by these proposals

Canon review will eventually need to decide which populations have authored reproductive-social behaviors, whether any display sites are established at campaign start, what seasonal windows exist, what data can be public, how disturbance is managed, and which relationships are known versus merely suspected.

None of those answers are established by Pass 179.
