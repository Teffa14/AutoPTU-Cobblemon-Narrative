# Water Management, Dams, Reservoirs & Canals — Seeds 97

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These concepts are candidates only.
Date: 2026-08-28

These seeds depend on `design/water-management-dams-reservoirs-canals-continuity-extension.md` and preserve the authority boundaries defined there.

## The Gate Opened, the Channel Did Not

A gate log shows a successful operation, but downstream observers report no change for several hours.

Possible explanations include an upstream shortage, a blocked channel, a sensor/reporting delay, a partially executed operation or a mistaken observation point.

The investigation should reconcile timestamps and physical evidence. It should not assume sabotage.

## The Reservoir Everyone Calls Empty

A reservoir appears dramatically low from the public overlook. Staff records classify it as within an authorized seasonal band.

The conflict is between visual expectation, old photographs, current operating regime and actual service impact.

The player may discover that the public comparison is to an unusually wet historical period rather than the normal baseline.

## The Temporary Canal Became Habitat

A bypass excavated during a repair incident remains for several seasons. Wild Pokémon and vegetation begin using it.

When the original channel returns, removing the bypass becomes a new civic/ecological decision rather than automatic cleanup.

## The Farm Lost Water, the Orchard Did Not

Two agricultural sites believe they share the same supply. One goes LIMITED while the other remains AVAILABLE.

The answer may be a branch difference, a private storage fallback, an outdated map or a local blockage.

The scenario should reward dependency tracing instead of inventing crop penalties.

## The Release Notice Was Correct Yesterday

A posted release schedule remains physically present after weather, inspection or a technical issue changes the active operating regime.

Public Notices owns the stale notice. Water Management owns the actual operation. Rumor/Testimony can record the local belief that “they opened it anyway.”

## The Old Intake Still Draws Visitors

A decommissioned intake has become a landmark, fishing access point or wildlife observation site.

A later emergency proposal considers recommissioning it. Current users and ecological conditions now matter to the decision.

## The Repair Finished Before the Water Returned

Maintenance completes the physical repair. Testing remains pending, then the connection must be verified downstream before service is restored.

The world visibly experiences several distinct states instead of one repair quest flipping everything back to normal.

## The Canal Map Is One Revision Behind

A survey map, maintenance plan and farmer’s hand-drawn route disagree about one branch.

All three can contain useful truth from different dates. The mystery is reconstructing when the branch changed and which consumers still depend on the old alignment.

## The Wildlife Is Near the Spillway

A group of wild Pokémon appears repeatedly near a spillway during one seasonal regime.

The observation can trigger monitoring. It does not prove attraction, distress, hazard, motive or a need to stop operations without further evidence.

## The Water Is Available, the Use Is Not

A channel carries water normally, but a separate quality case temporarily prevents one class of use.

Residents initially describe the situation as “the canal is closed,” even though technically the transport function remains operational.

This seed reinforces availability/quality separation.

## Mystery — Five Level Reports, Three Observation Points

Five reports claim different reservoir conditions on the same day.

Investigation reveals that two are photographs from different shoreline elevations, one uses an old local reference mark, and two refer to the same official monitoring point at different times.

Possible outcome:
A coherent timeline can be reconstructed, but the cause of one short-lived change remains unresolved.

## Mystery — Four Gate Commands, Two Verified Operations

The control log records four commands during an incident. Only two have downstream verification.

Possible explanations:

- one command was cancelled before execution;
- one produced a partial mechanical response;
- one occurred while the upstream connection was unavailable;
- one succeeded but lacks surviving downstream evidence.

The mystery should distinguish command, asset response and verified system effect.

## Long arc — A Valley Learns Its Water

Phase 1 establishes the ordinary seasonal pattern: farms, a settlement, a wetland edge and a managed reservoir all depend on the same network in different ways.

Phase 2 introduces a limited problem such as a blocked branch, inspection restriction or unexpected low-level observation.

Phase 3 reveals that the first explanation is incomplete. Residents, operators, growers and conservation staff each hold valid but partial observations.

Phase 4 creates a temporary operating regime or diversion. This solves one problem and changes another pattern elsewhere.

Phase 5 restores the original asset, but verification and reopening happen in stages.

Phase 6 returns months later. The temporary route, an altered shoreline, a business adaptation, a wildlife observation or a new public habit has become part of the valley’s history.

The arc should end with a revised operating baseline and retained records, not an abstract `water_system_level`.

## Encounter — Canal Service Path Conflict

Intended full version:
A narrow maintenance approach creates route-clearance pressure while territorial wild Pokémon prefer to hold or withdraw through specific paths.

Additional rich dependencies:

- complete movement/reactions;
- tactical zones if the narrow service path has mechanical meaning;
- objective-aware AI;
- adapter/playback.

Reduced version:
The canal segment is closed as world state. Workers wait outside the encounter. Battle occurs on an adjacent static clearing or dry path. Victory does not clear the blockage or restore irrigation.

## Encounter — Emergency Diversion Perimeter

Intended full version:
A temporary diversion remains operational while participants protect a dry access corridor. A future full implementation may include meaningful water-edge/weather zones and reaction-driven route control.

Reduced version:
The diversion is resolved before combat as an authoritative operational state. Water, gates, staff, pumps and equipment are excluded from the battle arena. The fight occurs nearby and cannot modify the diversion automatically.

## NPC/faction candidates

All are NON-CANON role archetypes, not named established characters.

A reservoir operator who remembers several historical operating regimes and distrusts simplistic “normal level” claims.

A grower association representative who understands practical downstream effects but does not have authority over infrastructure decisions.

A conservation field observer whose strongest contribution is longitudinal evidence rather than veto power.

A maintenance lead who can prove what was physically repaired but refuses to certify downstream restoration without verification.

A settlement archivist who has photographs and maps from before a channel realignment.

A public-information coordinator trying to keep notices synchronized with rapidly changing operations.
