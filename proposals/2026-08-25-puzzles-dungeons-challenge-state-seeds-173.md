# Puzzles, Dungeons & Challenge State Seeds — Pass 173

Status: NON-CANON / PROPOSED
Date: 2026-08-25

These candidates use `design/puzzles-dungeons-challenge-state-layer.md`. They establish no canonical Gym, dungeon, ancient culture, trap, reward, institution or puzzle language.

## Thirty original candidates

### 1. The Door With Three Solutions
A sealed research annex has its original control sequence, a maintenance override documented in another room and a repair route through a failed actuator. Different parties can reach the same space for different reasons, and Chronicle records which route each used.

### 2. The Challenge Everyone Remembers Wrong
A famous old Gym challenge has been revised four times. Former challengers confidently give advice that was correct for their year and useless now.

### 3. The Lever That Stayed Down
Explorers solve a ruin mechanism and expect it to reset after leaving. It never does. Years later the open route has become an ordinary shortcut used by locals.

### 4. The Museum Copy Is Easier
A museum builds an educational replica of a difficult historical mechanism and adds clearer feedback. Visitors later assume the replica preserves the original exactly, creating a small archaeology/public-memory dispute.

### 5. The Missing Color
A civic challenge originally relies on color-coded controls. An accessibility revision adds shape and position markers without changing the underlying constraints. Decades later both presentations are considered part of the institution’s history.

### 6. One Puzzle, Two Languages
The mechanism is simple, but its operating instructions exist in two historical translations that disagree on one term. Languages owns the translation dispute; the challenge layer records which interpretation each attempt used.

### 7. The Door Is Open, the Challenge Is Not Complete
Maintenance has physically opened a Gym route while replacing a motor. Challengers can walk through the doorway, but the institution suspends that revision because the challenge itself cannot be completed validly.

### 8. The Battle Was Optional
A guardian Pokémon blocks one route but another path can be opened through observation and repair. Players who choose the battle and players who avoid it can both reach the same deeper node without one route becoming the “true” story.

### 9. The Battle Did Not Count
A challenger wins a fight inside a trial but had entered through an unauthorized staff route. Battle Institutions keeps the result; Credentials and the challenge program decide whether it satisfies the official challenge.

### 10. The Wrong Key Fits
A century-old replacement key physically operates a lock even though archival records describe another key. Material Culture and Archaeology investigate the replacement history while the challenge accepts the physically valid route.

### 11. The Puzzle That Became Maintenance
A mechanism once designed as a ceremonial trial is now used daily to regulate water through an old garden. The mystery is not how to “solve” it, but whether changing the mechanism for modern reliability will erase useful historical information.

### 12. The Shortcut Discovered by Accident
Players enter a ventilation/service corridor for an unrelated reason and discover a valid route around half of an old dungeon. The discovery is retained as world history rather than blocked because it was not the designer’s intended solution.

### 13. The Hint Arrives First
A former challenger sends a clue before the party reaches the site. It refers to the current mechanism but removes only one layer of uncertainty. The run remains meaningful because the challenge has multiple decisions rather than one secret answer.

### 14. The Repair Changes the Puzzle
A damaged rotating chamber is restored using a later replacement part. The repaired version has slightly different stopping positions, so every guide written before the repair becomes historically valid but operationally outdated.

### 15. The Riddle With No Official Answer
A philosophy-oriented challenge asks participants to defend one of several interpretations. The evaluator stores reasoning and a scoped decision. There is no hidden canonical answer string in the database.

### 16. The Failed Attempt Opened Something Useful
A wrong control sequence does not open the objective door, but it exposes a maintenance panel containing the clue to another solution. Failure creates information rather than a dead end.

### 17. The Consumed Token Problem
An old mechanism expects three physical tokens. One was removed to a museum decades ago. The challenge cannot remain progression-critical until an institutional bypass, replica policy or alternate route exists.

### 18. The Puzzle Nobody Needs to Solve
A side chamber contains an intact mechanism, but the main expedition route no longer depends on it. Players may return years later when new evidence gives the room a different purpose.

### 19. The Gym Test Became a City Event
A small clue hunt once used only for challengers becomes a popular annual public event. The original Gym revision remains archived while Festivals/Public Space owns the civic edition.

### 20. The Player Solution Becomes Official
A party uses a clever but safe alternate route that staff had not considered. The institution accepts it for this run, tests it later and eventually publishes a new challenge revision incorporating the idea.

### 21. The Puzzle Resets for Everyone Except the Archive
Every challenger receives the same initial mechanism state, but attempt histories remain preserved. Researchers can study how strategies changed over twenty years without exposing future solutions to participants.

### 22. The Silent Mechanism
An acoustic cue in an old device has stopped functioning. The physical sequence remains possible only for someone who already knows the cue. Rather than treating that as intended difficulty, the site marks the challenge unavailable until an equivalent presentation is restored.

### 23. The Dungeon After Stabilization
An abandoned industrial complex begins as a hazardous expedition. Years of cleanup, structural work and route marking gradually convert most of it into a public heritage trail while one deep challenge node remains restricted.

### 24. The Pokémon Refuses the Station
A challenge includes a voluntary Pokémon-partner station. A participant’s partner does not approach it. The challenge offers another route instead of turning refusal into disobedience or forcing the Pokémon to continue.

### 25. The Mechanism Was Never a Puzzle
Visitors interpret a pattern of valves as an ancient trial. Archaeology and engineering evidence later show it was ordinary infrastructure. The mechanism remains interesting, but the world corrects the romanticized story.

### 26. The Puzzle Solved Between Visits
The party leaves an unresolved public-site mechanism and returns months later to find that another group solved it. Chronicle records the changed state and available history rather than preserving the world as if only players can act.

### 27. The Last Safe Reset
A multiplayer challenge suffers a technical state corruption after simultaneous interaction. Infrastructure recovery returns it to the last validated challenge snapshot. Attempt logs remain preserved and the rollback is never described as in-world time reversal.

### 28. The Dungeon Has an Exit
A dangerous ruin allows withdrawal from every major node. The expedition can end with “we learned enough and left” instead of requiring completion, capture or a boss fight.

### 29. The Old Champion’s Route
A retired public figure visits a preserved challenge revision from decades earlier. Their remembered route works on the museum replica but not the modern competitive version, linking institutional succession with public memory.

### 30. Nothing Happened in Chamber Four
A party correctly solves a mechanism. The next room is simply empty and structurally stable. Years later that ordinary successful attempt becomes useful evidence that a later malfunction was new.

## Longer-term arcs

### Five Revisions of Meridian Gym
Revision one is a simple route puzzle built around the founder’s profession. A second Leader adds a cooperative station. Accessibility improvements introduce equivalent presentations in revision three. Revision four accepts a player-discovered alternate route after institutional testing. A later Leader retires most of the mechanism but preserves one chamber as history. The Gym’s identity evolves without requiring every successor to copy the founder.

### The Dungeon That Became a Road
An old underground complex begins as a restricted expedition with unreliable maps and several unresolved mechanisms. Players restore one gate, engineers later stabilize a corridor, Wayfinding formalizes a route and eventually part of the dungeon becomes public infrastructure. The same doors and chambers accumulate challenge, maintenance and civic histories instead of resetting after the adventure.

### The Mechanism With Three Explanations
An ancient device has an archaeological interpretation, a local oral tradition and a modern engineering reconstruction. Repeated attempts prove how the device operates but never fully settle why it was built. New evidence can revise one explanation without changing the mechanism state or invalidating earlier observations.

## Encounter contracts

### Floodgate Logic Chamber — FULL

Narrative premise: a historical water-control chamber must be configured so technicians can safely inspect another section while wild Pokémon use adjacent passages. The puzzle state controls which dry routes are open. A separate threat may produce combat.

Dependencies: targeting/footprints/range/LoS VERIFIED for ordinary combat; base movement legality VERIFIED; complete movement BLOCKING if gates change routes during combat, actors must cross/withdraw, or forced movement/interception matters; core calculations VERIFIED; action economy/initiative VERIFIED; full turn/round lifecycle PARTIAL when complete lifecycle matters; full stateful damage PARTIAL when damage occurs; status lifecycle PARTIAL for exact statuses only; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions BLOCKING if active water, changing cells, pressure zones or environmental reactions affect battle legality; move-specific behavior PARTIAL for exact Moves; abilities PARTIAL; items PARTIAL; Trainer Features/perks PARTIAL; AI legal-action infrastructure VERIFIED; AI tactical policy BLOCKING for `REACH_CONTROL`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_TECHNICIAN`, `AVOID_GATE_PATH`; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback BLOCKING.

Reduced version: Challenge State resolves the water-control configuration before combat. Freshwater/Infrastructure freezes the chamber in a safe static state. Technicians leave. Any independent battle then runs in an ordinary fixed arena. The battle cannot operate valves or change the puzzle solution.

### Rotating Archive Hall — FULL

Narrative premise: a museum/archive challenge uses rotating shelving/wall sections to expose different document rooms while custodians attempt to preserve fragile material during an interruption.

Dependencies: complete movement BLOCKING for dynamically changing paths and moving custodians; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions BLOCKING if moving walls or protected cells affect tactical legality; AI tactical policy BLOCKING for `PROTECT_CUSTODIAN`, `REACH_EXIT`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`; adapter/playback BLOCKING. Ordinary targeting, movement-legality, calculations, action economy and legal-action infrastructure remain VERIFIED; exact damage/Status/Move/Ability/Item/Feature families remain PARTIAL when invoked.

Reduced version: the archive mechanism stops at one validated configuration before battle. Fragile material and staff are moved out through world state. AutoPTU receives a static room. Archive custody and puzzle progress resume afterward.

### Partner Coordination Trial — FULL

Narrative premise: an institutional test invites a Trainer and willing partner Pokémon to coordinate across two stations where actions on one side reveal information or open routes on the other. The intended goal is cooperation, not combat dominance.

Dependencies: complete movement BLOCKING for synchronized movement and withdrawal; AI tactical policy BLOCKING for non-hostile objectives such as `REACH_STATION`, `WAIT`, `WITHDRAW`, `COOPERATE`; adapter/playback BLOCKING. Trainer Features/perks, abilities, items and move-specific behavior remain PARTIAL if an exact mechanical option is used. No invented Loyalty or Command check is created by the trial.

Reduced version: coordination choices are resolved as world-state interactions outside battle. If the trial includes a final spar, that spar begins only after stations are locked into a static state and uses ordinary battle rules. A partner refusing a station opens an authored alternate route.

### The Three-Clue Ruin — NON-COMBAT / OPTIONAL COMBAT

A sealed historical node has three independent evidence paths toward the same route: physical wear, an archival maintenance note and a local oral account. Any one can reveal enough to continue. Contradictions can remain unresolved.

No battle capability is required for the puzzle itself. If an unrelated encounter occurs, it is handed to AutoPTU as a separate static battle and cannot determine the archaeological conclusion.

## Reusable NPC and institution candidates

A challenge curator who maintains historical revisions and is more interested in preserving how the test changed than defending one “pure” version.

A maintenance technician who knows every physical bypass but has no authority to certify official completion.

A retired challenger whose advice is precise, sincere and two revisions out of date.

An accessibility reviewer who tests whether alternate presentations preserve the intended decision rather than merely making the route shorter.

A Gym Leader who judges how challengers recover from wrong assumptions instead of rewarding memorized solutions.

A museum educator who builds replicas with transparent state indicators so visitors can understand mechanisms that would be unsafe or too fragile to operate directly.

A ruins researcher who refuses to call every mechanism a ritual trial until evidence supports that interpretation.
