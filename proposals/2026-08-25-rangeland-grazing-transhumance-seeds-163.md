# Pass 163 Proposals — Rangeland Managed Seasonal Movement & Monitoring

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. None of these concepts establish Ouros geography, institutions, species roles or mechanics.

Authority: Pass 79 `grasslands-grazing-rangeland-ecology-layer.md` remains authoritative. These concepts use the Pass 163 extension `rangeland-managed-seasonal-movement-monitoring-protocol.md`.

1. **The Waterpoint That Moved the Group.** A newly repaired waterpoint changes where a managed group spends time. Vegetation changes nearby, but monitoring separates water access, forage preference and route habit before attribution.
2. **The Fence That Helped One Species and Blocked Another.** A management fence improves distribution for a pastoral program while creating a barrier problem for wild movement. Both outcomes can be true.
3. **The Rest Year Everyone Thought Was Abandonment.** A pasture is deliberately left unused for a season. Visitors interpret empty shelters as economic collapse until old plans explain the decision.
4. **Two Green Pastures, One Forage Story.** Two fields look equally lush from the road, but observations show different composition and use histories.
5. **The Late Arrival.** A seasonal group reaches its destination weeks later than usual. Route conditions, water, individual readiness and scheduling remain competing explanations.
6. **The Early Departure.** A group leaves a summer area early without a crisis. Years later the event becomes useful when comparing climate, forage and route history.
7. **Three Surveys, One Range.** A daytime count, camera network and field-sign survey produce different use estimates. The disagreement becomes a methods problem.
8. **The Trail Became a Road.** A long-used seasonal route is formalized as a road. Travel improves for people while managed movement becomes harder to coordinate.
9. **The Road Became a Crossing.** A route that once crossed open ground now intersects a high-traffic corridor. Road Ecology and Pass 79 must coordinate a recurring passage.
10. **The Shelter Used Only One Week.** A remote shelter looks abandoned most of the year because its function is intensely seasonal.
11. **The Group That Split by Choice.** Several known Pokémon take a different route during movement. The system records the split without inventing defiance, illness or hierarchy.
12. **The Old Route Without a Permit.** Oral history documents decades of use, but the present access relationship is unresolved.
13. **The Permit Without Water.** A use grant remains valid while the destination waterpoint is unavailable.
14. **The Water Without Access.** Water is physically present, but the route crosses land without current passage authorization.
15. **The Old Restriction Sign.** A sign still says a pasture is closed even though the restriction expired. The object is authentic and the information stale.
16. **The Range Looked Empty at Noon.** A visitor reports no group use. Night observations later show substantial use outside the original survey window.
17. **The Loaded-Chunk Census Lie.** Players see a handful of Pokémon and assume the group shrank. Chronicle keeps the estimate independent of render distance.
18. **The Veteran Gogoat Stops Early.** A known Gogoat that historically completed the route stops at an intermediate camp. Aging/welfare are assessed elsewhere; the protocol records route outcome.
19. **The Wild Group Shares Water.** A wild collective uses the same waterpoint during a dry season. Co-use does not imply domestication, conflict or mutualism.
20. **The Replacement Waterpoint Changes Distribution.** A temporary source becomes popular enough that the old pattern does not return after repairs.
21. **The Seasonal Worker Knows the Route.** A person with years of practical knowledge notices a subtle change before official maps do. Their observation enters the evidence ledger without becoming unquestionable truth.
22. **The Former Partner Reappears Along the Route.** A released former partner is observed near a historic passage. Presence does not re-establish custody.
23. **The Drought Year That Did Not Become a Crisis.** Alternate water, reduced use and an earlier move work as intended. Preparedness removes a quest.
24. **The Quarry Dust Changed the Forage.** A neighboring extraction site is suspected after vegetation changes. Mining, Air Quality, Soil and Flora establish evidence before causation.
25. **The Orchard Edge Became a Rest Site.** A seasonal group repeatedly pauses beside an orchard after harvest. Agriculture, tenure and range use develop a stable arrangement.
26. **The Corridor Used in Opposite Seasons.** Wild migration and managed movement share a narrow passage at different times until timing shifts begin to overlap.
27. **The Festival Blocks the Historic Route.** A recurring festival expands onto a traditional passage. The story can end in schedule coordination rather than removing either tradition.
28. **The Monitoring Camera Missed Night Use.** A healthy camera framed the wrong approach. A low-use conclusion is revised without changing original images.
29. **The Product Question Is Still Open.** Old records mention Wooloo and textile trade but do not establish whether fleece was collected locally, traded in or authorized under modern rules.
30. **Nothing Happened at North Pasture.** The season follows plan and monitoring finds no meaningful deviation. Years later it provides a baseline.

## Longer arcs

### Five Summers on Cedar Range

Year one establishes route, waterpoints and monitoring. Year two brings a road upgrade. Year three shifts use after a dry spring. Year four sees wild migration overlap the passage. Year five tests whether earlier adjustments created a resilient system. The arc is accumulated knowledge rather than a single antagonist.

### The Seasonal Road

An old movement route begins as an informal track, gains markers and shelters, intersects settlement expansion, receives a detour and eventually becomes part of a public corridor. Chronicle preserves each revision and remembered landmark.

### The Pasture Without a Villain

Some condition indicators decline while others stay stable. Possible causes include water distribution, drought, altered timing, neighboring land use and survey bias. Several seasons of evidence gradually narrow the model without requiring a culprit.

Complete movement is BLOCKING for crossing/withdrawal/interception; tactical AI is BLOCKING for PROTECT_TECHNICIAN/CLEAR_ROUTE/WITHDRAW; adapter/playback is BLOCKING. ### Forage Condition Review

NON-COMBAT by default. Pass 79, Flora, Soil, Water and weather evidence are compared. The valid result may be `UNRESOLVED` or a revised range-use plan. A battle cannot establish forage condition or causal attribution.
