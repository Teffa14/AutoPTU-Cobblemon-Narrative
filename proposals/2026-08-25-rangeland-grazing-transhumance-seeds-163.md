# Pass 163 Proposals — Rangeland, Grazing & Managed Seasonal Movement

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. None of these concepts establish Ouros geography, institutions, species roles or mechanics.

These concepts assume the authority boundaries in `design/rangeland-grazing-transhumance-layer.md`.

1. **The Waterpoint That Moved the Group.** A newly repaired waterpoint changes where a managed group spends time. Vegetation changes nearby, but the story remains open until monitoring separates water access, forage preference and route habit.

2. **The Fence That Helped One Species and Blocked Another.** A management fence improves distribution for a pastoral program while creating a barrier problem for wild movement. Both outcomes can be true.

3. **The Rest Year Everyone Thought Was Abandonment.** A pasture is deliberately left unused for a season. Visitors interpret the empty shelters as economic collapse until old plans and current monitoring explain the decision.

4. **Two Green Pastures, One Forage Story.** Two fields look equally lush from the road, but observations show different composition and use histories. Visual presentation is not management truth.

5. **The Late Arrival.** A seasonal group reaches its destination weeks later than usual. Route conditions, water, individual readiness and institutional scheduling remain competing explanations.

6. **The Early Departure.** A group leaves a summer area early without a crisis. Years later the event becomes useful when comparing climate, forage and route history.

7. **Three Surveys, One Range.** A daytime visual count, camera network and field-sign survey produce different use estimates. The disagreement becomes a methods problem instead of a monster hunt.

8. **The Trail Became a Road.** A long-used seasonal route is later formalized as a road. Travel improves for people while the managed movement schedule becomes harder to coordinate.

9. **The Road Became a Crossing.** A route that once crossed open ground now intersects a high-traffic corridor. Road Ecology and Rangeland must agree on a safe recurring passage.

10. **The Shelter Used Only One Week.** A remote shelter looks abandoned for most of the year because its function is intensely seasonal. Its short active window matters more than annual occupancy.

11. **The Group That Split by Choice.** Several known Pokémon take a different route during a managed movement. The system records the split without inventing defiance, illness or hierarchy.

12. **The Old Route Without a Permit.** Oral history and markers document decades of use, but the present access relationship is unresolved. Historical use and present permission stay separate.

13. **The Permit Without Water.** A grazing-use grant remains valid while the destination waterpoint is unavailable. The legal and operational states diverge cleanly.

14. **The Water Without Access.** Water is physically present, but the route to it crosses land without current passage authorization. Solving hydrology does not solve tenure.

15. **The Old Restriction Sign.** A sign still says a pasture is closed even though the restriction expired. The block is authentic and the information is stale.

16. **The Range Looked Empty at Noon.** A visitor reports no group use. Night observations later show the same range is heavily used outside the original survey window.

17. **The Loaded-Chunk Census Lie.** Players see only a handful of Pokémon near a staging site and assume the group shrank. Chronicle preserves the real estimate independently of render distance.

18. **The Veteran Gogoat Stops Early.** A known Gogoat that historically completed the full route stops at an intermediate camp. Aging, welfare and individual preference are assessed elsewhere; Rangeland records the route outcome.

19. **The Wild Group Shares Water.** A wild collective begins using the same waterpoint during a dry season. Co-use does not imply domestication, conflict or mutualism.

20. **The Replacement Waterpoint Changes Distribution.** A temporary source becomes popular enough that the old use pattern does not return when the original is repaired.

21. **The Seasonal Worker Knows the Route.** A person with years of practical route knowledge notices a subtle change before official maps do. Their observation enters the evidence ledger without becoming unquestionable truth.

22. **The Former Partner Reappears Along the Route.** A released former partner is observed near a historic seasonal passage. Persistent identity may be investigated, but presence does not re-establish custody.

23. **The Drought Year That Did Not Become a Crisis.** Alternate water, reduced use and an earlier move work as intended. The quiet season becomes evidence that preparedness can remove quests rather than create them.

24. **The Quarry Dust Changed the Forage.** A neighboring extraction site is suspected after vegetation changes. Mining, Air Quality, Soil and Flora must establish evidence before causation is written.

25. **The Orchard Edge Became a Rest Site.** A seasonal group repeatedly pauses beside an orchard after harvest. Agriculture, tenure and pastoral use develop a stable coexistence arrangement.

26. **The Corridor Used in Opposite Seasons.** Wild migration and managed movement share the same narrow passage at different times. A climate-driven timing shift threatens that separation.

27. **The Festival Blocks the Historic Route.** A recurring festival expands onto a traditional passage. The long-term story can end in schedule coordination rather than removing either tradition.

28. **The Monitoring Camera Missed Night Use.** A camera was technically healthy but framed the wrong approach. A low-use conclusion is revised without changing the original images.

29. **The Product Question Is Still Open.** Old records mention Wooloo and textile trade nearby, but do not establish whether fleece was collected locally, traded in, or authorized under modern rules. Material Culture keeps the claim unresolved.

30. **Nothing Happened at North Pasture.** The season follows plan, monitoring finds no meaningful deviation and no intervention is required. Years later it provides an important baseline.

## Longer arcs

### Five Summers on Cedar Range

Year one establishes route, waterpoints and monitoring. Year two brings a road upgrade. Year three shifts use after a dry spring. Year four sees wild migration overlap the passage. Year five tests whether earlier adjustments created a resilient system. No single antagonist is required; the arc is about accumulated knowledge, institutions and changing landscape.

### The Seasonal Road

An old movement route begins as an informal track, gains markers and shelters, intersects a settlement expansion, receives a temporary detour and eventually becomes part of a public corridor. Chronicle preserves each route revision, access decision and remembered landmark. The final route may be safer while also being less direct and culturally different.

### The Pasture Without a Villain

A range declines according to some indicators while other measures stay stable. Possible causes include changed water distribution, drought, altered timing, neighboring land use and survey bias. Several seasons of evidence gradually narrow the explanation. The satisfying ending is a better model and revised practice, not necessarily a culprit.

## Encounter contracts

### Seasonal Crossing at Cedar Range

FULL: A managed group, wild movement and road traffic overlap during a narrow crossing window. The intended scenario includes moving non-hostile actors, dynamic withdrawal/crossing objectives and route protection. Required categories: targeting VERIFIED if combat occurs; base movement legality VERIFIED; complete movement BLOCKING; core calculations VERIFIED; action economy/initiative VERIFIED; full lifecycle PARTIAL; full damage PARTIAL; environment family BLOCKING if road barriers/weather become tactical; exact Moves/Abilities/Items/Features PARTIAL as invoked; AI legal-action infrastructure VERIFIED; AI tactical policy BLOCKING; adapter/playback BLOCKING.

REDUCED: World state stops traffic, reroutes the managed group and clears civilians before combat. AutoPTU receives a static verge arena with only actual combatants. The crossing outcome resumes afterward; winning never means the group crossed successfully.

### Waterpoint Rotation Conflict

FULL: Technicians redirect access while managed and wild Pokémon approach different waterpoints. Moving objectives and non-hostile withdrawal require complete movement, tactical AI and playback; water/mud effects additionally require the environmental family.

REDUCED: The system closes the contested source and redirects noncombatants before battle. The static encounter occurs away from the water infrastructure. Water availability and management review remain world-state outcomes.

### Fence-Line Repair During Seasonal Movement

FULL: A repair team works while a managed group and wild corridor use adjacent routes. Complete movement is BLOCKING for crossing/withdrawal/interception; AI tactical policy is BLOCKING for PROTECT_TECHNICIAN, CLEAR_ROUTE and WITHDRAW; adapter/playback is BLOCKING. Dynamic protected zones or hazardous ground also require the environmental family.

REDUCED: Repair pauses, groups are rerouted and workers leave the grid. A conventional static battle can occur on a safe segment, after which repair and route state continue independently.

### Forage Condition Review

NON-COMBAT by default. Agriculture/Flora/Soil/Rangeland compare surveys, use history and weather context. The correct result may be `UNRESOLVED` or a management revision. A battle outcome cannot establish forage condition or causal attribution.
