# Working Pokémon, Institutional Roles & Task Partnerships — Ouros Seeds, Pass 139

Status: NON-CANON proposals. All names, places, institutions and situations below are candidates only.

These concepts use the proposed working-Pokémon layer. They must not establish PTU mechanics, legal employment categories, universal Pokémon consent rules, species-wide work competence or canon institutions without review.

## Design goal

Use work to make Ouros feel inhabited between adventures. Most routine shifts should pass quietly. Story emerges when assignment, availability, equipment, staffing, public expectation, welfare, institutional history or environmental state changes.

## 30 candidate seeds

### 1. The Machoke Who Stops at One Stairwell

A freight hall has years of records showing the same Machoke moving goods throughout the building except through one renovated stairwell. Workers route around it without treating the behavior as misconduct. A later survey asks whether the change relates to geometry, equipment, sound, smell or an entirely different cause.

No inference of fear, Injury or low Loyalty is permitted without evidence.

### 2. The Relief Partner Arrives Early

A ferry's usual work partner becomes unavailable. The relief Pokémon arrives before the replacement crew member who knows its routine. The service has enough staffing to operate eventually, but the morning schedule slips because the team composition matters.

### 3. The Construction Crew Without Conkeldurr

A construction cooperative with a famous history of working alongside Conkeldurr now completes most jobs using ordinary machinery. Older buildings still reflect the old workflow. A restoration project exposes spaces that modern crews no longer understand immediately.

### 4. The Job That Became a Tradition

A simple seasonal task performed by one Pokémon decades ago became a recurring local ceremony. The work is now done by a rotating team and could be completed mechanically without Pokémon at all. The community must decide how much of the tradition is operational and how much is memory.

### 5. The Wrong Pokémon on the Shift Board

A public roster still lists a Pokémon who changed roles months ago. The replacement has been doing the work correctly. The discrepancy matters only when emergency responders use the old board to plan staffing.

### 6. The Harness Still Fits, the Task Does Not

A work harness remains physically usable after a Pokémon evolves, but the new body plan makes the original route awkward. The equipment is not mechanically harmful; the institution simply needs to reassess the assignment and path.

### 7. The Wild Helper at Floodgate

A local wild Pokémon repeatedly appears when workers inspect a floodgate and once assists during an obstruction. The institution records a temporary partnership for that incident. Future appearances remain observations, not a standing contract or capture opportunity.

### 8. The Ferry Pokémon Takes a Week Off

A well-known ferry partner stops participating for several days. The operator uses reduced capacity and alternate transport rather than sending players to “fix” the Pokémon. The reason may remain unknown.

### 9. The Job Request Nobody Accepts

An institution posts a legitimate Pokémon task request. Every available candidate remains unavailable, declines, or lacks validated requirements. The correct outcome may be rescheduling or redesigning the job.

### 10. Species Poster, Individual Reality

An old safety poster depicts a species as ideal for a particular job. Modern staff know that individual variation matters. A new worker mistakenly treats the poster as an eligibility rule.

### 11. The Retired Worker Still Visits

A retired Pokémon returns to the workplace at the same time each week. Staff welcome the visit. The system must not reactivate the old assignment or infer that the Pokémon wants to return to duty.

### 12. The Apprentice Human Learned From a Pokémon

A century-old workshop ledger credits a Pokémon with demonstrating a material-handling technique to an apprentice. The technique survives in modified form. Historians disagree about whether the original entry was literal, symbolic or exaggerated.

### 13. The Pokémon Learned From the Crew

A Pokémon develops a new routine after months around a maintenance team. Social Learning owns any claim that the behavior was transmitted. The work layer records only the task history and observations.

### 14. The Shift That Ends Mid-Task

A Pokémon withdraws halfway through an otherwise ordinary assignment. Another team finishes the job. Nobody is injured and no misconduct occurred. The interrupted assignment remains part of the history.

### 15. Two Institutions Claim the Same Availability

A rescue service and a research station both schedule the same partnered Pokémon because their calendars were not synchronized. The conflict is logistical, not a question of ownership.

### 16. Emergency Override Refused

An emergency dispatcher requests a familiar work partnership outside its normal scope. The Pokémon does not engage. The service switches to its fallback plan. The refusal creates operational history but no Loyalty change.

### 17. The Replacement Has Different Strengths

A retiring station partner is replaced by another individual of a different species. Staff initially try to preserve the old workflow, then realize the new team works better with a redesigned process.

### 18. The Famous Worker Was Never Staff

A Pokémon photographed for years near a depot becomes locally famous as “the station helper.” Archive review shows it was never formally assigned and may simply have used the area as part of its routine.

### 19. The Worksite Became Habitat

A maintenance yard used intermittently for years becomes habitat during long quiet periods. Returning seasonal work now intersects a real ecological use. Workplaces and Ecology must coordinate rather than labelling the Pokémon trespassers.

### 20. The Old Task No Longer Exists

Automation removes the work function that once defined a famous Pokémon's role. The institution keeps the Pokémon's residence and care arrangements while ending the assignment cleanly.

### 21. The Pokémon Has Two Roles

The same individual assists a clinic on certain days and a community garden on others. Neither institution owns its total availability. A schedule conflict exposes the need for better coordination.

### 22. Equipment Built for the Previous Evolution

A public-works partner evolves. Its old protective gear becomes a historical object while new equipment is designed. Evolution does not automatically make the Pokémon more or less qualified for the job.

### 23. The Route Moved, the Role Stayed

A river or landslide forces a service route to move. The work role persists, but the task context and equipment needs change enough to require a new eligibility review.

### 24. The Public Thinks It Was Fired

A famous working Pokémon stops appearing at a station after moving to another role. Fans invent a dismissal story. Work records show a planned transition, while the Pokémon's private/internal motives remain unauthored.

### 25. Three Years of Normal Shifts

Nothing dramatic happens. The same team performs ordinary work for three years. The accumulated ledger later becomes useful evidence during a service redesign or historical investigation.

### 26. The Job Board Is Outdated

A Rotomi-like institutional board still advertises a task after the work was completed through another route. A Trainer arriving for the “quest” discovers there is no quest anymore.

### 27. The Handoff Was Logged Wrong

A replacement team receives the correct equipment and completes the assignment, but the handoff record names the wrong outgoing Pokémon. Years later the error creates a false work-history claim.

### 28. The Pokémon Does Nothing Today

A Pokémon normally associated with a workplace spends the day resting, wandering nearby or simply not engaging. The world records no work assignment. Nothing needs to be fixed.

### 29. The Successor Is Not a Clone

A community expects the new rescue partner to behave like the beloved retired predecessor. The institution must rebuild routines around the new individual rather than training away every difference.

### 30. The Role Survived Three Generations

A role first filled by a Pokémon, later by humans and machinery, then again by a mixed team survives for decades. The persistent story is the institution adapting, not a single ideal staffing model.

## Longer-term arcs

### Arc A — Five Generations at Meridian Freight Hall

Year 1: a mixed human/Pokémon freight team uses old platforms and hand carts.

Year 2: rail expansion changes the cargo pattern. One famous working Pokémon's role becomes less central while a new route requires different coverage.

Year 3: an equipment retrofit creates a fit problem for one long-serving individual. The institution redesigns the station instead of declaring the Pokémon unfit by default.

Year 4: the veteran retires from freight duty. A relief team covers the role while the station documents old procedures and transfers selected equipment to a local museum.

Year 5+: the successor team works differently. Public memory still centers the old worker, while operational records show the freight hall has become more resilient because it no longer depends on one individual.

Possible endings include successful continuity, reduced freight scope, further mechanization or a new mixed-team model. None requires scandal, death or villainy.

### Arc B — The Concrete Guild Years

An old construction guild attributes a foundational building technique to historical collaboration with Conkeldurr. Surviving ledgers, architecture and oral histories agree on some details and conflict on others.

Modern projects reveal that the technique changed many times. The guild may preserve the attribution as cultural history even if researchers cannot prove the original event exactly.

A later project must decide whether to reconstruct an old process, use modern materials or exhibit both. No version grants crafting bonuses, Technology Education or PTU Features.

### Arc C — The Service That Learned to Pause

A regional emergency service begins with a culture of treating every familiar partner as always available.

Several years of minor incidents reveal schedule conflicts, equipment fit issues, changed behavior and occasional refusal. No single incident is catastrophic.

The service gradually adds relief teams, clearer task scopes, off-duty state, role reviews and handoff procedures.

By the final year, many situations that once generated urgent quests are handled routinely because the institution learned how to pause or replace a task safely. Institutional maturity reduces mandatory player intervention.

## Encounter contract 1 — Freight Hall Evacuation During Equipment Failure

Premise: a machine or loading fixture fails while a mixed crew is operating. The occupational problem is shutting down the worksite, accounting for staff/Pokémon and preserving cargo/task state. A separate hostile or panicked confrontation may remain afterward.

FULL version:

Moving workers or work Pokémon can leave danger zones, seek exits or protect a route while actual combatants act. Equipment and cargo may create tactical constraints only if validated mechanics exist.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — required;
- base movement legality — required;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — required for live evacuation, escorts or displacement;
- core calculations — required for ordinary combat;
- action economy/initiative — required;
- full turn/round lifecycle — required for complete combat timing;
- full stateful damage pipeline — required for complete combat resolution;
- status lifecycle — only when exact supported effects invoke it;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — required if the equipment failure creates a validated tactical hazard or reaction window;
- move-specific behavior — depends on chosen Moves;
- abilities — depends on combatants;
- items — required only for exact mechanical Items;
- Trainer Features/perks — depends on Trainers;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — required;
- AI tactical policy — required for `EVACUATE`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `PROTECT_WORKER`, `WITHDRAW` or `REACH_EXIT` goals;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — required.

REDUCED version:

The server shuts down the equipment and evacuates all noncombat staff/work Pokémon before battle. Cargo, tools and machinery remain frozen world state. AutoPTU receives a static legal arena with actual combatants only. After battle, Workplaces and Manufacturing/Infrastructure decide whether the equipment can resume. Battle victory never repairs machinery or proves why it failed.

## Encounter contract 2 — Floodgate Temporary Partnership

Premise: a local wild Pokémon has previously been observed around a floodgate and voluntarily participates in one obstruction-removal or inspection event. During the task a confrontation may occur.

FULL version:

The temporary partner may need to reach a task point, withdraw, assist with a validated world objective or leave the encounter after the task. If it becomes a combatant, battle control must be resolved from authoritative state rather than narrative assumption.

Primary dependencies:

- complete movement — needed for `REACH_TASK_POINT`, `WITHDRAW` or interception-sensitive movement;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — needed only if water/current or gate state has a real validated tactical effect;
- AI tactical policy — needed for task-aware behavior;
- adapter/playback — needed for the objective and temporary-partnership semantics;
- all ordinary combat families remain required according to the combatants and exact Moves/Abilities/Items/Features used.

REDUCED version:

Resolve the Pokémon's assistance entirely in world state before battle. The temporary helper remains off-grid unless authoritative AutoPTU state already supports its participation. If conflict remains, resolve a conventional static encounter. The help ends at the recorded task boundary. No capture, ownership, Loyalty or permanent job is inferred.

## Encounter contract 3 — Construction Yard Shift Handoff

Premise: one work team must hand a partially completed site to another during a disruption. The narrative objective is safe transfer of task state, equipment and warnings, not defeating every creature in the area.

FULL version:

Workers or Pokémon can move between staging points, protect a route and disengage. Equipment can matter tactically only when mapped to real mechanics.

Primary blockers:

- complete movement for live team movement/interception;
- AI tactical policy for `REACH_STAGING`, `PROTECT_HANDOFF`, `WITHDRAW`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`;
- adapter/playback for work-state semantics;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if a validated site hazard is active.

REDUCED version:

Freeze the construction task. Complete the handoff in world state first. Remove all work teams and movable equipment from tactical exposure. If a confrontation remains, AutoPTU resolves a static battle. Resume the task afterward from the handoff state rather than assuming the battle completed construction.

## Non-combat scenario — Worker Refuses Assignment

A familiar work Pokémon does not engage with a proposed task.

There is no battle objective.

The institution can:

- offer a different task;
- use a relief team;
- delay the work;
- investigate equipment/environment changes if evidence supports doing so;
- request a care review if there are independent welfare observations;
- end the role cleanly.

The generator must not turn refusal itself into a combat encounter, Loyalty loss, punishment or forced capture.
