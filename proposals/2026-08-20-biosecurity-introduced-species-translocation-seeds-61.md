# Biosecurity, Introduced Species & Translocation Seeds — Pass 61

Status: NON-CANON proposals.

Date: 2026-08-20

These concepts are original Ouros candidates derived from the abstract research patterns in Pass 61. They do not copy external plots, characters or dialogue.

## Seed 01 — The First Corral Sighting

A Pokémon species never previously recorded in the valley appears near livestock feed. One individual is confirmed. Nobody knows whether it escaped, arrived naturally or has been present unnoticed for years.

Use: observation, provenance, rumor control.

## Seed 02 — The Nursery Ledger Gap

A nursery's old intake ledger contains three Pokémon that were never formally transferred or released. Decades later, the same species exists wild nearby.

Use: archive research, custody history, uncertain causation.

## Seed 03 — The Species Everyone Calls Invasive

Residents use a familiar label for a recently common species, but no impact study has actually confirmed ecological harm.

Use: public belief versus evidence.

## Seed 04 — Long-Established Outsiders

A population was brought to the region generations ago and is now embedded in local farming, stories and habitat relationships.

Use: introduced does not mean removable or harmful.

## Seed 05 — Ferry Hitchhikers

Small Pokémon repeatedly appear around cargo after the same ferry route arrives. The operator may be an accidental pathway rather than a wrongdoer.

Use: transport, pathway analysis, port inspection.

## Seed 06 — One Escape, Many Reports

A single persistent Pokémon travels through several settlements and creates the false impression of a rapidly spreading population.

Use: persistent identity, photography, map evidence.

## Seed 07 — The Second Introduction

Two distant populations of the same species appear almost simultaneously. Research must distinguish one spread front from two independent arrivals.

Use: provenance graph, genetics only if canon supports it, transport records.

## Seed 08 — The Pollution Opportunity

A newly arrived Water-type thrives only in degraded channels where native competitors have already declined for unrelated reasons.

Use: pollution, ecological causation, restoration.

## Seed 09 — Tourism Before Science

A rare newcomer becomes a media attraction before researchers know whether it is transient or established.

Use: tourism pressure, conservation, media.

## Seed 10 — Released Partner, New Population

A previously released persistent Pokémon is observed living near an unfamiliar group. The game must not assume it founded the population or leads it.

Use: Pokémon agency, release continuity, collectives.

## Seed 11 — Storm Displacement

A severe storm carries several coastal Pokémon inland. They may return naturally when conditions normalize.

Use: weather, crisis, temporary presence.

## Seed 12 — The Wrong Cargo

A shipment manifest is accurate, but wild Pokémon entered the cargo after inspection.

Use: evidence, no automatic smuggling accusation.

## Seed 13 — The Closed Pathway

A nursery improves containment and no new escapes occur, but an already-established population continues to expand.

Use: arrival pathway versus establishment.

## Seed 14 — The Helpful Newcomer

An introduced species consumes a resource that residents consider problematic while also competing with another wild population.

Use: mixed ecological effects.

## Seed 15 — The Predator Panic

A newcomer is blamed for fewer nesting Pokémon. Long-term observation later shows the nesting decline started before the newcomer arrived.

Use: historical baselines, alternative explanations.

## Seed 16 — The Ranger Who Says Wait

A field specialist refuses to authorize intervention because evidence is still too weak, creating political pressure without making the specialist automatically correct.

Use: institutions, uncertainty, public conflict.

## Seed 17 — The Collector Surge

Rumors of an exotic Pokémon cause capture pressure that becomes a bigger immediate ecological problem than the population itself.

Use: media, tourism, conservation, illicit networks if evidence appears.

## Seed 18 — The Quiet Acceptance

After decades of monitoring and no confirmed harmful effect, a region formally stops treating a long-established population as an active biosecurity case.

Use: institutional review, evolving policy.

## Seed 19 — The Wrong Species

Early reports merge two visually similar species into one apparent spread event.

Use: photography, identification, science.

## Seed 20 — The Harbor Colony

A non-local species lives only around warm industrial discharge. A plant shutdown may remove the condition keeping it alive.

Use: infrastructure, ecology, ethical tradeoff.

## Seed 21 — The Seasonal Arrival

A population appears every year but leaves before breeding. Residents debate whether this is migration, range expansion or repeated transport.

Use: seasonality, observation windows.

## Seed 22 — The Conservation Translocation

A stewardship group intentionally relocates Pokémon away from a construction project. Years later, researchers investigate whether the receiving habitat changed.

Use: management accountability, long-term consequences.

## Seed 23 — The Unauthorized Release

A confirmed release event exists, but the actor's motive remains unknown and ecological impact is still unresolved.

Use: case layer without turning motive into fact.

## Seed 24 — The Rescue That Became Establishment

Pokémon temporarily housed after a disaster were released locally under emergency conditions. A small population later persists.

Use: crisis history, custody, management review.

## Seed 25 — The Empty Trap Network

Camera traps near the suspected spread front show nothing. The result may mean absence, poor placement, equipment failure or changed timing.

Use: visual evidence, null results.

## Seed 26 — The Food-Web Surprise

Reducing one newly established population increases pressure from another species because both used the same resource differently.

Use: indirect ecological consequences.

## Seed 27 — The Mine Rail Hitchhiker

A species appears along an old freight corridor connecting a mine and port. The line has carried multiple cargo types across decades.

Use: infrastructure history, provenance uncertainty.

## Seed 28 — The Region That Wants It

A community values a long-established introduced Pokémon culturally and economically while a scientific institution remains concerned about one habitat effect.

Use: mixed priorities, agreements, governance.

## Seed 29 — The False Eradication Claim

A program announces success after sightings stop, but the monitoring network lost coverage in the same period.

Use: data quality, institutional accountability.

## Seed 30 — The Species That Was Always There

Archived photographs reveal that an allegedly recent arrival existed locally decades earlier under a mistaken identification.

Use: archives, photography, public correction.

# Long Arc A — The Harbor Pathway

Year 1:
A handful of unfamiliar Pokémon appear around one dock.

Year 2:
Researchers map sightings against shipments and discover that several transport routes could explain arrival.

Year 3:
A containment change reduces new arrivals, but one local population remains.

Year 4:
Ecological effects remain mixed. The population uses waste resources, competes in one habitat and provides prey in another.

Year 5:
The region decides whether the case becomes routine monitoring, targeted management or accepted long-term presence.

The arc can end without a villain or eradication.

# Long Arc B — Three Introductions

Three populations of the same species appear in different districts.

One came from a nursery escape.
One represents natural range expansion.
One was deliberately released.

Players initially assume one regional event.

The campaign gradually separates the cases through transport records, photographs, field surveys and individual Pokémon identity.

The lesson is that identical species do not imply identical causation.

# Long Arc C — The Management Mistake

A region acts quickly on weak evidence and relocates a newly established population.

Later evidence shows that the intervention solved one problem but created another.

The important story is institutional learning:

- preserve why the original decision seemed reasonable;
- measure unintended effects;
- revise policy;
- restore affected habitat;
- avoid rewriting history as if the correct answer was obvious from the start.
