# Public Library Circulation, Access & Service Seeds — Pass 135

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.
Date: 2026-08-29

These seeds are intentionally separate from canon. They test the continuity layer introduced in Pass 135 and are designed to remain useful even if Ouros later chooses very different regional institutions, technologies or policies.

## Design principle

The strongest library stories in Ouros should usually emerge from movement of information, copies, people and institutions over time.

Avoid defaulting to forbidden books, magical archives, omniscient librarians or theft conspiracies.

Use chronology, branch history, old labels, service disruptions, incomplete metadata and competing legitimate interpretations.

## Seed — The Book Is Here, the Hold Is Not Ready

A requested title physically arrived at the destination branch this morning. The catalog still shows it in transit because receiving check-in has not completed.

The player can establish:

- shipment delivered;
- branch receipt pending;
- copy identity intact;
- requester queue position unchanged;
- no evidence of loss.

Narrative lesson:

`PHYSICAL_ARRIVAL != READY_FOR_PICKUP`.

No battle required.

## Seed — The Return Slot Belongs to the Old Branch

A sealed return slot remains in a wall after the library moved two streets away. Residents still refer to the corner by the old branch name.

A recent photo appears to show someone returning a book there.

Possible resolutions:

- the slot is decorative and no longer serviced;
- a temporary arrangement still collects returns there;
- the image predates the branch move;
- the pictured item was not a library copy;
- the building owner preserves the slot as local memory.

No vandalism or deception is required.

## Seed — Two Copies, One Annotation

Two editions of the same local-history work circulate in different branches. One contains a handwritten correction in the margin from a prior reader or staff member.

The annotation may be:

- accurate;
- partly accurate;
- based on now-superseded research;
- a personal memory;
- a pointer to another source.

The note itself is evidence of interpretation history, not world truth.

## Seed — The Mobile Stop Became the Meeting Place

A mobile library served a neighborhood during a long renovation. After the branch reopened, the stop ended, but residents still gather at the former location every week.

Possible persistent consequences:

- a market stall shifts its schedule to match the old stop day;
- a reading club continues there;
- a Pokémon that learned the routine still appears at the same time;
- a notice board remains in use;
- the branch later creates a small satellite service point because the routine persisted.

## Seed — The Queue Survived the Outage

A network transfer service was suspended for several days.

Existing holds remained valid, but notifications failed for part of the period.

The mystery is not who stole the queue. It is reconstructing which users were actually notified, which copies arrived during the outage and which pickup windows should be considered current under the eventual authored policy.

Do not invent a policy outcome until canon defines the institution's rules.

## Seed — The Library Is Open, the Local History Room Is Not

The main branch is operating normally. A local-history room is temporarily unavailable because material is being rehoused after a building issue.

The player needs one source from that room for another investigation.

Possible handoffs:

- Archives offers an authorized reproduction;
- another branch has a duplicate published edition;
- a catalog record points to a public copy elsewhere;
- staff can answer a narrow reference question without opening the room;
- the research must wait.

The scene demonstrates scoped service without arbitrary locked-door design.

## Seed — The Book Was Never Missing

A title was reported missing from a shelf.

Later evidence shows:

- it was checked in;
- moved to a hold shelf;
- then rerouted to another branch;
- the public catalog snapshot lagged behind the internal transfer event.

The apparent disappearance is produced entirely by legitimate state transitions.

## Seed — The Former Gym Shelves

A building once hosted a battle institution. Years later, the same rooms became a library branch.

Visible traces remain:

- floor markings under shelves;
- reinforced doors reused for stacks;
- an old challenge-board frame now holding community notices;
- a storage room still called by its former battle nickname;
- older residents using the predecessor name.

The exploration focuses on adaptive reuse rather than nostalgia exposition.

## Seed — One Address, Three Branch Names

A branch moved twice within one block over several decades. Old citations, local newspapers, branch stamps and oral histories use three names for what residents consider the same institutional lineage.

The player must distinguish:

- institution continuity;
- physical site continuity;
- naming continuity;
- collection continuity.

These need not align perfectly.

## Seed — The Reference Answer Changed

A librarian answered a local-history question five years ago using the best catalog and sources available at the time.

A newly processed collection changes the likely answer.

The older response remains historically honest.

Possible story consequences:

- a plaque now needs review;
- a school handout cites the older interpretation;
- a family memory disagrees with both;
- the library publishes a correction note;
- the underlying question remains partly unresolved.

## Seed — The Pokémon Waits at the Old Pickup Window

A recurring individual Pokémon used to accompany its Trainer to a temporary pickup counter during reconstruction.

The counter moved months ago, but the Pokémon sometimes still waits at the old spot before rejoining its Trainer.

Agency guardrail:

This does not prove ownership, intelligence level, reading ability or institutional role beyond what canon has established for that individual.

## Seed — The Branch That Accepts Returns but Cannot Lend

A small branch experiences a local service problem. Staff can still accept returns and answer reference questions, but circulation from the shelves is temporarily paused.

This creates visible everyday continuity:

- return carts fill faster than normal;
- holds are rerouted;
- residents travel to another branch for borrowing;
- the reading room remains active;
- a club meeting proceeds.

No crisis escalation is necessary.

## Seed — The Wrong Pickup Branch Is Still Correct

A patron believes their hold was routed incorrectly.

The history shows the pickup location was changed before the copy was assigned, then changed again after a notification had already been generated.

Both messages may be valid for their timestamps.

The task is chronology reconstruction, not proving staff incompetence.

## Seed — The Copy Came Back Different

A circulating book returns with a replacement cover or repaired binding.

The work, edition and copy identity remain the same unless the institution's own provenance rules say otherwise.

Questions:

- when was the repair performed;
- by whom;
- did the catalog note update;
- does an old photograph still help identify the copy;
- is the change conservation, ordinary repair or replacement of a component.

Archives/Museums handles preservation provenance if the item crosses into special-collection status.

## Seed — The Reading Room Moved Upstairs

During renovation, the reading room temporarily moved to another floor. It later returned to the original floor, but some signage, accessibility routes and community habits did not revert.

This can seed:

- an alternate entrance still preferred by regulars;
- a shelf-numbering mismatch;
- a former temporary desk reused for reference service;
- a new route through the building retained permanently.

## Mystery — Five Times the Book Was “Available”

Five records use the word available:

1. work exists in catalog;
2. a local copy is not checked out;
3. a returned copy is in check-in;
4. an assigned hold copy is ready for one requester;
5. a reading-room copy can be consulted but not circulated.

The mystery works by disambiguating service semantics.

## Mystery — Four Timestamps, One Return

Records show:

- exterior return-slot scan;
- staff collection from the slot;
- check-in event;
- reshelving event.

A witness remembers only one event: dropping the book off.

All timestamps can be true.

## Mystery — The Citation From a Branch That Did Not Exist Yet

A recent essay cites a branch name that was adopted after the historical source was originally catalogued.

Possible explanation:

The citation uses the current repository name for a digitized or transferred copy, not the historical holding location.

The player distinguishes source creation date from current access location.

## Exploration — The Basement With Three Shelving Systems

A branch expanded several times.

The basement preserves:

- original fixed shelf labels;
- a later movable-stack numbering scheme;
- a modern simplified section map.

Old photos and routing slips let players reconstruct how material moved through the building.

No hidden switch is required.

## Exploration — The Library Inside the Former Station

A discontinued transport building was converted into a branch.

Environmental storytelling can show:

- a platform edge reused as a reading terrace;
- old timetable frames reused for community notices;
- luggage rooms repurposed as stacks;
- branch-transfer crates moving through a former freight entrance;
- a route that still shapes pedestrian access even though trains no longer stop there.

The story can hand off to Transport/Construction/Public Memory without duplicating them.

## Long arc — A Town Learns Where Its Books Go

Phase 1 establishes normal routines:

- two branches;
- a mobile stop;
- a local-history room;
- recurring patrons and Pokémon;
- transfer days;
- reading clubs;
- ordinary holds and returns.

Phase 2 introduces a limited disruption:

- renovation;
- route closure;
- communications outage;
- catalog migration;
- branch move.

Only some services pause.

Phase 3 shows adaptation:

- pickup rerouting;
- temporary mobile stops;
- reference help through another branch;
- local returns still accepted;
- queue records preserved;
- residents learning new routines.

Phase 4 resolves physical service before social routine fully resets.

The main branch reopens, yet:

- one mobile stop remains popular;
- one club keeps meeting elsewhere;
- a Pokémon retains an old route;
- a temporary desk becomes permanent;
- new signage reflects a predecessor name;
- transfer volume shifts between branches.

Phase 5 reuses the accumulated history in a later investigation.

A source request years later can be solved because old routing labels, branch names and service records were preserved rather than reset.

## NPC archetypes

The Route-Memory Librarian

Knows how material used to move between branches. Useful but not infallible.

The New Cataloger

Understands the current system better than local historical naming. Needs older staff or records for predecessor context.

The Mobile-Stop Regular

Knows neighborhood routines and attendance patterns but has no special institutional authority.

The Branch Transfer Driver

Knows physical routing and delivery times, not catalog truth or patron eligibility.

The Local-History Volunteer

Can point to finding aids and public sources but must not be written as omniscient custodian of community truth.

The Reader Who Uses Three Names for One Place

Embodies long-term institutional continuity through ordinary speech.

## Faction / institution dynamics

Library Service ↔ Archives

Shared interest in access and preservation; different authority over circulating copies versus special collections.

Library Service ↔ Education

Students and instructors may use branches, but course credit and academic progression remain Education-owned.

Library Service ↔ Courier

Transfer reason and receiving state belong to Library Service. Physical shipment execution belongs to Courier/Logistics.

Library Service ↔ Construction/Maintenance

A branch can adapt around work without Construction deciding circulation state.

Library Service ↔ Communications

Notification delivery can fail while the hold state itself remains valid.

Library Service ↔ Accessibility

Access accommodations can shape pickup or reading-room service without inventing universal eligibility rules.

## Encounter concept — Branch Closing-Time Withdrawal

Full version:

Visitors and staff are still leaving while a tactical incident occurs near the exit. Rich play could include protected withdrawal lanes, Intercept, timing windows and objective-aware AI.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED baseline
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — PARTIAL
- core calculations — VERIFIED baseline
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED baseline
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for protected zones/reactions
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING

Reduced version — READY:

Library closes first. Civilians, staff, records, carts and noncombatant Pokémon leave BattleSpec. AutoPTU receives explicit combatants and static geometry. Victory only clears immediate access.

## Encounter concept — Transfer Crate Chokepoint

Full version:

A paused library shipment is caught near a route obstruction. Rich play could involve protection, escort, Intercept and objective-aware withdrawal.

Reduced version — READY:

The shipment remains stopped outside BattleSpec. Combat clears the route. Courier and Library Service later decide dispatch/receipt.

Victory never authenticates a copy, completes delivery or fulfills a hold.

## Encounter concept — Temporary Reading-Room Access Perimeter

Full version:

A temporary service location suspends access because of a nearby threat. Rich play would require protected exits, timing and semantic playback.

Reduced version — READY:

The temporary room closes before combat. Readers and material are removed. The tactical encounter happens nearby on static geometry. Reopening remains world-state authority.

## Canon questions intentionally unresolved

- Which Ouros regions operate public libraries?
- Are they municipal, League-linked, academic, guild, private-charitable or other institutions?
- Which branches circulate material?
- Does membership exist, and on what authored basis?
- What patron data is retained?
- Are borrowing histories retained at all?
- Do some regions have mobile libraries?
- What technologies support catalogs and notifications?
- How do remote settlements request material?
- Which libraries hold local-history material versus Archives?
- What inter-regional resource-sharing arrangements exist?
- How are damaged/lost circulating materials handled?
- Are any former Gyms, stations, schools or civic halls reused as branches?
- Which individual Pokémon have persistent, canon-authored roles in library routines?

None of these answers are established by this proposal.
