# Marea Sendero — identity emergence loop

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. This document does not approve a second named or persistent Fletchling.

A useful ecological world should be able to start with a population that is only partly individualized. Most local Pokémon do not need a permanent actor record on day one. If one already-counted member later accumulates meaningful history, Ouros can promote that source into a persistent internal individual while keeping the population total unchanged.

The player-facing experience remains quieter than the internal operation. A trainer may repeatedly notice a Fletchling, see different reactions to approach, record site-use episodes and gradually form a hypothesis that the same individual is recurring. Internally, Ouros may eventually need a stable record because an individual disturbance-response history, injury history, social relationship or other durable state can no longer safely live on a pooled anonymous slot. At that point one counted source is resolved into one persistent source. Nothing appears from nowhere.

The player is not told that this transaction occurred. Their field notes remain governed by the recognition evidence they actually possess. It is therefore possible for Ouros to know exactly which persistent actor is being projected while the character still believes only that it is possibly or probably the same Pokémon.

The reduced playable version needs no battle. The same already-counted source can be observed over several windows, acquire durable ecology history, be internally individualized, disappear on unload and later return through the persistent projection path. Population remains twelve throughout.

A richer version can turn the emerging individual into a longitudinal local character without forcing capture or quest ownership. Its tolerance may diverge from other Fletchling; it may change preferred micro-sites; observers may disagree about whether later sightings are the same individual; a strong diegetic marker could eventually confirm the character-facing identity. Those extensions must reuse Passes 253–257 instead of bypassing their epistemic limits.

If the player pursues or intercepts the individual, the encounter gains exact engine dependencies. Targeting/LoS and base movement are required for ordinary spatial pursuit. Interception, blocking, push/pull or forced movement require complete movement. A turn-structured chase or battle requires action economy and full lifecycle. Automated evasion requires AI legal-action infrastructure plus AI tactical policy. Terrain, weather, hazards, zones or reactions become dependencies only when authored into the scene. Damage, statuses, Moves, Abilities, Items and Trainer Features likewise remain opt-in dependency families rather than assumed capabilities.

Open canon decisions remain separate: which kinds of individual state justify creating a persistent record; whether some species are usually individualized earlier because of social structure or rarity; whether a research institution can deliberately resolve identities; and how long an unresolved source can safely retain temporary individual history before it must either resolve or decay back into pooled evidence.
