# Ouros Non-Canon Seeds — Loss, Mourning & Memorials — Pass 37

Status: proposals only. None of these are established canon.

These seeds are original structures derived from Pass 37 research. They do not copy Pokémon canon plots, fanfiction characters, dialogue, or specific real-world mourning traditions.

## 1. The Name That Should Not Be There

A memorial wall contains the name of a person who is still alive.

Possible causes:
- clerical error;
- two people with similar names;
- an old missing-person presumption that was never corrected;
- deliberate symbolic inclusion;
- vandalism;
- a disputed historical record.

The quest is about provenance and correction, not ghosts.

## 2. The Empty Marker

A marker has no name, only a date and an old institution symbol. Archives contain several plausible candidates but no decisive identification.

Resolution can remain unresolved while the site receives better documentation.

## 3. The Pokémon That Keeps Visiting

A wild or formerly partnered Pokémon repeatedly visits a memorial site.

The system records the visits. It does not infer grief, reincarnation, ownership, or a desire to be captured.

Players may discover a prior relationship, a food source, a nesting route, habit, or another explanation.

## 4. The Caretaker's Last Season

A long-serving memorial caretaker plans to retire.

The arc centers on knowledge handoff, site maintenance, archives, visitor routines and ecological relationships that were never written down.

No death is required.

## 5. Garden versus Habitat

A memorial garden has become an important habitat for a local Ghost/Grass/Bug population.

Traditional maintenance would remove vegetation the Pokémon now depend on. Conservation actors and memorial stewards need a workable compromise.

## 6. The Wrong Photograph

A public memorial display has used the wrong photo for decades.

Correcting it affects family memory, museum records, news archives and public interpretation but not the underlying historical event.

## 7. Last Known Route

A decades-old disappearance has a memorial near the last confirmed route.

A newly reopened path exposes evidence that changes the search history without proving death or survival.

## 8. The Second Plaque

A later community wants to add a plaque that acknowledges participants omitted from an older memorial.

The conflict is about historical record and stewardship, not automatically about destroying the first memorial.

## 9. The Annual Quiet Hour

A town observes a simple recurring period of remembrance. Over the years, transport, businesses and public events have adapted around it.

The player can experience the town physically changing pace without receiving a mandatory quest.

## 10. Offerings Are Not Loot

Players find valuable-looking objects at a memorial.

They are provenance-tracked offerings. Taking them without authorization creates a custody/event state rather than normal loot pickup.

## 11. A Memorial Moves

Erosion makes an old coastal memorial unsafe.

Moving the marker preserves access but changes the relationship between memorial, burial/resting place, landscape and historical meaning.

## 12. House of Memory, New Neighborhood

A memorial building predates the district now surrounding it. The neighborhood grew into a busy commercial center.

Residents disagree about traffic, quiet hours, visitor access and preservation.

## 13. The Missing Champion

A famous competitor disappeared years ago. Their tournament record is celebrated publicly, but no confirmed death exists.

New generations treat the person as a legend while the case layer remains open.

## 14. Partner Pokémon Custody Gap

A confirmed deceased Trainer left several Pokémon but no clear custody arrangement.

Possible actors:
- family;
- former club;
- nursery;
- research institution;
- long-time friend;
- Pokémon themselves as persistent actors.

No transfer is automatic.

## 15. The Team That Keeps the Seat Empty

A club or institution deliberately leaves one old role unfilled as a tradition.

A staffing crisis later forces debate over whether preserving the empty seat still serves the institution.

## 16. Archive Before Ceremony

A recurring memorial ceremony has repeated one historical claim for generations. A new archive discovery weakens that claim just before the next edition.

The question becomes how institutions handle correction without erasing the tradition's social meaning.

## 17. The Memorial Route

Several roadside markers along a mountain trail represent different historical incidents.

Players slowly discover that the route itself changed, meaning some markers no longer stand near the places they originally described.

## 18. Ghost Population Boom

A cemetery experiences a sharp increase in Ghost-type Pokémon.

Possible explanations include seasonal migration, food availability, reduced visitors, nearby construction or an authored supernatural phenomenon.

The generator must not choose “the dead are restless” by default.

## 19. Night Watch Volunteers

Local volunteers take turns watching an old tower after repeated damage.

Each volunteer has different observations. Their witness reports conflict because they saw different nights, not necessarily because anyone lies.

## 20. The Unclaimed Box

During archive cataloging, staff find personal effects associated with a deceased expedition member but no clear owner or intended recipient.

The items are evidence/provenance objects, not rewards.

## 21. Memorial Garden Expansion

A town wants to expand a memorial garden after a major regional event.

Possible constraints:
- habitat;
- housing pressure;
- transport access;
- archaeology;
- family privacy;
- maintenance capacity.

## 22. The Abandoned Tribute

A small player/NPC-created tribute has not been visited for years. The site is weathered but still meaningful to one person.

Restoration, documentation, relocation and leaving it untouched are all possible outcomes.

## 23. The Pokémon That Outlived Everyone

A long-lived Pokémon is associated with several generations of a family or institution.

It is a living actor, not a historical artifact. It does not automatically belong to descendants or serve as a perfect witness.

## 24. The Unfinished Monument

Construction began after a disaster but stopped because the institution collapsed or funding disappeared.

Decades later, the unfinished structure has become part of the landscape and public memory.

## 25. Two Communities, One Site

Two communities use the same memorial location for different historical reasons.

Their practices need not conflict, but scheduling, interpretation and stewardship can become complicated.

## 26. A Name Restored

Research establishes that an individual omitted from an old memorial was present during the event.

Adding the name is a public-memory correction. It does not require rewriting every historical source.

## 27. The Return

A person memorialized after a long disappearance returns alive.

Required world updates:
- mortality remains/returns ALIVE_CONFIRMED;
- case state changes;
- public records are corrected;
- memorial meaning is reviewed;
- old rumors persist historically;
- the returning actor decides their own response.

The system does not force a dramatic reunion emotion.

## 28. Memorial Day Without Crisis

A large annual remembrance event occurs with no antagonist, theft, disaster or boss battle.

Content comes from:
- visiting NPCs;
- old acquaintances;
- archive exhibits;
- food and hospitality;
- performances;
- quiet conversations;
- world-state callbacks.

This seed exists specifically to protect low-pressure cultural content.

## 29. The Broken Marker

A marker is damaged overnight.

Possible causes include weather, wild Pokémon, maintenance failure, accident or deliberate damage. The case begins with evidence, not an assumed vandal.

## 30. The Tower and the Radio

A memorial institution shares a district with newer communications infrastructure. Both are important.

The story concerns noise, visitor flow, archives, local identity and technical expansion rather than one side being inherently right.

# Longer Arc A — The People We Remember

Phase 1: players encounter several small inconsistencies across memorial records in different settlements.

Phase 2: archives show that a regional disaster was remembered differently because communities received different information at the time.

Phase 3: descendants, institutions and witnesses disagree about what should appear in a new regional memorial.

Phase 4: the players help establish provenance and facilitate choices, but do not decide everyone's private feelings.

Possible outputs:
- revised public record;
- multiple interpretive panels;
- unresolved questions preserved openly;
- new archive links;
- recurring remembrance event;
- improved communication between institutions.

No hidden villain is required.

# Longer Arc B — The Quiet Tower

Phase 1: Ghost-type activity rises around an old memorial tower.

Phase 2: visitors report lights, sounds and missing offerings. Some claims conflict.

Phase 3: ecological survey, caretaker records and night observations identify several ordinary causes, while one phenomenon remains unexplained.

Phase 4: players can solve practical problems without needing to prove or disprove an afterlife.

Possible outputs:
- habitat management;
- repaired tower;
- revised visitor policy;
- protected observation area;
- unresolved postmortem claim;
- new scientific or mythic research hooks.

# Longer Arc C — After the Expedition

An expedition ended years ago with confirmed deaths, missing members and survivors.

The world now contains:
- families with different information;
- an institutional report;
- personal field notes;
- lost equipment;
- a memorial;
- an unresolved missing-person case;
- a route that has changed environmentally.

Players reconstruct what is knowable without turning every missing person into a corpse or every rumor into truth.

The final state may remain partly unresolved.

- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if fog/fragile zones are mechanical

# Encounter Contract 2 — Cemetery Perimeter Evacuation

FULL:
- visitors/civilians moving to exits;
- protect/clear-route objectives;
- possible hazard zones;
- interception and objective-aware movement.

Key blocking families:
- complete movement/interception/forced movement;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if environmental danger is tactical;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft playback.

REDUCED:
- evacuation resolves in overworld before tactical combat;
- battlefield contains combatants only;
- a standard battle resolves the remaining threat.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Old Tower Night Watch

FULL:
- changing visibility/light state;
- interactable doors/switches;
- search/reach/withdraw objectives;
- phase-driven phenomena only if authoritatively implemented.

REDUCED:
- observation and switches remain overworld state;
- tactical encounter uses fixed map geometry;
- Ghost Pokémon are normal combatants unless a separate authored supernatural entity exists.

# Canon questions opened by Pass 37

- Can normal generated play ever produce permanent Trainer death?
- Can normal generated play ever produce permanent Pokémon death?
- What exact PTU/Caelo rules establish death rather than Fainted/Injury?
- What institutions handle custody of a deceased Trainer's Pokémon?
- What burial/resting practices exist in each Ouros region?
- Are there culturally distinct memorial traditions?
- Which Ghost-related metaphysical claims are canon, which are beliefs, and which remain intentionally unresolved?
- How should player-created memorials be protected from griefing?
- Can a player explicitly opt in to post-retirement/death legacy notes for their PC?
- How should multiplayer privacy handle memorials, family records and sensitive history?
