# Pass 99 Narrative Seeds — Winter Mountain Pass, Snow & Avalanche Operations

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Nothing in this file establishes locations, institutions, weather patterns, Pokémon roles or PTU mechanics.

## The Road Was Cleared Yesterday

A mountain corridor appears blocked despite a clearing operation recorded the previous afternoon. The investigation can resolve into new overnight accumulation, wind redistribution, a different sector sharing the same local name, or incomplete public information. The point is continuity: the clearing record remains correct even though it is no longer current.

## The Pass Is Open, the Shelter Is Not

Travel has resumed through one corridor, but a seasonal refuge remains unavailable because its water, heat, staffing, communications or inspection state has not recovered. Reopening a route does not reopen every dependent service.

## The Shelter Is Open, the Pass Is Not

A refuge remains staffed during a closure to support workers, researchers or stranded travelers. Players can reach it only through an explicitly authorized route or operational assignment. This creates social continuity without making the closed pass generally traversable.

## The Snow Opened a Way

Accumulation makes an otherwise inaccessible ledge, bank or seasonal connection usable. Cartography records the temporary alignment. Travel can use it only after the appropriate assessment. When melt or new snowfall removes it, the route disappears physically but remains in local memory and records.

## The Snow Closed the Old Door

A cave, mine, shrine approach or service entrance is buried while another part of the same site remains accessible. The buried entrance is not deleted from the location graph; it becomes an unavailable historical access point that may return in another season.

## The Avalanche Was Above the Road

Travelers report that the roadway itself looked clear when access was restricted. Later evidence shows the concern came from an observed or assessed slope above the corridor. The mystery teaches that hazard evidence can originate outside the footprint of the asset being closed.

## Everyone Calls It the Same Pass

Locals use one name for several connected sectors. A courier says the pass was open, a ranger says it was closed and a traveler says the road was being cleared. All three can be correct at the same time when their statements refer to different sectors or access classes.

## The Tracks Cross the Closed Sector

Repeated Pokémon tracks appear on a winter-closed route. Conservation may investigate whether the corridor is being used because human traffic decreased, because seasonal movement shifted, or for another reason. Winter Operations records the conditions and route state; ecology owns the interpretation.

## The First Report Came From a Traveler

A non-institutional observer reports fresh slide debris before the formal observation network notices it. The report gains importance because it is timestamped and location-specific, not because the observer becomes automatically authoritative.

## The Bulletin Arrived After Departure

A group left using the latest assessment available to them. A revision was issued later. Reconstructing receipt time matters more than judging the decision with hindsight.

## The Temporary Barrier Outlasted the Closure

The route is operational again, but an old barrier or sign remains in one approach. Public Notices, actor knowledge and route truth temporarily disagree. The discrepancy can generate confusion without requiring sabotage or institutional incompetence.

## The Crew Cleared the Wrong Name

Two sectors share a historical nickname. A work order is completed exactly as written, but later review reveals the requested local name was ambiguous. The episode can lead to better sector identifiers, signage or dispatch procedure rather than a villain.

## Mystery — Five Winter Reports, Three Sectors

Inputs:

- one traveler photograph showing a clear surface;
- one report of deep drift;
- one observed slide-debris note;
- one public closure notice;
- one clearing completion record.

Potential reconciliation:

- the photograph and clearing record refer to the lower approach;
- the drift report refers to a sheltered upper bend;
- the slide report refers to a path above a third sector;
- the notice covers the corridor as a whole because that was the access decision.

The investigation can remain partially unresolved if one report lacks precise location provenance.

## Mystery — Three Clearings, One Actual Reopening

Three operations are marked physically complete over several days, but the route reopened only once. New accumulation superseded the first clearing, a post-work assessment kept the second sector closed, and only the third sequence satisfied the route owner's current access requirements. The lesson is that clearing is evidence, not access authority.

## Recurring NPC/role candidates

These are role archetypes, not canon characters:

- a mountain-route worker who knows sector names and old alignments;
- a refuge steward who remembers who arrived during closures;
- a local courier who notices access changes early because their routine is sensitive to them;
- a field observer who records conditions without owning closure authority;
- a cartographer who maintains seasonal revisions;
- a conservation worker following winter wildlife observations;
- a visitor who repeatedly misreads old route information but later becomes a reliable source for one specific location.

## Long-form arc — A Pass Learns Its Winter

Early season establishes normal travel, recurring travelers, a small refuge and the distinction between lower and upper sectors.

The first meaningful snowfall changes one approach. A limited assessment is issued. One service changes schedule while another continues.

A later accumulation or verified slide closes a sector. The closure redirects travelers, commerce and wildlife observation. A clearing operation begins but does not instantly restore access.

A temporary alignment or refuge routine becomes important during the disruption. Some residents begin treating the temporary arrangement as ordinary.

Reopening occurs in phases. One road section is usable before another. Old notices and local habits lag behind the physical change.

In another season, the players return. A previous temporary route is gone, but its use altered a business, an ecological observation record or local wayfinding. An old closure report now helps interpret a new winter event.

The arc advances by persistent facts rather than escalating disaster scale.

## Full encounter candidate — Slide Debris Search Perimeter

Intended scene:

Search/recovery activity is ongoing after a verified slide when a separate conflict develops along the safe edge of the operational perimeter.

Rich dependencies:

- protected lanes and route-control objectives;
- full reaction/forced-movement behavior;
- reviewed terrain;
- tactical AI;
- exact extraction/burial mechanics if ever authorized;
- faithful playback.

Reduced implementation:

Suspend active search movement before combat. Keep the debris field, missing-person uncertainty, responders, equipment and noncombatants outside the BattleSpec. Resolve a static perimeter encounter. The result does not find, injure, rescue or recover a search subject and does not clear the road.

## Full encounter candidate — Winter Refuge Approach

Intended scene:

A refuge is occupied during severe conditions when conflict occurs at one approach.

Rich dependencies:

- reviewed winter terrain;
- withdrawal/protection goals;
- any exact Weather/visibility mechanics;
- complete reactions/movement;
- tactical AI;
- adapter/playback.

Reduced implementation:

Occupants remain inside. The approach is closed in world state. If combat is necessary, it occurs in a stable static area with no environmental cold, snowstorm or avalanche mechanics. The refuge's operational state remains controlled by its owner systems.
