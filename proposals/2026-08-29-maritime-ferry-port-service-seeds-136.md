# Ouros Maritime Ferry, Port & Passenger-Service Seeds — Pass 136

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Requires canon review before promotion.

## Purpose

These seeds exercise the maritime-service continuity architecture without defining Ouros ports, operators, laws or routes as established facts. Names are placeholders or descriptive titles only.

## The Ferry Left From the Other Pier

A recurring island service normally uses one berth. Maintenance moves two departures to another pier for several days. Most residents adapt immediately; a visitor follows an old notice and misses the sailing.

The playable question is not whether the operator is malicious. It is which notice was current, which signs changed, which passengers received the correction and which physical route through the terminal remained usable.

Persistent outputs can include corrected signage, a revised wayfinding habit and a local joke or complaint that survives after the berth returns to normal.

## The Boat Arrived, the Passengers Did Not Leave

A vessel reaches its destination on time, but disembarkation pauses because the receiving walkway becomes unavailable. The arrival record is correct. The passenger-presence record is also correct. Neither means the other process has completed.

This seed teaches `ARRIVED_AT_BERTH != DISEMBARKED` through ordinary world state.

## Three Counts, One Crossing

Boarding staff record one total. A later onboard count differs by one. The arrival-side count matches neither.

Possible authored explanations include duplicate counting, a late crew transfer, an intermediate-stop passenger, a stale booking assumption or a simple recording mistake. The mystery should preserve all observations rather than deleting the inconvenient one.

No default explanation is person overboard.

## The Sailing That Skipped One Island

Weather or another external owner forces one stop to be skipped while the rest of the route continues. The island is temporarily harder to reach, but the whole maritime network is not closed.

Follow-on stories can involve delayed deliveries, a missed appointment, a student taking an alternate route, a researcher extending a stay or a temporary local market adjustment.

## The Substitute Vessel Has the Old Nameplates

A different vessel covers a familiar service while the regular asset is unavailable. The replacement still carries signage from a former route.

Players reconstruct the vessel's operational history from plaques, cabin numbers, photographs and crew memories. The service identity remains current even while the asset carries older physical evidence.

## The Terminal Is Open, the Ferry Is Not

The waterfront market, waiting hall and local shops remain open during a service suspension. NPC routines change without making the district empty.

This seed gives settlements a believable response to transport disruption and avoids treating every operational problem as total closure.

## The Last Passenger Is on the Wrong List

A traveler boards through an authorized institutional arrangement that is recorded separately from the ordinary passenger list. A routine reconciliation detects an apparent mismatch.

The resolution depends on provenance and record scope rather than deception.

## The Pokémon Waits at the Old Gangway

A known local Pokémon continues to appear near a gangway that the service no longer uses. Residents treat the behavior as part of the place's continuity.

The scene may connect to observation, public memory or ecology. It does not imply ownership, partnership, aggression or a supernatural ability to predict sailings.

## The Morning Ferry Became the Afternoon Ferry

A service gradually changes schedule over years due to settlement patterns, maintenance windows or another canon-authored reason. Old posters, business opening hours and resident habits preserve the earlier timetable.

A present-day mystery uses several correct historical notices from different periods.

## The Ticket Is Valid, the Destination Is Not Served Today

A passenger has valid access under the local system, but the current sailing does not call at the intended stop.

The problem can be solved through information, transfer planning or waiting for a later service. The access record and the service pattern remain separate.

## The Shipyard Finished Before the Route Returned

Maintenance or construction completes work on a vessel or berth, but passenger service stays limited because another dependency remains unresolved.

This seed creates a clean handoff between Maintenance/Construction and maritime operations.

## The Ferry Carried the Festival Home

After a large regional event, several departures carry unusual mixes of performers, vendors, spectators and families. A normally compressed route becomes socially significant for one evening.

The journey can create callbacks, rumors and future relationships without becoming a forced battle corridor.

## The Cargo Went On, the Courier Did Not

A shipment continues on a sailing while the courier is rerouted by a separate problem. Courier/Logistics keeps item custody and route history; maritime continuity keeps the sailing and transfer events.

The item does not become owned by the ferry operator merely because it is onboard.

## The Island Heard the Cancellation Late

A cancellation notice is published correctly but reaches a remote terminal slowly because Communications has degraded coverage. Residents at the terminal continue preparing for a sailing that will not arrive.

The story belongs to the intersection of Maritime and Communications. The notice truth and recipient knowledge remain separate.

## Mystery — Five Times the Ferry Was “Late”

Five records use the same casual description but refer to different facts:

One sailing started boarding late.

One departed late but recovered time.

One arrived on time but disembarked late.

One called at a substitute berth, making passengers reach the town center later.

One was on time according to the revised schedule but late according to an old poster.

The mystery trains chronology and scope rather than culprit-hunting.

## Mystery — Two Vessels, Three Names

Archived photographs show three names associated with what appears to be two physical vessels. The explanation can involve renaming, service branding and vessel substitution.

The investigation depends on asset identity, service identity and time.

## Mystery — The Passenger Who “Never Arrived”

A household says a traveler never arrived on the island. The sailing record shows the person disembarked at the terminal.

The maritime owner has completed its question. Travel, Communications or another system now owns what happened after disembarkation.

Do not stretch maritime authority into the entire onward journey.

## Exploration — The Terminal Behind the New Seawall

An older passenger terminal survives behind newer coastal infrastructure. The building now has another use, but faded gangway numbers, old waiting-room tile, route maps and altered doorways reveal its previous function.

This can run entirely through static world state and provenance.

## Exploration — The Pier With Three Heights

A harbor has successive physical adaptations visible in different gangway levels, patched stairs and old mooring points. Players can reconstruct changes in vessel scale or water-access practice without needing ship mechanics.

## NPC archetype — The Timetable Keeper

A terminal worker or local enthusiast remembers service changes with unusual precision. Their memory is valuable but still evidence, not omniscient truth.

Useful tensions include disagreement between memory and archived notice, or a correct memory about the service but an incorrect assumption about why it changed.

## NPC archetype — The Relief Master

A recurring operator covers several routes when regular crew are unavailable. This NPC naturally connects settlements without becoming a universal exposition device.

No profession mechanics or authority are implied by the archetype.

## NPC archetype — The Island Commuter

A resident travels the same crossing frequently and notices small changes in crew, vessel assignment, shoreline activity and seasonal passenger mix.

The commuter can become a strong continuity witness precisely because their observations are narrow and repeated.

## NPC archetype — The Receiving Clerk

An arrival-side staff member knows when sailings actually berth, when passengers clear the terminal and when transfer records are processed. Their role demonstrates that arrival is a sequence, not one timestamp.

## Long arc — A Coast Learns What Connects It

Phase one establishes the maritime network as ordinary life. Players repeatedly see the same terminals, vessels or service identifiers, crew, commuters and market routines. Most crossings compress.

Phase two introduces a limited disruption: one berth closes, one sailing pattern changes or one vessel is substituted. Alternate routes and changed routines become visible.

Phase three creates a small reconciliation problem or missed connection whose solution requires comparing service notices, stop calls and actual passenger events.

Phase four reveals a broader but non-catastrophic dependency: coastal construction, navigation-aid maintenance, communications degradation, seasonal ecology or weather response changes how one route operates.

Phase five restores stable service, but not necessarily the original pattern. A temporary pier may remain useful. A market may shift opening hours. A commuter may keep using a new route. A former terminal may gain a new purpose.

Months later, the world still remembers the change through physical infrastructure, archived notices, NPC habits and route knowledge.

Boarding pauses before BattleSpec creation. Ordinary passengers, unselected crew, records and controlled cargo leave the tactical grid. Ouros selects explicit combatants. ## Encounter — Arrival-Side Chokepoint

Narrative premise:

A vessel has arrived, but a separate tactical incident blocks the public exit route from the terminal.

Full intended version:

The scene could involve protected withdrawal corridors, Intercept, staged movement and objective-aware AI.

Reduced version — READY:

Passengers remain onboard or are moved to a safe nonbattle world-state location before combat. The vessel, records and ordinary crew stay outside BattleSpec semantics. AutoPTU resolves a conventional static encounter. The maritime owner decides afterward whether disembarkation can begin or continue.

Victory never creates `DISEMBARKATION_COMPLETE` or `PASSENGER_REACHED_FINAL_DESTINATION`.

## Encounter — Substitute-Berth Access Perimeter

Narrative premise:

A service has been moved to a temporary berth, but a tactical threat prevents staff from opening the passenger approach.

Full intended version:

Rich semantics would need changing access zones, protected staff movement, reactions and tactical policy that understands route-clearing rather than simple damage maximization.

Reduced version — READY:

The berth remains operationally closed while the encounter runs. Staff, signs, passenger queues and service records remain outside BattleSpec. A conventional battle occurs on static geometry. Victory can provide `IMMEDIATE_APPROACH_CLEAR`; the maritime owner separately decides whether the substitute berth opens.

## Encounter — Unaccounted-For Inquiry Perimeter

Narrative premise:

A passenger-count discrepancy is already under investigation when a separate tactical threat makes the terminal exterior unsafe.

Full version rule:

The tactical scene must never turn the count discrepancy into a rescue objective unless Crisis/Rescue already owns an actual search state.

Reduced version — READY:

Reconciliation staff and records withdraw before combat. The battle protects immediate access only. Afterward the inquiry resumes from preserved evidence.

Victory never means the passenger was found, never proves someone went overboard and never resolves the discrepancy.

## Persistent consequence examples

A route can retain a new berth after an emergency period.

A shop can change opening hours around a revised ferry schedule.

A regular passenger can become a recurring NPC.

A former waiting room can become a club room or market stall area.

A substitute vessel can become preferred by residents.

A public notice correction can remain in archives.

A Pokémon observation near an old terminal can become an Ecology/Public Memory hook.

None of these outcomes creates PTU bonuses by itself.
