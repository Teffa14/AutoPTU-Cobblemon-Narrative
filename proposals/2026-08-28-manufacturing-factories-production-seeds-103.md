# NON-CANON Seeds — Manufacturing, Factories & Production Continuity — Pass 103

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-28
Depends on: `design/manufacturing-factories-production-continuity-extension.md`.

These seeds are story candidates. Names, products, institutions, regions, technologies and Pokémon roles are placeholders until canon review.

## The Run Finished, the Goods Did Not Leave

A production run reaches physical completion before a quality review finishes. Downstream clients hear that “the factory is working again” and assume supplies should already be available.

Useful structure:
- completed run;
- release still pending;
- courier or rail dispatch not yet authorized;
- storefront shortage persists for a valid reason.

No sabotage is required.

## The Replacement Part Is in the Building

A critical replacement component arrives and everyone expects immediate restart. Maintenance still needs installation, verification and a test cycle.

Useful structure:
- procurement fulfilled;
- custody confirmed;
- repair not complete;
- line not ready;
- public expectations move faster than operations.

## Two Lines, One Missing Input

Two production lines use the same specialist material. Only one has enough remaining stock to continue.

The interesting choice is institutional and social rather than a hidden optimization puzzle: which existing obligations receive priority, who has authority to decide, what evidence supports the decision and who is informed.

No universal scarcity-price formula is introduced.

## The Batch Everyone Calls Defective

A visible difference appears in an output batch. Workers, customers and local media start using the word “defective” before the review concludes.

Possible outcomes include harmless cosmetic variation, rework, partial release, genuine rejection or unresolved evidence.

The mystery is provenance and scope, not an automatic conspiracy.

## The Tour Saw the Old Process Map

Visitors notice that the public diagram no longer matches the machinery configuration they can see.

The explanation can be ordinary:
- the line was reconfigured recently;
- the tour material is stale;
- one cell is temporary;
- the diagram deliberately simplifies restricted details.

The discrepancy becomes a documentation/update quest rather than proof of wrongdoing.

## The Temporary Cell Became the Local Specialty

An outage forces a workshop or plant to improvise a small alternate production cell. Months later the original line is restored, but customers and workers have grown attached to the temporary product/process identity.

This seed allows a technical workaround to become culture without giving it bonus mechanics.

## The Factory Restarted, the Shop Is Still Empty

Manufacturing has released a new run, but outbound logistics remain constrained.

The story crosses several existing authorities:
- Manufacturing proves release;
- Batch Traceability identifies the output;
- Logistics owns dispatch/movement;
- Commercial Services owns shelf availability;
- Public Information owns any notice.

No layer may skip the others.

## The Old Plant Still Has a Shift Change

A former industrial facility has been repurposed, but local routines still reflect its old operation. Cafés open at an old shift-change hour, a crossing still has extra lighting, retired workers use the former gate as a meeting place and delivery directions still reference a vanished loading bay.

This seed creates industrial memory without requiring a memorial monument.

## The Production Record Has Two Start Times

A run has two plausible “start” timestamps:
- setup began at one time;
- actual material processing began later.

Both records can be correct. The quest is to determine which definition a contract, report or witness meant.

## The Line Is Running Empty

A line performs a test cycle after repair, producing no saleable output. Observers see movement and hear machinery and conclude production resumed.

The world state distinguishes TESTING from RUNNING and test output from released goods.

## The Pokémon Works Here, the Species Does Not

One individual Pokémon has an explicit production role supported by governing capability evidence and a persistent relationship with the facility. Visitors generalize that the entire species “works in factories.”

The story can correct that assumption through individual identity and actual assignments. No species-level labor rule is created.

## The Safety Stop Saved the Order

A safety stop delays a high-priority production run. The apparent failure later proves to have prevented damage to WIP, workers or equipment.

The consequence is operational trust and institutional memory, not a numerical reputation bonus unless another system explicitly defines one.

## The Rework Batch Has Two Histories

A held output is partially disassembled or returned to an earlier process stage, then enters a new production run.

The final batch must retain links to both histories:
- original inputs and run;
- review/hold decision;
- rework disposition;
- new run;
- final release.

This can later matter for recall scope or a historical investigation.

## Mystery — Five Status Boards, Three Actual States

Different displays around a facility show:
- yesterday’s schedule;
- current line status;
- public tour status;
- utility status;
- outbound dispatch status.

Characters initially treat them as contradictory. The solution requires identifying scope, timestamp and authority for each display.

No hidden truth score is required.

## Mystery — Four Output Counts, Two Released Batches

Four documents report different quantities because they refer to:
- produced physical units;
- units held for review;
- units released;
- units dispatched.

The investigation teaches the difference between production completion and availability.

## Long Arc — A District Learns Its Factory

Phase 1 establishes ordinary production, workers, nearby shops, suppliers, delivery rhythms and public tours.

Phase 2 introduces a constrained input or line interruption. The facility reduces output without closing completely.

Phase 3 creates secondary effects: another workshop receives priority, a carrier changes schedule, a store sells out, workers are reassigned, or a temporary cell opens.

Phase 4 restores the technical dependency but not every downstream state simultaneously. Testing, production, release and distribution return in stages.

Phase 5 revisits the district months later. The temporary workflow, an old status board, a repurposed warehouse, a recurring operator or a changed supplier relationship becomes relevant to a new story.

The district preserves history. There is no abstract `factory_level`.

## Mechanically rich encounter — Line Shutdown Withdrawal

Full intent:
A conflict breaks out during an operational incident and the objective is to withdraw through a protected route while preventing nonparticipants from being trapped in the hall.

Dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if live machinery, heat, process areas or moving equipment matter;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced form:
The line stops before battle. Workers, WIP, machines and nonparticipants are removed from tactical participation. AutoPTU receives explicit combatants in a static aisle or yard. Victory secures the route only. Manufacturing, Maintenance and Safety decide restart later.

## Mechanically rich encounter — Loading Bay Production Hold

Full intent:
A threat interrupts the boundary between released output and outbound handoff. Characters must protect a withdrawal corridor or keep opposing combatants away from a defined perimeter.

Dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including Intercept/forced movement;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- terrain/hazards/zones/reactions if vehicles, active dock equipment or moving loads participate;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback support.

Reduced form:
Custody freezes at the last verified handoff. Cargo and vehicles remain outside the grid. Battle occurs in a static perimeter with explicit participants. Victory cannot dispatch, deliver or transfer ownership/custody.

## Mechanically rich encounter — Reconfiguration Cell Perimeter

Full intent:
An experimental or reconfigured production cell becomes contested while access routes and test equipment matter spatially.

Dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement if route denial, Intercept or displacement matters;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for energized machinery or active process zones;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback support.

Reduced form:
The cell is de-energized. Staff and equipment remain outside the BattleSpec. AutoPTU receives a reviewed static arena. Winning provides access for later inspection/testing only.
