# Institutional Succession, Vacancies & Handoffs — Ouros Seeds — Pass 153

Status: NON-CANON CANDIDATES. Nothing below becomes established lore without explicit approval.
Date: 2026-08-24

These concepts use `design/institutional-succession-vacancies-handoffs-layer.md` and preserve the authority of Governance, Workplaces, Credentials, Aging/Retirement, Institutional Review, Battle Institutions, Archives, Identity, Digital Systems and Pokémon Agency.

## Thirty candidate hooks

1. The Office Is Empty, the Service Is Not — A regional field station director retires on schedule. The office remains vacant for six weeks, but ordinary staff continue all services that never required the director's personal approval. Players encounter continuity rather than institutional collapse.

2. The Acting Director Cannot Sign That — An acting museum director can keep collections, staffing and daily loans moving, but a proposed irreversible deaccession falls outside the temporary delegation. The issue is authority scope, not personal competence.

3. The Old Name on the Gym Door — A Gym has a new Leader, but one entrance plaque still names the predecessor because maintenance has not reached that wing. The obsolete sign becomes an Identity/Public Memory problem, not evidence of two simultaneous leaders.

4. The Successor Was Trained but Never Selected — A widely respected deputy shadowed the incumbent for years. When the office opens, the actual procedure selects someone else. Training history remains valuable without becoming an entitlement claim.

5. The Former Leader Still Comes Every Morning — A retired Gym Leader visits the facility regularly and offers advice. New challengers repeatedly mistake them for the current officeholder. Public familiarity and formal authority diverge.

6. Three Keys, Two Offices — A transition package contains physical keys for spaces that no longer map cleanly to current responsibilities. The predecessor's old key ring documents institutional history but does not determine modern access rights.

7. The Appointment Was Valid, the Account Was Not Updated — A new station head has formally assumed office, but a digital permission still names the predecessor. The institution must reconcile access without treating the new holder as unauthorized in every other domain.

8. The Acting Gym Leader — A Battle Institution uses a temporary Leader while its permanent holder is unavailable. Public challenges can continue only under already-approved contracts; any format change must wait for the proper authority.

9. The Candidate Withdraws — The person everyone expects to succeed a long-serving curator declines before final selection. The world adapts instead of forcing the NPC into a destiny implied by previous foreshadowing.

10. The Public Announcement Came Early — A press release names an incoming director before their term begins. For three days, both statements are true: the successor is selected, and the incumbent still holds office.

11. The Public Announcement Came Late — A successor has already assumed operational authority, but remote settlements still receive old bulletins naming the predecessor. Communications/public knowledge lag behind institutional truth.

12. The Emergency Delegate Becomes Too Famous — During a crisis, an acting coordinator becomes publicly associated with the office. When the ordinary holder returns, some residents incorrectly assume a demotion or political conflict occurred.

13. The Officeholder Returns — A leader who previously lost or left a position later qualifies for a new term. Chronicle preserves both tenures instead of overwriting the interruption.

14. The Handoff Notebook Ends Mid-Sentence — An otherwise routine succession inherits a notebook whose final entry stops abruptly because the outgoing holder was called away. The gap may be mundane; investigators should not manufacture sabotage.

15. The Deputy Knows the Building, Not the Network — The obvious acting choice knows operations perfectly but never managed interregional contacts. A second worker becomes essential during the transition without needing a formal leadership title.

16. The Successor Knows the Network, Not the Building — The reverse case. A newly selected external leader has strong institutional credentials but needs local staff to explain decades of informal workarounds.

17. The Gym's Challenge Outlived Its Author — A famous challenge format was designed by a former Leader. The successor keeps it for several years, then reviews whether it still expresses the institution's current philosophy. No format change is mechanically authored until PTU/AutoPTU validation exists.

18. The Succession Plan Names a Position That No Longer Exists — An old continuity document designates a deputy office abolished during a restructure. The institution must update the plan before the next emergency.

19. Two Procedure Versions — A nomination began under one institutional rule, but the rule was legitimately revised before the vacancy occurred. Archives and Governance must establish which version applies; neither document is fake.

20. The Founder Wants to Leave — A player-founded club has become stable enough that its PC founder wants to stop handling daily administration. The transition tests whether the institution can persist without turning the founder into a permanent quest giver.

21. The Successor Inherits Problems, Not Blame — A new transit supervisor takes office during an old maintenance backlog. Public frustration is real, but the backlog predates the new term and remains linked to its original history.

22. The Vacancy Improves the Archive — A difficult handoff exposes how much institutional knowledge existed only in private notebooks. Staff build a better archive afterward. The next transition becomes boring, which is a success state.

23. The Honorary Title Confusion — A respected former director receives an honorary title. Visitors interpret it as current command authority. Staff must clarify without erasing the former holder's contribution.

24. The Challenge Result Is Only One Input — A local battle institution uses an authored exhibition or challenge as one step in selecting a new Leader. The AutoPTU result can be authoritative for that battle while interviews, credentials or another local procedure still determine the office.

25. The Successor Changes Nothing — A new leader spends the first year maintaining existing programs, schedules and staff. Chronicle records continuity without forcing a dramatic reform arc.

26. The Successor Changes One Thing — The same premise, but one procedural improvement follows a documented handoff failure. Institutional change remains specific instead of becoming a complete aesthetic or ideological reset.

27. The Retired Ranger Refuses to Advise — A former chief is repeatedly asked to help the successor and politely declines. Retirement is respected; institutional continuity must not depend on forcing old NPCs back into service.

28. The Successor Was Already Doing the Work — A workplace discovers that an actor had informally coordinated most daily operations for months while the formal officeholder handled external responsibilities. Appointment formalizes some authority but still requires explicit credential and access changes.

29. The Missing Successor File — The institution cannot locate one historical transition package from thirty years ago. Old photographs, payroll records, public notices and oral histories can reconstruct part of the term sequence without requiring a perfect archive.

30. Nothing Happened at the Handoff — A carefully planned transition proceeds normally. Projects continue, access changes work, public notices arrive and no adventure occurs. Years later this quiet handoff becomes the baseline for understanding a genuinely abnormal transition.

## Long arc — Five Leaders of Meridian Gym

Term 1: a long-serving Leader maintains a stable challenge philosophy and begins documenting a future transition without naming a successor.

Transition 1: the office uses its authored procedure. A promising trainee is considered but withdraws. Another actor assumes the role after the necessary institutional steps.

Term 2: the new Leader keeps most existing formats while gradually changing one public-facing practice. Old League Cards, photographs and signage continue to preserve Term 1.

Transition 2: an unexpected absence activates an acting assignment. The acting Leader can run approved challenges but cannot redesign qualification policy.

Term 3+: the original holder returns or a permanent successor assumes office, depending on established world state. The Gym's identity remains continuous across all terms.

This arc can span many campaign years. The most important output is a reliable officeholding history, not escalating battle difficulty.

## Long arc — The Station That Learned to Handoff

Year 1: a field station director leaves with only an informal notebook and several undocumented contacts. The transition works, but some projects stall.

Year 2: staff create a formal handoff package, identify restricted records and establish temporary coverage rules.

Year 3: a short emergency absence tests the acting-authority policy. Most services continue without player intervention.

Year 4: a deputy retires. The institution discovers that succession planning must cover specialist knowledge as well as leadership.

Year 5+: a new director transition is almost uneventful because the institution has learned. Chronicle still records it, but no quest is generated unless another world-state issue intersects it.

## Long arc — The Club After Its Founder

A PC-founded organization grows beyond a personal project.

First phase: the founder personally approves most activity because the institution is small.

Second phase: committees, staff or authored roles acquire bounded responsibilities through Governance/Agreements/Workplaces.

Third phase: the founder reduces involvement. A successor or distributed leadership structure is chosen using the institution's approved procedure.

Later: the founder can return as member, advisor, competitor, donor, visitor or not at all. Land, money, Pokémon, items and credentials follow their own authority layers instead of being inherited by the successor automatically.

## Encounter — Handoff During Station Evacuation

Narrative premise: an unexpected facility incident occurs on the same day a research-station leadership handoff is scheduled. The outgoing and incoming holders must preserve critical project/custody information while ordinary emergency systems operate.

FULL version:

Moving staff, escorted records/equipment, exits and changing hazards would require complete movement, objective-aware AI, adapter/playback and the environmental family if the incident has tactical effects.

Dependencies retain the permanent map above: complete movement BLOCKING; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions BLOCKING when tactical; AI tactical policy BLOCKING; adapter/playback BLOCKING; lifecycle/damage/status/moves/abilities/items/Features PARTIAL as used.

REDUCED version:

Emergency Services evacuates staff and secures records before battle. Freeze one safe static arena for any independent hostile encounter. The handoff package remains world state. Battle victory does not complete credential changes or assumption of office.

## Encounter — Acting Leader Challenge Day

Narrative premise: a temporary Gym officeholder must conduct already-scheduled challenges while the permanent Leader is unavailable.

FULL version:

If the approved challenge itself uses complex objectives, classify those mechanics individually. The acting status has no combat effect.

REDUCED version:

Use the same already-approved static challenge contract the institution used before the vacancy. The acting holder's authority is validated before AutoPTU starts. No new battle rule is created by the succession layer.

## Non-combat — Two Procedure Versions

A transition appears disputed because two official documents describe different selection procedures.

Archives establishes publication/version history. Governance establishes which version was valid when the process began. Identity verifies actors. Institutional Review handles any authored review route.

Valid outcomes include `OLDER_VERSION_APPLIES`, `NEWER_VERSION_APPLIES`, `TRANSITION_RULE_EXISTS`, `INSUFFICIENT_AUTHORITY`, or `UNRESOLVED`.

No battle is required.

## Mechanical guardrails

These proposals never authorize:

- leadership title as a PTU Trainer Class;
- Command, Charm or Guile ranks from officeholding;
- extra actions or initiative from leadership;
- custom Orders because someone became Gym Leader;
- battle victory as automatic appointment;
- supporter popularity as appointment authority;
- family relationship as succession right;
- retirement as death;
- acting status as permanent authority;
- possession of keys/credentials as officeholding proof;
- Minecraft NPC replacement as an appointment event;
- an old sign as current authority;
- a former holder's advice as mandatory;
- automatic Pokémon ownership/custody transfer with a personal office;
- a succession ceremony as a buff, healing effect or morale modifier.
