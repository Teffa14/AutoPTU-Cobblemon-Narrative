# Ouros Worldbuilding Candidates — Scientific Research, Evidence, Publication & Replication — Pass 163

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Requires continuity, originality, PTU/Caelo and implementation review before promotion.
Date: 2026-08-31

## Design intent

These seeds use the Scientific Research continuity extension without assuming that every region has universities, journals, ethics boards, laboratories, grants or identical scientific institutions.

Research stories should create durable questions, observations, methods, arguments and consequences. They should not turn every uncertainty into fraud, conspiracy, Legendary activity or combat.

## The Migration Paper Everyone Cites

A regional research team published a widely repeated claim years ago about the seasonal movement of one Pokémon population.

The original field observations still exist. The old method was reasonable for its time, but it sampled only two accessible valleys and missed a third route that later infrastructure made easier to observe.

A new project does not simply prove the old researchers wrong. It establishes that the original claim was too broad for the evidence available.

Playable nodes:

- locate surviving field notebooks and route maps;
- repeat observations in the original sites;
- survey the newly accessible area;
- compare old and new method versions;
- decide how to communicate a narrower conclusion;
- watch guides, farmers, conservation staff and local media update at different speeds.

Long tail:

Old signage, school material and public conversation can continue to repeat the broad claim until their owner systems receive a correction.

## Five Times Someone Said “The Study Proved It”

Five actors invoke the same research project for different propositions.

One refers to a raw observation.

One refers to a preliminary field report.

One refers to an internal analysis.

One refers to the final publication.

One refers to a popular summary that simplified the scope.

The mystery is reconstructing which statement the study actually supported at each stage.

No liar is required.

## The Empty Result

A team spends an entire season testing a strong local belief that a particular environmental condition predicts unusual Pokémon behavior.

The project finds no reliable relationship within its observed scope.

The conclusion matters because:

- a field station stops prioritizing the wrong monitoring window;
- a route guide revises advice;
- a younger researcher proposes a better question;
- an older local expert remains unconvinced because their experience concerns a different site and season.

No villain, battle or supernatural reveal is necessary.

## The Dataset With Two Correct Versions

A project maintains one dataset containing all collected observations and another analysis-ready version excluding records that failed a documented quality criterion.

Years later, two researchers quote different totals from the same project. Both totals are correct for different dataset versions.

Adventure value comes from provenance, not accusation.

## The Field Notebook That Changes the Conclusion

A preserved notebook reveals that several observations in an old dataset were made after researchers deliberately used food, light or another intervention to draw Pokémon into view.

The observations remain real. Their interpretation as undisturbed behavior becomes weaker.

The correction affects only a subset of conclusions.

This seed is useful for showing why `OBSERVATION_TRUE` and `INTERPRETATION_VALID_UNDER_SCOPE` are different questions.

## The Research Station That Outlived Its Question

A small station was built to investigate a phenomenon that later became well understood or ceased to be locally important.

The facility remains useful because it has:

- long environmental records;
- experienced field staff;
- local trust;
- storage and communications infrastructure;
- established access routes.

A new generation debates whether to close it, repurpose it or give it a different research mandate.

This can connect Infrastructure, Civic Governance, Employment, Archives and Research without making the station a dungeon.

## The Professor Retired; the Project Did Not

A famous project continues after its original lead retires.

The incoming team inherits:

- unresolved questions;
- methods they may want to revise;
- public expectations tied to the founder;
- staff who remember why certain choices were made;
- old samples with incomplete metadata;
- a large body of citations.

The story centers on institutional continuity rather than proving the founder secretly corrupt.

## The Sample With Perfect Custody and Bad Context

A specimen has an immaculate custody trail from collection to archive.

The scientific problem is that the collection location was recorded too broadly to answer the current habitat question.

The object is authentic. The custody is sound. The provenance is still insufficient for a new use.

This seed forces custody, authenticity and scientific relevance to remain separate.

## The Replication That Finds Half the Effect

An independent team repeats an old study using a comparable method in a different locality.

One claim is supported.

A second claim is not observed.

A third cannot be compared because the environmental conditions differ too much.

The result creates a better regional model instead of a binary winner/loser dispute between research teams.

## The Famous Retraction With Useful Field Notes

A published paper is later retracted because a major analytical conclusion cannot be supported.

Its original field notes, photographs and specimen records remain valid source material for unrelated questions.

Years later a new project can responsibly reuse those records with explicit provenance.

`RETRACTED_PUBLICATION != ALL_SOURCE_MATERIAL_DESTROYED`

## The Correction Nobody Read

A respected institution publishes a correction to an old ecological claim.

The correction reaches specialists and archives. A popular travel guide, one remote settlement and an old training manual continue using the earlier version.

The adventure is about information propagation and consequence:

- a Ranger-like local role gives outdated guidance in good faith;
- players recognize the mismatch;
- research staff can show the correction lineage;
- Media/Communications determine who actually receives the update.

## The Rival Labs Agree on the Data

Two research groups dislike each other's methods, priorities and public style.

They independently collect evidence that points in the same direction.

Their rivalry remains interesting because agreement on one finding does not erase professional competition.

Possible long-term evolution:

- shared standard for one measurement;
- competition for access to a scarce field site;
- joint emergency work during a crisis;
- disagreement over interpretation of a second dataset;
- a future student trained by both institutions.

## The Research Subject Who Stops Participating

A particular trained or habituated Pokémon has participated in a long-running noncombat observation project.

Its owner, caretaker or the relevant actor later withdraws participation, or the Pokémon's circumstances change.

The project must adapt rather than treating prior access as permanent entitlement.

No mechanical Pokémon consent rule is invented. The authored relationship/access state simply changes.

## The Archive Box Marked “Unusable”

An old research collection was labeled unusable decades ago because it lacked the metadata needed for the original question.

A new method asks a different question that the surviving material can answer.

The value comes from changed context, not from discovering a magical forgotten specimen.

## The Instrument Upgrade Broke the Time Series

A monitoring project replaces old equipment with a more capable system.

The new instrument is better, but measurements before and after the change are not automatically interchangeable.

The team must preserve the equipment transition and decide how to compare eras.

The story can produce a small but durable piece of regional history: “before the upgrade” and “after the upgrade” become meaningful scientific periods.

## The Study Everyone Thought Was About Weather

A long-running project is popularly remembered as a weather study because the most famous chart showed seasonal conditions.

The actual research question concerned habitat use.

Years later, a public dispute reveals how an accessible visual can become the remembered identity of a much narrower project.

Public Memory may preserve the simplified story while Research retains the source lineage.

## The Underfunded Survey

A small institution has a legitimate unanswered question and enough expertise to design the work, but not enough transport, field time or staff to cover every site.

The player group can help by:

- carrying equipment through already-authored routes;
- making legal observations;
- introducing staff to a transport provider;
- comparing archived records;
- clearing a separate tactical incident near a site;
- documenting why a site could not be sampled.

The generator must not convert lack of resources into a fabricated grant economy.

## The Species Guide With a Footnote War

A widely used field guide has accumulated several editions.

Most entries are stable. One behavior note has changed three times as evidence improved.

Different researchers cite different editions, and a local expert still prefers the second because it matches their valley.

The quest can trace edition lineage and scope rather than asking which author is “the fraud.”

## The Student Replicates the Mentor

A younger researcher repeats a mentor's classic observation project as a training exercise.

The new result differs.

Possible causes remain open:

- ecological change;
- route disturbance;
- different season;
- observer intervention;
- method drift;
- genuine earlier overgeneralization.

The relationship story and the scientific question can advance together without turning disagreement into betrayal.

## The Researcher Who Published Too Early

A team releases a preliminary result because the finding has immediate public relevance.

Later evidence narrows it.

The interesting consequence is procedural:

- was the preliminary status communicated clearly?
- which institutions acted on it?
- did those actions remain reasonable given what was known then?
- how quickly does the correction propagate?

This avoids hindsight morality and preserves decision provenance.

## The Null Expedition

An expedition reaches a difficult site and finds none of the expected signs.

The absence may be meaningful, or the timing/method may have been wrong.

The expedition still produces:

- a safer map;
- infrastructure notes;
- environmental observations;
- route history;
- a refined future question.

Adventure success does not require a spectacular discovery.

## The Paper That Became a Policy Dependency

A civic body adopted a habitat-management practice partly because of a published local study.

Years later that study is corrected.

The civic rule does not automatically disappear. Governance must reassess the policy with the new evidence and any additional dependencies.

This creates a clean cross-system arc:

Research revision -> Media/Notice propagation -> Governance review -> Infrastructure/Ecology consequences.

## Long arc — Thirty Years of One Valley

A valley becomes one of the best documented ordinary places in Ouros because several generations of researchers keep returning.

Phase 1 establishes baseline observations and local relationships.

Phase 2 changes the method as technology and access improve.

Phase 3 introduces a major environmental or infrastructure change.

Phase 4 produces competing interpretations of the same long time series.

Phase 5 lets a later group revisit a question that no earlier team could answer.

The payoff is not a Legendary reveal. The payoff is that an ordinary place has scientific memory deep enough for the player to recognize change.

## Long arc — The Phenomenon Gets Smaller

A dramatic unexplained regional pattern begins with broad speculation.

Successive projects narrow it:

- it occurs only in one habitat type;
- only during one season;
- only after a specific environmental transition;
- several early reports were unrelated events grouped together;
- one residual anomaly remains unexplained.

The arc rewards better explanation rather than endless escalation.

## Long arc — The Scientific Record Is a Character

Across multiple campaigns, players encounter old publications, corrected maps, preserved samples, retired researchers, revised field guides and new replication projects.

The scientific record develops its own visible history.

A later generation can responsibly disagree with earlier work while still respecting the people who produced it under different evidence constraints.
