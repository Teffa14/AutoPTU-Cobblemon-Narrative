# Ouros Illicit Networks, Smuggling & Diversion Seeds — Pass 58

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No source plot or character is imported.

## 1. The Missing Crate That Wasn't Stolen

A research station reports a missing shipment of tagged sample containers. The expected delivery record and warehouse inventory disagree. Players eventually discover an emergency reroute that was verbally approved but never entered into the digital system.

Value: teaches that diversion is not automatically theft.

## 2. The Second Manifest

A ferry operator discovers two valid-looking manifests for the same cargo slot. Both were generated by real staff at different times after a schedule change. A third party later exploited the confusion to add an unauthorized container.

## 3. The Orchard Buyers

Several rare Berries begin disappearing from a restoration orchard. The first assumption is opportunistic theft. Evidence instead points to a broker paying seasonal workers for material they incorrectly believed they were allowed to sell.

## 4. The Empty Poké Ball Rack

A clinic's repository is short several registered Poké Balls after a crowded festival. The problem becomes a custody reconstruction across staff handoffs, patient returns and temporary storage rather than an immediate burglary hunt.

## 5. The Returning Partner

A Pokémon stolen months earlier is found working calmly beside a remote mechanic. The mechanic bought it through an apparently legitimate broker and has no evidence that it was stolen. Recovery must preserve the Pokémon's identity and agency while ownership and custody claims are reviewed separately.

## 6. The Unknowing Courier

A courier regularly carries sealed parcels between two towns. One parcel is linked to an active case. The courier followed a legitimate booking and had no reason to know the contents.

The transport service must not become `criminal=true`.

## 7. The Disappearing Tags

A conservation project notices that several tracking tags are being removed from wild Pokémon shortly before specific migration windows. The tags themselves are later found intact near a roadside market.

The network, if one exists, must be established from evidence rather than assumed.

## 8. The Nursery's Back Room

A popular nursery provides genuine boarding and care. One manager quietly uses an unused wing to hold Pokémon whose transfer records do not match their actual custody history. Most employees know nothing about it.

## 9. The False Front

Residents accuse a repair shop of serving as a front because suspicious cargo repeatedly arrives after closing. Investigation shows the late shipments are legitimate emergency parts. The real diversion occurs later at a shared depot.

## 10. The Broken Seal

A museum loan arrives with a damaged security seal but every object is present. The damaged seal becomes evidence of possible tampering without proving theft.

## 11. The Broker Who Never Touches the Cargo

A recurring alias appears in messages arranging purchases, but no witness has seen the broker at a transfer point. The players can map relationships and payment promises without converting the alias into a known physical identity.

## 12. The Client List

A recovered ledger appears to name buyers. Some entries are genuine purchases, some are quotations, some are declined offers and some are initials with no reliable identity match.

The ledger is evidence, not a guilt list.

## 13. The Harbor Locker

A locker at a harbor contains an abandoned container associated with several suspicious transfers. Nobody arrives to collect it. The story shifts from interception to provenance, fingerprints/logs where supported and route history.

## 14. The Rare Pokémon Rumor

A rumor says a buyer is paying heavily for a species currently migrating through a protected valley. No transaction is confirmed. Conservation staff must decide whether to increase patrols, redact locations or do nothing disruptive without turning the rumor into truth.

## 15. The Stolen Fossil That Never Left Town

A fossil disappears from public display. Everyone assumes it was moved through the interregional port. The real object is still inside the city, hidden during an attempted unauthorized private sale that never completed.

## 16. The Salvage Dispute

A wreck-recovery crew legitimately retrieves several old containers. One contains an item whose ownership history is unresolved. A buyer approaches before the custody review finishes.

## 17. The Wrong Pokémon

A photo circulated by investigators appears to show a stolen Pokémon. Pass 46 identity analysis later finds that the visual match is plausible but not conclusive. Players must avoid merging two persistent Pokémon entities prematurely.

## 18. The Retired Uniform

A carrier repeatedly gains access to secure loading areas while wearing an old institutional uniform. The uniform is genuine but no longer proves authorization. The access failure belongs to the credential system, not to magical disguise success.

## 19. The Clean Books

A front business has accurate accounting because the illicit transfers never pass through its official books. The real link is physical custody and a staff-only storage corridor.

## 20. The Dirty Books, Innocent Cargo

A company has chaotic accounting and unexplained cash discrepancies, but the cargo under investigation is legitimate. Financial irregularity and asset-flow wrongdoing remain separate questions.

## 21. The Capture Without a Buyer

A poaching pattern appears in a reserve, but no downstream market is found. The captures may be private collecting, relocation misunderstanding, a single actor's behavior or something else entirely.

The system must permit the investigation to end without inventing a syndicate.

## 22. The Buyer Without a Capture

A broker is confirmed to be seeking a protected species, but no successful acquisition occurs. The demand signal remains relevant world state even if the planned operation fails before contact.

## 23. The Interrupted Transfer

Players reach a freight yard during a handoff. They can preserve evidence by allowing a limited part of the transfer to occur under observation, intervene immediately or prioritize a Pokémon in immediate danger.

No option receives a hidden moral score.

## 24. The Fragmented Route

After one route is exposed, several local cells stop using it. One operation dissolves, another goes dormant and a third begins looking for a new carrier. The world reacts from existing resources rather than spawning an automatic replacement network.

## 25. The Complicit Specialist

A highly respected specialist knowingly certifies false paperwork for a small subset of shipments while continuing legitimate work for hundreds of clients. Their institution must review scope and evidence rather than erase every past service they performed.

## 26. The Coerced Insider

An employee changes custody records after threats against a family member. Their actions remain real. Their motive matters to character and institutional response but does not rewrite the records they changed.

## 27. The Museum Buyer

A collector offers to purchase an object before accession is complete. The offer may be legal, unethical, unauthorized, or harmless depending on canon rules that have not yet been established.

Players can investigate provenance and mandate without the generator inventing property law.

## 28. The Release Cover Story

A network claims several missing Pokémon were legitimately released. The release records exist, but location data and witness evidence do not match. The case becomes a provenance problem without assuming the records are forged until verified.

## 29. The Unclaimed Recovery

Players recover several Pokémon from a holding site, but prior ownership records are missing or contradictory. Temporary care and custody can proceed while permanent disposition remains unresolved.

## 30. The Route Everyone Uses

A mountain pass becomes associated with smuggling in public discussion. It is also the only practical route used by farmers, researchers, couriers and residents. Enforcement or monitoring choices can create real costs for legitimate users.

## Long Arc A — The Quiet Chain

Year 1 begins with three unrelated missing-asset cases: a research sample, a Pokémon from institutional care and a museum object. The cases resolve locally but preserve route, custody and broker evidence.

Year 2 reveals that the same freight intermediary appears in two cases. The intermediary handles mostly legitimate traffic and may not know the final buyers.

Year 3 identifies a small broker network using legitimate carriers, borrowed storage and temporary front services. Disrupting it creates several successor outcomes rather than a single boss finale.

Year 4 focuses on restitution, institutional reform, route monitoring and whether the demand side still exists.

The arc can conclude without identifying every buyer.

## Long Arc B — The Protected Valley Market

A rare seasonal migration becomes publicly known after successful conservation work. Interest creates tourism, legitimate scientific requests and an unverified buyer market.

Over several seasons players may:

- improve location redaction;
- investigate missing tags;
- identify unauthorized captures;
- recover individuals;
- distinguish legal researchers from buyers;
- trace brokers;
- redesign visitor access;
- discover that some suspected incidents were unrelated.

The conservation system remains the ecological authority. The illicit-network layer only explains human/organizational flows.

## Long Arc C — The Harbor Without a Mastermind

A port experiences a series of suspicious transfers over several years. No single organization controls them.

Different actors independently exploit the same weaknesses:

- outdated credentials;
- crowded festival schedules;
- poor custody handoffs;
- unmonitored lockers;
- fragmented jurisdiction;
- inaccurate manifests.

Players initially search for a mastermind but eventually discover an ecosystem of separate opportunists and procedural weaknesses.

The final resolution is institutional improvement, not the revelation of a secret supervillain.

## Encounter Contract B — Hidden Nursery Recovery

Full premise:
Players discover a compartmentalized holding area inside a legitimate nursery. Some staff and Pokémon need to be protected while hostile actors attempt to withdraw.

Full version needs:
- PROTECT/ESCORT or equivalent objective semantics;
- complete movement/interception;
- tactical AI;
- broad reactions if exits are controlled;
- Minecraft playback.

Reduced version:
Resolve evacuation and Pokémon identity/custody before battle. Put only confirmed combatants in the tactical snapshot. Recovery is never a battle reward roll.

## Encounter Contract C — Harbor Transfer Disruption

Full premise:
A known shipment window creates a limited chance to observe or stop a handoff.

Full version needs:
- timed or phase-aware objective state;
- BREAK_THROUGH / INTERCEPT / WITHDRAW behavior;
- multiple exit logic;
- tactical AI;
- complete movement;
- terrain/weather/hazards only if they are genuinely active mechanics;
- Minecraft playback.

Reduced version:
The overworld clock decides whether the handoff completed before combat. Any battle uses a static legal arena. The case system handles what was learned or recovered afterward.
