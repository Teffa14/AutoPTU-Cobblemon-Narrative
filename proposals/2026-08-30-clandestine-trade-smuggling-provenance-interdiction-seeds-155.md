# Ouros Worldbuilding Candidates — Clandestine Trade, Smuggling, Provenance & Interdiction — Pass 155

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. These concepts are candidates only.
Date: 2026-08-30

## Design intent

These seeds use clandestine trade as a world-state problem rather than a generic evil-market faction. The recurring dramatic material is incomplete provenance, compartmentalized knowledge, ordinary infrastructure used for disputed cargo, intermediaries with different degrees of awareness, and consequences that survive one successful interdiction.

No seed below establishes a universal criminal code, customs regime, police mandate, contraband list, black-market price table, protected-species list or punishment. Any restriction must be backed by a separately approved Ouros authority or remain a proposed premise.

## Character and faction-role candidates

### The Route Broker

A person who connects pickup and dropoff contacts without physically possessing the cargo. They understand timing and relationships but may not know exact origin or final use.

Narrative value:

- demonstrates that network participation can be informational rather than physical;
- makes communications and knowledge provenance important;
- allows a captured route segment to remain incomplete.

Do not infer organization membership merely because the broker has several contacts.

### The Warehouse Clerk Who Believed the Manifest

A legitimate employee receives sealed cargo with records that appear internally consistent. Later evidence shows one upstream origin claim was false or obsolete.

Narrative value:

- separates custody from intent;
- creates an NPC who can provide useful records without being a villain;
- allows the warehouse to remain a legitimate workplace after one compromised shipment.

### The Conservation Technician With the Missing Tag Ledger

A field worker notices that several tagged Pokémon or specimens disappear from monitoring records at different times. The commonality is not obvious until route or buyer evidence appears later.

Narrative value:

- begins from ecological continuity instead of a criminal exposition scene;
- supports cross-layer investigation with Wildlife Monitoring;
- keeps rarity or protected status dependent on actual authored rules.

### The Legitimate Trader Who Fires One Supplier

A merchant discovers that one supplier cannot reconcile provenance for a class of goods. They stop buying from that source and cooperate selectively with investigators.

Narrative value:

- shows that ordinary markets can correct themselves without being secretly controlled by antagonists;
- creates downstream consequences from player investigation;
- can reduce demand for one route without solving the entire network.

### The Sealed-Parcel Courier

A carrier handled several parcels under normal service procedures and had no access to contents. Their route history is useful evidence, but possession alone does not establish knowledge.

Narrative value:

- forces the case to distinguish carrier from organizer;
- makes timestamps and handoffs useful clues;
- provides an innocent or ambiguous witness inside an illicit chain.

### The Buyer Who Asked No Questions

A buyer wanted a rare material or Pokémon and deliberately avoided asking about origin, but current evidence cannot automatically establish what they actually knew.

Narrative value:

- creates a morally difficult actor without requiring a legal conclusion;
- allows motive, knowledge and responsibility to diverge;
- supports later adjudication if Ouros canon creates an institution with authority over the matter.

### The Processor Who Changes the Evidence

A workshop transforms raw material into a finished product. The transformation makes source identification harder even if the workshop itself did not create the original provenance problem.

Narrative value:

- creates a genuine investigative consequence from ordinary production;
- lets Material Culture and crafting history matter;
- avoids magical forensic certainty after transformation.

## Local quest and mystery candidates

### The Crate With Three Origins

A sealed crate has three plausible origin records from different points in its history. One refers to the material source, one to the packing location and one to the last warehouse that issued the shipment.

The mystery works because all three records can be genuine while answering different questions.

Possible closure:

- identify which record represents which stage;
- leave ultimate source unresolved if evidence is insufficient;
- update future inspections without accusing unrelated workers.

### The Route That Looks Legal

Every visible movement uses ordinary ferries, warehouses, couriers and storefronts. The provenance break happened before the goods entered those systems.

Player challenge:

Map the chain without treating every downstream participant as a conspirator.

Potential consequence:

A legitimate carrier introduces new verification steps for one supplier class after the case.

### The Broker Who Never Touches Cargo

Investigators find repeated communications linking otherwise separate handoffs, but no evidence that the broker ever possessed the subjects.

The case can establish coordination more easily than ownership or direct custody.

### The Buyer Who Asked No Questions

A collector or specialist buyer repeatedly purchases items with weak provenance. The player can document purchasing behavior and seller connections, but knowledge of illicit origin remains a separate claim.

This seed is stronger if the buyer has also made legitimate purchases from the same market.

### The Carrier Who Wasn't a Member

A driver, courier, sailor or Pokémon-assisted transport worker moved one disputed shipment. Public rumor immediately labels them part of the responsible faction.

Investigation can reveal a narrower truth: they were hired for one leg and knew only the delivery address.

This can become a Public Memory correction story afterward.

### The Tagged Pokémon That Came Back Without Its Tag

A monitored Pokémon reappears after a long absence. The telemetry tag is gone. A later storage record contains a damaged tag with a matching identifier, but the chain between those events remains incomplete.

Possible outcomes:

- evidence supports capture and later release;
- tag loss happened independently;
- the damaged tag was moved separately;
- accepted ambiguity remains.

The Pokémon's current agency and welfare stay outside the criminal-market inference.

### The Auction Lot With a Missing Week

An item appears at a legitimate auction with a mostly coherent provenance except for one week when custody cannot be reconciled.

The auction can pause or withdraw the lot if its own authored policy supports that action. Missing provenance does not automatically prove theft.

### The Ferry Manifest Was Accurate

A manifest correctly lists every container aboard. The disputed item was already concealed inside one correctly listed container before loading.

The useful reveal is that a correct manifest and a compromised cargo chain can coexist.

### The Workshop That Bought the Wrong Batch

A respected craft workshop bought raw material from a supplier whose origin claim was later challenged. Finished pieces are now distributed across the region.

Possible followups:

- provenance review;
- voluntary customer notice;
- recall only if another governing system establishes a reason;
- historical preservation of one commissioned item despite the controversy;
- supplier replacement.

### The Interdiction That Moved the Market

Players stop one recurring handoff point. The immediate route closes. Weeks later, ordinary world-agency evidence indicates different actors are testing another corridor.

The new path should exist only if those actors have knowledge, motive, resources and access. It cannot spawn automatically because a “crime meter” remains high.

### The Empty Compartment

A vehicle has a concealed compartment, but it is empty when inspected. The compartment proves a physical feature, not what it previously carried or who knew about it.

Old repair records, witness accounts and route timings may generate leads, but not an automatic conclusion.

### The Old Permit, New Shipment

A genuine authorization document relates to a previous project, route, species, quantity or time window. Someone presents it with a later shipment.

The credential can be authentic while insufficient for current scope.

Possible mystery:

Was the old record reused deliberately, mistakenly copied or attached by an uninformed clerk?

### The Route Through Ordinary Shops

Several small retailers unknowingly receive goods from the same compromised wholesaler. The player first sees the pattern as unrelated shop inventory.

After investigation, some retailers switch suppliers immediately, one disputes the evidence and another cannot afford the transition.

This turns supply-chain correction into local social consequences rather than a single raid.

### The Recovery Nobody Can Return Yet

A valuable object or Pokémon is recovered from a disrupted chain, but multiple ownership or custody claims conflict.

The correct short-term result can be safe neutral custody while the case remains unresolved.

`RECOVERED != RETURNED_TO_OWNER`

For Pokémon, safe care and agency take priority over treating the individual as loot.

### Three Handoffs, Two Lies, One Missing Witness

Three transfers are independently documented. Two participants gave false statements about timing, but their lies do not necessarily concern the origin of the cargo. The only person who may know the source is currently unavailable.

A satisfying resolution can prove the route while leaving source intent unresolved.

## Provenance mysteries

### Five Times Someone Said “They Were Caught With It”

Five records use similar public language but refer to distinct facts:

- the item was in the same vehicle;
- the person had physical custody;
- the person signed the warehouse release;
- the item was found inside their rented space;
- the person was present when another actor opened the container.

The mystery is resolved by decomposing the phrase into precise events. None of those facts alone establishes ownership, knowledge or guilt.

### One Item, Four Places, No Teleportation

A historical item appears in source-site notes, a transit photograph, a warehouse ledger and a storefront inventory within a plausible timeline. People later interpret the records as contradictory because each document uses a different item name.

Identity and chronology can reconcile the records without invoking a duplicate or supernatural event.

### The Shipment That Changed Names Three Times

A batch is described by source material, processing grade and retail product name at different stages. An investigator initially mistakes these as three different cargos.

The payoff comes from provenance reconstruction rather than discovering a hidden mastermind.

## Longer arc candidate: The Market That Keeps Changing Shape

Status: NON-CANON campaign arc proposal.

Opening phase:

Small local anomalies appear independently: missing tagged Pokémon, a damaged archaeological site, unexplained material shortages, one shop with weak supplier documentation and recurring sealed deliveries at an inconvenient hour.

No source initially proves a common network.

Middle phase:

Players can follow several routes. One leads through wildlife monitoring, another through a merchant, another through courier records, another through a warehouse or port-call discrepancy. Some participants are knowingly involved, some are negligent, some are deceived and some simply have relevant records.

The investigation gradually establishes a handful of real handoffs. A broker or organizer may emerge, but the graph remains partial.

First major intervention:

A handoff, storage site or route can be disrupted. The physical win matters: subjects can be recovered, one source protected, one buyer cut off, one route rendered unusable or one intermediary exposed.

The Chronicle does not declare the whole market defeated.

Adaptation phase:

World Agency checks which remaining actors have enough knowledge and resources to respond. Some stop participating. A legitimate business changes supplier. One intermediary disappears from the chain. Another tests a new route. Demand can fall if players address the buyer side or alternative supply becomes available.

Late campaign payoff:

The strongest ending may have no single final boss. Possible closures include:

- a major chain mapped and closed while smaller unrelated trade persists;
- source communities gain better stewardship and monitoring;
- legitimate traders establish stronger provenance expectations;
- a faction loses a revenue stream and changes strategy;
- a buyer network fragments;
- recovered Pokémon receive individually appropriate care or placement;
- a historical collection has its provenance corrected;
- a disputed case remains open because intent cannot be proved.

This arc is intended to make supply, custody and local economies persistent parts of the world rather than temporary villain scenery.

## Encounter contract candidates

### Warehouse Handoff Perimeter

Full intended version:

Two or more groups are present around a scheduled handoff. Some want to complete the transfer, some want to delay or withdraw and some may protect an exit. Cargo location and custody matter tactically.

Mechanical dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including Intercept, forced movement and contested withdrawal;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline and status lifecycle as selected combat content requires;
- terrain/zones/reactions if doors, loading lanes or reaction windows are active;
- individually audited move-specific behavior, abilities, items and Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for flee, delay, protect and route choices;
- adapter/playback for doors, cargo position and environmental state.

Full version: BLOCKED under Pass 155 evidence.

Reduced version: READY at narrative-contract level.

Reduced constraints:

- cargo remains outside BattleSpec;
- custody is frozen before initiative;
- all noncombatants are removed from the tactical roster;
- geometry is static;
- explicit combatants only;
- no carry, steal, escort or semantic protect objective;
- permitted result: `IMMEDIATE_WAREHOUSE_HANDOFF_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

Ouros determines later custody and whether the handoff actually occurs.

### Transit Yard Withdrawal Corridor

Full intended version:

A carrier or intermediary attempts to leave a rail, road or port transfer area while vehicles, cargo lanes and other actors create changing tactical routes.

Full dependencies include complete movement, lifecycle, hazards/zones/reactions, tactical AI and adapter/playback for moving or changing infrastructure.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

Vehicles are stationary for the tactical slice. Cargo and uninvolved workers remain outside BattleSpec. The allowed physical result is `IMMEDIATE_TRANSIT_YARD_EXIT_ROUTE_CLEAR`.

A clear route does not prove that any particular actor escaped; that requires an explicit post-battle world-state transition.

### Holding-Site Recovery Perimeter

Full intended version:

The player reaches a site where Pokémon or other living subjects are being held. A rich version would support protection, contested extraction, escort/rescue movement and opponents choosing whether to delay, capture, flee or protect resources.

Primary blockers:

- escort/rescue semantics;
- complete movement;
- reactions/zones if active;
- lifecycle interactions;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: CONDITIONAL READY only when existing Ouros facts allow the living subjects to be moved to a safe non-tactical state before initiative. If the rescue itself must be simulated tactically, the encounter remains blocked.

Permitted battle result: `IMMEDIATE_HOLDING_SITE_PERIMETER_CLEAR`.

`BATTLE_WON != POKEMON_OWNERSHIP_TRANSFERRED`

`TACTICAL_FAILURE_WITHOUT_RESCUE_CONTRACT != HARM_CONFIRMED`

### Market Exit Chokepoint

Full intended version:

An exposed seller, intermediary or buyer attempts to leave a temporary market site while allies choose between withdrawal, delay and carrying goods.

The rich form requires complete movement, potentially object carrying, turn lifecycle, tactical policy and playback support.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

Sellers, buyers and goods that are not explicit combatants remain outside BattleSpec. Static geometry and an explicit roster are used. Permitted result: `IMMEDIATE_MARKET_EXIT_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

The battle cannot establish who owned the goods, whether a sale occurred, who knew their origin or whether the wider chain remains active.

## Adapter-facing visual opportunities

When authoritative state already exists, Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics can make this layer visible through:

- changing warehouse inventory and empty shelves;
- tagged or formerly tagged Pokémon models;
- sealed and relabeled crate variants;
- legitimate storefronts changing suppliers;
- stopped vehicles during reduced tactical slices;
- investigation boards showing only discovered route segments;
- public notices after an authored policy or case decision;
- facilities becoming quieter, busier or abandoned after chain disruption.

These are projections of decided state. Presentation never creates illicit status, guilt, route knowledge or combatants.

## Canon questions raised

Before any of these seeds become canon, Ouros still needs explicit decisions about which resources or activities are restricted in which places, what institutions have inspection or custody authority, whether wildlife capture or trade is regulated beyond PTU capture mechanics, what forms of ownership records exist, how recovered Pokémon are handled, and whether any recurring clandestine markets or organizations are part of established regional history.
