# Ouros Residential Life & Relocation Seeds — Pass 56

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

These candidates are original Ouros material derived from high-level patterns in the Pass-56 research note. Names, places, institutions and social structures are placeholders until canon review.

## 1. Three Roofs, One Problem

Premise:
A resident needs to move because their current home conflicts with one persistent household requirement. Three plausible residences exist nearby.

Playable structure:
- inspect the current home to confirm the actual problem;
- visit three alternatives;
- each alternative fails or succeeds for a different concrete reason;
- discover that one apparent deal-breaker can be solved by a small adaptation;
- allow the resident to choose using the evidence rather than a hidden "best" answer.

Possible constraints:
- difficult access to a care service;
- recurring night noise;
- poor route connection to work;
- unsuitable outdoor space for an observed Pokémon routine;
- seasonal exposure;
- unresolved maintenance.

Persistent outputs:
- a changed residence record;
- a new recurring route;
- old neighbors remain contacts;
- the chosen home's maintenance/accommodation history begins.

No rent, ownership, tenancy law or housing reward is established by this seed.

## 2. The Balcony Visitor

Premise:
A wild Pokémon repeatedly shelters on the same balcony or roof edge. The household is divided over whether the situation is dangerous, harmless or simply inconvenient.

Arc:
- document when and why the Pokémon appears;
- distinguish one individual from a species-wide behavior claim;
- check whether human activity created the attraction;
- test non-mechanical accommodations or deterrence;
- escalate only if actual encounter state supports conflict.

Potential long-term outcomes:
- coexistence;
- a changed household routine;
- a stewardship referral;
- relocation of a human activity rather than the Pokémon;
- a later callback when the same individual returns after a seasonal change.

Canon caution:
The Pokémon is not owned, befriended or domesticated automatically.

## 3. Room Over the Workshop

Premise:
A craftsperson or service worker lives above their workplace. Expansion of the business creates a conflict between residential quiet, storage, customers and work capacity.

Player decisions can include:
- map actual usage of each room;
- identify which activity creates the conflict;
- move storage or customer flow;
- arrange a separate workspace if existing world state allows it;
- choose whether the resident keeps a live-work arrangement.

The point is to show that economic growth can change daily life without producing a generic "business level up."

## 4. Last Address on the Old Route

Premise:
A transport or public-works change makes one residential pocket harder to reach. One long-term NPC remains there after most routine traffic shifts elsewhere.

Hooks:
- deliveries become less reliable;
- visitors stop arriving casually;
- the resident's daily route changes;
- a mobile service becomes more important;
- a later civic proposal may emerge from accumulated evidence.

The arc should not assume abandonment, poverty or forced relocation. The affected resident can prefer to stay.

## 5. The Spare Room Promise

Premise:
An NPC previously offered temporary lodging to another person if a specific future need arose. That need now exists.

Gameplay:
- verify the promise and current household capacity;
- check whether circumstances changed;
- preserve the host's right to revise or limit the offer;
- coordinate a temporary move;
- create a review trigger instead of treating temporary lodging as permanent residence.

Social boundary:
Do not infer family, romance or friendship labels from the arrangement.

## 6. Water in the Wall

Premise:
Several adjacent homes show small signs of the same maintenance problem, but no single household has enough evidence to identify the cause.

Structure:
- compare observations across residences;
- map timing and location;
- separate rumor from confirmed damage;
- trace the issue to a shared infrastructure, weather or site-state dependency if evidence supports it;
- hand off any settlement-scale repair to the civic/public-works system.

This creates a neighborhood mystery without making every house a dungeon.

## 7. The Move That Changes a Routine

Premise:
A recurring NPC relocates within the same settlement.

Implementation purpose:
This is deliberately small. The value comes from changing several future encounter surfaces:
- morning route;
- nearby shop/service;
- neighbors;
- travel time to work;
- where the NPC is likely to appear during an event;
- which environmental state they observe first.

The old home remains in history and can still matter later.

## 8. Shared Courtyard Agreement

Premise:
Several households share a courtyard or similar semi-public residential space. Different routines now conflict there.

Possible pressures:
- Pokémon exercise;
- deliveries;
- quiet hours;
- temporary event overflow;
- habitat overlap;
- repair access;
- children's or club activity where canon supports it.

Resolution should preserve multiple interests. A single persuasion roll should not decide the whole space.

## 9. Recurring arc — A Block Learns Its Residents

Purpose:
Create residential continuity across multiple seasons without a soap-opera obligation.

Stage 1 — Familiarity
Players learn a handful of recurring residents, routes and shared spaces.

Stage 2 — Small mismatch
One routine, service or environmental state starts causing friction.

Stage 3 — Adaptation
A household changes a route, room use, accommodation or schedule.

Stage 4 — External pressure
A public work, festival, ecological change or service interruption tests the new arrangement.

Stage 5 — Memory
Later residents refer to the earlier change as part of how the neighborhood works now.

The arc remains optional. It should surface when player history intersects it rather than demanding routine check-ins.

## 10. Encounter seed — Stairwell Evacuation

Status: mechanically rich; full version currently blocked by several engine families.

Premise:
A residential incident leaves one legal exit obstructed by panicked or hostile Pokémon activity.

Full intended version:
- residents move toward a safe zone;
- the map has narrow circulation;
- forced displacement and interception can change route safety;
- hazards may close or reopen tiles;
- opponent AI may prioritize escape, territory or obstruction;
- players may protect rather than defeat.

Dependency categories:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

Reduced executable version:
Evacuate all residents through narrative state before combat. Run one static-map legal battle with only combatants. The result determines whether the building can reopen, remains closed or needs another noncombat solution. No escort rules, moving hazards or civilian tokens are simulated.

## 11. Encounter seed — Courtyard Habitat Conflict

Status: mechanically rich; full version currently blocked by objective-aware AI, dynamic zones and adapter support.

Premise:
Recurring wild Pokémon activity around a shared residential courtyard becomes an active confrontation while residents are already outside the tactical space.

Full intended version:
- territorial or withdrawal goals matter;
- dynamic occupied space may matter;
- some routes should remain open for retreat;
- destructive victory may create worse ecological and neighborhood outcomes;
- AI needs to understand non-KO objectives.

Reduced executable version:
Clear residents first. Use a fixed legal battlefield and normal combatant roster. Resolve any stewardship, repair, relocation or coexistence outcome afterward from authoritative battle state plus prior observations.

## 12. Noncombat seed — The Apartment Fit Survey

Premise:
A Trainer and one known Pokémon are evaluating a move. The Pokémon has one observed environmental preference, while the Trainer has a separate work or service constraint.

The scene can run entirely through narrative systems:
- inspect candidate homes;
- compare route/service state;
- review observed Pokémon behavior;
- check maintenance and access;
- let the player or NPC decide.

No species-wide housing bonus or PTU modifier is created.
