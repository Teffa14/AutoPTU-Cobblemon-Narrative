# Severe Wind, Windthrow, Debris & Impact Recovery Seeds — Pass 132

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.

These seeds use the proposed severe-wind impact continuity architecture. They do not establish regional climate, agencies, storm classes, warning thresholds, legal authority or tactical wind mechanics.

## 1. The Branch Was Gone Before the Survey

A major branch blocked a narrow road shortly after a wind episode. The route owner removed it quickly because access mattered. Hours later, another team arrives to understand the incident and discovers that the original resting position is gone.

Available evidence can include:

- a resident photograph;
- the clearance crew's timestamp;
- a vehicle camera image;
- the tree's prior maintenance record;
- an early route report;
- the later cleared scene.

The question is not whether cleanup was wrong. The interesting problem is reconstructing what was known before a legitimate operational action changed the site.

Potential owners: Road, Forestry, Public Space, Archive/Photography.

## 2. The Sign Came From Somewhere Else

A large sign panel appears in a courtyard after strong wind. Everyone initially assumes it came from the damaged storefront across the street.

Small details make that origin uncertain:

- old fixing holes do not match;
- the paint layer reflects an older business identity;
- an archived photograph shows a similar panel two blocks away;
- the assumed source building lost a different component.

The mystery becomes material provenance rather than sabotage.

Potential owners: Commercial Services, Found Property, Public Notices, Building Safety.

## 3. Three Reports, One Tree

A control room receives three reports:

- tree across Hill Street;
- branches blocking the clinic route;
- line damaged beside the old stairs.

The first map plots three incidents. Later evidence shows that one large tree fell across a boundary where all three descriptions are locally reasonable.

The early map remains historically valid as an information state. Dispatch and recovery records can explain why three teams were almost sent.

## 4. The Tree Was Already Leaning

After an event, residents disagree about whether a prominent tree had changed.

One group remembers it always leaning. Another insists the lean is new.

Evidence may include:

- seasonal photographs;
- a survey image;
- maintenance notes;
- old paintings or postcards with uncertain perspective;
- recent roots exposed after rain;
- a pre-event observation by a known local Pokémon watcher.

No conclusion about tree health should be created until the appropriate owner interprets the record.

## 5. The Park Reopened Around the Tree

Most of a park is usable again. One sector remains under review because damaged vegetation has not been fully assessed.

Residents begin treating the temporary perimeter as a new walking loop. Pokémon routines adapt to the quieter sector edge.

Weeks later the sector reopens, but the alternative loop remains popular.

The recovery produces a durable place change without requiring a dramatic disaster.

## 6. The Wind Ended at Midnight, the Street Opened at Ten

Several NPCs say the incident “ended” at different times.

Possible milestones:

- last damaging gust observed;
- public weather notice expired;
- first assessment access approved;
- obstruction removed;
- route inspection completed;
- route reopened;
- public notice updated.

All can be true.

This seed works as a chronology tutorial for the player.

## 7. The Line Is Down, Nobody Knows Its State

A cable or line is observed on the ground after wind damage.

The first narrative fact is only the observation.

The area can be kept clear through owner decisions, while Grid determines its actual state. NPC dialogue must not convert appearance into `LIVE`, `DEAD`, `SAFE` or `RESTORED`.

This creates disciplined tension without requiring the player to perform invented electrical mechanics.

## 8. The Shop Uses the Side Door Now

A storefront's main entrance was temporarily unavailable after a wind episode. The business used a side entrance for several weeks.

After full restoration, regular customers and nearby Pokémon still approach the side first. Deliveries have also become easier there.

The owner considers keeping the new pattern.

A tiny emergency adaptation becomes persistent world history.

## 9. The Pokémon Were Here Before the Storm

Residents report a group of wild Pokémon in a courtyard after severe wind and assume they were displaced.

A night-shift worker has photographs from weeks earlier showing the same group using the courtyard after closing.

The storm may still have changed their routine, but their presence itself is no longer evidence of displacement.

Potential owners: Coexistence, Wildlife Monitoring, Ecology.

## 10. The Pokémon Were Not Here Before the Storm

The reverse case also matters.

Longitudinal records show that an individual or group appears for the first time immediately after an event. That supports a new investigation but still does not prove why they moved.

Possible hypotheses:

- storm displacement;
- food availability changed;
- a prior habitat became inaccessible;
- routine seasonal movement coincided with the event;
- unrelated human activity changed nearby.

The narrative should preserve competing explanations.

## 11. The Temporary Barrier Outlived the Hazard

A temporary barrier was installed around a wind-damaged feature. The hazard is eventually resolved, but the barrier stays because removing it was never assigned.

Months later residents treat it as ordinary street furniture.

A maintenance sweep discovers that the physical object remains even though every related incident record is closed.

This creates light environmental storytelling about the gap between world state and institutional memory.

## 12. The Missing Awning Has Two Histories

A market stall loses an awning during a wind event.

The current operator says it was new. Archive photographs suggest an older frame was reused. Procurement records show replacement fabric but not the frame.

The player reconstructs which physical components belong to which period.

This can connect Construction, Commercial Services, Procurement and Public Memory without requiring wrongdoing.

## 13. The Hill Street Map Changed at Lunch

An 08:00 impact map shows four sites. The 13:00 map shows seven.

The additional three were not necessarily new damage. They may be:

- reports that arrived late;
- duplicate reports resolved to new scopes;
- areas that could not be accessed earlier;
- impacts discovered only when services resumed.

Both maps should remain available in the Chronicle.

## 14. The Fence Moved and Nobody Owns the Conclusion

A fence section appears several meters from its recorded location after strong wind.

Land records can establish previous mapped geometry. Construction/Maintenance can identify the fence. The wind-impact record can preserve the displacement observation.

None of those facts alone changes a property boundary.

This seed explicitly exercises the Land layer guardrail.

## 15. The Old Windbreak Is Now a Story

A line of trees historically sheltered a market road. One event removes part of that cover.

Recovery choices can create different long-term outcomes:

- replanting;
- redesigned stalls;
- seasonal closures;
- a new sheltered path;
- a memorial interpretation of the old tree line;
- no replacement at all.

The key is to let Forestry, Public Space, Commercial Services and Memory own their pieces.

## Mystery: Five Times the Storm “Ended”

Status: NON-CANON MYSTERY SEED.

A later argument about response quality relies on five dates/times all labeled informally as the end of the storm.

The player reconstructs:

1. last forecast validity time;
2. last damaging wind observation in the district;
3. end of an access restriction;
4. completion of route clearance;
5. closure of the specialized impact episode.

The answer is that the records describe different questions.

No falsified timestamp is required.

## Mystery: Four Roof Panels, Three Buildings

Status: NON-CANON MYSTERY SEED.

Four detached panels are documented after an event. Three nearby buildings show exterior damage.

The challenge is to reconstruct likely origin using:

- dimensions;
- fastener patterns;
- material/color layers;
- pre-event photographs;
- construction records;
- location and timing of discovery.

One panel may predate the event and have been stored outside.

The mystery should not automatically establish structural safety or liability.

## Mystery: The Same Fallen Tree in Two Archives

Status: NON-CANON MYSTERY SEED.

Two photographs appear to show the same distinctive fallen tree, but metadata places them years apart.

Possible resolution:

- they are different trees with similar form;
- one file inherited an old caption;
- the location name changed;
- an archived photograph was reused in a later public notice;
- one image shows the tree before removal and the other a preserved trunk segment elsewhere.

This is an Archive/Photography provenance puzzle first. It should not default to evidence tampering.

## Exploration: The Sheltered Lane Behind the Market

Status: NON-CANON EXPLORATION CANDIDATE.

A narrow service lane now looks oddly overbuilt for its quiet use.

Environmental evidence reveals that it once became the main pedestrian path during a prolonged recovery period:

- reinforced door hardware;
- faded temporary route markings;
- a removed barrier anchor;
- old delivery-number plates;
- photographs of market stalls facing the opposite way;
- resident stories about the weeks when the exposed street was closed.

The player reconstructs how a temporary adaptation changed commercial geography.

Implementation today:

READY using static world geometry, archive evidence and ordinary exploration. No tactical wind rules required.

## Exploration: The Ridge With Two Tree Lines

Status: NON-CANON EXPLORATION CANDIDATE.

A ridge shows two visibly different generations of planted trees. The break between them corresponds to an old wind-impact episode and later restoration project.

Evidence can include:

- planting records;
- forestry photographs;
- old path maps;
- public notices;
- a surviving bench or shelter that changed orientation;
- Pokémon observations before and after the canopy changed.

The location becomes a readable history of recovery rather than a lore plaque.

Implementation today:

READY if all relevant facts are already authored by owner systems.

## Long arc: A Ridge Learns Which Side Takes the Wind

Status: NON-CANON LONG-TERM ARC.

### Establishment

The district is introduced during ordinary weather. Players learn exposed and sheltered routes through normal errands rather than a tutorial popup. Local Pokémon have established routines. Businesses use familiar securing practices.

### Warning

A forecast indicates unusually strong wind is possible. Different owners respond according to their own mandates. Some preparations are visible; others are not.

### Impact

The event remains geographically uneven. One tree obstructs a road. Several signs or fixtures are displaced. A grid fault appears. A public-space sector needs review. Most buildings remain usable.

### Information problem

Early reports contain duplicates and uncertain origins. Cleanup begins before all surveys are complete. The player can preserve evidence, compare timestamps and route information to the correct owners.

### Recovery

Road, Grid, Building Safety, Maintenance, Forestry and Public Space resolve their own scopes at different times.

### Social persistence

A temporary lane remains popular. A market changes its layout. One business keeps a side entrance. Tree cover changes a Pokémon routine. A resident becomes the person everyone asks for old photographs.

### Later reuse

Years later a smaller wind event occurs. The old episode provides baselines, photographs, known exposed locations and institutional memory. The second story can therefore be richer without being larger.

## NPC archetypes

### The First Mapper

Builds an early practical impact map under uncertainty. Later revisions should not erase their original information state.

### The Clearance Foreperson

Changes the physical scene because access has to be restored. Their work can be both correct and inconvenient for later evidence reconstruction.

### The Before-Photo Collector

Maintains mundane photographs of storefronts, trees and streets. Their archive becomes valuable after change.

### The Owner-System Liaison

Knows which question belongs to which institution and prevents one team from turning an observation into another team's conclusion.

### The Local Pokémon Watcher

Recognizes recurring individuals or group routines well enough to establish baseline presence without claiming telepathic knowledge of motive.

### The Temporary-Route Regular

Represents how recovery changes habits. They keep using the workaround after the original problem is gone.

## Faction dynamics

### Route operator versus survey team

Both can be correct. One needs immediate access restored; the other wants the original scene preserved.

### Grid operator versus public rumor

A visible downed line creates understandable fear. The Grid owner still controls the technical state.

### Business owners versus public-space recovery

A street can technically reopen before customers return, tree cover is restored or pedestrian habits reset.

### Forestry versus simple storm narrative

Prior vegetation condition may complicate the clean story that “the wind did it.” This should produce evidence work, not automatic blame.

### Ecology versus capture-first response

Unusual Pokémon presence after a storm creates an observation question before it creates a removal or capture question.
