## 1. The Pool That Cooled First

One pool in a multi-zone bathhouse becomes noticeably cooler over several mornings. Visitors immediately assume the source is weakening because a famous regional spring once failed in the past.

Evidence says otherwise: the other pools remain stable, and the affected pool is the only one fed through an older branch line.

Playable structure:
- compare temperature/flow observations by zone and time;
- speak with a morning regular who noticed the change before staff did;
- inspect old maintenance records;
- trace the dependency graph;
- determine whether the problem is line-specific, configuration-specific or still unresolved.

Persistent consequence:
A repair can leave visible replacement pipework, a new monitoring point or a revised service notice. The old public explanation may remain in local memory even after correction.

## 2. The Spring Everyone Swears Heals

A small spring has a long local reputation for helping people after difficult travel. Visitors repeat increasingly specific claims about what it can cure.

The important conflict is informational rather than supernatural: an inn uses careful historical language, a new promoter uses stronger medical-sounding claims, a clinician objects to the wording, and longtime residents disagree about what the tradition ever meant.

Possible player work:
- trace the oldest accessible versions of the claim;
- separate personal testimony, cultural language, advertisement and medical assertion;
- help revise public information without declaring the tradition false or mechanically true;
- observe how tourists react to the correction later.

Mechanical boundary:
No soak creates HP, Injury, status, AP, Move-frequency, Loyalty or buff effects. If characters spend qualifying downtime there, ordinary PTU Rest rules may apply through the authoritative time/rest system.

## 3. Morning Bath, Evening Habitat

A natural outflow pool is used by residents in one part of the day and repeatedly visited by wild Pokémon after closing.

A proposal to extend public opening hours appears harmless until monitoring shows that the later window overlaps the recurring wild use.

Possible outcomes:
- keep current hours;
- shift human access earlier;
- create an alternate viewing area;
- close the natural pool while keeping indoor facilities open;
- gather more evidence before changing anything.

The wild Pokémon are not automatically hostile, protected, owned or individually persistent unless other systems establish those facts.

## 4. The Old Pipe Under the New Courtyard

A recently renovated courtyard repeatedly develops a narrow warm strip in cold weather. Current facility drawings do not show a line there.

Investigation can connect:
- old photographs;
- a retired worker’s memory;
- a superseded facility map;
- a thermal observation;
- maintenance records from before the renovation.

The solution may reveal an abandoned line that is still warm, an old channel reused by newer infrastructure, or insufficient evidence to know its purpose.

This can become archaeology of ordinary infrastructure rather than ancient ruins.

## 5. Steam Without Hot Water

A visible plume near the rear of a bathhouse convinces visitors that the thermal source is stronger than usual. Inside, several pools are actually cooler.

The plume and reduced service can share a cause, have separate causes or merely coincide.

The scenario is designed to punish visual overconfidence without requiring a villain. Minecraft particles make the symptom legible, while Ouros stores the observation and provenance.

## 6. The Guest Who Knows the Old Temperature

A returning visitor insists that a particular pool “was always much hotter years ago.” Staff records do not go back that far.

The witness can provide useful local-history evidence but cannot establish an exact technical baseline alone.

The player may compare:
- older reviews/guides;
- photographs showing steam under comparable weather;
- archived maintenance notes;
- other longtime users;
- geological monitoring if any exists.

The final output may remain a bounded claim rather than a solved mystery.

## 7. The Overflow Channel Became a Nursery

A formerly ignored warm outflow channel now shows recurring juvenile Pokémon activity. Maintenance wants to clear vegetation and restore full drainage capacity before the busy season.

The actual question is sequencing:
- what condition does the drainage asset require?
- how persistent is the ecological use?
- can work occur in another window?
- can flow be rerouted without inventing new infrastructure?
- does Conservation need a longer observation period?

No breeding mechanics are inferred from juveniles being present. Nursery/lineage rules remain separate.

## 8. Closed Pool, Open Town

One famous pool closes for structural repairs. Media and visitors start describing the entire thermal district as “closed”.

In reality:
- other baths remain open;
- lodging continues;
- local residents maintain ordinary routines;
- the source itself is healthy;
- only one zone is unavailable.

The scenario tests information granularity and how an inaccurate but understandable shorthand can affect visitor behavior.

## 9. The Water Is Fine, the Bath Is Closed

A source inspection finds normal flow and temperature, but the facility remains closed.

Possible unresolved dependencies:
- staffing gap;
- damaged changing/service area;
- access-route closure;
- pump/holding-tank inspection;
- utility outage;
- unresolved wildlife presence in a service corridor.

The source being “fine” is not a command to reopen.

## 10. The Sand Bath Is Older Than the Building

A current bathhouse is built around a thermal practice/site that predates the present structure. Renovation exposes an older surface, marker or channel that changes the historical interpretation of the place.

This can bridge:
- Material Culture;
- Archives;
- Public Memory;
- Geology;
- Maintenance;
- local tradition.

The discovery does not prove ritual meaning or ownership. Those remain separate claims.

## 11. The Quiet Pool Becomes the Famous One

A secondary pool receives unexpected attention after a widely shared image or public figure visit. Visitor pressure shifts away from the facility’s original main attraction.

Consequences can include:
- queues in a zone never designed for them;
- resident routines displaced;
- new signage;
- maintenance pressure;
- promotion disputes;
- ecological disturbance around an adjacent outdoor area.

Nothing needs to be mechanically “better” about the famous pool.

## 12. Two Temperatures, One Sign

A facility sign describes one thermal source, but the building was expanded years ago and now uses two supply lines with meaningfully different conditions.

The sign is historically understandable and operationally misleading.

This becomes a small Public Notices + Archives + Maintenance correction story.

## Mystery — Three Pools, Two Supply Lines

Premise:
Three baths change differently during the same week. One gets cooler, one retains normal temperature but loses flow late in the day, and one appears unaffected.

Evidence package:
- morning/evening observations;
- source readings if available;
- facility maps from two revisions;
- maintenance work order history;
- statements from regular users;
- a recently changed valve or pump configuration;
- public rumor blaming a visible wild Pokémon near the upstream path.

Possible solution families:
- two pools share a line and one has a local branch restriction;
- an old map hides a later reroute;
- one observation came from a different time window;
- source output varies but a holding tank masks the change for one zone;
- evidence remains insufficient.

No solution should be selected merely to create a culprit.

## Mystery — Four Stories About the Same Spring

Four sources describe why a spring is important:
- a tourist guide;
- a local oral-history interview;
- an old facility brochure;
- a geological report.

They appear contradictory because they answer different questions: cultural importance, marketing history, physical source and personal memory.

The investigation teaches source scope rather than revealing that three people were “wrong”.

## Long arc — A Spring Town Learns Its Source

Stage 1 — Baseline

Establish ordinary thermal life before a crisis:
- resident use;
- visitor use;
- recurring staff/regulars;
- which facilities depend on which source/line;
- any wild use around natural zones/outflow;
- existing public stories.

Stage 2 — Minor anomaly

A small change appears: one pool cools, a flow window changes or a maintenance reading no longer matches old expectations.

The problem is playable but does not close the entire district.

Stage 3 — Competing explanations

Residents, visitors, staff and specialists hold different evidence. A popular explanation may spread faster than technical review.

Stage 4 — Bounded intervention

A repair, source-access change, schedule adjustment, ecological protection or revised operating practice addresses the evidence actually established.

Stage 5 — Reopening and correction

Source restoration, facility readiness and public correction occur as separate steps. Some spaces can reopen before others.

Stage 6 — Second-order consequence

A successful change creates a new issue:
- visitor pressure returns quickly;
- an alternate route becomes permanent;
- a previously quiet pool becomes popular;
- wildlife changes its use window;
- a new monitoring practice exposes an older discrepancy.

Stage 7 — New baseline

The thermal district remains the same place but carries visible history: repaired infrastructure, changed signs, revised routines, different use windows and remembered earlier explanations.

## Encounter concept — Bathhouse Evacuation Perimeter

Narrative premise:
Wild Pokémon enter a service edge while guests are present. The facility closes affected zones before anyone knows why the intrusion occurred.

FULL version intentions:
- civilians move toward multiple exits;
- protection/clear-route positioning matters;
- facility zones can matter if tactical zone support exists;
- Intercept/reactions can protect withdrawal;
- AI understands territory/retreat;
- presentation remains synchronized to AutoPTU events.

Dependencies are the same broad families as Thermal Source Access Withdrawal, with particular reliance on complete movement, terrain/zones/reactions, tactical AI and adapter playback.

REDUCED version:
All guests and noncombatant staff leave before BattleSpec creation. Ouros closes the bath zones in world state. A conventional battle occurs in a dry service yard or access court. No pool, steam, heat, slippery surface or civilian target receives invented combat mechanics. The facility stays closed until its owning systems complete review.

## Encounter concept — Warm-Outflow Boundary Dispute

Narrative premise:
A maintenance project needs access to an outflow where wild Pokémon have developed a recurring use pattern.

FULL version intentions:
- CLEAR_ROUTE/WITHDRAW rather than simple defeat;
- protected work boundary;
- territorial/retreat AI;
- potentially dynamic water/terrain state;
- complete movement and reactions around narrow access.

REDUCED version:
Maintenance pauses. Workers leave. Conservation and facility state define a fixed no-work boundary. Any combat is an ordinary static encounter with explicitly selected actors. Afterward, access policy is still decided by evidence and stewardship, not by victory.
