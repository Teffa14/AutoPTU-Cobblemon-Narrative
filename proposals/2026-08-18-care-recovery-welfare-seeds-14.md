# Care, recovery and Pokémon welfare seeds — pass 14

Status: NON-CANON Ouros candidates.

These are original design seeds derived from broad research patterns. None should become canon or receive mechanical healing values until reviewed against PTU/Caelo and AutoPTU.

## 1. The Route Six Pattern

A local clinic notices that several unrelated wild Pokémon arriving from the same route show similar observable distress. The clinic can publish an aggregate advisory without identifying individual patients.

Possible branches:
- environmental exposure;
- construction runoff;
- changed food source;
- displaced wild collective;
- false correlation caused by incomplete reports.

The investigation should not start with a predetermined culprit.

## 2. The Center With No Beds

A regional event leaves a normally quiet Center operating above capacity. Routine healing still follows governing mechanics, but referrals, waiting space, staff schedules and transport become world-state problems.

Players can help with logistics without being turned into unqualified doctors.

## 3. A Specialist Across the Water

A Pokémon has an unusual condition that the local facility cannot confidently assess. The meaningful problem becomes getting records, patient and specialist into the same care network while weather and ferry availability evolve.

No new disease mechanics are required.

## 4. The Patient Who Keeps Returning

A wild Pokémon is treated, released and later returns to the same safe location. Staff record the repeat behavior.

Possible interpretations remain open:
- reliable food/water;
- remembered safety;
- unresolved habitat problem;
- proximity to its normal range;
- simple coincidence.

Do not automatically label the Pokémon bonded to the staff.

## 5. Recovery Garden

A settlement converts unused land beside its care facility into a quiet recovery and enrichment space.

The project can involve:
- local growers;
- artisans;
- researchers;
- volunteers;
- Pokémon with validated capabilities;
- neighboring residents with competing land-use concerns.

Its reward is persistent place-state, not a fabricated healing bonus.

## 6. The Wrong Route Home

After a wild patient is stabilized, the clinic discovers that its original release route is temporarily unsafe because of a current world event. Players help identify a valid return path using real ecology and travel state.

## 7. The Quiet Week

A clinic that usually treats many minor wilderness cases suddenly receives almost none from one area.

Absence becomes evidence.

Possible causes:
- route closure;
- population migration;
- fewer Trainers traveling there;
- a new safe passage;
- wildlife avoiding the area;
- records missing because another facility is handling cases.

## 8. The Traveling Care Van

A mobile care service visits remote communities on a schedule. Its arrival affects local services, supplies and NPC routines.

Later arcs can involve:
- breakdown;
- reroute;
- temporary expansion;
- new settlement requesting a stop;
- specialist riding with the team for one circuit.

## 9. The Veteran Nurse’s Map

An experienced caregiver keeps a practical map of where recurring injuries tend to originate: unstable cliffs, thorn patches, bad crossings and dangerous corners.

The map is accumulated observation, not omniscient world truth.

Players can verify or challenge old assumptions as the region changes.

## 10. After the Gym Battle

A recurring rival appears at the same care facility after a major match. The scene focuses on recovery, professional respect, embarrassment, strategy discussion or silence depending on actual prior relationship state.

The system records observable interaction only.

## 11. Supply Shelf Empty

A common field-care supply is temporarily unavailable because an existing transport route failed.

The story can connect crafting, transport and clinic logistics without changing the item’s PTU effect or price automatically.

## 12. The Clinic Apprentice

A young NPC is learning practical care but is not yet mechanically qualified for every procedure.

Their arc revolves around:
- observation;
- supervised tasks;
- referral judgment;
- confidence;
- boundaries of competence.

Do not grant Medicine ranks or Features through narrative prose.

## 13. The Restless Patient

A Pokémon is mechanically stable but repeatedly tries to leave the recovery area. Staff need to understand what observable stimulus or need is driving the behavior.

The answer might be environmental, social, routine-based or unknown.

The system should not call it “stubborn” or “traumatized” without authored evidence.

## 14. The Old Center Wing

A care facility contains an older section built for a regional problem that no longer occurs often. A new event makes that forgotten infrastructure relevant again.

This can reveal institutional history through architecture rather than exposition.

## 15. The Sanctuary Debate

A community proposes creating a long-term sanctuary for Pokémon that cannot immediately return to normal habitat.

Stakeholders may disagree over:
- location;
- funding;
- ecological impact;
- capacity;
- public access;
- ownership assumptions;
- whether temporary care is becoming permanent captivity.

No side starts automatically correct.

## 16. One Symptom, Three Causes

Three patients display a similar visible symptom but have different underlying causes.

This seed exists specifically to prevent the generator from teaching players that repeated appearance always means one conspiracy or outbreak.

## 17. The Field Station

An expedition establishes a temporary field-care station before entering a hazardous region.

Preparation may include:
- verified supplies;
- evacuation route;
- communication plan;
- rest location;
- transport contingency;
- personnel availability.

The station reduces logistical uncertainty, not mechanical damage by fiat.

## 18. A Pokémon Helps at the Clinic

A specific Pokémon with legally validated abilities/capabilities assists staff in a narrow role.

Examples should be authored only after rules validation.

The narrative value comes from an individual having a job and routine, not from species stereotypes.

## 19. Night Shift

Players visit a familiar Center at an unusual hour and meet a different staff rotation, different local visitors and different kinds of cases.

This expands institutional life without generating a crisis.

## 20. The Environmental Referral

A clinician determines that treating individual patients will not solve the recurring problem and refers the issue to ecological researchers or local authorities.

The care case produces a world investigation rather than a stronger medicine.

## 21. Recovery Milestone

A significant Pokémon companion reaches an authored recovery milestone after a prior event.

Examples:
- resumes normal exploration behavior;
- returns voluntarily to a place it previously avoided;
- rejoins a familiar collective;
- completes light training.

This milestone never substitutes for Injury removal or mechanical readiness.

## 22. The Visiting Specialist

A specialist arrives in town for several days. Their presence temporarily changes referral options and may attract NPCs or researchers from surrounding areas.

The specialist should have a reason for being there beyond serving the player.

## 23. The Waiting Room Network

Several unrelated NPCs meet while waiting for patients. Their conversations create low-stakes social links, rumors and future introductions.

Private medical details stay private unless a character explicitly shares them.

## 24. Reopening Day

A damaged Center or clinic reopens after a crisis-recovery project.

The event can include:
- staff return;
- community gratitude;
- altered building state;
- new emergency procedures;
- memorialization of the disruption;
- a backlog of ordinary care.

The building’s reopening becomes a settlement milestone.

## 25. The Wild Release

Players assist with returning a treated wild Pokémon to an appropriate area.

The challenge lies in ecology, transport and observation, not capture.

Possible outcomes:
- clean release;
- patient refuses to leave immediately;
- home range changed;
- collective moved;
- route became unsafe;
- follow-up observation scheduled.

## 26. Caregiver Burnout Without a Meter

A recurring caregiver’s schedule and behavior show they are overextended during a prolonged regional event.

The game can make workload visible through:
- missed breaks;
- reduced availability;
- delegation;
- shorter conversations;
- another staff member covering duties.

Do not assign a mental-health diagnosis or hidden “burnout score” automatically.

## 27. The Trainer Is the Patient

A case reminds the world that Trainers can require treatment too. Their Pokémon remain nearby according to authored behavior and facility policy.

This avoids treating human harm as narratively invisible while Pokémon receive all institutional care.

Exact Trainer healing remains PTU/Caelo mechanics.

## 28. Clinic Records From Ten Years Ago

Aggregate archived records show a past pattern that resembles a present one.

The older records may be incomplete because:
- diagnostic standards changed;
- routes were different;
- species distributions changed;
- the old facility served a different catchment.

History guides investigation without becoming proof.

## 29. A Center Known for One Ecosystem

A care facility becomes regionally respected for experience with Pokémon from a nearby marsh, coast, mountain or industrial district.

Its specialization emerges from repeated local work over time.

This can shape referrals and NPC identity without granting automatic mechanical bonuses.

## 30. The Best Outcome Is Rest

A quest ends with the correct decision being to stop pushing forward and allow recovery rather than immediately pursuing the next objective.

The design challenge is to provide meaningful alternative activity during that downtime so the player does not experience recovery as punishment.

## Longer-term arc candidates

### The Care Network Arc

Several settlements begin with different levels of medical access. Over many smaller stories, players can help improve transport links, referral routes, specialist coverage, field stations and clinic resilience.

No single villain is required.

Regional progress becomes visible through shorter travel-to-care distances and new services.

### The Unusual Cases Arc

A sequence of apparently unrelated patient observations slowly creates a credible ecological hypothesis. Some cases support it; others prove to be unrelated.

The final resolution may involve environmental restoration, policy, infrastructure or research instead of combat.

### The Center Through Generations Arc

A facility’s staff, building and specialties change over years of world time. Apprentices become practitioners, old staff retire, additions are built, and previous crises become institutional memory.

Retired player characters may be referenced only through confirmed prior actions and consent-safe legacy rules.

## Mechanical and canon guardrails

These proposals must not define:
- healing amounts;
- Injury removal values;
- Center treatment times;
- Medicine DCs;
- status cures;
- restorative item effects;
- loyalty changes;
- trauma mechanics;
- disease mechanics;
- ownership rights over treated wild Pokémon;
- mandatory prices;
- staff qualifications;
- sanctuary law;
- player medical privacy law.

Those require PTU/Caelo, AutoPTU implementation and eventual Ouros canon decisions.
