# Pass 169 Proposals — Structural Inspection, Load Rating, Condition Monitoring

Status: NON-CANON WORLD BUILDING CANDIDATES.

Date: 2026-08-25

Authority note: these candidates use `design/structural-inspection-load-rating-condition-monitoring-protocol.md`, which extends Architecture and Public Works. None establishes an Ouros structure, institution, engineering code or PTU mechanic until separately approved.

## 30 candidates

### 1. The Crack That Was Already There
A post-storm inspection finds a prominent crack in a bridge pier. Archived photographs show the crack predates the storm. The question becomes whether it changed, not whether the storm “caused the crack.”

### 2. The Bridge Passed Last Month
A routine inspection found no restriction necessary. A later impact triggers a special inspection. The old inspection remains correct for its date and does not immunize the structure from later change.

### 3. Three Inspections, One Defect
Three teams observe the same corroded member over twelve years using different documentation methods. Chronicle must reconcile the records before deciding whether deterioration accelerated.

### 4. The Load Limit Changed; the Bridge Did Not
A new service vehicle or revised assessment changes the permitted operational class. Minecraft geometry is unchanged. Road Transit must reroute only the affected service.

### 5. The Heritage Truss and the New Bus
A historic crossing remains suitable for ordinary local use but cannot immediately support a proposed heavier transit service. The civic question becomes route design, strengthening, replacement or a different vehicle—not “save history versus progress” by default.

### 6. After the Flood, the Deck Looks Fine
The visible deck is undamaged after high water. A foundation/scour review remains open because the relevant evidence is below the waterline.

### 7. Scour Below Clear Water
A calm day after a flood creates false reassurance. Earlier monitoring recorded a deeper transient scour condition than the current streambed shows.

### 8. The Sensor Drifted, Not the Bridge
A displacement monitor appears to show gradual movement. Metrology review finds instrument drift. The alert was legitimate; the structural conclusion changes.

### 9. Repair Complete, Restriction Remains
A contractor completes the authored repair scope, but the operational restriction stays active until verification and reassessment finish.

### 10. The Closed Bridge Brings Back the Ferry
A temporary bridge restriction revives an old passenger crossing. When the bridge reopens, the ferry may disappear again, survive as a niche service or become part of public memory.

### 11. The Wildlife Nest in the Inspection Bay
A scheduled inspection cannot reach one zone without disturbing an active nesting site. Research Ethics/Conservation and the inspection program must negotiate timing or a different access method.

### 12. The Missing Inspection Photograph
A report references an image that cannot be found. Other measurements remain valid. Missing documentation reduces confidence for that point instead of invalidating the whole inspection.

### 13. Two Maps, Two Load Limits
Two public maps show different limits because they were published under different restriction revisions. Both were correct when issued.

### 14. Temporary Shoring, Ten Years Later
Emergency shoring installed after an old incident is still present. Its continued presence is evidence of an unresolved institutional history, not automatic proof that collapse is imminent.

### 15. The Rust Rumor
A viral photograph makes surface corrosion look catastrophic. The institution needs an inspection, but the public image alone does not determine structural capacity.

### 16. The Bridge With No Visible Damage
Repeated inspections find little visible change, yet an authored load/capacity assessment remains conservative because an important foundation detail is undocumented.

### 17. The Old Crew Knows One Detail
Retired workers remember a construction change that never made it into the final drawings. Oral History preserves the account; an inspection can test whether physical evidence supports it.

### 18. The New Method Finds an Old Problem
A newer inspection method detects a condition that likely existed earlier. Chronicle records “first detected now,” not “began now.”

### 19. The Pedestrian Conversion
A road bridge loses vehicle service but remains useful as a pedestrian crossing and civic landmark. Architecture retains the same structure identity while Travel changes the allowed journey classes.

### 20. The Inspection Route Is the Hard Part
The structure itself is stable, but reaching an underside or tower zone requires specialist access. A quest can be about safe access and evidence collection rather than emergency repair.

### 21. The Repair That Hid the Evidence
A well-intentioned cosmetic patch covers a defect before it is documented. The patch may be harmless, but it complicates comparison with earlier observations.

### 22. The Foundation Nobody Can See Today
High water prevents an underwater/foundation inspection. The correct result is `ACCESS_BLOCKED`, not a fabricated safe/unsafe answer.

### 23. The Bridge Carries Less During Festival Week
An institution temporarily changes use/occupancy controls for an event while the structure itself remains unchanged. Public Space and Event systems consume the restriction.

### 24. The Replacement Structure Is Not Open Yet
The new bridge is physically complete enough to see in Minecraft while commissioning/verification remains unfinished. The old detour continues for a short period.

### 25. One Storm, Three Structures
The same storm triggers special inspections of a road bridge, market roof and water tower. Each produces a different outcome: no change, temporary restriction and repair project.

### 26. The Wrong Cause Was Famous
Residents remember a historic closure as “the flood damage year.” Later archive work shows the final restriction was actually driven by an unrelated pre-existing defect discovered during the post-flood inspection.

### 27. The Monitoring Network Outlived the Emergency
Sensors installed as a temporary response become a long-lived scientific/engineering record after the immediate concern is resolved.

### 28. The Pokémon-Made Structure Is Nearby, Not the Cause
A Bibarel dam changes local hydrology upstream of a bridge. Its existence motivates study but does not prove it caused a foundation problem. Pokémon-built Structures and Freshwater remain separate authorities.

### 29. The Inspection That Prevented a Quest
A routine review finds a small issue, Public Works repairs it during normal maintenance and nothing escalates. Chronicle records institutional competence rather than forcing player intervention.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Inspection Four
No relevant deterioration, restriction or repair follows the inspection. Years later it becomes an important baseline showing when a later change had not yet occurred.

## Long arc 1 — Five Inspections of Meridian Bridge

Year 1 establishes a detailed baseline with no restriction. Year 3 discovers a stable defect. Year 5 a flood triggers a special foundation review but no visible superstructure change. Year 7 the assessment method is revised and a limited freight restriction appears. Year 10 a targeted strengthening project is verified and the restriction changes again.

The arc works without a villain. The persistent story is accumulated evidence, institutional memory, transport adaptation and public interpretation.

Potential cross-system callbacks include an old ferry temporarily returning, a transit route choosing lighter vehicles, a heritage debate over strengthening and a wildlife monitoring program sharing access equipment.

## Long arc 2 — The Bridge With Three Load Limits

A single historic crossing carries three different operational restrictions during its life:

- an early conservative restriction after incomplete records are discovered;
- a revised limit after better inspection/analysis;
- a later scope-specific restriction when regional freight changes.

The physical bridge may change only once. The point is that operational meaning can change through evidence and use context.

A public map archive, old road signs and oral histories can all preserve different but historically correct versions.

## Long arc 3 — From Crossing to Civic Landmark

A road bridge is gradually bypassed by newer transport infrastructure. Traffic falls. The old crossing remains useful for pedestrians, local events and heritage interpretation. Later a condition issue makes vehicle service uneconomic to restore, but the structure is stabilized for a new use rather than demolished by default.

The arc can connect Architecture, Travel, Public Works, Museums/Public Memory, Accessibility and river ecology.

## Encounter contract — After-Flood Foundation Survey

Narrative premise: high water has passed. The bridge is temporarily restricted while a team documents foundation/streambed condition. Wildlife or another independent confrontation prevents access to a required observation point.

FULL version:

- dynamic researcher movement to observation points;
- possible withdrawal/crossing through threatened space;
- water/current or unstable-bank effects only if exact environmental rules are validated;
- tactical goals such as `PROTECT_RESEARCHER`, `REACH_OBSERVATION_POINT`, `WITHDRAW` and `CLEAR_ROUTE`;
- Minecraft playback of technicians, restricted lanes and equipment;
- inspection result remains world-state evidence after the battle.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED for ordinary combat;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING for dynamic access/withdrawal;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL when relied upon;
- full stateful damage — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL for actual Status effects;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if current, unstable banks, falls or protected zones have tactical meaning;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL for exact Moves;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:

Close the bridge and resolve water/foundation state outside battle. Move the team to a stable approach. If an independent confrontation remains, use an ordinary static arena with no current, collapse, falling or inspection-equipment mechanics. After battle, the team completes observations in world state. Winning never determines bridge safety.

## Encounter contract — Emergency Shoring Access

Narrative premise: a structure is already under a scoped restriction. Public Works needs access to place or inspect temporary stabilization while another conflict blocks the approach.

FULL version:

- technicians/custodians may need to reach a fixed work point;
- objective-aware AI must preserve noncombatant behavior;
- moving/falling structural elements are allowed only with validated environmental/forced-movement support;
- shoring never grants Cover, DR or structural HP unless an exact battle contract defines it.

Primary blockers are complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback; the environment family is also BLOCKING if the structure actively changes during combat.

REDUCED version:

Evacuate the work zone, freeze the structure state and place the battle on a stable adjacent approach. Public Works resumes stabilization only after the battle result is returned to world state.

## Encounter contract — Heritage Bridge Inspection Disturbance

Narrative premise: a scheduled close inspection of a historic structure coincides with a wildlife disturbance, unauthorized crowd surge or other separate event.

FULL version:

- multiple restricted zones and moving civilians/researchers;
- objectives such as `EVACUATE`, `PROTECT_INSPECTOR`, `WITHDRAW` and `CLEAR_ROUTE`;
- exact tactical treatment of scaffolds, narrow platforms or height only when supported by engine contracts.

Dependencies follow the same permanent map: complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback are BLOCKING; environment is BLOCKING when height/fall/platform states have mechanics; damage/Status/Move/Ability/Item/Feature families remain PARTIAL when invoked.

REDUCED version:

End the inspection, evacuate the structure and preserve the incomplete scope in world state. Any confrontation occurs on a static plaza/approach. A later inspection event resumes from the documented gap.

## Non-combat scenario — Condition Review Board

Architecture, Metrology, Public Works, Travel and Institutional Review compare several inspection revisions, a repair record and a proposed operational restriction.

Possible legitimate outcomes include:

- restriction unchanged;
- restriction narrowed;
- restriction widened;
- more inspection needed;
- repair proposed;
- monitoring continued;
- evidence insufficient / `UNRESOLVED`.

No battle engine is required. No persuasion roll should be allowed to erase technical uncertainty or authored institutional mandate.
