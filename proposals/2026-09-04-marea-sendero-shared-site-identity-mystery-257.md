# Marea / Sendero — shared-site identity mystery

Status: PROPOSED. The observation site, individual turnover pattern, and player-facing scene are not canon.

A familiar micro-site can become useful narrative geography without becoming a scripted spawn point. The player repeatedly notices Fletchling activity around the same small location. One visit produces a clear sighting, another produces nothing, and a later visit produces a superficially compatible Fletchling. The useful question becomes whether the place is familiar, whether the bird is familiar, or both.

The reduced scene does not resolve that question through combat. It rewards longitudinal observation. Same-place recurrence can motivate the player to compare time, behavior, route, visible traits, provenance, and later evidence. If another already-counted member uses the same place, Ouros preserves that ambiguity instead of silently assigning every sighting to the first persistent actor.

Consequences can remain small but persistent: an observer can build a field note around the site; two NPC observers can disagree about whether they saw the same individual; a later marker-confirmed or otherwise discriminative observation can retrospectively improve interpretation of earlier records without rewriting their original uncertainty.

Failure also produces content. A missed observation means the site was not detected as occupied during that window. It does not authorize the story to claim the bird died, emigrated, was captured, or abandoned the region.

A richer future version could involve two known counted individuals approaching the same usable ledge or the player following one away from it. That scene must wait for the exact movement, lifecycle, tactical-policy, terrain/reaction, and adapter capabilities it actually uses. The Minecraft layer must not fake interception or site-defense rules that AutoPTU-Java has not verified.

Canon questions deliberately left open: whether this location is merely a convenient perch, a repeat-use feeding vantage, a roost, part of a territory, or something else; how strongly Fletchling as a species tends to reuse such places in Ouros; whether seasonal or social context changes that tendency; and what evidence characters need before inferring individual identity from location history.
