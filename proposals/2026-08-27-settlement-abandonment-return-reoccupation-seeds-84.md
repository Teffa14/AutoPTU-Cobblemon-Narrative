# Ouros Settlement Abandonment, Return & Reoccupation Seeds — Pass 84

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes a settlement, institution, resident, disaster, law, historical event or ecological fact in Ouros canon.

These candidates exercise `design/settlement-abandonment-return-reoccupation-extension.md` while preserving the authority boundaries of Crisis, Residential, Maintenance, Conservation, Public Memory and AutoPTU.

## Design intent

The candidates share four principles:

- low occupancy is playable state rather than empty-map decoration;
- returning people have individual motives and constraints;
- wild Pokémon use during human absence is current ecology, not a temporary obstacle waiting to be deleted;
- recovery produces a new version of a place rather than restoring an old snapshot.

## Seed — The Town With One Light On

Premise:

Regional maps and public conversation call a small settlement abandoned. On arrival, one occupied building is unmistakably active.

Possible source state:

- most former households relocated years ago;
- one household or caretaker remained;
- one route still technically reaches the settlement;
- no current public information distinguishes `mostly abandoned` from `empty`.

Playable decisions:

- learn why the remaining actor stayed without treating them as a lore dispenser;
- update institutional/public records if the actor consents and a valid channel exists;
- inspect which services actually remain available;
- determine whether visitors have been relying on false assumptions about the place.

Persistence:

The settlement label may eventually change, but the old label remains in historical/public-memory state. The remaining household does not become mayor, owner or steward unless separate canon establishes that role.

## Seed — Return Before Reopening

Premise:

A former resident chooses to move back before the settlement has regained normal services.

Possible constraints:

- housing is usable;
- a route is open;
- local retail remains closed;
- care access requires travel;
- communications are limited;
- the resident has a personal reason to accept those constraints.

Playable value:

The player can help verify facts or dependencies, but cannot decide that the resident must return. If the move occurs, Residential state owns it.

Persistence:

The first returning household becomes a new occupancy fact without changing the whole settlement to `restored`.

## Seed — The Road Came Back First

Premise:

A repaired transport connection reaches a settlement whose buildings and services remain largely inactive.

Consequences:

- survey teams can arrive more easily;
- visitors may assume reopening happened when it did not;
- deliveries may resume to a caretaker or temporary field team;
- former residents may visit before deciding whether to return;
- increased traffic may disturb wildlife that had used the quiet corridor.

The route owner determines route state. Pass 84 only records how settlement use changes in response.

## Seed — The Clinic Never Left

Premise:

Most households left a settlement after a long decline or disruption, but a small care facility continued operating for a wider rural catchment.

Questions:

- does staff commute from elsewhere?
- is anyone housed locally?
- which services remain available?
- what dependencies keep the facility viable?
- did the clinic become the only reason transport still stops here?

Boundary:

Care owns treatment and capacity. This candidate never grants medical authority or invents healing rules.

## Seed — The Garden Took the Street

Premise:

During several quiet years, vegetation and recurring Pokémon activity spread through a former residential street.

Initial observations:

- former fences no longer define actual movement;
- one drainage channel has become a regular water source;
- several Pokémon are repeatedly observed using sheltered structures;
- no stewardship designation currently exists.

Conflict:

A return proposal needs the street for access, but simply clearing everything would destroy current ecological state before it is understood.

Possible outcomes:

- redesign the access route;
- retain part of the street as low-disturbance space;
- continue observation before committing to a plan;
- reopen a different zone first;
- proceed with restoration after evidence shows the overlap is temporary.

No outcome is preselected as morally correct.

## Seed — Three Houses, Two Return Plans

Premise:

Three former households are contacted during a settlement return review. Their present needs differ enough that one universal reconstruction plan does not serve all three.

Examples of differences:

- one household wants the former residence repaired;
- one is settled elsewhere and only wants belongings/history preserved;
- one is interested in returning but needs a different accessible layout or service dependency.

Playable question:

Can the settlement return plan preserve history without treating former residents as a homogeneous constituency?

Boundary:

No property, inheritance, tenancy, compensation or land-right rule is invented here.

## Seed — The Notice Still Says Evacuated

Premise:

A public notice continues to say an area is evacuated even though a limited return has been authorized for specific zones.

Possible causes:

- the notice was never revised;
- a new notice exists at only one entrance;
- the physical board is damaged;
- a partial return was communicated privately to affected households before public signage changed.

Gameplay:

Players reconstruct current authority using source records rather than treating the visible sign as truth.

This links directly to the public-notices layer.

## Seed — The Institution That Learned to Live Elsewhere

Premise:

A local institution relocated during abandonment and has now spent long enough at its temporary site that both locations have legitimate history.

Current tension:

- the original settlement may be returning;
- staff routines changed;
- the temporary host community now depends on the institution;
- archived objects and traditions are split between places;
- not everyone agrees that `return` is automatically desirable.

Possible end states:

- return fully;
- operate in both places;
- keep the new location and restore the former building for another use;
- return only some functions;
- defer the decision.

The institution identity persists whichever location outcome is chosen.

## Seed — The Former Market Is a Nesting Route

Premise:

A covered market arcade has not hosted commerce for years. Repeated observations now show wild Pokémon using the roofline and interior passage as a regular movement/rest corridor.

A commercial reopening proposal exists.

Gameplay lanes:

- ecology observation;
- building-condition review;
- interviews with former vendors/residents;
- alternate market layouts;
- staged reopening;
- a possible encounter only if active world state causes one.

Boundary:

The wild use does not automatically create ownership, legal protection or a capture restriction. The reopening proposal does not automatically override ecology.

## Seed — The Archive Was Moved, the Building Wasn't

Premise:

A local archive was evacuated to another institution. Years later, the original archive building still stands and is being considered for reopening.

Complications:

- the collection now has a functioning home elsewhere;
- the old building has different environmental/maintenance conditions;
- former residents associate the building with local identity;
- some furniture, indexes or non-accessioned material may still remain;
- reopening the building does not require moving the collection back.

This candidate can produce public-memory, archival and building-use decisions without a battle.

## Seed — Temporary Base, Permanent Traces

Premise:

An abandoned settlement was used as an expedition or response base for several weeks. The operation ended, but physical and documentary traces remain.

Possible traces:

- repaired roof;
- numbered staging rooms;
- temporary power/communications hardware;
- route markings;
- stored non-sensitive equipment;
- waste/remediation records;
- a field notebook describing the settlement during the operation.

Question:

What happens when later residents encounter a layer of history created after the original abandonment but before their return?

The temporary users never gain ownership automatically.

## Seed — The House Nobody Returned To Is Still in the Route Guide

Premise:

A travel guide, courier address index or old local map still identifies a former residence as an active waypoint.

Consequences:

- visitors keep arriving at an empty building;
- deliveries are redirected elsewhere;
- a wild Pokémon may now use the porch/yard;
- the former resident may prefer the outdated information to be corrected without publicly exposing their current address.

The resolution is an information/privacy problem, not a mandatory haunting or crime story.

## Seed — A Rebuilt Center, a Smaller Town

Premise:

One central service building is fully rebuilt, but only a fraction of the historical residential footprint returns.

Design purpose:

Test the principle that a settlement can be viable at a smaller scale instead of `restoring population to 100%`.

World changes may include:

- consolidated housing;
- some streets converted to other uses;
- retained ruins or memorials;
- reduced utility network footprint;
- public space moved closer to active households;
- ecological corridors left intact.

No numeric restoration meter is required.

## Mystery — Seven Occupied Buildings, Five Resident Records

Premise:

A current reopening map marks seven buildings as occupied, but Residential state explains only five permanent households.

Evidence sources:

- move events;
- temporary work-team assignments;
- commuting staff records;
- caretaker schedules;
- direct observations;
- service-node state;
- notices;
- courier handoffs;
- interviews.

Possible explanations:

- two buildings are temporary staff lodging;
- one record is duplicated;
- a building is active but not residential;
- the map is outdated;
- occupancy is real but current evidence is insufficient to classify it.

The mystery must not default to trespassing, squatters, fraud or illegal occupancy.

## Mystery — The First Return Was Earlier Than the Plaque Says

Premise:

A commemorative plaque says the settlement was reoccupied on a specific date. Personal records and maintenance logs suggest people were already spending nights there earlier.

Question:

What did `return` mean to the institution that authored the plaque?

Possible distinctions:

- first authorized permanent household;
- first reopened public service;
- first night spent by a work crew;
- formal reopening ceremony;
- first restored residence;
- date chosen later for commemorative convenience.

The objective is provenance and definition, not exposing a liar by default.

## Arc — A Settlement Learns What Returning Means

Stage 1 — Quiet baseline

Players first encounter the place in LOW_OCCUPANCY. A remnant household, caretaker or commuting service makes clear that it is not empty.

Stage 2 — Access changes

A route, bridge or service dependency improves. More people can visit, but normal occupancy does not immediately return.

Stage 3 — Survey and contradictions

Building condition, ecology, public records and former-resident memories disagree in useful ways. Some structures remain viable; others changed function or became habitat.

Stage 4 — Provisional return

One household or institution returns through an explicit decision. Minecraft visibly changes only the relevant zone.

Stage 5 — New conflict created by success

Increased use affects a current wild corridor, service capacity, route pressure or temporary institution. The problem exists because the place is becoming active again.

Stage 6 — Divergent futures

Former residents, new arrivals, institutions and ecological stakeholders support different combinations of restoration, conversion and preservation.

Stage 7 — Stable new use

The settlement reaches a durable state that may be smaller, mixed-use or substantially different from its historical peak. Former phases remain visible through records and physical traces.

The arc can pause at any stage indefinitely.

## Encounter — Return Survey at Old Main Street

Full intent:

A survey team must withdraw while selected Trainers/Pokémon manage an encounter in a partially reopened zone. Intercept, forced movement, multiple exits, objective-aware AI and actual tactical environmental state could matter.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — PARTIAL;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING when environmental state is tactical;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Surveyors leave first through Ouros world state. Unsafe structures remain outside the arena. Ouros selects an explicit combatant manifest. AutoPTU resolves an ordinary static encounter. The survey resumes only after a separate readiness decision.

## Encounter — Reclaimed Courtyard Conflict

Full intent:

Returning residents encounter wild Pokémon that repeatedly used a former civic courtyard during low human occupancy. Territorial withdrawal, dynamic protected space, Intercept/forced movement and non-KO AI priorities could matter.

Reduced version:

Residents leave before battle creation. Ecology observations remain valid regardless of battle result. Ouros selects only active combatants. AutoPTU resolves the static encounter. Conservation/Public Space/Residential systems then determine future use.

Winning cannot delete the habitat record or authorize reopening.

## Noncombat sequence — First Week Back

A household has just returned provisionally.

Possible scenes:

- check whether the old delivery address is still valid;
- learn which neighboring buildings remain empty;
- confirm a service schedule;
- notice a changed wild Pokémon routine;
- receive a former neighbor visiting rather than returning;
- decide which stored belongings to recover;
- discover that a familiar shortcut is now closed;
- attend a public meeting or information session if the local system supports it.

Routine domestic activity should compress. The week becomes playable only where changed state creates a decision.

## Cobblemon materialization notes

Use existing safe Cobblemon/Minecraft capabilities aggressively:

- persistent overworld Pokémon presentation;
- forms/models/textures;
- walk/swim/fly/idle/look animations where appropriate;
- cries and ambient audio;
- signs, doors, blocks, containers and decorative assets;
- vegetation/world props;
- particles;
- NPC/Pokémon schedule presentation;
- client/server synchronization;
- interaction UI;
- entity tracking and persistence hooks.

Ouros owns occupancy/use facts and explicit encounter composition. AutoPTU owns tactical facts. Cobblemon BattleState never determines who is fighting or what the battle result means.

## Canon questions exposed by these candidates

- Which settlements, if any, have already declined or been evacuated in approved Ouros geography?
- Which causes are compatible with established regional history?
- What counts culturally or institutionally as `return`?
- Who can publish OPEN/CLOSED/EVACUATED status?
- What authority controls access to damaged public spaces?
- What happens to former residences during long vacancy?
- How are new residents accepted or recorded, if at all?
- Which services can continue without permanent population?
- How does each region treat ruins retained inside an active settlement?
- What ecological uses of former human space are already established?

No answer is proposed as canon in Pass 84.
