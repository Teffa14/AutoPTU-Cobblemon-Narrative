# The Answer Belongs to the Person Who Receives It — Pass 427

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON

Premise

A named NPC asks another person for help. The recipient later answers. The return message becomes new information for the requester, not an automatic command to the world.

If Teo accepts Mara's request, Mara may still need to decide whether help is still needed, where to meet, what task remains, whether another solution already exists, or whether the relevant window has closed. If Teo rejects, Mara can seek another path. If he defers, the delay becomes part of the situation. If he counterproposes, Mara receives an alternative that she may accept, revise or decline.

The important state is the chain of owned decisions. Mara owns the request. Teo owns his answer. Mara owns what she does after receiving it.

Existing canon surfaces

Mara Veyra and Teo Lark are canon residents of Puerto Bruma. Mara coordinates field reports and practical assistance. Teo maintains ordinary equipment and field instruments. Those facts make them useful regression surfaces only. This proposal creates no new incident, duty, relationship, schedule, repair job or standing assistance agreement.

Reduced implementation

The full premise can run without a battle. Required systems are private knowledge, semantic time, communication latency, schedules, obligations, travel constraints, durable world-action intents and selective replanning.

Example shape: Mara asks for field support. Teo receives the request after finishing another obligation. He accepts. His answer returns after Mara has already rerouted the field team. Mara receives the acceptance and decides that Teo should inspect the returned equipment instead. Every step remains causally understandable without retroactive quest-state mutation.

Mechanically rich version

A later field-assistance scene may involve protection, escort, retrieval, rescue or extraction. The story premise does not require those mechanics to exist. If they are used, their dependencies must remain explicit.
