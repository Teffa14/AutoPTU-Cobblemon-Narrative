# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Evacuation Shelter, Reunification & Departure — Pass 128

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Every concrete institution, location, relationship and historic event requires later canon review.

These seeds use Pass 128's continuity model. They are deliberately written so their value comes from chronology, evidence, ordinary-world persistence and handoffs between existing systems rather than from invented emergency law or unsupported combat mechanics.

## 1. The Empty Cot Still Has a Name Card

A former temporary shelter has already returned to ordinary civic use, but an archived photograph shows one cot with a handwritten name card after the person had apparently left the building.

Possible explanations:

- the roster board was updated later than the departure;
- the cot was kept reserved while the actor was temporarily offsite;
- the card belonged to a registration episode, not current presence;
- the actor transferred to Care while belongings remained;
- the photograph timestamp predates the recorded departure despite being filed later.

Useful systems: Crisis shelter history, photography, archives, roster snapshots, departure records.

No combat required.

## 2. Three Shelters, One Household

An explicitly established household was split during evacuation because different members left by different routes.

One reached the designated civic hall. Another traveled with a neighbor to a secondary site. A third was transferred after arriving at a reception point.

The quest reconstructs the household's movement and restores contact without inventing family relationships, guardianship or blame.

Long-term callback: one of the secondary routes later becomes a familiar neighborhood contingency path.

## 3. The Pokémon Arrived First

A known partner Pokémon appears at an authorized temporary accommodation site before its Trainer's location is established.

The Pokémon's identity is supported by pre-existing Pokémon Agency evidence. The accommodation site records temporary presence without becoming owner or active Trainer.

Later possibilities:

- the Trainer is at another shelter;
- the Trainer is in Care;
- the Trainer independently evacuated elsewhere;
- contact occurs before physical reunion;
- the association is genuine but another custody question remains unresolved.

The story must not assign emotions or Loyalty changes automatically.

## 4. The Bus Left, the Registry Did Not

A transport manifest correctly records an actor boarding a bus. A shelter roster correctly records the same actor earlier. An old public copy of the roster is still visible after the transfer.

The apparent contradiction is resolved by purpose and timestamp.

World change: the site later adopts version timestamps on public-safe occupancy summaries if canon approves that practice.

## 5. The Message Was Delivered to the Old Site

A message intended to reconnect two separated actors reaches the shelter where the recipient had been registered several hours earlier.

The recipient has since moved.

The quest follows:

- message attempt;
- delivery to the location/service;
- discovery that the recipient departed;
- handoff to the next authorized communication channel;
- eventual contact.

The old message remains evidence of what was known at that time.

## 6. Two Similar Pokémon at Two Different Sites

Two shelters report what appears to be the same Pokémon because species, coloring and an ordinary descriptive feature match.

Further evidence shows two individuals.

Useful lesson: species and rough visual description are not durable identity.

Potential evidence:

- separate authoritative Pokémon IDs;
- different prior association records;
- simultaneous timestamped observations;
- different documented markings;
- existing care or monitoring records.

No invented biometric mechanic is needed.

## 7. The Reunion Happened Before the Roster Update

A photograph shows two people together while an operational roster still lists them at separate sites.

The question is not whether the photograph or roster is false. The player determines whether the photograph records:

- a physical reunion during a temporary visit;
- a permanent departure from one shelter;
- a meeting at a neutral location before both returned to different sites;
- a later event misfiled under an earlier date.

## 8. The Gymnasium That Still Has Tape on the Floor

Years after a crisis, a battle or community gymnasium still has faint tape marks, wall anchors and a rearranged storage room from its temporary shelter use.

Players reconstruct its old layout through:

- photographs;
- archived floor plans;
- public notices;
- equipment inventories;
- oral histories;
- an old accessible entrance retained after the event.

The site's ordinary present-day function stays intact.

## 9. The Temporary Pokémon Yard Became Something Else

A fenced outdoor area beside a civic building was temporarily used for authorized Pokémon accommodation during an evacuation.

After ordinary operations resumed, the space was repurposed under whatever local owner system canon later establishes.

A future quest uncovers the old shelter identity through mounting points, archived signs and photographs.

Do not assume the facility had animal-shelter rules, ownership transfer or universal species segregation.

## 10. The Actor Who Never Checked Out

A shelter record has no departure entry for an actor who is known to have been safe afterward.

Possible explanations:

- self-departure was observed but not entered in the same system;
- the actor left with an authorized group transfer recorded elsewhere;
- a communications outage prevented synchronization;
- the shelter closed and unresolved roster entries were archived;
- the actor's later contact proves safety but not exact departure time.

This seed normalizes incomplete records without defaulting to misconduct.

## 11. The Care Transfer Changed the Search

A missing-person inquiry points toward a shelter. The actor did arrive there, but an earlier care transfer means their current location is protected by another visibility scope.

The search can progress from `LOCATION_HISTORY_VERIFIED` to `CONTACT_REQUEST_REFERRED` without disclosing private medical location.

Care remains authoritative over health state.

## 12. The Pokémon Was Found, the Trainer Was Contacted, the Handoff Waited

A Pokémon identity is confirmed. Its Trainer or prior legitimate association is contacted. A physical handoff does not happen immediately because another authoritative constraint remains open.

Potential constraints, only when canon supports them:

- Care treatment still active;
- transport unavailable;
- destination access blocked;
- identity/custody evidence under review;
- the other party cannot safely travel yet.

This preserves `IDENTIFIED`, `CONTACTED` and `HANDED_OFF` as different states.

## 13. The Wrong Meeting Point Was Once the Right One

Residents remember a meeting location from an older emergency plan. Newer public information points elsewhere.

During a later low-level disruption, several actors independently travel to the old location.

The story investigates document versioning and public memory rather than making the residents foolish.

Long-term consequence: the old point may remain a useful landmark even if it no longer has official status.

## 14. Four Rosters, Three Times

Mystery seed.

Four surviving roster products appear to disagree about the same afternoon.

The player discovers:

- one is an intake snapshot;
- one is an operational headcount created later from partially synchronized sources;
- one is a public-safe summary without actor names;
- one is a retrospective archive reconstruction.

All four answer different questions.

Resolution depends on metadata, timestamps and provenance.

## 15. Five Times They Were “Found”

Mystery seed.

Different witnesses say the same separated actor was “found” five times.

Those statements refer to:

1. a credible sighting;
2. a matching shelter registration;
3. a later verified location;
4. successful two-way contact;
5. physical reunion.

The scene teaches players how the world's information system works without exposition dumps.

## 16. The Departure Board Has No Destination

A departure record confirms that someone left a shelter, but no authoritative destination is known.

Later evidence confirms the actor was safe elsewhere.

The system preserves the historical gap instead of backfilling a fictional destination into the original record.

This can become an archive/research quest years later.

## 17. The Old Shelter Map Solves a New Building Mystery

An old emergency floor map contains room names that disappeared after renovation.

A present-day archive, maintenance or public-space mystery becomes solvable because the shelter map preserves one short-lived configuration of the building.

This is a direct dense-world callback from emergency history into an unrelated future story.

## 18. The Child's Pokémon Is Not a Guardianship Record

A young actor and Pokémon arrive together at a safe site.

The world can record co-arrival and an existing Pokémon association if supported. It cannot infer that the Pokémon is a guardian, that another human actor has lost guardianship, or that the Pokémon may consent to legal/care decisions.

This seed exists mainly as a canon-safety test for procedural generation.

## 19. The Shelter Closed, the Household Did Not Return

The emergency site returns to ordinary use, while an explicitly known household remains temporarily displaced because Residential still has a return blocker.

Possible blocker owners:

- Building Safety;
- Maintenance;
- Roads/Travel;
- Utilities;
- Care;
- Accessibility.

The shelter closing is therefore visible progress without being total recovery.

## 20. The Last Public Notice Is Technically True and Practically Stale

A notice says a particular shelter is receiving evacuees. It was valid when published. Intake later moved elsewhere, but the physical notice remained posted for a time.

The investigation distinguishes publication timestamp, operational state and removal/update time.

Public Notices owns whether and how the information was delivered.

## 21. The Volunteer Knows a Name, Not a Location

A volunteer remembers processing an actor's registration but was never authorized to access later transfers.

The testimony supports historical presence but not current whereabouts.

Community Aid remains the authority for the volunteer role; this layer stores only the evidence supplied through legitimate scope.

## 22. The Reunion Route Is Safe Later Than the Message

Two separated actors establish two-way contact while the route between their safe locations remains unavailable.

The emotional/narrative goal can advance before movement does.

Travel later restores a usable journey. Physical reunion becomes a separate callback rather than an automatic cutscene teleport.

## 23. The Same Building Sheltered People Twice, Differently

A civic building serves as an authorized shelter in two different historical incidents.

The second operation uses different rooms, entrances or support arrangements because the building and neighborhood changed.

Records must keep the two episodes separate even though local oral history may blend them.

## 24. Exploration — The Gymnasium That Still Has Tape on the Floor

Implementation status: READY as narrative/exploration content.

Premise:

A present-day ordinary venue contains subtle physical remnants of emergency use. Players reconstruct where registration, sleeping, information and temporary Pokémon accommodation areas once existed.

Required mechanics:

- static overworld navigation;
- archive/provenance interaction;
- observation of canon-approved physical details.

Not required:

- shelter mechanics;
- crowd simulation;
- escort;
- dynamic hazards;
- tactical environmental effects.

Potential reward:

A historical route, building alias, old contact point or institutional relationship becomes available for future stories. No mechanical bonus is assumed.

## 25. Long arc — A Town Learns Where Everyone Went

Status: NON-CANON arc template.

Phase A: ordinary baseline.

Players already know several civic buildings, households, travel routes and recurring Pokémon before any emergency occurs.

Phase B: bounded disruption.

Crisis activates an evacuation and more than one temporary site. Actors use different routes. Some known people and Pokémon are separated.

Phase C: distributed records.

Shelter intake, transport, care transfers, self-relocation and temporary Pokémon accommodation create partial views of where people went.

Phase D: reconnection.

Most cases resolve through mundane contact and movement. One or two require investigation because records are stale or identities uncertain.

Phase E: shelter closure.

Sites return to ordinary use at different times. Residential return remains separate.

Phase F: persistence.

Months later:

- one route is used more often;
- one building keeps an accessibility change;
- a temporary information board becomes a community board;
- a known Pokémon retains a documented connection to a former shelter location;
- old records answer a new historical mystery.

The arc does not require a permanent disaster storyline.

## 26. Encounter — Shelter Loading Bay Withdrawal

Full intended form:

Evacuees leave in waves while explicit combatants protect a route near the loading bay. Active nonparticipant movement, Intercept, forced movement, timing, protected zones, reactions, objective-aware AI and semantic playback may matter.

Capability exposure:

Targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED baseline.

Base movement legality: VERIFIED.

Complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: PARTIAL.

Core calculations: VERIFIED.

Action economy/initiative: VERIFIED.

Full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL.

Full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL.

Status lifecycle: PARTIAL.

Terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING for protected corridors, dynamic safe zones or crossing reactions.

Move-specific behavior: PARTIAL.

Abilities: PARTIAL.

Items: PARTIAL.

Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL.

AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED.

AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for PROTECT/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE.

Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING.

Reduced form: READY.

Crisis completes or pauses the population movement before BattleSpec creation. All evacuees, private records, controlled belongings, transport objects and noncombatant Pokémon remain off-grid. AutoPTU resolves a static exterior battle among explicitly selected combatants.

Victory only secures immediate access.

## 27. Encounter — Reunification Route Chokepoint

Full intended form:

Two already-located parties need to traverse an obstructed route and could require escort/interception, protected movement and objective-aware AI.

Reduced form: READY.

Their identity and contact are resolved first. Both remain outside BattleSpec. A conventional encounter clears a static chokepoint. Travel/Crisis decides afterward whether movement can occur. The continuity layer records physical reunion only after it actually happens.

A win cannot manufacture a reunion.

## 28. Encounter — Temporary Registration Site Diversion

Full intended form:

A temporary registration site is operating when an unrelated threat requires staff withdrawal and operational shutdown.

Reduced form: READY.

Intake pauses. Staff leave through world state. Records and equipment are secured outside the grid. AutoPTU receives a static perimeter.

Battle victory cannot:

- authenticate identity;
- reveal private information;
- register an evacuee;
- check someone out;
- create guardianship;
- transfer Pokémon custody;
- reopen the site.

## 29. Canon questions generated by these seeds

The proposals intentionally leave unresolved:

- which Ouros institutions operate emergency shelters;
- what site types can be used;
- whether regions use formal registration at all;
- what information is collected;
- how privacy and disclosure work;
- who can request reunification support;
- which human dependency/guardianship rules exist;
- how Pokémon are accommodated during human displacement;
- whether people and partner Pokémon are commonly co-located or use linked facilities;
- which Pokémon identity records are available during emergencies;
- who can authorize handoff or temporary custody;
- how self-departure is recorded;
- how unresolved roster entries are archived;
- which past shelter sites are part of canon history.

No seed should answer these questions merely because an adventure needs a convenient procedure.
