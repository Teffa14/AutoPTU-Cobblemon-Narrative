# Anomalous Spaces & Dimensional Exploration Seeds — Pass 50

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros concepts derived from high-level research structures. No external plot, character or distinctive setting is copied.

These seeds use `design/anomalous-spaces-dimensional-exploration-layer.md` and inherit the permanent encounter-capability rules from `design/encounter-implementation-contracts.md`.

## 01 — The Door That Changed Its Address

Surveyors verify the same stone doorway twice, weeks apart. Both crossings are real, but the second arrival point differs from the first. The investigation is about destination stability, not immediately about who changed the portal.

Useful links: cartography, science, public memory, anomaly anchors.

## 02 — Three Rooms That Never Move

A large reconfiguring ruin changes corridors on every verified expedition, but three chambers always remain present. Players slowly discover that the stable chambers matter more than the routes between them.

The semantic anchors stay persistent even when topology versions change.

## 03 — The Empty Twin Station

A spatial echo resembles a functioning transit station in the ordinary city. Architecture is recognizable, but no staff records, advertisements, timetables or ownership claims automatically carry over.

The mystery is built from differences, not from evil duplicate NPCs.

## 04 — Orchard Beyond the Wall

A pocket space accessible from an old agricultural wall contains a small stable ecosystem. Nobody can yet prove who has stewardship, whether the space was created intentionally or whether its inhabitants cross into the surrounding farms.

## 05 — The Returning Pokémon

The same persistent wild Pokémon is observed entering an anomaly and returning on three later dates. The system records the individual and crossing history without assuming why it travels.

## 06 — Survey Pack on the Wrong Side

An expedition's tagged equipment is recovered near a different anomaly anchor than the one the team used. The equipment keeps its provenance. The event does not prove teleportation by the missing team itself.

## 07 — The Safe Room

Inside a changing dungeon, a small room has remained stable across every recorded topology version. Players begin using it as a survey hub. Its stability becomes a long-term research question rather than an automatic supernatural sanctuary buff.

## 08 — The False Shortcut

Players discover a portal that appears to connect two distant settlements. The route is physically real, but it does not automatically override protected-area access, cargo restrictions, interregional permissions or custody rules.

The conflict becomes institutional as much as spatial.

## 09 — Public Portal, Unknown Return

A harmless-looking distortion becomes a local attraction before researchers have established return conditions. Tourism pressure forces a decision about access without requiring the anomaly itself to be hostile.

## 10 — The Corridor That Is Longer Inside

A maintenance structure has an interior path that consistently measures longer than its exterior footprint. The effect is repeatable. It does not grant combat teleportation or extra movement speed.

## 11 — Two Portals, One Hypothesis

Two research teams believe distant anomalies connect to the same realm. Their observations overlap but do not yet prove a shared destination. Players can improve the evidence graph without needing a final answer immediately.

## 12 — Spillover Nest

A small group of wild Pokémon repeatedly appears near an intermittent anomaly. A temporary nesting site forms on the ordinary side. Conservation, research and local land use all become relevant.

## 13 — The Map with Yesterday's Hallway

A printed survey map is internally accurate for the topology version in which it was made. Visitors mistake it for a current map and report that the institution published false information.

The real issue is versioning.

## 14 — The Closed Anchor

A town has built normal life around an anomaly anchor that has not activated in decades. Its return would affect housing, tourism, archaeology, safety planning and public memory even before anyone crosses it.

## 15 — The Pocket Workshop

An artisan discovers a bounded space suitable for storage and work. Before it becomes a magical inventory expansion, the setting must answer access reliability, ownership, safety and whether objects left inside persist normally.

## 16 — No One Crosses Twice the Same Way

Several expeditions return through different exits, but every participant and tagged object remains accounted for. The challenge is understanding route behavior without inventing duplication or memory alteration.

## 17 — The Unmanned Mirror Clinic

A mirror version of a clinic contains recognizable rooms but none of the real clinic's current patient records or staff. Researchers must resist treating resemblance as shared database state.

## 18 — The Door Below the Waterline

A submerged anomaly becomes reachable only during a seasonal low-water window. Seasonality determines access to the anchor. It does not automatically create a PTU weather or underwater combat effect.

## 19 — The Ruin That Refuses a Fourth Room

Every generated layout can contain many paths, but attempts to map a fourth permanent chamber always fail. The system records this as an empirical spatial rule claim until enough evidence supports it.

## 20 — Expedition Zero

An old archive contains records suggesting an anomalous space was surveyed decades before the current research institution existed. The archive is incomplete, and modern maps use different coordinate conventions.

## 21 — The Quiet Spillover

Nothing dangerous crosses a portal. Instead, a few seeds, spores or small ecological traces appear near the anchor. The story becomes a long research and stewardship problem rather than a boss arc.

## 22 — The Failed Demonstration

A public institution schedules a controlled portal demonstration, but the anomaly simply does not activate. No sabotage is assumed. Funding, reputation and scientific interpretation respond to a legitimate null result.

## 23 — A House with a Second Basement

A residence has a door leading to a bounded extra space that previous owners used differently. Household history, access rights and physical safety matter more than combat.

## 24 — The Anchor Under Construction

Public works uncover a stable anomaly anchor during excavation. Construction cannot simply continue or stop forever. Surveying, archaeology, safety and civic process all intersect.

## 25 — The Pokémon That Will Not Follow

A partner repeatedly stops before a verified portal. The system records refusal behavior. It does not infer fear, precognition, Injury or hidden Loyalty mechanics.

## 26 — The Missing Exit Marker

An expedition enters a reconfiguring dungeon and finds that the normal visual exit marker is absent. The topology validator still confirms a legal route to an emergency exit. The incident becomes a test of presentation versus actual feasibility.

## 27 — The Not-Quite Festival Street

A mirror region contains the physical outline of a street used for a famous annual festival, but no current decorations or vendors. This makes public-memory differences visible without duplicating the ordinary world's event state.

## 28 — The Survey Drone Returns

A tagged instrument sent through a portal later returns through the same anchor with valid recorded observations. The system treats the instrument log as evidence, not as perfect proof of the space's global geometry.

## 29 — The Faction That Controls the Door, Not the Destination

A faction gains physical control of the only known anchor but still cannot predict where or when it opens. Authority over access does not become authority over the anomalous realm itself.

## 30 — The Last Stable Crossing

Measurements suggest an anomaly is becoming less frequent. The remaining access window creates pressure to choose what research, recovery or stewardship work matters before the connection becomes dormant.

It does not justify trapping PCs or deleting unresolved content automatically.

# Long Arc A — The Returning Hall

A ruin first appears to be a conventional maze. On later expeditions, corridors differ. Over many visits, players and researchers identify a stable semantic graph beneath the changing layouts: a survey hall, a damaged archive, a biological chamber and an exit structure recur even when the routes connecting them vary.

The arc can evolve through mapping, null results, lost assumptions and improvements to the topology model. Eventually the institution can predict constraints without predicting the exact map.

Possible outcomes include opening controlled expeditions, preserving the site, maintaining it as a research reserve or abandoning active exploration while keeping long-term monitoring.

The mystery does not require a villain or Legendary explanation.

# Long Arc B — Across the Same City

A mirror region resembles one district of an existing city. Early visitors assume it is a parallel copy. Later evidence complicates that interpretation: some buildings correspond closely, others have no equivalent, and public records do not match.

The arc crosses science, archives, housing, infrastructure, photography and public memory. Players help determine what is actually shared and what only looks similar.

The system should preserve several hypotheses until evidence narrows them. It does not automatically create alternate versions of existing PCs/NPCs.

# Long Arc C — The Thin Places

Small spatial anomalies appear across several regions over a long period. Different institutions initially treat them as unrelated because their presentation differs: a cave doorway, a city distortion, a submerged anchor and a reconfiguring ruin.

Players can build a cross-regional evidence network while factions, conservation groups, tourists and researchers respond differently.

The eventual result may show a shared cause, several unrelated phenomena or a mixture. The arc should not force one cosmological answer merely because the anomalies were grouped into one research program.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Folding Causeway

Full experience:

A causeway inside an anomalous realm changes usable routes during the encounter. The objective is to reach a stable survey anchor while hostile or territorial Pokémon also move through the area.

Exact capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including forced movement/interception — BLOCKING if route shifts move actors or create intercept behavior;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL for timed changes;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL if exact statuses are used;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for disappearing/reappearing route zones;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for REACH_ANCHOR/WITHDRAW policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced experience:

Freeze one validated causeway topology for the battle. Any spatial change occurs between scenes. The encounter resolves with ordinary supported movement and combat. Reaching the survey anchor occurs after battle as overworld state.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Portal Ecology Spillover

Full experience:

Wild Pokémon can enter or withdraw through an active portal while the party tries to keep the route clear and observe crossing behavior rather than simply defeat everything.

Exact dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement — BLOCKING for live crossing/interception;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full lifecycle — PARTIAL if portal timing matters;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for active portal zones;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for retreat/crossing goals;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced experience:

Resolve crossings before the tactical scene. The battle contains only Pokémon already on the ordinary side. The portal cannot be entered from the grid. Results write back to the same persistent Pokémon identities and ecological records.
