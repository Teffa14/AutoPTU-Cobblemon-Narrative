# Pass 168 worldbuilding candidates — risk sharing, coverage, loss, and claims

Status: NON-CANON / PROPOSED

These concepts require authored institutions or customs before promotion to canon. They do not presume that Ouros has modern insurance.

## Short concepts

### The Claim Filed Before the Damage Was Understood
A workshop owner reports a loss immediately after a regional incident. The first report is accurate about visible damage but wrong about the cause. Later inspection changes the explanation without making the original report fraudulent.

### Five Times Someone Said “It Was Covered”
Five NPCs use the same phrase to mean five different facts: the object was listed in an arrangement, that risk was within scope, a claim was opened, a decision approved compensation, and money was actually transferred. The mystery is documentary precision rather than deception.

### Two Correct Damage Estimates
Two assessments disagree because one measures restoration to former function and the other measures replacement with current materials. Both are correctly scoped. The later claim history preserves both.

### The Workshop Repaired Before the Claim Was Decided
Neighbors and apprentices restore essential function before institutional review finishes. The claim later concerns documented loss and eligible costs, while Material Culture preserves which original fabric survived.

### The Storm Everyone Assumed Counted
A communal protection custom is widely remembered as covering “storm damage,” but the surviving rule version is narrower. Public Memory, actual arrangement rules, and community expectations diverge without forcing one into the other.

### The Community Pool With No Money Left
Several legitimate losses arrive close together. The arrangement recognizes the claims but Finance shows insufficient reserves. This creates obligations, negotiation, prioritization, or later fundraising without changing which losses were accepted.

### The Old Coverage Map
A geographic protection scheme references an old boundary map. Settlement Geography later changed, so two interpretations appear plausible. The claim remains pending until the authority question is resolved rather than inventing which map controls.

### The Replacement That Changed the Craft
A covered tool is destroyed and replaced. The replacement performs the same broad function but changes how an artisan works. Compensation closes economically while Material Culture records a genuine break in technique and provenance.

### The Lost Parcel With Two Arrangements
A shipment is covered by a merchant guarantee and also by a recipient-side mutual fund. Courier continuity establishes what happened to custody. The two arrangements can reach different decisions because their authored scopes differ.

### The Claim Nobody Owned
A public-facing structure is damaged, but records disagree about whether the relevant asset belongs to a household, guild, civic body, or trust-like institution. Coverage cannot settle ownership; ownership must be resolved through its own authority.

### The Rejected Claim That Was Not Fraud
The loss happened exactly as reported, but the applicable arrangement did not cover that category. The claimant can be truthful and disappointed simultaneously.

### The Archive of Paid Losses
A small institution preserves generations of claim summaries. Researchers later use it to study historical storms, fires, thefts, and rebuilding patterns, while Scientific Research treats those records as evidence with biases rather than direct world truth.

## Longer arcs

### Three Storms, One Mutual Fund
A local risk-sharing institution experiences three major loss episodes decades apart. After each event its authored rules, membership, reserves, recordkeeping, and community expectations change. The arc can be experienced by different player generations. The institution’s continuity matters more than a single villain or catastrophe.

### The Town Learns What “Covered” Means
A famous historical payout becomes folklore. Later residents assume the old precedent guarantees compensation for a new kind of loss. Investigation reveals distinctions among historical rule versions, public memory, actual payments, and changed geography. The resolution can revise policy prospectively without rewriting past facts.

### The Workshop Rebuilt Differently
A multigenerational workshop suffers a canon-authored loss. Immediate safety, claim review, financing, repair, material provenance, employment continuity, and public memory proceed on different timelines. The final building functions again but is not identical to what was lost. Years later, apprentices debate whether the rebuilt workshop is “the same place.” Chronicle can answer with multiple precise continuities instead of a binary retcon.

## NPC and faction roles

Potential roles include a mutual-aid record keeper, rotating fund steward, independent damage assessor, skeptical craftsperson who documents original materials, courier liaison, community advocate, archivist preserving obsolete rule versions, and former claimant who understands process but has no current authority.

None of these roles automatically grants PTU Trainer Classes, Features, Skills, money, legal power, or tactical effects.

## Promotion requirements

Before any concept becomes canon, authors must decide the institution or custom, applicable authority, geographic and subject scope, effective dates, evidence rules if any, financial implementation, relation to ownership, privacy expectations, and whether Pokémon-related costs or care can ever be included without treating Pokémon as property.

Mechanically rich scenes must use the encounter contracts in `design/risk-sharing-coverage-loss-claims-continuity-extension.md` and the current readiness snapshot. Reduced versions should be preferred whenever the full premise depends on blocked capability families.
