These situations deliberately avoid choosing a canonical fuel technology. Terms such as terminal, depot, supply point and fuel system refer only to a region where canon later establishes the relevant technology.

## Situation — The Depot Has Supply, the Village Does Not

A regional depot reports verified supply on site. Two downstream settlements still report interruption.

Useful truths that can coexist:

- stock is present;
- part of it is already allocated;
- one distribution path is unavailable;
- an alternate route reaches only one sector;
- the village service point has not completed its own readiness check.

Playable work:

- reconcile time-stamped reports;
- identify which handoff is actually blocked;
- trace how travelers and businesses adapted;
- decide whether an authored authority can redirect an allocation;
- preserve the temporary workaround as future history.

No sabotage, theft or hoarding is implied.

## Situation — The Roadside Stand Became the Newsroom

A remote service point normally functions as an ordinary travel stop. A regional supply interruption makes it a gathering place for drivers, couriers, hikers and local workers exchanging route and availability reports.

The location becomes narratively valuable because its ordinary function connects several social networks.

Possible later callback:

Months after normal supply returns, travelers still use the stand as an informal information point.

The stand does not gain communications authority merely because people gather there.

## Situation — The Reserve Is Real, the Release Is Not

Public rumor says a nearby reserve should solve the shortage immediately.

The operational record shows:

- physical presence was verified;
- release authority has not yet been established for the affected service;
- the normal distribution path cannot currently reach every sector;
- several downstream services would still require their own restart checks.

The tension comes from different meanings of "available", not from a secret villain.

## Situation — The Delivery Arrived Yesterday

A carrier insists a delivery was completed yesterday. The institution waiting for fuel says nothing arrived.

Both statements may be locally correct if:

- the carrier completed a handoff at the terminal;
- the downstream allocation was created later;
- the local service-point leg remains pending;
- the institution is speaking about usable supply rather than depot receipt.

This is a provenance mystery before it is a misconduct case.

## Situation — The Temporary Supply Point Became a Landmark

A temporary service point is placed during a prolonged route interruption.

Over several weeks it acquires:

- a nickname;
- a food stall or rest area nearby;
- recurring workers;
- a Pokémon individual that regularly visits;
- notices and improvised wayfinding;
- a new footpath or stopping pattern.

When normal service returns, removing the temporary installation does not erase its social history. Public Memory, Travel and Ecology may keep consequences.

## Situation — The Old Depot Still Has a Shift Change

A decommissioned industrial site has been repurposed, but local routines still preserve traces of the old operation.

Examples:

- a bus stop name survives;
- a café still gets busy at the former shift-change time;
- an old access road is used as a walking route;
- former workers maintain social ties;
- a Pokémon individual continues visiting a familiar corner.

The site is not automatically contaminated, dangerous or abandoned.

## Situation — The Service Point Is Open, the Supply Is Not

The storefront/office remains staffed and accessible. The relevant fuel supply is unavailable.

This distinction supports scenes where:

- staff provide information rather than sales/service;
- travelers alter plans;
- people wait for a verified delivery;
- notices become stale as conditions change;
- a nearby business experiences unusual traffic.

Open building state must not be collapsed into supply availability.

## Situation — The Supply Returned, the Heat Did Not

An authored heating-fuel delivery reaches a building after an interruption. Residents expect immediate restoration.

The downstream facility still needs its own technical readiness check.

This seed exists only for a region where heating fuel is established canon. It creates no heating mechanics, cold damage or consumption rate.

## Situation — The Pokémon Is Always Near the Loading Area

Workers repeatedly observe the same individual Pokémon near a terminal boundary.

Possible explanations remain open:

- food availability;
- shelter;
- warmth;
- habitual route;
- relationship with a worker;
- interest in vehicle traffic;
- coincidence;
- an unknown cause.

Do not infer work assignment, fuel affinity, sabotage, ignition risk, contamination immunity or technical competence from species or Type.

## Situation — The Allocation Board and the Public Notice Disagree

An internal allocation changed after a public notice was issued.

The discrepancy can create:

- an unnecessary trip;
- an avoidable queue;
- distrust in an operator;
- a correction workflow;
- a small investigation into timestamps and communication handoffs.

The Communications/Public Notices layer owns message distribution and corrections. Fuel Continuity owns the underlying operational state.

## Situation — The Port Is Working, the Depot Is Not

An incoming shipment has reached the region and completed the transport owner's handoff. The receiving depot remains constrained by a separate technical or access issue.

This allows a visually obvious supply arrival without magical normalization of the local system.

## Situation — The Depot Works, the Road Does Not

The terminal is operational and release-ready, but a downstream route is closed.

Potential consequences:

- a temporary service sector boundary;
- rerouting through another settlement;
- an alternate local pickup point;
- different businesses receiving traffic;
- delayed restoration of a dependent service.

Road Operations retains closure/reopening authority.

## Mystery — Five Availability Reports, Three Meanings

Evidence packet:

1. terminal log: "available" at 08:10;
2. allocation record: "reserved" at 08:35;
3. carrier note: "ready" at 09:20;
4. service-point report: "unavailable" at 10:05;
5. public notice: "limited service" at 10:40.

Resolution grammar:

- identify each subject ID;
- identify whether each statement concerns presence, allocation, release, transport, or customer-facing service;
- compare effective times;
- preserve all statements that were correct inside their scopes.

No hidden truth score is needed.

## Mystery — Four Deliveries, Two Actual Handoffs

Four actors use the word "delivery" for different transitions:

- arrival at regional terminal;
- release to carrier;
- arrival at local service point;
- downstream consumer receipt.

The investigation builds a handoff graph and resolves which events actually occurred.

This pattern can support commercial tension without inventing fraud.

## Long arc — A Corridor Learns Its Supply Routes

Phase 1 — Ordinary circulation

Establish a small set of recurring places and people:

- one terminal/depot;
- two service sectors;
- a roadside service point;
- one carrier or transport interface;
- recurring workers and travelers;
- at least one downstream service whose dependence is authored.

Phase 2 — Local constraint

A real but bounded interruption affects one path or facility. Observations disagree because each actor sees a different part of the system.

Phase 3 — Temporary adaptation

A temporary route or service point changes traffic and routines. New social and ecological observations accumulate.

Phase 4 — Staged restoration

Supply presence returns before every sector and endpoint. The player can observe the difference between operational checkpoints rather than watching the world flip from OFF to ON.

Phase 5 — Residue

Months later:

- an old temporary stop has a nickname;
- one business changed its customer pattern;
- a worker moved roles;
- a decommissioned asset became a landmark;
- an old shortage notice resurfaces as misleading evidence;
- a Pokémon continues using a route established during the disruption.

The arc accumulates facts rather than raising a `fuel_level` meter.

## Exploration concept — The Closed Transfer Gallery

Premise:

A decommissioned industrial corridor contains old route markers, maintenance labels and connection diagrams from several operational eras. The site has been isolated from active fuel operations.

Immediate playable version:

- compare old maps with current physical anchors;
- identify which labels refer to removed assets;
- find evidence of later reuse;
- distinguish historical operating state from present condition;
- hand any safety or environmental concern to the correct owner system.

Do not make old pipe geometry a live fuel puzzle. Do not create fumes, ignition, pressure, spills or explosion mechanics.

## Encounter — Fuel Depot Access Withdrawal

Full narrative intent:

An unrelated hostile encounter occurs after operations have been stopped, but before every worker/nonparticipant has cleared a reviewed access route. A future full tactical version can support withdrawal/protection behavior and contested routes.

Full dependency profile:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — required, VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — required, VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — required when route contest uses Intercept/displacement, PARTIAL;
- core calculations — required, VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — required, VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — required for complete battle fidelity, PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — required for complete battle fidelity, PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — depends on actual combatants/effects, PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — required for generalized reactions or any active technical/fuel hazard, BLOCKING;
- move-specific behavior — as used, PARTIAL;
- abilities — as used, PARTIAL;
- items — as used, PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — as used, PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — required, VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — required for withdrawal/protection, BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — required for full presentation, BLOCKING.

Reduced form:

Fuel operations are already isolated. Workers, travelers, nonparticipant Pokémon, vehicles and handling equipment leave the tactical space. Fuel-specific assets are excluded or inert. Ouros creates explicit participants and a static reviewed access arena. The battle result can secure immediate access only.

## Encounter — Delivery Yard Perimeter

Full narrative intent:

A distribution handoff pauses while a conflict blocks the safe yard perimeter. The tactical objective is to protect/clear access, never to capture the cargo.

Full dependency emphasis:

- complete movement — PARTIAL if Intercept or forced displacement matters;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if active vehicles/equipment or technical zones matter;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for route protection/withdrawal;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING;
- standard verified targeting, base movement, core calculations, action economy and legal-action infrastructure remain usable.

Reduced form:

Freeze the external handoff at the last verified state. Remove cargo, workers, vehicles and transfer equipment from BattleSpec. Fight on adjacent static ground. Victory allows later workflow resumption but changes no custody, allocation, release or delivery state.

## Encounter — Isolated Service Point Diversion

Full narrative intent:

Travelers have already been redirected from an isolated local service point. A territorial or hostile encounter remains near one safe approach.

Full dependency emphasis:

- reviewed multiple routes;
- complete movement if displacement/Intercept matters — PARTIAL;
- generalized reactions/technical zones — BLOCKING;
- objective-aware AI — BLOCKING;
- adapter playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced form:

The service point is closed before battle. Staff and customers are absent. All fuel-specific props are mechanically inert. Combat takes place on ordinary reviewed terrain. Winning can permit a later inspection; it does not reopen service or verify supply.
