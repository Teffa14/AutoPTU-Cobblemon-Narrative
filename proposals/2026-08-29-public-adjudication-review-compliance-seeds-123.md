# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Adjudication, Review & Compliance — Pass 123

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Nothing in this file establishes institutions, powers, outcomes or regional procedure.
Date: 2026-08-29

## Design intent

These seeds use the Pass 123 continuity layer to create playable disputes from chronology, scope, records, review and downstream implementation. They avoid a generic courtroom campaign and avoid assuming that Ouros has one shared legal system.

Each seed requires an existing canon-authored deciding body and mandate before promotion.

## The Notice Reached the Board, Not the Ranger

A route steward insists they never received the notice that changed access conditions. The deciding body has a valid issued record, while delivery history shows that the message reached the institution's office but not the field actor before the next patrol.

Playable work:
compare notice IDs, dispatch routes, duty rosters and timestamps; determine which later actions were taken with which knowledge.

Possible consequence:
a corrected communication procedure and public clarification without rewriting the original decision.

## Five Dates on One Decision

Residents give five different dates for when a market-access dispute was resolved. Archive records show a session date, decision issue date, delivery date, effective date and implementation date.

The mystery resolves through provenance. Nobody has to be lying.

## The Finding That Survived

Public rumor claims a review erased an entire earlier decision. The actual lineage shows one finding was preserved, one was changed and one question was returned for more fact-finding.

The surviving finding explains why a temporary restriction remains even though the headline says the earlier decision was overturned.

## The Record Was Complete Yesterday

An old photograph is authenticated after a decision is issued. The first proceeding did not ignore it; the object was genuinely unavailable at the time.

The new question is whether canon provides a review route for newly discovered evidence. The player can establish provenance without deciding the outcome.

## The Pokémon Was in Two Records

A named Pokémon appears in two institutional records under different descriptive labels. One office recorded a local nickname; another used a research tag. The apparent two-witness contradiction is actually one individual observed twice.

Promotion requires persistent identity support from established world state. Species alone cannot resolve identity.

## The Decision Arrived After the Repair

A facility operator completes a voluntary repair while a formal matter is still pending. The later decision requires verification of that same physical state.

The repair can be complete while compliance verification remains open. Maintenance owns repair truth; adjudication only records whether the authored condition was later verified.

## The Temporary Route Became the Expected Route

A contested path is rerouted temporarily while a review is pending. Months pass. Businesses and Pokémon routines adapt to the temporary alignment.

A later decision restores formal access to the old route, but Travel, Ecology and local actors now have reasons not to reset instantly.

## The Correction Nobody Read

A public summary contains a scope error. The authoritative decision is correct and a correction is published the next day, but an older notice remains physically visible at a remote station.

The story follows publication, delivery and memory rather than fraud.

## The Review Body Asked One Question

A faction tells the player that the entire original case is being reopened. The actual review scope asks only whether one evidence item was connected to the right incident.

This seed teaches that proceeding scope is data, not rhetoric.

## Compliance Without Reconciliation

Two rivals remain openly hostile after one party performs every required action from an authored decision. The world records verified performance but does not fabricate forgiveness, trust or friendship.

A later arc can use the still-damaged relationship without falsely marking the matter as noncompliant.

## The Hearing Hall Closed for an Unrelated Reason

A scheduled session is adjourned because an infrastructure failure or wild incident affects the venue. The matter itself has not changed.

This creates a clean handoff to Infrastructure/Crisis while preserving the proceeding's status and records.

Reduced tactical version:
participants and records leave before BattleSpec creation; a static encounter secures the exterior; the proceeding is rescheduled through world state.

## The Courier Took the Long Road

A review record package must reach another institution, but a route closure forces a documented diversion. Rumor later says the record was tampered with because its travel time was unusually long.

Courier and Evidence Custody can prove the actual custody chain. A battle, if present, protects route access only and never establishes authenticity.

## The Condition Was Performed Twice

An actor completes a required return or restoration, but the first verification visit cannot confirm it because the site becomes inaccessible. They repeat part of the work before the next review.

Both performance events remain in history. The later verification does not delete the earlier attempt.

## The Old Decision Still Runs the Sign

A settlement changes an institutional decision, but a legacy sign or automated Minecraft display still renders the superseded public summary.

Players can discover a synchronization failure without letting the adapter become authority over world state.

## Mystery — Seven Records, Three Scopes

Materials:
- complaint intake;
- investigation summary;
- proceeding notice;
- hearing record;
- decision;
- review decision;
- compliance report.

All seven use similar language around the same location. The puzzle is to group them by scope and time rather than assume they are duplicate or contradictory files.

Resolution outputs:
- record lineage map;
- corrected public chronology;
- one unresolved historical question preserved for later use.

## Exploration — The Review Office Above the Old Station

A small regional review office occupied two floors above a transport station before both services moved elsewhere. The building now stores archived route maps, hearing notices, decision indexes and old public summaries.

Current-safe version:
all accessible rooms are stable static geometry; clues are dates, document IDs, old station names, photographs and persistent location aliases. No procedural scene or combat rule is needed.

Narrative payoff:
a supposedly vanished historical decision is located under the old station name, revealing why a present-day boundary follows an apparently irrational line.

## Long arc — A Valley Learns What Review Changes

Chapter 1 establishes routine local processes through mundane notices and records before any major dispute.

Chapter 2 introduces a contested access or stewardship matter. Investigation and mediation produce evidence and attempted agreement, but a canon-authored body eventually issues a scoped decision.

Chapter 3 focuses on implementation. One condition completes quickly; another depends on maintenance, route state or ecological monitoring.

Chapter 4 introduces credible later evidence or a record-scope problem. A review begins. The first decision remains historically visible while people argue over what still applies.

Chapter 5 preserves some findings and returns one question for additional work. Temporary arrangements change local routines during the delay.

Chapter 6 issues a new decision on the remanded question. Compliance happens by subsystem rather than through a global resolved flag.

Chapter 7, much later, reuses the entire chronology when a new project, Pokémon routine, land-use question or family memory intersects the same boundary.

## Encounter — Record Transfer Diversion

Full premise:
a custodian carrying authenticated records is caught near an unrelated hostile incident. The tactical goal protects withdrawal and route clearance while record truth remains world state.

Full dependency families:
targeting/footprints/range/LoS VERIFIED; base movement legality VERIFIED; complete movement PARTIAL for escort/Intercept/forced movement; core calculations VERIFIED; action economy/initiative VERIFIED; lifecycle PARTIAL for timed departure; damage PARTIAL; status lifecycle PARTIAL; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions BLOCKING if protected corridors or generalized reactions matter; move behavior/abilities/items/Trainer Features PARTIAL as selected; AI legal actions VERIFIED; AI tactical policy BLOCKING; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics semantic playback BLOCKING.

Reduced version:
transfer custody before combat, remove custodian and package from the grid, then resolve a conventional static battle at the chokepoint. The battle can reopen immediate access but cannot grant review or authenticate a record.

## Encounter — Hearing Hall Evacuation

Full premise:
an unrelated disturbance forces people to leave an active session while threats occupy the exterior route.

Additional pressure:
complete movement PARTIAL, lifecycle PARTIAL, zones/reactions BLOCKING when a protected corridor is tactical, tactical policy BLOCKING for WITHDRAW/PROTECT, semantic playback BLOCKING.

Reduced version:
world state adjourns the session and relocates all noncombatants first. AutoPTU receives an exterior static arena. Victory does not resume the session automatically.

## Encounter — Compliance Site Perimeter

Full premise:
a maintenance, return or inspection action tied to an authored decision is underway when a separate encounter threatens the site.

Reduced version:
pause the compliance action before BattleSpec creation; move workers and controlled objects off-grid; resolve a static legal battle; later let the responsible owner system complete work and let the authored verification route decide compliance.

## Canon questions before promotion

Which Ouros institutions, if any, have authority to decide contested matters?

Which subjects can each institution decide?

What outcomes can each body actually authorize?

Does any region have review, reconsideration, appeal or rehearing routes, and who owns them?

Which records are public, private or protected?

How are Pokémon represented or heard when their interests are directly affected?

Can any existing League, Gym, guild, stewardship, settlement or research institution hold such authority, or must this remain unused until future canon establishes one?

The safest default is no universal answer.
