## Premise

A field specialist previously promised to inspect a site during one time window. Conditions changed and both sides completed an explicit renegotiation. The requester accepted a later window and that acceptance reached the specialist.

The new agreement now controls future scheduling. The old promise remains visible in history.

This creates a useful distinction for later stories. A resident can truthfully remember that the first appointment existed. The specialist can truthfully show that replacement terms were accepted. A later social dispute can depend on the complete chain rather than on a rewritten calendar entry.

## Reduced version

The scene needs no battle runtime.

The replacement commitment enters the specialist's ordinary agenda. The original schedule no longer competes for future execution. Before the new window begins, world evidence may change again: access can reopen or close, weather can alter travel, another duty can compete for time, or the need for inspection can disappear.

The episode can resolve through travel, observation, communication and a report. Nothing requires a KO objective.

## Rich version

If the new window opens into an unsafe site, the same premise can become a survey, escort, protection, retrieval, containment or extraction encounter.

Examples of optional escalation include unstable terrain that changes routes, weather that changes safe timing, Pokémon territorial behavior that forces positioning decisions, a protected object that must be moved without defeating every opponent, or a retreat condition after enough evidence is collected.

These are optional implementations, not facts of the proposal.
