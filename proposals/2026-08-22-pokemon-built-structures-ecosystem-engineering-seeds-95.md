# Pokémon-Built Structures & Ecosystem Engineering — Worldbuilding Seeds

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.
Pass: 95

These concepts are original Ouros candidates derived from the research and systems layer. They do not establish PTU mechanics, species distribution or canon history.

1. The Dam That Became a Road
A Bibarel-built dam has been used as an informal crossing for years. A recent expansion raises the water level enough that the crossing is no longer reliable, forcing the settlement to decide whether to reroute traffic, reinforce a separate crossing or interfere with the structure.

2. The Pond Nobody Planned
A dam creates a pond that gradually becomes a valuable wetland. Years later, the original builders are gone but several other species depend on the new habitat.

3. The Missing Builders
A large dam is freshly maintained, but no Bibarel have been directly observed for weeks. Players can investigate activity windows, hidden access routes or mistaken assumptions about who is doing the repairs.

4. The Old Dam Under the New Dam
Surveyors discover that a current structure has been rebuilt over multiple older generations. Sediment layers and old materials preserve a history of changing occupation.

5. The Structure That Outlived the Colony
A formerly occupied Durant nest is now empty. Its tunnels remain and begin to affect drainage, construction planning and later habitat use.

6. Three Entrances, One Network
Three towns think they have separate burrow systems beneath them. A long survey gradually suggests that the passages may be connected.

7. The Tunnel in the Platform Wall
Transit maintenance reveals an Excadrill-sized opening into an older underground network. There is no evidence yet that the current occupants caused the damage.

8. The Nursery Beyond the Survey Line
A proposed infrastructure corridor overlaps a deep colony chamber that was not shown on older maps. The conflict is about evidence, timing and route design rather than defeating the residents.

9. The Bunnelby Quarter
Repeated nesting beside a settlement creates a shifting pattern of small entrances and filled-in burrows. Residents learn which areas are stable, active or seasonally abandoned.

10. The Bridge of Silk
A recurring canopy route built by local Bug Pokémon becomes useful to other species. After a storm breaks one segment, multiple groups alter their movement routes.

11. The Combee Wall Archive
A long-used nectar-storage site becomes a landmark for researchers and local guides. Changes in occupancy become an early signal of broader floral change.

12. The Empty Honey Chamber
A chamber still contains old structural material but no active storage. Tourists assume the colony is gone, while surveys show activity deeper inside.

13. The Structure Mistaken for Ruins
Researchers initially catalog a geometric mound as human-made. Later observations suggest repeated Pokémon construction, forcing an archive correction without erasing the earlier interpretation.

14. The Ruin Mistaken for a Nest
The inverse case: a site assumed to be recent Pokémon construction contains older architectural material incorporated into a newer nest.

15. Maintenance Night
A structure appears unchanged during daytime surveys but camera records show regular maintenance at night. The discovery changes when and how the area can be visited safely.

16. The Material Trail
Branches used in a dam come from several distinct upstream locations. Their provenance reveals a wider foraging and transport route than expected.

17. The Flood After Removal
A structure considered obsolete is dismantled. The later water response shows that the system had become part of the local hydrology even after the original builders left.

18. The Road Around the Nest
A public project is redesigned around a colony instead of through it. Years later the bypass itself creates a new ecological boundary and becomes a Road Ecology issue.

19. The Burrow That Became a Drain
An abandoned tunnel begins carrying stormwater during heavy rain. Engineers need to decide whether to stabilize, block or incorporate it into drainage planning.

20. The Fallen Lodge
A storm damages an old lodge and exposes preserved material inside. The site now touches Decomposition, Archives and habitat management at the same time.

21. Reused by Strangers
A structure built by one species is later occupied by another. Players must avoid assuming builder, owner and current occupant are the same thing.

22. The False Builder
A rare Pokémon is repeatedly photographed near an unusual structure and becomes publicly credited as its builder. Later evidence points elsewhere.

23. The Shared Water Edge
A dam creates habitat valued by wildlife, irrigation users and a downstream settlement. Every group has a legitimate stake in the structure's future.

24. The Structure That Moves
A collective repeatedly relocates nest material a short distance over several seasons. Old versions remain visible as abandoned footprint traces.

25. The Excavation Shortcut
A burrow network unintentionally connects two cave systems that humans previously thought were separate. Travel gains a possible shortcut, but only after occupancy and stability are resolved.

26. The Winter Entrance
A tunnel is visible only during part of the year because snow, water or vegetation obscures the entrance during other seasons.

27. The Survey Marker Inside the Nest
An old human survey marker appears incorporated into a later Pokémon-built structure. Removing it would destroy useful provenance about both histories.

28. The Dam That Saved Nothing
Residents credit a dam with preventing a flood. Hydrology records show the flood peak never reached the area. The story remains culturally important even after the causal claim weakens.

29. The Dam That Mattered More Than Anyone Knew
The reverse: a structure long treated as scenery is shown through multi-year measurements to be influencing groundwater, vegetation and downstream timing.

30. The Builder Returns
A persistent Pokémon previously associated with a structure returns after years away. The system records the observation without assuming it intends to reclaim, repair or defend the site.

## Longer arcs

### Five Generations of the River Builders
Year 1 establishes a currently active dam and lodge. Year 2 records expansion and new wetland habitat. Year 3 brings a storm and partial damage. Year 4 the original builders disappear and another group reuses the pond margin. Year 5 a public-works proposal forces the region to decide which parts of the structure's accumulated ecological and historical function should be preserved. The arc can continue without requiring the same Pokémon to remain present.

### Beneath Meridian
A tiny Bunnelby burrow discovered near an old transit district leads to a larger network, then to evidence of different builders across different decades. Some sections intersect utilities, some are abandoned, some remain active habitat and some connect to natural caves. The eventual regional map becomes a joint product of Infrastructure, Cave Ecology, Cartography and Pokémon-built-structure history.

### The Structures Atlas
Researchers begin by documenting dams, nests, burrows, hives and other built habitats. Over years, the atlas gains builder confidence, occupancy history, reuse, environmental effects and archived versions. Player discoveries can correct earlier entries instead of replacing them. The atlas becomes a source of future quests because the world changes around known structures.
