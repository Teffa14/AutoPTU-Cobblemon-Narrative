# Marea ecosystem-engineer seeds — pass 224

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-03

These seeds extend Marea ecology without adding any species to canon.

## The Crossing That Changed

A familiar low-water crossing becomes impassable because a wild engineer population has altered the channel upstream. Residents first blame storm debris. Survey evidence instead points to maintained biological construction.

The same change creates a new wet edge used by other wild populations, so removing the obstruction solves one access problem while destroying a newly formed habitat opportunity.

Reduced implementation: persistent world-state change, altered path geometry, NPC testimony, survey markers and a detour. No tactical water hazard required.

Full implementation: optional live confrontation around the structure, difficult terrain, movement control and intervention choices. Exact battle mechanics inherit the capability families used.

## The Empty Lodge, Living Wetland

The builders are gone, but their structure remains functional. The player is asked to explain why the local species mix still differs from older maps.

The answer is habitat legacy: the ecosystem remembers through hydrology, sediment, vegetation and shelter even after the population that caused the change leaves.

This supports a multi-visit arc where the same site transitions from maintained structure to degraded wetland to a different successional patch.

## The Good Field, Bad Garden

A soil-working population improves one agricultural patch while damaging a neighboring vegetable plot. Two residents give contradictory accounts because both are correct at different spatial scales.

The player can map soil condition, feeding evidence and crop damage before any management decision.

The seed is deliberately species-neutral until a regionally enabled official candidate is approved.

## The Engineer Became Infrastructure

A settlement quietly relies on a wild-built structure for seasonal water retention or route stability. When builder abundance falls because of capture pressure, migration or disturbance, maintenance stops and the human dependency becomes visible.

The resulting quest is about delayed causality rather than a monster attack: the dangerous event occurs weeks after the ecological cause.

## The Wrong Fix

A human crew breaches an engineered structure to restore a path. The immediate objective succeeds. Later windows show downstream sediment movement, loss of a refuge patch and redistribution of dependent species.

No moral answer is pre-authored. The point is that interventions create measurable trade-offs.

## Long-term arc: A Landscape With Authors

Across several seasons, the player learns that parts of Marea's terrain were shaped by wild Pokémon populations rather than being static geography.

Possible beats:

- old survey records show a channel in a different position;
- field signs identify active maintenance;
- another species begins using the new habitat;
- residents disagree about whether to protect or remove the structure;
- builder numbers change through normal demography;
- the structure persists after departure;
- succession creates a new patch;
- later NPC dialogue and maps retain the history.

This creates environmental storytelling where the map itself records population history.
