# Pass 175 — Civic Volunteering, Donations & Community Support Seeds

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from the research patterns in Pass 175. None are established lore, institutions, mechanics or rules.

## 30 worldbuilding and adventure candidates

1. The Hall That Opens Only When Needed
A neighborhood assembly hall spends most of the year as a quiet meeting room. During storms, festivals and regional disruptions it becomes a volunteer intake point. Its history is visible through old route maps, handwritten rosters and revised procedures.

2. Too Many Hands at Cedar Bridge
A damaged footbridge attracts far more helpers than the repair team can safely supervise. The useful challenge is redirecting people into survey support, signage, supply carrying and meal logistics without turning goodwill into a crowd hazard.

3. The Donation Nobody Can Use
A respected business donates expensive equipment to a rural clinic. The equipment is genuine and valuable but incompatible with the clinic’s power system. The story becomes a chain of respectful redirection rather than fraud or incompetence.

4. The Drive That Filled Early
A winter clothing collection reaches its actual need halfway through the event. Organizers must stop stale advertising and redirect later donations to another region instead of accumulating a warehouse of redundant goods.

5. The Volunteer Who Knows the Old Road
An older resident signs up for routine distribution work and becomes valuable because they remember a service road absent from current maps. Their memory is evidence to verify, not automatic route truth.

6. The Quiet Specialist
A person who never joins the local rescue association regularly volunteers at its repair bench. Years later, equipment records show how much institutional continuity depended on someone who held no formal title.

7. The Wrong Donation Rumor
A rumor claims an institution refused emergency supplies out of pride. Records show the goods arrived after the need changed and were redirected immediately to a neighboring settlement.

8. The Staging Area Became a Market
A plaza used repeatedly for relief sorting gradually accumulates storage hooks, public noticeboards, shade structures and transport connections. Decades later it becomes a normal civic market while preserving its emergency history.

9. The Last Shift Before the Festival
The regular volunteer crew finishes trail preparation the night before a major festival. Nothing dramatic happens. Years later, those routine records explain why the route safely handled an unusually large crowd.

10. The Pokémon Who Helps Once
A wild or visiting Pokémon assists with a simple, observable task during a community workday and then leaves. The event is remembered, but no permanent institutional role, capture eligibility or willingness is inferred.

11. The Donation With a Name Attached
A family offers a large contribution if a new reading room carries a relative’s name. The library can accept, renegotiate or decline the condition according to its authored governance without making the donor villainous.

12. The Empty Volunteer Board
A rural station has many open tasks but very few available residents during harvest season. The world does not generate more NPC volunteers to balance the quest board; the institution narrows services and postpones noncritical work.

13. The Old Vest
A former volunteer keeps an obsolete service vest as a personal object. Years later someone mistakes it for current authority. Identity/Credentials resolves the confusion; the vest remains historically authentic.

14. The Unclaimed Equipment Crate
A drive receives a crate with no usable donor information. The items can enter a documented unknown-donor review without inventing theft, contraband or hidden motives.

15. The Best Volunteer Is Redirected
A highly qualified person arrives at a public cleanup but their expertise is needed more urgently at a water-service assessment. They can choose whether to accept the referral; the system does not commandeer them.

16. Two Drives, One Town
A school and a neighborhood group independently collect the same type of goods. Neither acted wrongly. Coordination after the fact produces a shared needs board and reduces duplication next year.

17. The Volunteer Who Leaves Early
A participant exits halfway through a workday for a private reason. The record says the session ended early. No resentment, laziness or personal explanation is generated.

18. The Useful Refusal
A receiving institution declines a donation of perishable food because cold storage is full. The donor instead funds transport for goods already waiting elsewhere. The refusal improves the actual response.

19. The Community Repair Ledger
For thirty years, residents have volunteered one day each spring to repair authorized trail furniture and signs. The ledger becomes useful evidence when Wayfinding researchers try to date several marker revisions.

20. The Emergency Volunteer Becomes Staff
After several documented service periods, an NPC later applies for a paid institutional role. Their volunteer history is relevant experience but does not automatically hire them.

21. The Staff Member Becomes a Volunteer
A retired worker returns only for an annual public event in a bounded role. Their former employment does not restore old credentials or authority.

22. The Donation That Outlived the Institution
A community group dissolves while several accepted but unused durable items remain in storage. Agreements, Material Culture and governance must decide the handoff rather than deleting or auto-redistributing them.

23. The Public Thank-You Error
A published recognition list omits several participants and includes one person who registered but never served. Correcting the publication does not rewrite the underlying service records.

24. The Relief Map Was Drawn by Volunteers
A volunteer mapping group documents temporary distribution points during a crisis. Years later the map becomes an archive object useful for Demography, Public Memory and urban-history research.

25. The Donation Box Is Not an Inventory API
Players discover that placing random items into a public collection chest does not make them accepted stock. The UI explains current needs and routes unsupported items back to the player or a review state.

26. The Week Nobody Volunteers
A normally reliable program has a week with no participants because several unrelated events overlap. The institution cancels low-priority work without manufacturing an emergency.

27. The Donor Changes Their Mind Before Settlement
A pledged contribution is withdrawn before funds transfer. Finance preserves the pledge history and the project adjusts scope. No stolen money exists.

28. The Volunteer Group That Never Formalized
For years, the same residents help maintain a riverside path without forming a club or association. Their recurring collaboration is historically real while membership remains undefined.

29. The Community Support Atlas
Several towns document where volunteers, storage spaces, shelters and accessible staging points have actually worked over decades. The atlas becomes an operational and historical resource, with dated revisions rather than a perfect regional capacity map.

30. Nothing Happened at Intake Three
A planned high-demand event produces only routine volunteer traffic. The quiet day becomes a useful baseline for later crowd and staffing comparisons.

## Long arc 1 — Five Emergencies at Meridian Hall

Year 1: an unexpected storm produces a spontaneous volunteer surge and severe intake congestion.

Year 2: the hall creates a needs board, clearer assignments and a small standing volunteer program.

Year 3: a different incident exposes that donated goods, not volunteers, are now the main bottleneck.

Year 4: organizations coordinate before the season, so most routine support happens without player intervention.

Year 5: the hall handles a major event smoothly. The playable story is elsewhere, while Chronicle records that preparedness worked.

Design value: institutional competence should reduce repeated crisis quests rather than raising difficulty forever.

## Long arc 2 — One Donated Generator, Six Histories

A generator is donated to a community center, redirected to a water-service site, later returned to reserve storage, loaned during an outage, repaired by an authorized workshop and eventually preserved by a local museum after retirement.

The same persistent item can cross Civic Support, Supply Chains, Technology, Workplaces, Material Culture, Crisis and Museums without losing provenance.

Design value: donation history is one chapter in an object’s life, not a permanent item type.

## Long arc 3 — The Service Road

A group of residents begins by volunteering to clear an old footpath. Over decades, the route becomes a recognized trail, emergency access corridor and eventually a formal public connection.

Different systems become authoritative at different stages: Civic Support for work sessions, Wayfinding for guidance assets, Public Works for authorized construction, Land Tenure for access, Emergency Services for operational use and Archives for historical records.

Design value: small civic actions can have long-lived consequences without magically creating legal infrastructure.

## Encounter contract A — Volunteer Staging Area Evacuation

Narrative premise:
A sudden wildlife movement reaches the edge of a large community-support staging area while dozens of volunteers are waiting for assignments.

FULL version:
- evacuate noncombatants through multiple exits;
- allow wild Pokémon to prefer withdrawal/crossing rather than standard hostility;
- protect a bounded route to the safe assembly point;
- optionally protect staff closing the intake area;
- preserve the staging site and assignment state after the encounter.

Permanent capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED for combat targeting;
- base movement legality: VERIFIED;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement: BLOCKING for dynamic evacuation/crossing;
- core calculations: VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL if any exact Status mechanic appears;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING if crowd lanes, environmental effects or reactions matter tactically;
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL;
- abilities: PARTIAL;
- items: PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for EVACUATE/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE/PROTECT_STAFF;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:
Civic Support and Crisis suspend intake and evacuate all volunteers before combat. Wildlife movement is resolved in world state until only an independent confrontation remains. AutoPTU receives a static legal arena with combatants only. Afterward, the staging area reopens or remains closed according to world state.

A battle victory never counts as completing volunteer assignments.

## Encounter contract B — Donation Warehouse Route Blockage

Narrative premise:
Accepted relief goods are physically in a warehouse, but the outbound route becomes inaccessible during a separate Pokémon incident.

FULL version:
- warehouse staff withdraw from the affected loading lane;
- combatants may need to clear a route rather than defeat every opponent;
- forklifts, pallets and goods remain world objects unless exact mechanics exist;
- inventory remains under Supply Chains authority throughout.

Dependencies:
The same VERIFIED/PARTIAL/BLOCKING baseline applies. Complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback are BLOCKING for a true CLEAR_ROUTE objective. `terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions` is BLOCKING if unstable shelving, spills, moving equipment or blocked lanes have tactical effects. Items remains PARTIAL if any donated PTU Item must be used mechanically.

REDUCED version:
Stop loading, move workers away, freeze all goods in authoritative inventory state and place the battle in a clear static exterior/loading-yard arena. After combat, Supply Chains decides whether the route and stock are usable.

No KO, capture or destroyed Minecraft block changes inventory quantity by itself.

## Encounter contract C — Trail Stewardship Workday Interruption

Narrative premise:
A routine volunteer trail-maintenance day intersects with a territorial or displaced wild Pokémon encounter.

FULL version:
- workers withdraw along the route;
- Pokémon may attempt to leave instead of fight;
- route tools and partially completed maintenance remain persistent;
- the tactical objective may be to preserve a safe corridor rather than eliminate opponents.

Dependencies:
Complete movement is BLOCKING for live withdrawal/interception. AI tactical policy is BLOCKING for non-hostile withdrawal and corridor goals. Adapter/playback is BLOCKING. Terrain/hazards becomes BLOCKING only when slopes, falling material, unstable surfaces or dynamic route changes are actual tactical mechanics.

REDUCED version:
End the work session, account for volunteers and equipment, withdraw wildlife in world state where possible, then run a static encounter only if conflict remains. Wayfinding/Public Works records partial maintenance separately.

## Encounter contract D — Donation Review Dispute

Narrative premise:
Two community groups disagree about whether an offered shipment should be accepted or redirected.

Engine requirement:
None. This is a noncombat institutional/social scenario.

Possible evidence:
- actual needs;
- storage capacity;
- item condition/provenance;
- donor intent;
- transport availability;
- alternative recipients;
- public messaging history.

Valid outcomes include full acceptance, partial acceptance, redirection, delayed decision or refusal. No social Skill roll is invented by this layer.

## Mechanical guardrails

Pass 175 must never infer:

- volunteer service -> XP, Trainer Level, Skill Rank, Edge or Feature;
- volunteer vest -> credential or authority;
- repeated service -> employee status;
- emergency work -> responder qualification;
- donation -> Friendship/Loyalty;
- donor wealth -> Influence mechanic;
- fundraising -> guaranteed settlement;
- donated PTU Item -> mechanically usable before authoritative inventory/item validation;
- accompanying Pokémon -> working Pokémon;
- Pokémon helping once -> future willingness;
- crowd size -> morale bonus or penalty;
- service history -> Charm/Command bonus;
- Minecraft chest contents -> accepted donation inventory.
