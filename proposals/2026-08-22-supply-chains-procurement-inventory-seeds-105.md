# Ouros Supply Chain, Procurement & Inventory Seeds — Pass 105

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Every concept requires continuity, canon and PTU/Caelo review before promotion.

These candidates use the Pass 105 supply-chain layer. They are written to generate consequences from real dependencies rather than generic fetch quests.

## 1. The Stock That Cannot Be Used

A clinic appears well supplied, but most of one critical batch is reserved for a forecasted regional emergency. The immediate problem is an allocation decision, not a hidden theft.

Useful links: Care, Health Surveillance, Crisis, Finance, Agreements.

## 2. Twenty-Four Expected, Twenty Received

A freight receipt shows fewer units than the shipping notice. The first investigation is reconciliation: loading error, legitimate partial shipment, damaged stock, earlier transfer, data lag or actual diversion all remain possible.

Useful links: Cases, Illicit Networks, Postal/Freight provenance.

## 3. The Wrong Revision

A relay station has several replacement parts in storage, but all fit an older machine revision. A nearby settlement still operates that older model and has the opposite problem.

Choice: trade, manufacture, reroute, temporarily downgrade, or wait.

## 4. Emergency Stock From Last Winter

A mountain depot contains an emergency reserve created after a previous crisis. Nobody has needed it since. The current problem is verifying readiness, condition and access rather than discovering a magical untouched cache.

## 5. The Festival Order

A festival creates a predictable local demand spike. One vendor’s large order is legitimate but leaves nearby ordinary shops temporarily thin. The event is not sabotage; the question is whether local allocation practices need revision.

## 6. The Cold Room With a Missing Hour

Temperature monitoring has a one-hour gap during a power event. The room looks normal now. The batch cannot be declared safe or ruined from appearance alone.

Possible outcomes include review, restricted use, replacement sourcing or evidence that a backup system preserved conditions.

## 7. Supply Before the Pass Closes

A seasonal pass is expected to close soon. A remote clinic, observatory and workshop all submit final resupply requests. Carrying everything is not possible under the authored transport capacity.

The story is priority and preparation rather than mandatory combat.

## 8. The Spare Pool

Three small settlements jointly maintain a pool of compatible technical parts. A breakdown in two places at once tests the agreement for the first time.

No actor needs to be malicious for the agreement to become strained.

## 9. The Supplier Came Back

A supplier closed during a crisis and later reopened. In the meantime, customers built alternate sources. The old company returns to a world whose demand graph has permanently changed.

## 10. The Warehouse Built for Rail

An old freight warehouse was designed around a rail line that no longer carries most local cargo. The building remains useful, but its doors, storage zones and staffing pattern now mismatch road/ferry distribution.

Links: Railways, Architecture, Workplaces.

## 11. The Good Batch With Bad Paperwork

A food or research batch appears physically sound and may be authentic, but provenance is incomplete. It cannot automatically enter a sensitive use scope.

The objective may be reconstructing records rather than replacing the material.

## 12. The Report That Never Reached Purchasing

A mechanic noticed a component failing more quickly months ago, but the observation remained in a maintenance log. Procurement still follows the old replacement cycle.

The shortage begins as an information-flow failure.

## 13. Three Shortages, Three Causes

Three towns report the same item as unavailable.

Town A: demand spike.
Town B: route disruption.
Town C: stock exists but is on quality hold.

A regional headline collapses them into “regional scarcity.”

## 14. The Substitute Nobody Has Approved

A workshop knows of a material that may work in place of the missing standard input. The issue is not finding it; the issue is whether technical and mechanical equivalence can actually be validated.

No substitute gains a PTU effect by analogy.

## 15. The Reserve That Solves Today

Players can release emergency stock to keep a service operating during a minor disruption. Doing so is legal, but it leaves less buffer for the storm season arriving soon.

This creates delayed consequence without punishment scripting.

## 16. The Route Reopens Too Late

A blocked ferry route finally reopens, but the destination already obtained replacement stock by rail. The original consignment is now surplus at the wrong place.

Players can help redirect, store, return or repurpose it.

## 17. The Mobile Depot

A disaster-response convoy finishes its original mission. The temporary stock point it created is now the best distribution node for several remote communities.

Question: does it become permanent, rotate seasonally, or close as planned?

## 18. The Last Compatible Part

A legacy pump remains operational because one exact replacement part is still stored regionally. Another machine of the same generation also needs it.

The decision may trigger a longer adaptive-reuse or modernization project.

## 19. The Orchard Packaging Bottleneck

The orchard harvest is healthy and transport routes are open, but reusable containers are unavailable after a supplier failure. Food is abundant while distribution is constrained by an ordinary secondary input.

## 20. The Empty Shelf That Lied

A public shop shelf is empty, creating a rumor of shortage. The warehouse has normal stock; the actual issue is a staffing backlog preventing restocking.

Links: Workplaces, Media, Public Memory.

## 21. The Over-Order

A rumor about future scarcity causes several institutions to place larger-than-normal requests. The demand spike itself creates the shortage people feared.

The design should record the causal chain without moralizing or simulating macroeconomics.

## 22. Expedition Request After Departure

A research team realizes it needs an additional tool after entering a route whose next service window is days away. The equipment exists and funding exists. Timing is the constraint.

Possible solutions: continue without it, return, arrange a rendezvous, use a validated substitute, or change the research question.

## 23. The Pokémon Carrier Opts Out

An institutional Pokémon that has routinely helped move stock stops participating. The observable fact is non-participation. The generator may not invent Injury, fear, mistreatment or disloyalty as the cause.

Operations must adapt while care/agency state is investigated separately.

## 24. The Recall Without a Crime

A supplier identifies a technical defect in one batch and asks institutions to hold it. The hold creates shortages, but no sabotage or fraud is implied.

The historical record of where that batch moved becomes immediately important.

## 25. The Shared Cold Store

A clinic, research lab and nursery share one refrigeration facility. A capacity reduction forces them to negotiate allocations between different legitimate needs.

The temperature system remains technical world state unless a PTU mechanic explicitly uses it.

## 26. The Freight That Arrived First

A museum loan, scientific sample or specialized part arrives before the relevant authorization documents because the communication route and freight route had different delays.

The object can be physically present and still remain held.

## 27. The Old Cache on the New Map

A historical emergency cache appears on an archive map. The building still exists, but nobody knows whether the cache was consumed, relocated, expired, forgotten or incorporated into another institution.

This is an investigation, not guaranteed loot.

## 28. The Backorder Becomes a Design Change

A prolonged shortage of one component causes engineers to redesign a public asset around a more locally serviceable part. The supply disruption leaves a permanent architectural/technical consequence.

## 29. The Mutual-Aid Test

Two institutions signed a mutual-aid stock agreement years ago and never used it. A real shortage activates it for the first time, revealing small ambiguities in scope and transport responsibility.

The solution can be amendment rather than betrayal.

## 30. The Warehouse Chronicle

A regional depot maintains decades of receiving ledgers, batch corrections, route changes and emergency releases. A present-day problem becomes solvable only because older records preserve why a strange stock arrangement exists.

This turns logistics records into world memory rather than administrative flavor.

# Long Arc A — The Spare Parts Years

Year 1: several settlements rely on incompatible generations of the same infrastructure.

Year 2: one shortage exposes how fragmented sourcing has become.

Year 3: institutions create a shared compatibility catalog and spare pool.

Year 4: a crisis tests the pool and exposes one category nobody included.

Year 5+: replacement projects gradually reduce some dependencies while preserving legacy assets elsewhere.

Possible outcomes are not ranked as success/failure. A region can decide that keeping old equipment serviceable is worthwhile, or that standardization is worth the transition cost.

# Long Arc B — The Cold Chain

A clinic network, nursery and research program depend on sensitive stored goods.

Early stories concern monitoring gaps, backup equipment and handoffs. Later stories expose route problems, shared storage capacity, emergency reserves and how one infrastructure upgrade made years earlier changes the result of a regional crisis.

The arc must never invent medical temperature thresholds or PTU healing rules. The world-state problem is preservation of authored requirements and evidence.

# Long Arc C — Five Shortages That Weren't the Same

Across several years, the same settlement experiences five apparent shortages:

1. a real production shortfall;
2. a blocked route;
3. an inventory-record mismatch;
4. a quality hold;
5. an allocation decision during a demand spike.

By the fifth event, residents, institutions and players have better tools for diagnosis. The region’s history changes how quickly it avoids the wrong explanation.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Regional Depot Chokepoint

FULL premise:

A disruption near a depot blocks the only currently usable freight exit. Combatants have objectives around clearing, protecting or withdrawing rather than universal KO.

Dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if depot hazards/interactables matter;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:

All cargo and workers leave the grid. A static chokepoint becomes a conventional encounter. After AutoPTU returns the authoritative result, world state determines whether freight can move. No cargo HP, escort rules or interception are simulated.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Cold Storage Alarm

FULL premise:

A technical monitoring alarm and a Pokémon disturbance happen at the same facility. Staff need to secure stock while the encounter unfolds.

Additional full dependencies:

- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if cold, steam, electrical equipment or protected storage areas are mechanical;
- complete movement — BLOCKING if staff/cargo move through the grid;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for AVOID_STOCK / CLEAR_EXIT / WITHDRAW;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:

Storage assessment and staff evacuation happen first in world state. Any battle occurs in a static safe aisle. The batch’s acceptance state is resolved later from monitoring evidence. The encounter cannot create cold damage, Ice terrain, Snow/Hail or item spoilage.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Emergency Convoy Split

FULL premise:

A convoy at a junction must redirect while two communities have urgent demand and an environmental disruption changes route feasibility.

Full dependencies:

- complete movement/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if the route condition is tactically active;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT_CONVOY / WITHDRAW / REACH_EXIT;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback — BLOCKING;
- standard combat families retain their permanent statuses above.

REDUCED version:

Allocation is decided outside combat. Cargo remains off-grid. The route obstruction becomes static geometry for a normal encounter if battle is needed. Remaining inventory and delivery consequences resolve afterward.
