# The Promise Survives the Restart — Pass 429

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON

## Premise

A named NPC accepts a future assistance window. The player leaves, sleeps, changes dimension, unloads the area or otherwise causes the presentation layer to stop showing that actor. When the world resumes, the accepted obligation still exists with the same causal provenance and scheduled window.

This proposal does not canonize a specific incident, resident assignment or location.

## Reduced version

The scene needs no battle.

1. Actor A requests help.
2. Actor B receives the request and accepts.
3. The acceptance reaches Actor A.
4. Actor B gains a scheduled commitment.
5. The world crosses a persistence boundary.
6. Actor B still owns that commitment after restore.
7. New information may cause a fresh replan, but it cannot erase the historical fact that the promise existed.

Possible outcomes include arriving as planned, arriving with a revised plan, deferring through a new explicit decision, missing the window, or discovering that the original problem changed before arrival.

## Narrative value

This gives off-screen life continuity. A promise made yesterday can shape tomorrow without requiring the player to remain nearby. It also makes reliability and conflict emerge from actual history rather than scripted labels.

The player can encounter evidence of this continuity indirectly: a prepared tool kit, a person waiting at a rendezvous, a workshop job delayed because someone left to honor a promise, or a follow-up message explaining why the window was missed.

## Full encounter version

An accepted assistance window can lead into a route inspection, escort, retrieval, rescue, containment or repair scene. The tactical layer begins only if the selected work requires structured mechanics.

The narrative premise does not require the tactical version. A mechanically reduced implementation can preserve the same story with travel, investigation, dialogue, scheduling and world-state changes.
