# Ouros Urban Heat & Microclimate Seeds — Pass 85

Status: NON-CANON worldbuilding candidates.

These concepts are original Ouros material derived from high-level research patterns. They do not import external plots, characters or mechanics.

## 1. The Square That Stays Hot

A central plaza remains warm hours after sunset while nearby streets cool normally. Residents have noticed it for years, but no one has run a proper evening survey.

Possible causes remain open: stored heat in paving, equipment below the square, poor ventilation, recent redevelopment or several smaller causes.

## 2. Three Blocks, One Forecast

A regional heat warning is accurate, but three adjacent blocks experience very different conditions because one has mature canopy, one is an exposed market and one is a narrow stone corridor.

The quest is about mapping the difference, not disproving the forecast.

## 3. The Missing Shade Hour

A transit platform is shaded every morning and late afternoon but fully exposed during the exact hour when commuter traffic peaks.

The useful intervention may be schedule change, temporary cover or a long-term redesign.

## 4. Cooling Courtyard

A clinic courtyard becomes an informal refuge during hot afternoons.

The clinic never designed it for that purpose. Increased use begins to interfere with deliveries, creating a public-space and operations problem without a villain.

## 5. Night Market Revision

A market moves two hours later after repeated hot seasons.

The new schedule improves comfort but changes transport demand, lighting, noise, staffing and nocturnal Pokémon observations.

## 6. The Tree Everyone Misses

A large shade tree is removed after structural damage.

Only afterward do several systems change at once: a bench becomes unused, a shop awning overheats, runoff changes, and a wild Pokémon loses a recurring resting site.

The story is about recognizing dependencies that nobody had recorded.

## 7. Roof Number Twelve

A public heat survey repeatedly identifies one rooftop as anomalously hot.

The building owner believes the sensor is wrong because the interior remains comfortable.

Both observations can be true.

## 8. Water Without Cooling

A decorative fountain remains operational during a hot period but measurements show little local cooling beyond immediate shade.

The city must decide whether the fountain is still valuable for other reasons or whether resources should go elsewhere.

## 9. The Broken Misting Line

A market's cooling system fails during a major event.

The repair itself is ordinary. The narrative comes from deciding which activities continue, which move, and which services get priority.

## 10. Shadow Map

A cartographer creates a map showing how shade moves through a district from morning to evening.

The map becomes useful to vendors, walkers, researchers and wild-Pokémon observers for completely different reasons.

## 11. The Alley Wind

A narrow lane is consistently cooler than nearby streets because of a reliable airflow pattern.

A proposed building extension could block that flow.

The project becomes a planning dispute built around measurements rather than abstract opinions.

## 12. Hot Stop, Cool Stop

Two transit stops on the same route have very different thermal conditions.

Service planners initially treat passenger complaints as inconsistent until a survey compares shade, material, traffic and waiting time.

## 13. The Park Edge Problem

The center of a park is cool, but one boundary beside a large paved district remains hot well into the evening.

Wild Pokémon use shifts toward the interior during certain hours.

The correlation becomes a research hook, not immediate causation.

## 14. Cooling Center, Closed Door

A community hall is designated as a cooling space, but a maintenance failure makes one entrance unusable.

Accessibility, staffing and public information matter more than battle mechanics.

## 15. The Rooftop Garden Trial

Three buildings install different rooftop interventions.

One improves measured temperatures, one mainly helps stormwater, and one struggles because irrigation and maintenance were underestimated.

No project is treated as a simple success/failure flag.

## 16. Waste Heat at Closing Time

A workshop district cools normally during weekends but stays warmer on production nights.

Players can compare operating schedules with thermal observations before anyone blames a particular facility.

## 17. The Hot Museum Wing

A museum's old wing becomes difficult to operate during hot periods while the newer wing remains stable.

The problem intersects collections preservation, staffing, visitor flow and architecture.

## 18. The Tree-Canopy Promise

A public works project promised shade improvement, but several newly planted trees fail during a dry year.

The project remains valid; its assumptions and maintenance plan need revision.

## 19. The Bench Migration

A familiar group of NPCs gradually changes its meeting place over several weeks.

The system records the shift first. Why they moved remains unknown until conversation, observation or evidence establishes it.

## 20. Station Heat Atlas

Workers begin recording temperature along the entire station concourse at different times.

The hottest location turns out to be neither the platform nor the roof but a transfer corridor with poor ventilation and continuous equipment use.

## 21. The Wrong Sensor

A mobile survey repeatedly records extreme values in one district.

Later review shows the sensor mount itself was receiving unusual radiant heat.

The records stay in history with quality flags rather than disappearing.

## 22. Old Stone, New Glass

An older district remains cooler during afternoons but warmer overnight after a new glass-covered arcade is built nearby.

The project produces benefits and trade-offs that change by time of day.

## 23. The Schoolyard Retrofit

A schoolyard is mostly hard surface and becomes difficult to use during hot afternoons.

A redesign proposal must balance play, events, habitat, maintenance, visibility and drainage rather than merely maximizing trees.

## 24. Waterfront Relief

A waterfront promenade feels cooler during some weather patterns but offers little relief during calm humid evenings.

Local reputation says it is always the coolest place in town.

The difference between public belief and measurement becomes a small research story.

## 25. Hot Route, Quiet Route

A popular walking route remains busy because guidebooks and signs still recommend it, while a shaded alternative is known mostly to residents.

Tourism, mapping and public information all contribute to the pressure.

## 26. The First Cool Night

After a long hot period, one district finally cools rapidly overnight while another does not.

Researchers use the contrast to test hypotheses about stored heat and ventilation.

## 27. Heat After the Festival

Temporary stages, generators, stalls and barriers create a different thermal pattern for several days.

The event is over, but teardown timing still affects public-space use and wildlife observations.

## 28. Former Partner on the Cool Roof

A previously released Pokémon is repeatedly photographed on one shaded rooftop during hot afternoons.

The system records presence. It does not infer that the Pokémon returned for its former Trainer.

## 29. The Retrofit Nobody Notices

A small awning-and-tree project quietly makes one bus stop more usable for years.

It never becomes a famous landmark, but its long-term observation record makes it one of the best-documented interventions in the city.

## 30. Thermal Archive

An archive finds decades of maintenance logs, photographs and temperature records from before major redevelopment.

The historical material helps reconstruct how a district's microclimate changed over time without assuming the old city was better or worse in every respect.

# Long arc A — Five Summers in Meridian Ward

Year 1: residents report uneven heat but measurements are sparse.

Year 2: players and institutions build a proper morning/afternoon/evening survey network.

Year 3: two retrofit projects begin with different objectives.

Year 4: a drought stresses new canopy while energy demand rises.

Year 5: follow-up surveys show some zones improved, some shifted and one new hotspot emerged after redevelopment.

The arc can end with better institutional knowledge rather than a final boss.

# Long arc B — The City That Cools Unevenly

Several districts adopt different strategies:

- shade infrastructure;
- tree canopy;
- cool roofs;
- schedule changes;
- water-space restoration;
- transit redesign.

The story follows how each solution interacts with local ecology, maintenance, culture, funding and architecture.

No single intervention becomes the universal answer.

# Long arc C — Night Heat Atlas

A long-term science/public-space project maps which places remain warm after sunset.

The atlas gradually connects:

- material history;
- building geometry;
- vegetation;
- nightlife;
- energy systems;
- Pokémon activity;
- health-service demand;
- transport schedules.

New editions remain versioned as the city changes.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Heat-Survey Station Interruption

Narrative premise:

A sensor transect through a dense district must be completed before the afternoon observation window closes.

FULL version:

Moving crowds, competing access routes and protected sensor points can become tactical objectives.

Required capability families:

- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement when actors can block or escort;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions only if validated thermal field mechanics exist;
- AI tactical policy for route/protection objectives;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

REDUCED version:

Resolve crowds and sensor placement in overworld state. If battle begins, freeze one static location. Heat has no tactical effect unless separately validated.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Cooling-Courtyard Access

Narrative premise:

A courtyard important for public operations is disrupted by an unrelated wild-Pokémon conflict.

FULL version dependencies:

- complete movement/interception for evacuation and protected access;
- AI tactical policy;
- terrain/zones only if exact PTU mechanics are verified;
- adapter/playback.

REDUCED version:

Evacuate civilians before combat. Keep the courtyard's cooling/service function outside the grid. Run a conventional legal battle with actual combatants only.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Night-Heat Relay Failure

Narrative premise:

An energy/cooling relay reports inconsistent status while a district remains abnormally warm after sunset.

FULL version dependencies:

- interactable-object objectives;
- field/environment mechanics if ever validated;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback.

REDUCED version:

Investigate the relay in overworld state. Any battle uses a static rooftop/service area with no invented heat damage, visibility modifier or equipment bonus.

# Mechanical guardrails

These proposals do not add:

- heat damage;
- Burned;
- dehydration;
- fatigue;
- Sunny Day;
- Fire-type environmental immunity;
- Ice-type environmental weakness;
- cooling healing;
- shade Accuracy/evasion bonuses;
- water cooling buffs;
- automatic spawn changes;
- heat-based capture modifiers;
- crowd mechanics;
- building heat damage.

Any future mechanical effect must be sourced from PTU/Caelo and verified against AutoPTU/AutoPTU-Java.
