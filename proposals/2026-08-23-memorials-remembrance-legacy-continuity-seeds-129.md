# Memorials, Remembrance & Legacy Continuity — Seeds 129

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are candidates for future Ouros authoring, not established facts.

All seeds use the systems boundaries in `design/memorials-remembrance-legacy-continuity-layer.md`. None implies PTU death, inheritance law, Ghost activity, supernatural truth, private grief or Pokémon ownership unless separately authored and mechanically validated.

## 30 worldbuilding candidates

### 1. The Missing Expedition Marker

A small stone marker records the names of an expedition whose outcome remained unknown. Years later, one member returns alive with partial records. The marker is revised to distinguish returned, still missing and confirmed outcomes without erasing the earlier version.

### 2. The Bridge Names Were Incomplete

A bridge memorial credits one rescuer after a historical flood. Archive work later shows several workers and a Pokémon rescue team participated. The town must decide how to revise the inscription while preserving why the earlier account existed.

### 3. The Living Service Garden

A retired service Pokémon receives a garden recognizing decades of work while still alive and independent. Visitors repeatedly assume the garden is a grave until signage is improved.

### 4. Two Names, One Person

An old memorial lists two historical names that Identity/Record Linkage later resolves as the same actor under different aliases. The original carving remains historically authentic even after the current interpretation changes.

### 5. The Monument Shows the Wrong Route

A famous relief depicts an evacuation crossing the east bridge. Later maps show the bridge did not yet exist. The monument remains culturally important while historians investigate what the artist was reconstructing.

### 6. The Memorial That Moved With the River

Channel migration makes a riverside marker inaccessible. The community moves the marker inland while leaving a small foundation record at the old coordinates.

### 7. The Accessible Path Revision

A memorial site predates modern accessibility work. A new route changes the entrance, landscape and procession path. Older photos remain valid for earlier revisions.

### 8. The Public Memorial, Private Family

A widely known public figure is commemorated by an institution while relatives or former partners prefer privacy. The system must preserve separate scopes instead of treating public attention as consent to reveal private records.

### 9. The Replica Became the Familiar One

A fragile original memorial object moves into a museum for conservation. A durable replica remains outside and becomes the version most residents recognize. Both acquire independent provenance.

### 10. The Old Gym Became a Clinic

A former Gym building is adaptively reused decades after a major local event. Its commemorative function moves to one preserved wall and a garden rather than freezing the whole structure forever.

### 11. The Quiet Anniversary

A major anniversary passes with routine maintenance and a handful of visitors. No crisis, mystery or quest occurs. Chronicle records continuity rather than manufacturing drama.

### 12. The Population Returns

A marker remembers the local disappearance of a Pokémon population. Years after habitat restoration, the species recolonizes naturally. The memorial is revised to remember the loss period rather than removed as “incorrect.”

### 13. The Memorial Tree Fell

A long-standing memorial tree falls in a storm. Possible responses include retaining the stump, creating a new object from part of the wood, planting another tree, or letting the site change. No option is mechanically superior by default.

### 14. The Photograph From the Wrong Year

A photograph used on an anniversary display is authentic but belongs to the following year's event. Correcting the caption creates a new publication revision without changing the photo itself.

### 15. The Station Clock

A clock preserved after a rail station closes becomes a local memorial to the workers and passengers who used the route. When the building is redeveloped, the clock's custody and the station's commemorative identity must be handled separately.

### 16. The Evacuation Marker With No Deaths

A town installs a marker after a successful evacuation in which everyone returned safely. The memorial celebrates preparedness and collective action rather than tragedy.

### 17. The Missing Pokémon Marker

A Trainer maintains a small marker for a former partner who disappeared after release. The marker deliberately says “missing” rather than “deceased.” Years later, a credible field observation may or may not identify the same Pokémon.

### 18. The Annual Walk Changes Date

A remembrance walk was historically date-fixed. Ecological pressure on the old route causes the community to shift the event window. Observance history records both date and route revisions.

### 19. The Digital Page Outlives the Hall

A memorial hall is demolished during redevelopment after its objects move elsewhere. A versioned digital archive remains accessible and preserves photographs of each physical phase.

### 20. The Name Omitted on Purpose

Researchers notice one participant is absent from a public memorial despite strong evidence of involvement. Archive records eventually show the omission was a documented privacy request rather than error or political exclusion.

### 21. The Sponsor Line Changed

A sponsor funded restoration of a memorial and receives a small acknowledgement. A later sponsor replaces it. The commemorated subject and historical event do not change with the funding relationship.

### 22. The Caretakers Disagree About Method

Two caretakers share the same stewardship goal but differ on whether to restore an old marker, preserve weathering, or replace it with a replica. The conflict concerns conservation method, not ownership or loyalty.

### 23. The Pokémon Visitor

The same wild Pokémon is observed near a memorial on several anniversaries. Researchers can record timing and behavior. The generator may not state that the Pokémon is grieving, related to the subject or sensing a spirit without authored evidence.

### 24. The Memorial Grove Becomes Habitat

A planted grove matures into important nesting habitat. A popular annual ceremony now creates disturbance. Conservation and commemoration need a new access plan rather than one system automatically overruling the other.

### 25. The Equipment Between Archive and Memorial

Tools recovered from an old expedition are historically important. Some become museum collection objects; one remains at a memorial site; notebooks go to Archives. They share provenance without needing one institution to own every component.

### 26. The Forgotten Former Site

A modern memorial stands in a central square. Cartography and old photographs reveal that two earlier versions occupied different locations. Foundations at one former site become a small archaeology/public-memory project.

### 27. The Bell With No Mechanical Effect

A bell is rung annually as part of a civic remembrance practice. It produces sound, attendance records and public memory. It does not heal, summon Ghost-types, change Weather or activate a battle field effect.

### 28. The Memorial Without a Quest

A route contains a modest marker maintained by local workers for decades. Players may pass it dozens of times. Its inscriptions and surroundings change gradually, but no special mission is required to justify its existence.

### 29. The Caretaker's Last Season

A long-term caretaker announces retirement. Several possible successors can accept different scopes: grounds maintenance, archive work, event organization or public communication. No single “heir” is assumed.

### 30. The Disaster Name Changes

A historic disaster is known by one name locally and another in regional archives. A new memorial revision displays both without deciding that one community's terminology was illegitimate.

## Long arc 1 — The Names on Meridian Bridge

Year 1: an aging bridge carries a modest plaque naming people credited with a decades-old rescue.

Year 2: an archive digitization links one name to an alias used later in life.

Year 3: infrastructure inspection requires moving the plaque temporarily while the bridge is rebuilt.

Year 4: worker-association records and photographs reveal additional participants omitted from the original account.

Year 5: the rebuilt bridge receives a revised accessible memorial installation. The original plaque enters a local collection rather than being discarded.

Possible later state: a new flood tests the rebuilt crossing. The memorial can gain another layer without collapsing old and new events into one story.

Design value: Identity, Archives, Worker Associations, Architecture, Museums, Public Memory and Emergency Services all contribute different evidence without any one layer owning the whole arc.

## Long arc 2 — The Lantern Hill Caretakers

Initial state: a hilltop remembrance garden has been maintained informally by one actor for many years.

Phase 1: the caretaker reduces their workload and documents which tasks were personal habits versus actual institutional responsibilities.

Phase 2: different actors offer to maintain the path, archives and annual public event.

Phase 3: Land Tenure reveals that the site itself belongs under a different stewardship arrangement than the memorial objects.

Phase 4: an accessibility project changes the approach and maintenance burden.

Phase 5: responsibility becomes a small network rather than passing intact to one successor.

Design value: succession can mean continuity of responsibility without inventing inheritance, bloodline authority or ownership transfer.

## Long arc 3 — The Expedition That Stayed Missing

Year 1: a research party disappears after communication failure. Search efforts produce no confirmed outcome.

Year 2: families, colleagues and institutions establish a marker explicitly using missing-status language.

Year 3: recovered equipment adds evidence but does not establish what happened to each person or Pokémon.

Year 4: one member returns or is independently located. Their testimony resolves some records and contradicts others.

Year 5: the memorial becomes a history of the expedition and the years of uncertainty rather than a false collective grave.

Possible branch: one Pokémon associated with the expedition is observed later but identity remains uncertain until corroborated through Pokémon Agency/Photography/Tracking.

Design value: the world can revise knowledge without treating earlier uncertainty as a mistake or requiring a supernatural explanation.

## Encounter contract A — Memorial Procession Route Interruption

Premise: a recurring remembrance walk encounters a route obstruction or independent threat.

FULL version:

- procession/civilians continue to have spatial goals;
- actors may need to withdraw, cross or protect a route;
- external threats can reposition relative to the moving group;
- semantic objectives survive into playback.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED;
- base movement legality: VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: BLOCKING;
- core calculations: VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL when used;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING only if the obstruction is mechanically environmental;
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL;
- abilities: PARTIAL;
- items: PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING.

REDUCED version:

Stop the procession in world state, move civilians out of the arena, freeze one legal street segment, run a conventional static battle and decide afterward whether the walk resumes, reroutes or ends early. The battle does not define beliefs or emotional state.

## Encounter contract B — Archive Recovery at Old Memorial Hall

Premise: records from an earlier memorial revision become temporarily inaccessible during an independent safety incident.

FULL version:

If the site is dynamically unstable, it requires complete movement, tactical AI, adapter/playback and any relevant verified terrain/hazard/reaction mechanics.

REDUCED version:

Cordon unsafe rooms in world state. Move noncombatants and fragile records outside the grid. Use one validated static room/corridor for battle. Recover and interpret records only after battle under Archives/Cases/Public Memory.

Winning cannot determine historical truth.

## Encounter contract C — Caretaker Succession at Lantern Hill

Premise: stewardship responsibilities must change hands because a caretaker retires.

FULL version:

This is primarily non-combat. If a separate crisis interrupts the meeting, dynamic evacuation/protection requires complete movement, tactical AI and adapter/playback.

REDUCED version:

Resolve proposals, scope and acceptance through Agreements, Credentials, Land Tenure and Institutional Review. Create a `legacy_handoff` only after authority and acceptance are established. No battle is needed.

Winning any unrelated battle cannot award stewardship, land, custody, money or Pokémon authority.

## Explicit exclusions

These proposals do not create:

- universal burial or funeral customs;
- inheritance law;
- resurrection;
- automatic Ghost activity;
- death from Fainted/Injury;
- grief or morale stats;
- memorial buffs;
- ritual healing;
- Legendary encounter triggers;
- Friendship/Loyalty changes;
- automatic family or next-of-kin relationships;
- ownership transfer of Pokémon or property;
- sacred status from memorial status alone;
- quests on every anniversary.
