# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Human Identity, Name & Record Continuity — Pass 137

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Every item in this file remains optional until explicitly approved.
Date: 2026-08-29

## Design intent

These seeds use human identity continuity as a source of grounded mystery, service friction, regional texture and long-term NPC continuity.

They do not establish a universal identity authority, legal-name regime, Trainer-license system, citizenship, passports, biometrics or public access to private identity records.

Any institution named below is a role placeholder unless Ouros canon already establishes it.

## Seed — The Name on the Old Roster

A current academy participant appears in a years-old activity roster under another name form.

The apparent contradiction can resolve through a verified historical linkage, a contextual name, a transcription variant or a genuinely different person.

Useful owners:

- Formal Education for enrollment/attendance;
- Archives for historical custody;
- Human Identity continuity for record linkage;
- Personal Records only if private correspondence is voluntarily introduced.

The player never needs to learn why a former name changed unless the actor or governing record makes that relevant and disclosable.

## Seed — Two Trainers Named Ilya

Two unrelated regulars share the same displayed name. A tournament archive, market tab or club photo has gradually mixed references to them.

The mystery resolves through chronology, Pokémon association references, known locations and issuer-local identifiers where those are already canonized.

The solution must not infer that a Pokémon belongs to someone merely because the Trainer name matches.

## Seed — The Correction Reached the Clinic First

An upstream identity record was corrected. The clinic copied the update, while the library, school or local club still holds a prior form.

Both downstream records are authentic snapshots from different times.

The quest is about propagation and service continuity, not exposing a forged file.

## Seed — The Poster Uses the Stage Name

A performer is known publicly under a stage or professional name. A backstage service record uses another verified form.

The challenge is to coordinate access without forcing unnecessary disclosure to fans, volunteers or unrelated NPCs.

This seed becomes valid only if canon establishes the actor's public/professional naming choice.

## Seed — The Returnee Has Two Spellings

A person who spent years in another region returns. Local and host-region records render the same name differently.

The player can help establish a scoped linkage so an academy, research institute, club or other owner can recognize a prior record.

The story should preserve both local forms rather than declare one spelling universally correct.

## Seed — The Archive Refuses to Guess

Two historical records may refer to the same person, but surviving evidence is insufficient.

The satisfying resolution is `ACCEPTED_AMBIGUITY`.

A later discovery can reopen the question, but the current quest closes because the institution correctly records uncertainty rather than fabricating certainty.

## Seed — The Old Badge Is Genuine

A credential is authentic, but the holder name on it no longer matches the current public name record.

Identity continuity can establish the linkage. Credentials still decides whether the old badge or authorization remains valid for the requested scope.

`IDENTITY_LINKED != CREDENTIAL_VALID` is the central lesson.

## Seed — The Wrong Mara Got the Notice

A public notice or appointment reminder was routed using a name string rather than the intended actor reference.

There are two local people with that name.

The correction requires identifying the intended recipient without exposing the other person's private information.

Communications/Public Notices owns routing and publication; identity continuity only resolves the collision.

## Seed — The Shop Sign Never Changed

A long-running shop still carries the founder's old public name even though the current operator uses a different family, professional or personal form.

Environmental storytelling makes the sign a historical object rather than evidence that the operator is using a false identity.

Commercial Services owns business state. Public Memory may explain why residents preserve the old sign.

## Seed — Three Membership Cards, One Person

A recurring NPC possesses old membership artifacts from three institutions. Each uses a different abbreviation, spelling or title.

The player can trace issuer history and dates to understand how the records relate.

No universal card format is implied.

## Seed — The Photo Caption Was Correct Then

An old community photograph contains a caption that no longer matches the actor's current name.

A well-meaning NPC proposes rewriting the original caption. The archive instead preserves the historical caption and adds a modern linkage annotation where policy permits.

The story demonstrates preservation without freezing identity forever.

## Seed — The Name Nobody Needs to Know

A service needs to confirm that the person at the counter is the same actor tied to an appointment. It does not need a former name that appears in an older record.

The quest rewards information minimization: resolve the required linkage without exposing irrelevant history.

## Seed — The School Knows, the Club Does Not

An academy has already verified a record linkage. A separate local club has not.

The academy cannot automatically disclose its private evidence.

The player must find a permitted path for the club to resolve its own narrow question or accept the mismatch.

## Seed — The Author Returned Under a Familiar Name

A writer published early work under one credited form and later work under another.

A library exhibit wants to connect the works. The linkage may be public, private, disputed or not yet approved for display.

Public Library and Archives own display/access. Identity continuity only stores the proven or proposed link.

## Seed — The Tournament Database Copied Yesterday

A tournament check-in list was generated before an upstream correction. The participant arrives after the correction became effective.

The current record and the roster disagree without either being fabricated.

Battle Institutions decides registration and participation. Identity continuity provides provenance and the correction chain.

## Mystery — Five Times Someone Was “The Same Person”

Five records each seem to refer to one familiar NPC:

1. same displayed name and same actor;
2. same name but a different actor;
3. different name but verified same actor;
4. different spelling with unresolved linkage;
5. a cover identity already established by Infiltration.

The mystery trains the player to stop treating name strings as identity truth.

No single universal clue type resolves every case.

## Mystery — Four Dates, Three Names

A local institution has four dated records using three name forms.

The player builds a timeline around record creation, effective correction date and later propagation.

Possible resolution:

- Record A was correct for its date.
- Record B copied A after a change but before notification.
- Record C reflects the new current form.
- Record D contains a genuine transcription error.

The records disagree for different reasons.

## Mystery — The Person Who Was “Never Enrolled”

An alumnus appears in school photographs and project logs but not under their current name in the old enrollment index.

The tempting answer is a secret student. The safer investigation tests:

- former/contextual name;
- spelling variant;
- archive indexing gap;
- temporary placement;
- genuine non-enrollment participation;
- two same-named people.

Formal Education remains the authority on enrollment.

## Mystery — The Pokémon Knows Both Names

A recurring partner Pokémon reacts consistently to an NPC whom different records name differently.

That behavior may support continuity as an observation but cannot prove human identity, ownership, emotional state or motive by itself.

Pokémon Agency preserves the observed association. Human identity still requires appropriate evidence.

## Exploration — The Office With Three Directories

An old municipal, academy or institutional building contains directories from three eras.

Each directory reflects a different period of staff names, titles, room assignments and organizational structure.

The player can reconstruct who worked where and when without any battle mechanics.

Static-world requirements:

- dated signage or directory props;
- authored institutional history;
- Chronicle references;
- privacy filtering for records not meant to be public.

## Exploration — The Hall of Old Team Photos

A club or academy corridor has decades of group photos. Captions reveal changing naming conventions, professional titles and recurring people.

The exploration can seed future questions while allowing some old captions to remain deliberately ambiguous.

No image recognition mechanic is assumed.

## Sidequest chain — The Downstream Copies

Phase 1: a small service mismatch reveals that one downstream roster still uses an older name form.

Phase 2: the player discovers several downstream copies created at different times.

Phase 3: each owner must decide whether an update is relevant to its own service.

Phase 4: one historical archive intentionally does not alter the original record, adding only an annotation.

Phase 5: the actor's current routine continues normally while the Chronicle preserves the correction history.

The chain can involve Education, Library, Care, Battle Institutions or Workplaces without allowing the identity layer to usurp their decisions.

## Longer arc — A Town Learns Who Its Records Refer To

Opening state:

A town has ordinary institutions whose records were created independently over decades. Most residents never notice that the systems use different local identifiers, abbreviations and display conventions.

Inciting event:

A routine transfer, application or historical project reveals a mismatch around one familiar NPC.

Middle movement:

The mismatch is legitimate. Resolving it exposes several adjacent cases: a homonym, an old public name, a delayed correction and an archive that contains insufficient evidence.

Institutional consequence:

Local organizations improve record-linkage practice and stop treating name equality as actor equality. They do not necessarily create a centralized registry.

Character consequence:

One recurring NPC gains more control over which name is displayed where. Another chooses to preserve an old professional form in a public setting. A third historical question remains unresolved.

Environmental consequence:

Updated directories, annotation cards, corrected event lists and old untouched archival artifacts coexist in the same settlement.

Long-tail callback:

Months later, a visitor from another region arrives with records that use yet another naming convention. The town now has better habits but still needs a scoped recognition decision.

This arc advances world maturity without requiring a conspiracy or legal-drama spine.

## Faction and institution dynamics

### Archive versus Service Desk

The archive values historical fidelity. The service desk values current usability. Both can be right because they answer different questions.

### Local Institution versus Host Region

Both can recognize one actor while preserving different display forms and identifiers.

### Privacy Advocate versus Public Historian

One wants to minimize disclosure. The other wants an accurate exhibit. The conflict should be solved by scope and consent/authority, not by declaring one side anti-truth.

### Credential Issuer versus Identity Verifier

The issuer can confirm a credential was real. A separate process may still be needed to link the credential to the current presenter. Neither process should silently absorb the other.

## NPC archetypes

The Record Linker remembers how old systems were indexed but must still cite sources.

The Same-Name Regular has learned to ask which record people mean before accepting a claim.

The Returnee understands two regional conventions and can translate context without acting as universal authority.

The Privacy-Conscious Archivist can confirm a linkage exists while refusing to disclose the underlying private evidence.

The Downstream Clerk works from a stale but authentic copy and becomes useful once chronology is reconstructed.

The Public-Facing Professional is known under a stable public identity that need not expose every private record form.

## Encounter — Record Transfer Chokepoint

Status: FULL VERSION BLOCKED FOR RICH SEMANTICS. REDUCED VERSION READY.

The full form would use escort/Intercept, protected-object routing, staged movement and objective-aware AI.

The reduced form pauses Courier/Archives custody movement before battle. Records stay outside BattleSpec. Combat clears the route. The custody owner later decides whether transfer resumes.

Battle victory never authenticates a packet, transfers custody or links records.

## Encounter — Verification Appointment Perimeter

Status: FULL VERSION BLOCKED FOR RICH SEMANTICS. REDUCED VERSION READY.

Applicant, verifier, records and identity evidence remain outside the battle. The tactical outcome can only produce a narrow access fact such as `IMMEDIATE_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

The service resumes proofing independently.

`TACTICAL_VICTORY != VERIFIED_IDENTITY`.

## Minecraft presentation candidates

Potential presentation-only world details:

- old and new directory plaques;
- dated event rosters;
- archived posters using historical public names;
- corrected spelling notices;
- separate school/library/club numbering conventions;
- NPC dialogue that uses context-appropriate forms;
- old business signage retained as local history;
