# Pass 68 Proposals — Light, Darkness and Night Ecology

Status: NON-CANON. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns.

No proposal below defines PTU mechanics. Any mechanical visibility, Blinded, Glow, Darkvision, Flash, stealth, Accuracy, weather or terrain effect requires exact PTU/Caelo validation plus engine support.

## 30 worldbuilding / quest seeds

### 1. The Beacon That Missed One Night

A harbor beacon fails once after decades of reliable service. Shipping records, operator logs, a maintenance visit and wildlife observations all preserve different pieces of the night. The interesting question is why one failure mattered so much to public memory.

### 2. The Dark Corridor

A newly lit service road cuts across a forest edge. Researchers establish a deliberately dark vegetation corridor beside it and compare activity over several seasons. Early results are mixed rather than dramatic.

### 3. Five Nights Without the Lights

A population known for regular biological-light displays stops appearing for five nights. Weather, moonlight, tourism, water quality and seasonal movement are all plausible factors. No disappearance is assumed.

### 4. The Old Lighthouse Partner

A long-serving Pokémon associated with a lighthouse retires from the role. The institution must decide how to replace the service while preserving the Pokémon’s agency and the landmark’s public identity.

### 5. Streetlights at the Marsh

A settlement extends street lighting along a new walking route. Residents appreciate the safer navigation. Ecologists later notice a change in nighttime observations near the marsh. The project becomes a review rather than a morality play.

### 6. The Night Market Trial

A town pilots a monthly night market. It increases evening foot traffic, staffing demand, waste, light and transportation use. The trial remains reversible while institutions collect evidence about effects.

### 7. Pattern 217

Researchers studying recurring Volbeat/Illumise-style light displays catalog a pattern they have never seen before. Local oral tradition gives it one meaning; researchers refuse to assign meaning without repeated observation.

### 8. The Missing Harbor Glow

Chinchou-like flashes normally appear offshore before dawn. A week of clear conditions produces none. Fishers, researchers and ferry crews each notice for different reasons.

### 9. Observatory Blackout Night

An observatory schedules a dark-sky test by coordinating nearby businesses, streetlights and event venues. The sky improves, but one transport operator reports an unexpected operational problem. The next version must balance both needs.

### 10. The Bright Warehouse

A distribution center installs new high-intensity lighting. Night-shift productivity improves. Nearby cameras begin recording different wild activity. Causation remains open until comparative data exists.

### 11. Glare on the Water

Harbor floodlights reflect across calm water. Residents report seeing “more predators” near the surface. Researchers test whether actual abundance changed, detectability changed or hunting behavior shifted.

### 12. The Forest Light

Travelers repeatedly report a soft light in a forest that stays dark even during the day. Multiple possible biological sources exist. The first objective is observation, not following the light.

### 13. Lantern Route Archive

A historic route used different beacon patterns before modern electrical lighting. Restoring one surviving tower reveals that old travel logs only make sense when the signal code is understood.

### 14. Emergency Path Lights

After a crisis, a town installs temporary illuminated evacuation markers. Months later nobody has decided whether they should become permanent. The decision connects emergency planning, energy use, dark-sky concerns and accessibility.

### 15. The Night Shift Naturalists

Workers at a treatment plant become the best observers of local nocturnal Pokémon because researchers usually visit during the day. Their informal records become the start of a formal monitoring program.

### 16. Stadium Afterglow

A large competition ends, but field lights remain on for hours during cleanup. A nearby ecological study discovers that event nights differ from ordinary nights. The venue considers operational changes that do not require ending the event.

### 17. Three Lamps, Three Histories

Three identical-looking street lamps were installed decades apart under different public-works projects. Only one is tied to a historic route marker. A planned replacement requires archival work before work begins.

### 18. The Wrong Light on the Map

A navigation chart marks a fixed beacon where the actual signal was moved years ago. The old location is now an ecological monitoring point. A correction must preserve the historical record instead of deleting it.

### 19. Flashing in the Tunnel

A maintenance crew reports repeated light patterns beyond a closed tunnel section. Machinery, reflected signals, biological light and unauthorized entry are all plausible explanations.

### 20. The Quiet Dark Village

A small settlement deliberately uses low, shielded lighting. Visitors initially interpret it as underdevelopment. Residents describe it as a long-standing choice tied to astronomy, insects and local identity.

### 21. Light Sensitive Exhibit

A museum must reduce illumination around a fragile object while keeping the exhibit accessible. The resulting design becomes a small project involving preservation, visitor experience and accessibility rather than a puzzle.

### 22. Moonless Survey

A field team schedules observations around a very dark lunar phase. Clouds arrive anyway. The “failed” night still produces useful information about instrumentation, staffing and unexpected wildlife activity.

### 23. The Beacon Pokémon Returns

A former institutional partner that was released or retired appears near the old beacon during one season. The institution records the visit without claiming the Pokémon has returned to service.

### 24. Harbor Lights and Migration

A migration route passes near a growing port. Over several years, route timing appears to shift. Light is one hypothesis among shipping noise, weather, prey distribution and construction.

### 25. The Darkroom District

Photographers prefer an older district because its nighttime lighting is unusual and visually distinctive. Popularity brings more visitors and new businesses, gradually changing the lightscape that attracted them.

### 26. Cave Survey With Two Teams

One team uses instruments and portable lighting. Another surveys the same cave using Pokémon with authoritative visual capabilities. Their maps match in geometry but differ in what features were noticed.

### 27. The Flickering Clinic Sign

A clinic sign starts failing intermittently. Residents interpret it as evidence the clinic itself is failing. In reality, clinical capacity is normal; the technical issue still matters because it affects wayfinding at night.

### 28. Light at the Nesting Shore

A seasonal nesting area overlaps with a popular evening promenade. The region tests timed lighting, shielding and temporary rerouting rather than imposing an immediate permanent closure.

### 29. The Festival of Small Lights

A traditional festival uses hundreds of small lights rather than one bright display. Modern safety staff propose stronger illumination. The event becomes a careful design question involving heritage, crowd movement, accessibility and ecology.

### 30. The Last Unlit Road

A remote route is the last major road without permanent lighting. Residents disagree about an upgrade for practical reasons rather than ideological ones. A pilot segment gives everyone evidence before a final decision.

## Three longer arcs

### Arc A — The Changing Night of Port Meridian

Year 1: a port expands and installs new lighting.

Year 2: ferry schedules extend later, restaurants stay open longer and night-shift staffing grows.

Year 3: aquatic and shoreline monitoring begins after residents report different wildlife activity.

Year 4: the port tests shielded fixtures, darker ecological corridors and revised cleanup schedules.

Year 5: the settlement has a distinct night economy and a better-understood lightscape, but no single “perfect” brightness level.

This arc connects Technology, Workplaces, Tourism, Maritime, Freshwater, Ecology, Public Works and Astronomy.

### Arc B — The Lantern Network

Players discover a chain of old signal towers across several routes.

Early arc:

- each tower appears unrelated;
- some use mirrors, some firelight, some biological partners, some later electrical retrofits;
- maps disagree because signal sites moved over time.

Middle arc:

- archives reveal several historical revisions to the network;
- one surviving signal pattern is still used by a remote community;
- a modern emergency-planning project considers reusing part of the network.

Late arc:

The final outcome may be restoration, documentation only, partial reuse or preservation as heritage. No supernatural explanation is required.

### Arc C — The Nocturnal Atlas

A regional research program starts with scattered reports of night-active Pokémon.

Over several years:

- baseline surveys are collected;
- artificial-light changes are mapped;
- weather and seasons are separated from lighting effects;
- individual persistent Pokémon and collectives are distinguished;
- photography and soundscape records are linked;
- public summaries are produced without publishing sensitive locations.

The atlas becomes a source of future questions instead of a static Pokédex replacement.
