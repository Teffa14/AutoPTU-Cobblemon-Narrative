## Premise

A minor field task sends someone to inspect an old survey-side structure near the existing road network. The location looks ordinary. The useful result is a copied marking, measurement, photograph, rubbing, sketch or other bounded observation that nobody can confidently interpret at the time.

The task can end there.

Much later, an unrelated archive review exposes an older route record whose terminology or geometry makes the earlier observation newly relevant. The old evidence now points toward a question worth testing in the field.

The second expedition returns to the same area for a different reason than the first.

## Why the first trip matters

The first observation has value because it was collected at a known time from a known place by a known actor.

If the site changed later, the earlier record becomes more valuable rather than obsolete.

If a persistent NPC team performed the first task while the player was elsewhere, that team owns the corresponding knowledge until it is communicated or published through existing systems.

If nobody performed the task, the later archive clue cannot invent the missing observation retroactively.

## Investigation structure

First pass:
- inspect an ordinary site;
- record one or more observations;
- leave without a complete explanation.

Later interpretation:
- an archivist, researcher or other qualified actor encounters a record that changes the question;
- old and new evidence remain separate provenance objects;
- communication determines who learns the new interpretation.

Return expedition:
- revisit the location;
- compare physical revision, access and measurements;
- decide whether the earlier observation described a stable feature, a temporary condition, a recording error or something still unexplained.

The mystery can remain mundane. Nothing requires supernatural causation, conspiracy or deception.

## Longer-term arc potential

This structure can recur across Ouros without turning every mark into the same mystery.

Several small observations from independent trips can later reveal a regional pattern only after enough provenance-backed evidence exists. Other observations may never become important. This keeps exploration useful without making the world feel pre-scripted around the player.

The intended cadence is fieldwork, delayed context, reinterpretation, return. Each stage can be performed by different persistent actors.
