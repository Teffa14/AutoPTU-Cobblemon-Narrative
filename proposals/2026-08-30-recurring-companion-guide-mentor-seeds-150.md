# Recurring Companion, Guide & Mentor Seeds — Pass 150

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These are original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. Names, places, institutions, NPCs and events in this file are not established canon unless separately approved.

Date: 2026-08-30

## Design target

These seeds test recurring NPCs who participate for a bounded reason, leave causally, continue to exist independently and may later return under changed circumstances. None assumes a permanent follower slot or automatic battle participation.

## The Guide Who Stops at the Ridge

A local guide accepts a route job through foothills they know well. At a high ridge, the old markers end and the terrain beyond falls outside the guide's lived knowledge.

The guide does not become suddenly incompetent. Their useful scope simply ends.

Player options may include:

- continue with maps and observations already gathered;
- return and seek another specialist;
- investigate why newer route records disagree;
- establish a temporary camp;
- ask the guide what they know about people who traveled farther.

Persistent outputs:

- one closed `LOCAL_GUIDE` companion episode;
- route knowledge attributed to the correct source;
- a clear boundary between confirmed local knowledge and speculation;
- possible future recontact if later work returns to the guide's actual territory.

No battle is required.

## The Friend Who Chose the Other Road

A recurring friendly NPC and the player share a regional objective but discover that two urgent responsibilities now point in different directions.

The NPC chooses one route because of their own obligation. The player chooses independently.

The separation can later create:

- different observations of the same regional event;
- information neither party possesses alone;
- a reunion where each side has changed context;
- conflicting public accounts without interpersonal betrayal.

The seed deliberately protects `LEFT_PARTY != RELATIONSHIP_BROKEN`.

## The Mentor Returns as a Witness

An NPC previously established as a mentor reappears months later during a formal review of an event they directly observed.

Their present role is `WITNESS_OR_OBSERVER`, not `MENTOR_FIELD_EPISODE`.

The player may still have a mentorship relationship with them, but that does not give the witness decision authority. Their testimony is bounded by what they actually saw and remember.

This seed links Social Bonds, actor knowledge and adjudication without letting mentorship swallow those systems.

## The Researcher Who Travels for One Leg

A researcher joins an expedition because one route segment passes the monitoring site relevant to their work. Once that fieldwork is complete, they return with the next available service while the player's expedition continues.

Useful consequences:

- the researcher can later publish, revise or dispute an interpretation through Science state;
- the player's later discoveries may change the meaning of the earlier measurement;
- the NPC can plausibly be absent from the dangerous deeper expedition;
- a later reunion may occur at a laboratory, hearing, field station or unrelated journey.

Participation never makes the researcher's conclusions automatically true.

## The Companion Who Refuses the Finale

A recurring ally receives a plausible invitation to a major convergence but declines.

Possible causal reasons include:

- another obligation is more important to them;
- they disagree with the proposed approach;
- their competency is irrelevant there;
- they are protecting something elsewhere;
- they cannot reach the site in time;
- they consider their earlier promise already fulfilled.

The campaign must accept the absence. It cannot teleport the character into the climax because the audience recognizes them.

A later aftermath scene can make that absence meaningful without framing it automatically as cowardice or betrayal.

## The Ally Who Returns With Different Priorities

An NPC who once accompanied the player during a rescue operation returns after joining a local organization.

They still recognize and may respect the player, but current obligations now constrain what help they can offer.

The reunion can produce cooperation, refusal, negotiation or a narrow shared objective. It should not restore the exact earlier companion role by default.

## The Camp They Left Behind

A temporary expedition companion departs when weather, transport or schedule makes their return unavoidable. Their old campsite remains as environmental continuity.

Months later the site contains:

- repaired or missing equipment owned by the correct system;
- a changed notice or field marker;
- evidence of subsequent visitors;
- a route condition that has changed since the original episode.

The companion does not need to be physically present for their prior participation to remain visible.

## The Specialist Everyone Assumed Knew the Mountain

A community repeatedly describes one NPC as “the mountain guide.” The player eventually learns that the specialist knows one valley and two seasonal routes extremely well but has never crossed a famous northern traverse.

The conflict comes from reputation compression rather than fraud.

Potential mystery:

- who first expanded the local title into a region-wide reputation?
- did an old map credit the wrong person?
- has a later visitor confused two guides with similar names?

The specialist can be honest and still fail to possess the knowledge others attribute to them.

## The Temporary Interpreter

A multilingual specialist accompanies the party only through a district where communication is difficult. At the next settlement, ordinary shared language makes their role unnecessary.

The player later encounters them in a different professional role.

The seed reinforces that role completion is a normal departure reason and that translation does not automatically expose private knowledge or guarantee correct cultural interpretation.

## The Boat Operator Who Walks the Last Mile

A transport operator normally ends responsibility at the dock. After a local disruption, they choose to accompany the travelers on foot to verify that a specific passenger reaches a staging point.

This creates a one-off companion episode caused by current world state rather than a permanent upgrade to the operator's service.

After the incident, the operator returns to ordinary work.

## The Old Teacher at the New Field Station

A former teacher appears at a new field station. The player's social history recognizes them, but the NPC is now working under another institution and can only act within its current remit.

Possible outcomes:

- field collaboration;
- a professional disagreement;
- a brief supervised exercise;
- simple conversation with no quest;
- referral to a different specialist.

The callback succeeds because the context changed.

## Mystery — Five Times They Said “She Was With Us”

Five records describe the same NPC as being “with” an expedition.

The records actually refer to different facts:

- registered during planning;
- traveled on the first route leg;
- slept at the same staging camp;
- was present at the approach to a battle site;
- was an explicit BattleSpec combatant in one encounter.

The mystery is resolved by reconstructing participation scope rather than choosing one record as a lie.

Potential closure:

`MULTIPLE_COMPATIBLE_PRESENCE_STATES_CONFIRMED`.

## Mystery — The Missing Guide Was Never Missing

Later stories claim a guide vanished during an expedition. Contemporary records instead show a planned route-boundary departure followed by poor communication between two groups.

The dramatic version became public memory because nobody preserved the ordinary departure note prominently.

The historical answer may remain incomplete if the guide's exact later route cannot be reconstructed.

## Mystery — Three Reunions, One Promise

Different people remember a recurring NPC promising to “come back.”

Possible evidence reveals that the statement referred to:

- returning to the settlement;
- returning after a work shift;
- returning for next year's seasonal survey.

The narrative should preserve which interpretation is actually supported and avoid converting vague expectation into an enforceable promise.

## Mystery — The Mentor Who Was Present but Did Not See It

A mentor was physically present at a site during an important incident, so later accounts treat them as an authoritative eyewitness.

Actor-knowledge reconstruction shows that their line of sight, location or attention did not cover the crucial moment.

Presence and observation become separate facts.

## Long arc — The People Who Travel With You for a While

The campaign introduces several recurring NPCs through ordinary bounded participation rather than assembling a permanent supporting cast.

Early phase:

A local guide accompanies one route. A researcher shares one expedition leg. A mentor supervises one field task. Each episode ends cleanly.

Middle phase:

World Agency moves those characters independently. One changes jobs. One remains geographically close but becomes busy. One becomes connected to an institution the player later needs. Another does not appear for months.

Recontact phase:

New world events cause some, but not all, characters to overlap with the player again. Their roles have changed. The old guide may now need directions outside their home territory. The researcher may possess a new claim but no authority over an institutional decision. The mentor may ask the former learner for help in a domain where roles have reversed.

Convergence phase:

A large regional event creates several plausible participation candidates. Campaign Convergence evaluates knowledge, reach, motive and availability. Some former companions attend. Others do not. No reunion is mandatory.

Aftermath:

The Chronicle can show a history of short shared journeys instead of a permanent party roster. Places, correspondence, public records and personal memories preserve those episodes after people separate.

## Encounter — Separated Expedition Reunion Perimeter

Full version:

Several expedition participants approach a rendezvous from different routes and may arrive at different tactical times while hostile actors pressure the site.

Key extra dependencies are full lifecycle for timed arrivals, AI tactical policy for multiple objectives, and adapter/playback for authoritative arrival presentation. Complete movement and terrain/reactions become relevant when the authored variant uses them.

Full version status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

Ouros resolves arrival before BattleSpec. The roster and arena are fixed. AutoPTU may establish `IMMEDIATE_REUNION_PERIMETER_CLEAR`; it does not decide who reached the site.

## Encounter — Mentor Withdrawal Chokepoint

Full version:

A mentor or specialist withdraws while hostiles contest the route. Intercept, reactions, forced movement and tactical withdrawal priorities can matter.

Full version status: BLOCKED because complete movement is PARTIAL, generalized terrain/reaction support is BLOCKING where used, AI tactical policy is BLOCKING, and adapter/playback remains BLOCKING.

Reduced version: READY.

The mentor is removed from tactical state before initiative. The remaining explicit combatants fight in static geometry. Victory can clear the immediate approach but cannot prove the mentor's later survival, destination, relationship role or return.

## Canon questions exposed by these seeds

Future review must decide which actual Ouros NPCs, guide practices, research expeditions, mentor relationships and institutions exist. The seeds intentionally avoid establishing a universal guide profession, follower roster, companion control scheme or automatic helper bonus.

The strongest reusable story principle is that an NPC can matter deeply because they travel with the player for a while and then continue living without them.
