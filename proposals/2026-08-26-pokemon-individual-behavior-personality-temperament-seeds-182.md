# Ouros Pokémon Individual Behavior, Personality & Temperament Seeds — Pass 182

Status: NON-CANON WORLD­BUILDING CANDIDATES
Date: 2026-08-26

These are original Ouros candidates derived from Pass 182 research. None are established lore, mechanical rules or guaranteed encounters.

## 30 candidates

### 1. The Pokémon Bold Only After Closing

A persistent wild Pokémon avoids the market while crowds are present but regularly explores stalls after closing. Locals call it shy; researchers describe the narrower pattern: visitor-density-dependent approach behavior.

Hook: a renovation changes closing procedures and the old pattern stops.

### 2. Two Identical Natures, Different Histories

Two Pokémon have the same authoritative PTU Nature but years of observation show very different responses to novelty and people. A training academy uses the pair to teach why mechanical Nature is not a personality biography.

### 3. The First Observation Was the Outlier

A famous early report calls an individual exceptionally fearless after it crossed a busy worksite once. Ten later observations show routine avoidance. The historical report remains authentic but no longer representative.

### 4. The Quiet Pikachu Problem

A visitor-facing institution assumes a resident Pokémon should enjoy guest interaction because other individuals of the species do. Repeated withdrawal causes the facility to redesign the experience around voluntary participation.

No mechanical fear or disobedience is inferred.

### 5. The Crowd Changes the Result

A structured exploration assay produces one pattern with observers present and another when recorded remotely. The institution must revise what it thought it was measuring.

### 6. The Handler Effect

One Pokémon enters a care station rapidly with two familiar caretakers but hesitates with everyone else. The result matters operationally without proving affection, fear or Loyalty.

### 7. The Same Test, New Building

A Pokémon previously assessed as highly exploratory becomes cautious after a research facility moves. The question is whether the old assessment was context-bound, the building changes affordances, or both.

### 8. The Veteran Became Selective

An older institutional Pokémon still performs familiar routines reliably but engages with fewer optional activities. Aging and Care review the change; Pass 182 preserves the behavioral history without diagnosing decline.

### 9. The Shy One Leads Once

An individual known for withdrawing from unfamiliar people is first through a new wildlife crossing during a storm. One surprising event enriches its history but does not instantly reverse years of evidence.

### 10. The Explorer Who Never Touches Anything

A Pokémon readily enters unfamiliar places but almost never manipulates novel objects. Exploration and cognition stay separate.

### 11. The Object Inspector Who Hates New Rooms

The inverse case: one individual cautiously avoids unfamiliar spaces but thoroughly investigates new objects placed inside its known home area.

### 12. The Public Label Outlived the Pokémon's Behavior

A famous retired battler is still marketed as `the fearless one` years after its current public appearances become quiet and selective. Public Memory and current behavioral assessment diverge.

### 13. The Former Partner at Two Distances

A released former partner repeatedly approaches its old Trainer when alone but maintains distance when the Trainer arrives with a large group. The pattern supports no automatic reunion or capture eligibility.

### 14. Evolution Changed the Context

A Pokémon evolves and can now access spaces and movement options unavailable to its former body. Its behavior changes. Researchers cannot tell how much reflects development versus changed physical affordances.

### 15. The Recovery-Year Mislabel

A behavior assessment made during recovery from injury becomes widely cited as the individual's normal temperament. Years later a Care archive reveals the timing problem.

### 16. The Researcher Who Labels Too Quickly

A respected young researcher publishes strong trait words after three observations. The institution later adopts a stricter evidence standard and revisits the old archive without accusing the researcher of misconduct.

### 17. The Pokémon That Is Consistent Only With One Person

An individual behaves predictably around one long-term handler but variably around everyone else. The pattern becomes valuable evidence about context without becoming a Friendship or Loyalty stat.

### 18. The Same Species, Four Individuals

Four persistent Pokémon use the same orchard. One approaches equipment first, one waits for others, one investigates only at night and one shows no repeatable pattern. All remain normal members of the same population.

### 19. The Night Shift Saw a Different Pokémon

Day staff describe an individual as inactive. Night workers have years of observations showing extensive voluntary exploration after midnight. Timekeeping and Diel Activity help reconcile the records.

### 20. The Camera Made It Look Consistent

A camera trap covers only one narrow path. The Pokémon appears to repeat the same route for months until a second camera reveals multiple alternatives. Observation geometry had produced the apparent tendency.

### 21. The Training Prop Became a Preference Test by Accident

A retired training apparatus remains in a yard. Several Pokémon use it differently during free time. Training no longer owns the activity; Play and Pass 182 document voluntary responses.

### 22. The Confident Captive, Cautious Release

A rehabilitated Pokémon readily approaches novelty inside the familiar care facility but becomes cautious after release. Rehabilitation records the transition; Pass 182 treats both patterns as valid for their contexts.

### 23. The Local Folklore Says Stubborn

Residents describe a long-lived wild Pokémon as stubborn because it repeatedly refuses one old footbridge. Spatial Ecology later finds that it uses three other crossings regularly. The folk label remains part of local culture.

### 24. The Boldness Study That Was Really a Food Study

An apparent difference in approach behavior disappears when researchers realize food availability differed between trials. The project becomes a methodological case study rather than a personality discovery.

### 25. The Pokémon That Changes With the Group

An individual explores quickly when alone but waits when moving with a collective. Wild Collectives may explain group state; Pass 182 preserves the within-individual difference without assigning motive.

### 26. The Assessment Nobody Needs to Resolve

Two institutions disagree whether an individual should be described as cautious or context-sensitive. Both can operate safely with the raw evidence, so the terminology dispute remains open for years.

### 27. The Old Caretaker Was Right for the Wrong Reason

A caretaker predicted that one Pokémon would avoid a new annex. The prediction was correct, but later evidence suggests noise—not the caretaker's personality theory—was the relevant context.

### 28. The Behavior Changed Before the City Did

Archive footage reveals a persistent individual had already shifted its routine before redevelopment began. A popular story blaming construction must be revised.

### 29. The Trait That Exists Only in the Brochure

A tourism brochure calls a famous local Pokémon `playful`. Play records show little evidence either way. The brochure becomes Public Memory, not behavioral truth.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Observation Twelve

A scheduled repeat observation reproduces the established pattern with no surprise, crisis or quest. Years later it becomes an important baseline when behavior finally changes.

## Long arc 1: Five Assessments of Cedar Individual 12

Year 1: the individual is observed entering novel structures earlier than most monitored peers. The assessment remains provisional because coverage is sparse.

Year 2: repeated yard observations support an exploration tendency in familiar institutional grounds.

Year 3: relocation produces much slower entry into unfamiliar structures. The assessment becomes context-dependent rather than being discarded.

Year 5: after the new site becomes familiar, exploration increases again.

Year 8: a public article still calls the Pokémon `naturally fearless`, forcing a distinction between public reputation and the actual longitudinal record.

Narrative value:

The arc teaches players that a persistent individual's characterization can be recognizable without being static.

## Long arc 2: The Label That Outlived the Evidence

An early field guide gives a famous wild Pokémon an informal label after a memorable encounter. The phrase becomes common local language, then appears on merchandise and museum displays.

Over twenty years:

- new observation methods produce a more conditional picture;
- the individual ages;
- the habitat changes;
- younger researchers question the old terminology;
- older residents defend it as part of local history;
- no one needs to decide that the original observers were dishonest.

The final story concerns how a community updates knowledge without erasing cultural memory.

## Long arc 3: Three Contexts, One Pokémon

The same persistent Pokémon is monitored in three recurring contexts:

- a quiet orchard;
- a crowded transit edge;
- a research yard with unfamiliar objects.

Its behavior differs consistently among the three. The Chronicle follows the individual through infrastructure change, changing staff, one care episode, an Evolution or life-stage transition if mechanically/authored appropriate, and eventual movement to a different home range.

The arc never produces a single universal personality score. The durable product is a versioned behavioral history.

## Encounter contract: Former Partner Behavior Review

Narrative premise:

A released former partner has been repeatedly observed near an old shared location and appears to respond differently to its former Trainer than to ordinary visitors. Researchers want a documented comparison without assuming reunion, ownership, Loyalty or capture willingness.

FULL version:

Static battle primitives can use VERIFIED targeting/base movement/core/action/legal-choice infrastructure for an independent threat. Autonomous approach/withdrawal, former-Trainer tactical relationship, escort or non-hostile goals require complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback, all BLOCKING. Exact Loyalty/Command interaction remains unresolved.

REDUCED version:

All observations occur outside battle. Historical association grants no battle control. If an independent threat appears, the observed Pokémon withdraws through world state while a separate static AutoPTU encounter resolves the threat.

## Non-combat scenario: Is This Individual Actually Consistent?

Caretaker logs, photography, telemetry-adjacent observations and structured trials disagree about whether one persistent Pokémon has a stable exploration tendency.

Possible valid conclusions:

- `SUPPORTED_FOR_SCOPE`;
- `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT`;
- `MIXED`;
- `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`;
- `METHOD_CHANGED_TOO_MUCH`.

No battle is required, and the lack of a simple answer is not a failed quest.

## Explicit mechanics prohibitions

These proposals do not authorize:

- behavioral observation -> PTU Nature;
- Timid Nature -> shyness;
- Brave Nature -> risk-taking;
- curiosity -> Pokémon Education, Perception or intelligence;
- confidence -> Accuracy/Evasion/Initiative;
- caution -> Speed penalty;
- persistence -> immunity to withdrawal;
- aggression -> Intimidate Ability;
- sociability -> Pack Mon/shared Initiative;
- repeated cooperation -> Loyalty increase;
- withdrawal -> fear Status or disobedience;
- public personality label -> battle AI profile;
- idle animation/pathfinding -> temperament evidence;
- player-owned Pokémon behavior -> procedural private emotion.
