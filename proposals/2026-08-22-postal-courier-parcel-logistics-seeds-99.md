# Pass 99 Proposals — Postal, Courier & Parcel Logistics

Status: NON-CANON candidates. These are worldbuilding seeds, not established Ouros lore or mechanics.

## 30 candidate seeds

### 1. The Letter With No Route
A letter has a clear recipient name but references a landmark that no longer exists. The interesting work is reconstructing the old address from maps, neighbors and archive editions. The system must not reveal the destination automatically.

### 2. Last Mail Before Snow
A seasonal mountain route is about to close. The local office has ordinary backlog plus three genuinely urgent shipments. Players can help prioritize and route them without turning every parcel into a quest.

### 3. Two Hubs, Same Name
Two settlements historically used the same station name. A parcel was correctly sorted under an old convention but is wrong under the current one. Nobody needs to be incompetent or malicious.

### 4. The Forwarding Card
An NPC moved months ago. Their former household continues to receive letters because one institution never updated its records. The new address remains private even while the postal service can forward correctly.

### 5. The Parcel That Arrived First
A museum loan reaches the destination before the documentation packet. Custody of the object is clear; permission to unpack or display it is not.

### 6. The Documentation That Arrived First
The inverse problem: the receiving lab has the chain-of-custody paperwork but the specimen is delayed by a route closure. The delay creates scheduling consequences without implying loss.

### 7. Route Piggyback
A small clinic asks a travelling party to carry a sealed non-sensitive package because their expedition already follows the needed road. The commission should disappear if the party changes route before accepting custody.

### 8. The Missing Scan
Tracking appears to stop at a ferry terminal. Investigation later shows that the item moved normally but one handoff was never recorded. Missing data is not missing cargo.

### 9. The Wrong Sack
Two mailbags are visually identical after storm damage ruins their external tags. Stable item IDs and internal handling history matter more than appearance.

### 10. Former Clubhouse Mail
A club dissolved years ago, but its old headquarters still receives correspondence. The current occupant must decide whether to forward, return or contact alumni without the system inventing ownership of the old organization.

### 11. The Post Office That Moved
A settlement rebuilt after a flood and the post office now occupies a different building. Old maps remain historically accurate but cause modern delivery exceptions.

### 12. Festival Backlog
A major event doubles temporary population and overwhelms a small depot. Most effects stay aggregated. Only genuinely important items become persistent records.

### 13. The Courier Pokémon's Day Off
A Pokémon normally associated with a service is unavailable. The institution must reroute or reduce service instead of spawning a replacement of the same species.

### 14. No One at the Cabin
A parcel reaches a remote research cabin during a planned field season, but nobody is there. Weather, schedule and expedition logs offer several explanations. Do not convert absence into disappearance.

### 15. The Broken Seal
A package arrives with a damaged seal. The contents may still be intact. The incident can trigger documentation and custody review without automatically creating a theft case.

### 16. The Old Sorting Code
A legacy sorting convention still appears on decades-old institutional stationery. New staff interpret it incorrectly until an archivist recognizes the obsolete system.

### 17. One Parcel, Three Modes
A single shipment travels by rail, ferry and local Pokémon-assisted courier. Every leg remains part of one physical-item history rather than three cloned deliveries.

### 18. The Returned Birthday Parcel
A sender learns that the intended recipient has been travelling for months. The parcel returns with its complete route history. A later forwarding attempt can reuse the same physical object.

### 19. The Temporary Field Post
A crisis response creates a temporary dispatch point for supplies and correspondence. When the crisis ends, the institution must decide which records and functions move back to permanent hubs.

### 20. Mail for the Missing
A letter arrives for a person whose case status is MISSING. Postal policy, case privacy and family/household state collide. The system cannot decide death or open the letter for convenience.

### 21. The Retired Trainer's Address
A famous retired Trainer still receives fan mail at an old League facility. Public reputation does not grant the public access to their current residence.

### 22. Courier Apprenticeship
An NPC begins as a sorter, learns a local delivery route, later coordinates regional handoffs and eventually mentors new staff. Their career can progress through competence and responsibility without battle progression.

### 23. The Wrong Recipient, Right Institution
A package reaches the correct university but the named researcher transferred departments. Institutional receipt is valid; personal delivery is unresolved.

### 24. The Package Nobody Ordered
A workshop receives a correctly addressed parcel from an unknown sender. Postal records can establish its route without establishing why it was sent or who owns the contents.

### 25. Old Mail in the Wall
Renovation uncovers a sealed bundle that fell behind a sorting-room wall decades ago. Each piece remains an old physical record, but delivering it now may require archival, privacy and public-memory judgment.

### 26. The Ferry Delay Cascade
A ferry suspension leaves mail at one hub. The later rail connection runs normally, but the missed handoff creates a multi-day delay. This teaches players that route systems can cascade without every service failing.

### 27. The Parcel Photograph
A press photo accidentally captures a distinctive parcel at a hub. The image is genuine but does not prove who sent it, what it contains or where it ultimately went.

### 28. Delivery to a Memorial Office
A donation intended for memorial upkeep arrives after the responsible institution merged into another organization. Finance, public memory and postal routing all need to agree on the current receiving authority.

### 29. The Last Rural Round
A low-volume delivery route may be reorganized because the settlement has changed. Residents, workers and transport staff can disagree about service priorities without a villain.

### 30. The First Letter Home
A novice Trainer sends a letter during their first journey. The physical letter can arrive while the Trainer remains out of direct contact. This connects First Journeys to Communications without granting omniscient check-ins.

## Three longer arcs

### The Route That Kept Moving
Year 1: a small road-based postal route serves three settlements.
Year 2: a bridge closure creates a ferry handoff.
Year 3: a new rail stop changes sorting patterns.
Year 4: the old road reopens but no longer carries most mail.
Year 5: the original post office becomes a historical/community site while the delivery network uses a newer hub.

The arc preserves route editions, workplaces, buildings and public memory instead of resetting logistics after every infrastructure change.

### The Missing Parcel Years
A unique but non-magical historical object disappears in transit. Early evidence only proves the last confirmed handoff. Months later a case develops several hypotheses. Years later the object can reappear in storage, a private collection, an old depot or another authored location depending on evidence and canon decisions.

The object must keep one persistent identity. The system must never solve the case by inventing a duplicate.

### Letters Across Generations
A regional archive acquires correspondence donated over several decades. Some letters reached their recipients, some were returned, some were never opened, and some contain claims later shown to be wrong. The collection becomes useful for public memory, family history, migration research and changing place names while preserving privacy boundaries.

## Encounter contract candidates

### Parcel Transfer at North Junction

FULL:
A physical carrier must reach a handoff point while another group tries to intercept or block passage. Intended version may use PROTECT_CARRIER, INTERCEPT, BREAK_THROUGH and dynamic platform/vehicle state.

Current dependency families:
- VERIFIED foundations: targeting/footprints/range/LoS, base movement legality, core calculations, action economy/initiative, AI legal-action infrastructure.
- PARTIAL when exact effects appear: full lifecycle, full stateful damage, statuses, Moves, Abilities, Items, Trainer Features/perks.
- BLOCKING: complete movement/interception/forced movement, environment/zones/reactions when used, AI tactical policy, Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback.

REDUCED:
Keep the parcel under secured off-grid custody. Resolve a conventional static battle to clear the junction. Perform the handoff afterward if world state still allows it.

### Address Unknown — Last Mile

FULL:
An address-resolution investigation can become an escort/withdrawal scenario only if the story produces an actual confrontation.

Primary dependencies are overworld state: address versions, maps, privacy, Contacts/Communications and Travel.

REDUCED:
Keep the entire identification problem outside battle. If a battle occurs, make it an independent static encounter. Do not place the parcel or intended recipient in tactical danger merely to create action.

### Storm Backlog Field Dispatch

FULL:
Several urgent parcels depart a temporary field post over routes with changing weather/terrain. Autonomous couriers have delivery objectives.

BLOCKING families for that version:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- complete movement if route geometry changes in battle;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft playback.

REDUCED:
Choose routes and assignments in world state. Each risky leg can produce a static battle only when required. Successful encounters write back to the same shipment records.

## Proposal guardrails

These candidates do not establish:

- a canonical Ouros postal service;
- modern street addresses;
- postal law;
- prices or postage;
- courier Skills or Features;
- Pokémon carrying capacity;
- privacy law;
- customs;
- parcel HP;
- escort mechanics;
- delivery-related battle bonuses;
- automatic theft or sabotage explanations.

Any candidate promoted later must cite the relevant research and validate every mechanical dependency against PTU/Caelo plus the actual engine implementation.
