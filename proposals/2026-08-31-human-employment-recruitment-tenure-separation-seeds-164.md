## Design goal

These candidates make ordinary careers visible over years. They focus on who applied, who actually started, how responsibilities changed, who covered an absence, how a departure was handed over and what remained afterward.

None establishes labor law, salary formulas, automatic promotion, PTU progression or Pokémon employment status.

## The Job Still Posted After Someone Was Hired

A local service filled a role several days ago, but the physical notice remains on a board because nobody has replaced the paper yet.

New applicants continue arriving. One believes the vacancy is current because the notice is physically present. The workplace record correctly says the position is filled. The notice is also authentic; it is simply stale.

Useful connections:

- Public Notices owns the physical artifact and its displayed information;
- Workplaces owns actual staffing capacity;
- Employment owns the filled opportunity and successful tenure start;
- Rumor/Testimony may store local claims that the workplace is “still hiring.”

No deception is required.

## Five Times Someone Said “She Works Here”

Five witnesses use the same sentence for different relationships:

1. one refers to an active employee;
2. one remembers a former employee who left recently;
3. one refers to a temporary project collaborator;
4. one saw the person covering a single shift through another institution;
5. one assumes employment because the person wears a familiar uniform during a public event.

The investigation reconstructs relationship type and date rather than deciding which speaker is lying.

## The Offer Accepted, Start Delayed

A specialist accepted a role before a route interruption prevented arrival at the new workplace.

The institution has a valid accepted offer. The old workplace already prepared for the departure. The new tenure has not begun. Temporary coverage is therefore necessary at both ends for different reasons.

The story can resolve through transport, scheduling and handoff without any employment dispute.

If a tactical incident blocks the route, use the reduced `Emergency Coverage Access Chokepoint` contract unless richer capability families become verified.

## Two Correct Last Days

A long-serving employee tells friends that Friday was the last day. The workplace record lists Monday.

Friday was the last normal shift. Monday was a short handover and equipment-return visit before the tenure formally ended.

Both dates should remain queryable:

- last normal shift;
- last physical workplace appearance;
- effective tenure end.

This creates archival texture without retconning either record.

## The Acting Worker Who Never Took the Job

A deputy covers a vacant role for months but repeatedly declines the permanent position.

The public begins to treat the deputy as the holder because they are the visible face of the service. Workplaces correctly records recurring coverage. Employment never records acceptance of the permanent opening.

Later an external hire arrives. The deputy remains an important senior worker without becoming a “failed candidate.”

## The First Day During a Storm

A new employee’s planned first day coincides with a regional weather disruption.

The institution does not cancel the employment relationship. It delays onboarding, assigns remote or alternate orientation where plausible, or changes the actual start date.

The memorable story comes from an ordinary career milestone colliding with an existing world event rather than from generating a special disaster for the newcomer.

Weather remains owned by the weather/crisis layers. Employment stores only the effect on the start episode.

## The Worker Who Returns Seasonally

An experienced worker appears at the same institution during one recurring season and leaves afterward.

Each season creates a separate tenure episode linked by rehire history. The person’s repeated presence becomes part of local rhythm without establishing a permanent full-year job.

Potential uses:

- ferry or visitor-season staff;
- harvest support;
- festival operations;
- mountain-route service;
- research field season;
- competition circuit support.

Seasonality owns the calendar reason. Employment owns the repeated relationship episodes.

## The Former Employee Who Still Has the Key

A worker has left, but one issued access credential was not returned immediately.

The credential is real. The employment is over. Whether the credential still functions depends on Credentials/access state, not on possession alone.

Possible causes include ordinary administrative delay, an incomplete handoff or a scheduled later return. Do not infer malicious intent.

This seed can connect Employment, Credentials, Shared Equipment and Investigation without requiring an antagonist.

## The Ex-Employee Everyone Still Calls

A specialist departed months ago after a legitimate handoff, but staff still contact them because certain historical edge cases were never fully documented.

The former worker has knowledge. They no longer have ordinary authority.

Over time the workplace can decide to:

- document the missing knowledge;
- train current staff;
- create a consulting or temporary assignment if canon allows it;
- accept that some knowledge was personal experience and cannot be reconstructed perfectly.

This supports institutional memory stories where departure has consequences without portraying the successor as incompetent.

## The Trainee Who Became the Supervisor

An NPC first appears as a learner in a formal or workplace training context. Years later they hold a different employment tenure and are responsible for onboarding another newcomer.

Training history explains observed preparation. Employment history explains the role transition. Neither one automatically grants a PTU Feature or Trainer Class.

The payoff is recognition: the player encounters a familiar procedure from the opposite side of the teaching relationship.

## The Job That Changed Around the Person

A worker remains with the same institution while the service itself changes.

Their original role gradually accumulates responsibilities until the institution formally restructures it. The old tenure remains historically accurate for the earlier scope. A new role or tenure records the later structure.

This seed is useful after infrastructure upgrades, new technologies, service expansions or institutional mergers.

Do not compress the entire history into the newest job title.

## The Person With Two Jobs, Neither Secret

A recurring NPC works regular shifts at one workplace and a smaller recurring role elsewhere.

Players who visit at different times may correctly encounter the person in either place. Their availability follows authored commitments rather than a random spawn schedule.

This supports ordinary social density and prevents a character from being reduced to a single location marker.

## The Promotion Nobody Saw as Promotion

A worker’s responsibilities increased slowly over years. The institution eventually records a new role title, but coworkers describe the transition as formalizing work the person was already doing.

The promotion event is still real. It does not need a ceremonial announcement or sudden personality change.

No PTU progression is granted by the new title.

## The Vacancy That Saved the Service

After a departure, the institution discovers that the old role bundled several unrelated responsibilities around one experienced person.

Instead of hiring a direct replacement, it splits the role among multiple workers and changes procedures so no single absence can stall the service again.

The original vacancy closes because the role itself is superseded, not because a replacement was chosen.

This can permanently improve institutional resilience while preserving the prior worker’s history.

## The Pay Was Promised Before the Grant Arrived

A small institution offered a temporary role expecting an already-approved funding transfer. The employment commitment and funding relationship are both genuine, but the money has not yet been received.

The story tracks separate states:

- employment offer and acceptance;
- funding agreement;
- payment commitment;
- actual finance transfer;
- any later employment payment event.

Do not create debt, illegality, penalties or wage claims unless future canon explicitly defines them.

## The Quiet Separation

A familiar worker simply reaches the end of an authored tenure and leaves after a complete handoff.

There is no scandal, villain or mystery. The emotional/worldbuilding value comes from seeing a known routine change and another person gradually occupy the space.

This can be one of the strongest time-passage markers in a persistent settlement.

## The Departure Nobody Can Classify Yet

Records prove that a worker stopped appearing and that another person took over. Available evidence does not prove resignation, dismissal, retirement or transfer.

Employment records the tenure as ended with separation type `UNSPECIFIED`.

A later archive, correspondence record or direct testimony may clarify it. Until then, the system preserves uncertainty rather than inventing a dramatic explanation.

## The Applicant Who Became a Rival Institution’s Best Hire

A qualified applicant was not selected by one workplace and later joined another institution.

Years later both organizations work on the same regional project. The old application becomes historical context, but there is no mandatory resentment.

Possible relationship outcomes remain open:

- professional respect;
- friendly competition;
- indifference;
- collaboration;
- tension only if later events actually create it.

## The Same Desk, Three Careers

Players repeatedly encounter the same public-facing workstation over many years while the staff member changes.

The first holder retires or otherwise leaves through an authored event. A temporary worker covers the gap. A later successor changes the procedure. The desk remains visually stable while institutional knowledge and public habits evolve.

This uses environmental continuity to show time without relying on exposition.

## Long arc — A Career in Three Institutions

A recurring NPC begins in a modest role at one workplace. Their next tenure is at a different institution that uses related skills. Much later they return to the original community in a senior but different capacity.

The arc preserves:

- each application or recruitment route;
- separate offer and start episodes;
- actual workplace relationships;
- training and credentials as external evidence;
- handoffs at each departure;
- old coworkers who remember different versions of the person;
- no automatic “career level” score.

The payoff is a believable adult life unfolding alongside the player’s adventures.

## Long arc — The Town Changes Shift

A settlement’s ordinary services change workers over several campaign years.

A clinic receptionist leaves. A ferry operator reduces hours before a successor begins. A junior archive worker becomes experienced. A seasonal market coordinator returns for several cycles. A mechanic transfers to a new facility.

No single transition is a major quest. Together they make the settlement visibly historical.

Players eventually realize that they know the town not only by buildings but by who used to work where and which routines survived them.

## Long arc — The Vacancy That Outlived the Crisis

A crisis temporarily removes or reassigns a specialist. Emergency coverage keeps the service alive, so the immediate story resolves.

Months later the underlying staffing need remains. Backlogs, delayed training and reduced hours become the actual long-term consequence.

Possible endings:

- external hire;
- internal succession;
- role redesign;
- inter-institution partnership;
- service permanently reduced;
- original worker returns through a new authored tenure.

The arc turns recovery into institutional change rather than treating “crisis over” as a reset.

## Encounter candidate — First-Day Access Interruption

Full premise:

A newcomer and supervisor are physically moving through a contested approach while tactical actors try to protect or clear their route.

Full dependencies include the permanent families for complete movement, lifecycle, stateful damage/status as selected content requires, hazards/zones/reactions when active conditions exist, individual Move/Ability/Item/Feature behavior, tactical policy and adapter/playback.

Current intended status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version:

The newcomer and supervisor remain outside BattleSpec. Static geometry is frozen before initiative. A conventional audited battle may produce `IMMEDIATE_WORKPLACE_APPROACH_CLEAR`. Employment then decides whether onboarding can actually begin.

## Encounter candidate — Shift Handover Perimeter

Full premise:

Outgoing and incoming staff overlap when a local tactical incident occurs. Protection and withdrawal timing matter.

Current intended status: BLOCKED because safe withdrawal/protect semantics require capability families beyond legal-action enumeration.

Reduced version:

The handover pauses before initiative. Staff and issued property leave BattleSpec. AutoPTU may return only `IMMEDIATE_HANDOVER_PERIMETER_CLEAR`. Responsibility and custody transfer happen afterward through their owning systems.

## Encounter candidate — Departure-Day Equipment Return Perimeter

Full premise:

A departing worker carries issued property through a contested access point.

Current intended status: BLOCKED because protected-object carrying/escort and tactical policy are not verified globally.

Reduced version:

Equipment is secured outside BattleSpec before the battle. AutoPTU can clear a static access point only. The employment separation and equipment return remain independent records.

## Encounter candidate — Emergency Coverage Access Chokepoint

Full premise:

A temporary coverage worker needs to reach a thinly staffed service while a local confrontation blocks passage.

Current intended status: BLOCKED for rich escort/route-control/hazard behavior.

Reduced version:

The worker is not a combatant. AutoPTU may return `IMMEDIATE_COVERAGE_ROUTE_CLEAR`. Workplaces later decides whether coverage starts and whether capacity actually improves.

## Noncombat adventure lane — The First Week

A player or recurring NPC begins a new role. The interesting content is ordinary uncertainty and institutional texture:

- finding the correct workplace entrance;
- meeting coworkers;
- learning which records are current;
- understanding who actually owns each decision;
- receiving equipment;
- observing the first handoff;
- discovering that public assumptions about the role differ from the real responsibilities.

No battle, salary math or progress reward is required. The payoff is that the workplace becomes a known social location players can understand later when something meaningful changes.

## Promotion boundaries

Before any candidate here becomes canon:

- confirm the workplace and role already exist or deliberately author them;
- confirm why the relationship is employment rather than volunteering, membership, public office, contract service or temporary collaboration;
- confirm PC agency for any player-controlled decision;
- link qualifications and training rather than inventing them;
- keep compensation in Finance;
- keep equipment/credential custody in its owner system;
- identify the exact evidence for separation reasons;
- cross-check PTU/Caelo whenever a job is claimed to grant mechanical competence;
- audit every battle content item individually;
- never let Minecraft entity presence or costume decide employment or combat authority.
