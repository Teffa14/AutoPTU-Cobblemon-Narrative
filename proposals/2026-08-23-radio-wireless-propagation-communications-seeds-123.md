# Radio, Wireless Propagation & Communications Seeds — Pass 123

Status: PROPOSALS / NON-CANON. None of these concepts become established Ouros lore without explicit canon approval.

These candidates use the Pass 123 radio/wireless systems layer. They avoid assigning real frequencies, spectrum law, fixed technical ranges, PTU bonuses or universal Electric-type technology roles.

## 30 worldbuilding candidates

### 1. The Valley With Four Bars and No Calls

A public coverage display suggests reliable service throughout a valley, but field teams repeatedly fail to establish two-way contact. The later investigation must distinguish stale modeling, backhaul failure, device differences and local obstruction before anyone declares sabotage.

### 2. The Relay Everyone Can See

A large relay tower dominates the skyline. Residents assume it serves them directly. It actually supports a different route, while the town depends on a smaller site hidden behind the ridge. A maintenance closure reveals how wrong the public mental map had become.

### 3. Two Rangers, Two Networks

Two legitimate response organizations arrive at the same incident with fully functioning equipment but cannot communicate directly. They must create a temporary interoperability bridge without merging command structures.

### 4. The New Building in the Path

A new civic building opens successfully. Months later, a nearby block begins reporting intermittent coverage. The building is one hypothesis among several. The story can connect Architecture, Metrology and Communications without framing redevelopment as automatically harmful.

### 5. The Forest That Became a Dead Zone

A reforested slope gradually changes local coverage over several years. The ecological restoration remains successful. Communications planners now need a new relay strategy rather than treating the recovered forest as a mistake.

### 6. The Summer Leaves Problem

A route works reliably in winter and poorly during peak leaf cover. Old survey records appear contradictory until their dates are compared. This can become a seasonal expedition-planning detail rather than a crisis.

### 7. The Repeater That Still Has Power

A remote repeater reports normal power and healthy hardware. Service remains unavailable because the relay path or configuration is wrong. Repairing a machine and restoring a service become distinct tasks.

### 8. The Backup Channel Nobody Practiced

An institution has a documented fallback plan that has not been exercised for years. The first real outage reveals outdated contacts, inaccessible staging points and staff who interpreted the procedure differently.

### 9. The Station That Hears but Cannot Reply

A research station receives regional broadcasts clearly but cannot establish outbound contact. Visitors initially assume the entire network is operational because information is arriving.

### 10. The Old Coverage Map

An archived map shows strong service in a place that is now unreliable. The old document is accurate for its era: different vegetation, a previous relay and an older settlement skyline explain the change.

### 11. Three Devices, Three Results

Three field teams stand at the same overlook and report different service quality. The discrepancy starts an investigation into device state, service type and measurement method rather than producing a single “correct” observation immediately.

### 12. The Harbor Congestion Hour

Every evening, passenger arrivals, freight operations and local businesses use the same regional service heavily. Brief communication delays become a predictable operational window and eventually shape staffing and fallback procedures.

### 13. The Festival Saturation

A famous annual festival produces a recurring communications bottleneck. Five editions of the festival can show different mitigations: extra staff, temporary relays, changed schedules, public notices and finally a redesigned local network.

### 14. The Quiet Ridge

Researchers notice that a remote ridge has unusually low background interference and begin using it for sensitive measurements. The site becomes valuable before any formal restriction exists. Future access decisions require Science, Land Tenure and Governance rather than an automatic “radio quiet zone.”

### 15. The Research Station Asks for Silence

A station requests temporary limits on nearby transmissions during a scheduled observation. Residents and travelers have legitimate communication needs. The scenario is about coordination and evidence, not researchers receiving universal authority.

### 16. The Wrong Interference Suspect

A group of Magnemite is repeatedly seen near a facility during communication dropouts. Historic logs later show the problem began before the group arrived. The Pokémon observation remains true while the causal claim weakens.

### 17. The Rotom Phone Rumor

A rumor spreads that one Trainer’s Rotom Phone can connect anywhere. The actual record shows only that the device worked during several incidents where other systems also remained available. The public persona of the device becomes more famous than its demonstrated capability.

### 18. The Mobile Repeater Caravan

A seasonal expedition route uses a portable relay that moves between staging sites. Over years, the route itself changes as roads, weather and settlements change. The equipment becomes an expedition tradition without becoming magical fast travel for messages.

### 19. The Ferry Out of Range

A ferry route includes a short communications gap that crews understand and plan around. A timetable revision pushes a scheduled check-in into the gap, creating apparent missing-contact incidents until operations adapt.

### 20. The Tunnel Handoff

A railway tunnel breaks direct regional service but has local infrastructure inside. Travelers can communicate within the tunnel while temporarily losing outside reach. A future infrastructure upgrade may bridge the two systems.

### 21. The Rail Cut Dead Zone

New rail excavation changes the geometry around an old relay path. The transport project succeeds while communications coverage needs separate mitigation. Neither project has to be portrayed as incompetent.

### 22. The Emergency Bridge Between Networks

A portable gateway originally purchased for drills becomes essential during a real regional emergency. Afterwards, institutions must decide whether the temporary solution should become permanent, remain emergency-only or be retired.

### 23. The Weather Station With a Separate Link

A remote weather station uses a telemetry service independent of the public network. During a public outage, its observations still arrive. Residents incorrectly conclude that public service is being withheld because “the weather station still works.”

### 24. The Broadcast That Reached Farther Than Expected

A public broadcast is reported beyond its expected service area under unusual conditions. The observation becomes a Science question. Ouros does not turn the anomaly into supernatural evidence without more support.

### 25. The Clinic’s Local Fallback

A clinic can communicate with nearby emergency teams during a regional outage through a local service, but cannot contact distant specialists. The distinction changes what care coordination is possible without closing the clinic entirely.

### 26. The Tower Decommissioned, Coverage Survived

Residents expect a district to lose service when an old tower is retired. Redundancy installed years earlier carries the load. The incident becomes a rare story about preparedness working correctly.

### 27. The Tower Survived, Coverage Changed

A storm leaves the main tower standing and powered, yet coverage changes because another relay, access path or downstream dependency failed. Visual inspection alone does not explain service state.

### 28. The Same Message Arrived Three Ways

A safety notice reaches one settlement through broadcast, a delayed direct message and a courier copy at different times. Chronicle preserves each arrival. Later historians can reconstruct how information actually propagated during the incident.

### 29. The Missing Acknowledgement

A field team receives instructions but its acknowledgement never returns. Command knows the message was delivered only if another evidence source establishes that fact. The uncertainty changes decisions without making either side incompetent.

### 30. The Coverage Atlas With Dates

A regional institute maintains transparent overlays showing coverage observations from different years and seasons. The atlas becomes valuable to expeditions, emergency planners, historians and researchers precisely because it preserves disagreement and change instead of pretending coverage is timeless.

## Long arc A — Five Years of Signal at Meridian Valley

Year one begins with a reliable ridge relay and several known shadow areas. A development project changes part of the skyline. The next summer, vegetation changes a second route. An emergency later exposes a hidden interoperability problem between two institutions. A temporary field repeater becomes part of the response. By year five, the valley has several coverage-map editions, exercises, corrected assumptions and a more resilient network.

The arc has no required villain. Correct maintenance can happen. A mitigation can work. A model can be wrong without negligence. Pokémon can be present without causing the problem.

Persistent outputs can include:
- coverage revisions;
- relay history;
- exercise records;
- public maps;
- archived failed hypotheses;
- changed expedition procedures;
- landmarks whose communications role changes over time.

## Long arc B — The Ridge Relay Years

A chain of remote relay sites originally serves research and transport. Settlements grow along the corridor. One relay is retired, another is moved, and a third becomes a public landmark. A landslide changes access without destroying the service. Later, a new route bypasses part of the chain.

The campaign can revisit the same sites across decades of Chronicle. Some become ruins, museums, emergency backups or ecological observation points. Their current technical role and historical significance remain separate.

## Long arc C — The Two Networks Become One Enough

Two institutions begin with incompatible communication systems. They do not merge. Instead, repeated exercises and real incidents gradually produce shared contact procedures, temporary gateways, compatible message types and agreed fallback locations.

The final state can remain intentionally partial. Routine operations stay separate while emergencies achieve enough interoperability to work. Institutional identity and authority survive the technical integration.

## Encounter contract — Relay Ridge Access

FULL premise: technicians must reach a relay site while a separate conflict makes the approach unsafe. The battle may include moving technicians, protected access lanes and an objective to clear or protect a route.

FULL dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when technicians or equipment move tactically;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING only if validated ridge hazards are mechanically active;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT/CLEAR_ROUTE/REACH_OBJECTIVE;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED version: technicians remain off-grid. Technology isolates the equipment into a safe maintenance state. A conventional static battle can occur at the access platform. After combat, technicians inspect the site and this layer records coverage observations. Battle victory cannot directly set the service to restored.

## Encounter contract — Festival Channel Saturation

FULL premise: a festival overloads a local service while a separate wildlife or security incident creates a need for rapid coordination among moving crowds and responders.

FULL dependencies:
- complete movement — BLOCKING for civilian movement and route clearing;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for EVACUATE/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING for crowds and semantic communication-state presentation;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — only required if a validated environmental hazard also exists;
- standard combat families remain at their permanent VERIFIED/PARTIAL classifications.

REDUCED version: crowd routing and communications degradation resolve before battle. Responders clear a static side street or plaza. The wireless failure only affects who knows what and when. It never applies Accuracy, initiative, Command or damage modifiers.

## Encounter contract — Emergency Interoperability Bridge

FULL premise: two organizations have functioning but incompatible systems. A portable gateway must be carried to a useful site and protected while an incident continues.

FULL dependencies:
- complete movement including interception — BLOCKING for equipment carriers and moving objectives;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT/REACH_OBJECTIVE/CLEAR_ROUTE;
- items — PARTIAL only if the gateway is ever modeled as a PTU item rather than world-state equipment;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING;
- environment family only when a separate validated hazard is active.

REDUCED version: the gateway is deployed before the battle and kept outside tactical authority. AutoPTU handles a static confrontation nearby. Afterwards, the institutions run a compatibility/coverage test. Victory does not create interoperability automatically.

## Canon questions deliberately unresolved

Ouros still needs decisions on regional technology level, public versus institutional wireless services, broadcast ownership, emergency interoperability, portable field equipment, research telemetry, privacy, player-operated local services and whether any radio-quiet practices exist.

No candidate above authorizes real-world frequencies, licenses, spectrum regulation, signal ranges, jamming, electronic damage or PTU Skill modifiers.
