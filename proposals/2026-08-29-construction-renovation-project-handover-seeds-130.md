# Ouros Construction, Renovation & Handover Seeds — Pass 130

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-29

These seeds are original Ouros candidates derived from the high-level research structures in `research/2026-08-29-construction-renovation-project-handover-scan-130.md`.

They do not establish specific Ouros institutions, building codes, contractors, ownership, construction technology, inspection authority, Pokémon labor roles or historical projects.

## Design objective

Use physical change as persistent narrative continuity.

A project should create stories before, during and after construction without requiring sabotage, corruption or combat. The useful tension often comes from scope, chronology, discoveries, temporary arrangements and handoffs between institutions that each own a different truth.

## Seed — The Street Opened One Block at a Time

Premise:

A route project is already authorized. One completed block begins normal use while the next remains under construction and a third waits on material delivery.

What players notice:

- old and new paving or streetscape meet abruptly;
- one temporary crossing remains active;
- vendors have adapted to the usable block;
- locals disagree about when the project was “finished.”

Evidence solution:

Several dates are all legitimate: physical completion of the first block, scoped handover, route activation, completion of the second block and final project archive closure.

Potential owner handoffs:

Roads/Travel, Commercial, Public Space, Civic Governance.

Canon questions:

Which project and settlement, if any, use this pattern?

## Seed — The Foundation Nobody Planned For

Premise:

A work package exposes older masonry not present on the current project records.

Construction records only:

- where it was observed;
- when;
- which work package stopped;
- which area was protected;
- which specialist owner received the evidence.

Possible interpretations remain open:

- earlier structure;
- utility work;
- retaining feature;
- later fill;
- mapping error;
- genuinely important historical site.

The quest succeeds by preserving provenance and reaching the correct owner. It does not require the players to “solve archaeology” through a generic roll.

## Seed — The New Hall Is Finished, the Clinic Is Not Open

Premise:

A new or renovated clinic scope has been physically handed over, but Care has not yet activated service there.

Possible valid reasons:

- staff transition remains incomplete;
- equipment handoff is separate;
- a service-specific verification remains open;
- opening was intentionally scheduled later;
- another unrelated dependency exists.

The construction team is not automatically at fault. Physical completion and clinical readiness are different world states.

## Seed — The Temporary Footbridge Got a Name

Premise:

A temporary pedestrian connection created during a project becomes locally useful and gains an informal name.

The original project reaches the trigger for removing it, but residents, nearby workers or vendors now value the connection.

Narrative branch:

Construction does not simply retain it. A new proposal or owner decision must decide its future.

Persistent consequence:

Even if removed, the informal name can survive in local speech, maps or memories.

## Seed — The Old Loading Dock Is Inside the New Library

Premise:

Adaptive reuse preserves one physical fragment of a predecessor facility inside a new public-facing place.

Players later discover:

- the floor geometry makes more sense on an older plan;
- a sealed doorway once faced an exterior yard;
- staff use an old industrial term for one room;
- a historical photograph aligns with the retained wall.

This is environmental storytelling rather than a secret crime by default.

## Seed — The Drawing Was Revised, the Fence Was Not

Premise:

An authorized scope revision changed the intended work boundary. One temporary fence still reflects the previous stage.

Competing truths:

- the current scope record is newer;
- the old fence is physically real;
- workers may be following an updated route;
- a passerby may reasonably believe the fence still marks the active boundary.

The quest is about reconciling physical presentation with authoritative project state.

It does not establish property boundaries.

## Seed — The Work Stopped at Three Meters

Premise:

One package is complete up to a precise visible break, then remains untouched.

Possible causes:

- later discovery;
- dependency not delivered;
- scope revision;
- protected feature;
- utility conflict;
- project reprioritization;
- unknown pending evidence.

The visible break is evidence of chronology, not proof of cause.

## Seed — Two Entrances, One Handover

Premise:

A building renovation creates a new public entrance while the old entrance remains necessary for another active phase.

One scope can be in ordinary use while another stays under project control.

Useful stories:

- deliveries keep using the old entrance;
- old signage confuses visitors;
- a Pokémon or NPC routine still follows the previous path;
- a later accessibility decision changes which entrance remains permanent.

No accessibility standard is invented by this seed.

## Seed — The Project Board Is More Accurate Than the Building

Premise:

A public project board shows the intended final design, but work has paused after an authorized change.

The board has not yet been replaced.

Mystery value:

Players initially think the world is inconsistent. Records reveal that representation lagged behind a legitimate scope change.

Public Information owns correction/publication. Construction owns the execution history.

## Seed — The Building Is Complete, the Old Name Survived

Premise:

A redevelopment fully replaces a predecessor structure but the neighborhood continues using the old site name.

Potential consequences:

- couriers receive both names;
- older residents give directions using the predecessor;
- historical records sort under the old identity;
- visitors use the new official name;
- a local Pokémon route is described by the old landmark.

The place becomes richer through layered identity without requiring a naming dispute.

## Seed — The Correction Is Younger Than the Paint

Premise:

A later correction work package sits behind a surface finish that visually appears older because the finish was retained or restored.

Players trying to date a change from appearance alone reach the wrong conclusion.

The answer comes from work-package records, photographs and handover notes.

## Seed — The Market Opened Around the Worksite

Premise:

A phased public-space or market project returns completed scopes to use while another bounded area remains active construction.

The social geography adapts:

- vendors cluster around the usable edge;
- foot traffic creates a new preferred path;
- one temporary stall becomes locally important;
- a completed entrance changes which street feels like the “front.”

After final completion, some social patterns remain even when barriers disappear.

## Seed — The Ruins Stopped One Project and Started Another

Premise:

A construction discovery is referred to History/Archaeology. The original project is revised rather than abandoned entirely. Later, a separate interpretation or public-memory project grows around the preserved discovery.

This seed is inspired by the general pattern of development halted by archaeological discovery, transformed into an original Ouros structure.

The construction system never decides historical significance itself.

## Seed — The New Station Exists Before the Train Stops There

Premise:

A station or platform construction scope has been handed over, but the transport service has not yet activated a stop.

Residents can physically see the finished infrastructure.

Travel/Rail owns service launch.

Useful tension comes from expectations, old timetables, public notices and businesses preparing for traffic that has not begun.

## Mystery — Six Times the Project Was “Done”

NON-CANON situation template.

Six sources give six dates:

1. last major physical installation;
2. work package reported physically complete;
3. bounded verification passed;
4. first scope handed over;
5. service began using that scope;
6. residual work/project record closed.

All six may be accurate within different scopes.

Resolution method:

Reconstruct what each speaker means by “done.”

Avoid defaulting to fraud, incompetence or conspiracy.

## Mystery — Three Plans, Two Buildings

NON-CANON situation template.

An archive contains:

- original predecessor plan;
- approved redevelopment scope;
- later construction revision.

The current site has a retained predecessor fragment inside the successor structure.

Players must distinguish planned geometry, built geometry and retained historical fabric.

The solution comes from dates, scope labels, photographs and site observations.

## Mystery — The Fence Moved Before the Project Did

NON-CANON situation template.

A temporary barrier was relocated to prepare for a later phase, but the work package behind it had not started yet.

Residents remember “construction reaching the corner” on the fence-move date. Project records show physical work began later.

Both memories can be sincere.

## Exploration — The Unfinished Service Road

Readiness: READY with current non-tactical world systems.

Premise:

A short paved service road ends beside an old foundation and never connects to the current main route.

Players can reconstruct its history through:

- scope versions;
- old site photographs;
- temporary access records;
- predecessor maps;
- drainage or utility records where accessible;
- local testimony;
- physical remnants.

Possible resolution:

The road belonged to an earlier project phase that was superseded before the successor facility opened.

No combat or construction Skill check is required.

## Exploration — The Floor With Two Numbering Systems

Readiness: READY.

Premise:

A renovated building uses new room numbers, but one retained corridor still carries marks from the predecessor layout.

Players reconstruct which walls, doors and rooms were changed across two project phases.

This can support a later archive, delivery or historical quest without turning the renovation itself into a crime plot.

## NPC archetype — The Project Historian by Accident

Status: PROPOSED ARCHETYPE.

A long-serving worker, vendor, custodian or nearby resident has watched several phases of the site change.

Strength:

They remember practical chronology that formal records do not emphasize.

Limitation:

Memory is perspective, not perfect authority.

Narrative use:

Combine their observations with records rather than using them as an exposition oracle.

## NPC archetype — The Receiving Operator

Status: PROPOSED ARCHETYPE.

An actor responsible for the future service enters the story before handover.

Their concerns differ from the construction team:

- access;
- maintainability;
- staffing;
- operating routines;
- public movement;
- documentation.

They do not automatically have construction authority, and construction completion does not force them to launch service.

## NPC archetype — The Scope Keeper

Status: PROPOSED ARCHETYPE.

A project coordinator who is unusually careful about which revision is current.

Useful tension:

Other actors may hold old but formerly valid drawings. The Scope Keeper can explain lineage without declaring earlier actors foolish.

## Faction/institution pattern — Builder, Owner, User

Status: PROPOSED RELATIONSHIP PATTERN.

Three institutions or groups can want the same project to succeed for different reasons:

- execution team wants a bounded handover;
- receiving operator wants an operable asset;
- public/user group wants the service or place available.

Their timelines need not match.

This creates grounded tension without requiring adversarial factions.

## Faction/institution pattern — Preservation Versus Continuity of Service

Status: PROPOSED RELATIONSHIP PATTERN.

A discovery or retained predecessor feature matters to one group while another group depends on the future service.

Useful outcomes include:

- local redesign;
- phased handover;
- preserved fragment;
- documented removal where canon allows it;
- alternate site access;
- indefinite pause if no acceptable path exists.

The generator should not force a binary “progress versus history” moral choice.

## Long arc — A District Builds Around the Detour

Status: PROPOSED LONG-FORM ARC.

Phase 1 — ordinary place

Establish the route, businesses, households, facility users, ordinary Pokémon routines and current site before construction starts.

Phase 2 — work begins

One bounded area changes access. A temporary path or stop appears. Daily routines adapt.

Phase 3 — the world learns the temporary layout

Vendors, commuters and Pokémon begin using the new path. No crisis is required.

Phase 4 — discovery

One work package encounters an unexpected condition and pauses. Another package continues.

Phase 5 — revision

The responsible owner interprets the discovery. The project receives a revised scope rather than an automatic cancellation.

Phase 6 — partial handover

One completed area enters use. Adjacent work remains visible.

Phase 7 — full physical completion

The major work overlay disappears, but residual tasks and downstream service activation remain distinct.

Phase 8 — social persistence

The temporary path, vendor cluster, nickname or changed Pokémon routine does not fully revert.

Phase 9 — later callback

Months or years later, a maintenance fault, archive mystery, property question, public-memory project or new expansion references the old construction record.

The completed arc becomes reusable history instead of disposable quest scenery.

- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;

- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE;

Reduced execution:

Construction pauses work and secures authored equipment before BattleSpec. Workers, records, controlled materials and noncombatants leave the grid. AutoPTU receives a static exterior/perimeter arena and explicit combatants chosen by Ouros.

Victory only secures the immediate area for later reassessment.

## Encounter proposal — Partial Handover Corridor

Status: REDUCED VERSION READY; FULL VERSION DEPENDENT.

Full narrative intent:

A finished scope is waiting to transfer to the receiving operator while adjacent work remains active. A conflict blocks the static access corridor.

Full dependencies:

Complete movement remains PARTIAL for Intercept/forced displacement. Lifecycle remains PARTIAL for phased transit. Terrain/zones/reactions, tactical policy and semantic adapter playback remain BLOCKING for the full objective form.

Reduced execution:

The handover pauses. Receiving staff remain outside BattleSpec. The fight occurs on a static approach. Afterward Construction and the receiving service owner independently decide whether transfer resumes.

Combat cannot issue the handover event.

## Encounter proposal — Discovery Protection Perimeter

Status: REDUCED VERSION READY; FULL VERSION DEPENDENT.

Full narrative intent:

A newly exposed feature has been isolated pending specialist review. A threat makes the surrounding perimeter unsafe.

Full dependencies:

Dynamic exclusion zones and crossing reactions require terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions. Protection behavior requires AI tactical policy. Forced displacement/Intercept relies on the PARTIAL complete-movement family. Semantic evidence/perimeter projection needs adapter/playback.

Reduced execution:

The discovery and records are outside BattleSpec. Workers and specialists withdraw first. AutoPTU resolves a conventional nearby battle. The specialist owner returns later if world state permits it.

Victory cannot identify or authorize treatment of the discovery.

## Guardrails for all proposals

Do not infer:

- building ownership;
- permits;
- inspection powers;
- construction licenses;
- union or labor arrangements;
- engineering competence;
- exact project duration or price;
- structural adequacy;
- Pokémon work aptitude from species or Type;
- environmental hazards from worksite appearance;
- public access from Minecraft doors or barriers;
- project completion from block placement;
- service launch from physical completion;
- handover from battle victory.

Every seed remains NON-CANON until explicitly promoted.
