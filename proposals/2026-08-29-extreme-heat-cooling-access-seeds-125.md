# Ouros Narrative Proposals — Extreme Heat & Cooling Access — Pass 125

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Nothing in this file becomes Ouros canon without approval.
Date: 2026-08-29

## Design intent

These seeds use the Pass 125 heat-continuity architecture to create local stories, mysteries, recurring NPC roles, exploration and longer arcs without inventing medical mechanics or tactical heat rules.

Each concept separates observations, institutional decisions and battle requirements.

## Situation seeds

### The Hall Is Open, the Cooling Is Not

A public building appears on an old heat-response map as a support site. The building is open today, but its cooling function is unavailable after an unrelated equipment problem.

Useful systems:
Weather, Facility Maintenance, Electric Grid, Public Space, Public Notices.

Persistent consequence:
Residents begin using a shaded courtyard next door. The courtyard may remain socially important even after the building returns to normal.

Canon questions:
Who operates the building? What cooling technology exists? Who may designate it for heat-response use?

### It Cooled Down, the Market Stayed Early

A market shifted to early-morning hours during a past hot period. Conditions later normalized, but vendors and customers preferred the new schedule.

The hook appears when an old timetable conflicts with current NPC routines and a visitor assumes someone is violating the rules.

Persistent consequence:
A temporary adaptation becomes local culture without requiring a formal crisis state forever.

### The Route Is There, Nobody Walks It at Noon

A mapped trail remains intact. During one unusual heat window, local guides avoid using it at midday and prefer a different time or already-authored mode of travel.

The mystery is why an apparently open route has no one on it during one period.

Guardrail:
Do not mark the route mechanically impassable. Travel owns the journey decision. No heat damage is inferred.

### The Night Notice Matters More

Visitors focus on a daytime forecast, but local operators care about whether overnight conditions improve before reopening a particular activity.

The players reconstruct the difference between forecast windows, observations and the service owner's review criteria.

No universal overnight-health mechanic is created.

### The Temporary Shade Became the Meeting Place

A simple temporary shaded waiting space used during a past episode becomes where residents now exchange news, play games and meet couriers.

Months later Civic/Public Works considers removing, replacing or formalizing it.

The story is about legacy and use, not emergency response.

### The Pokémon Returned to the Roof First

An individually known Pokémon resumes resting on a familiar rooftop before a public activity returns there.

Some residents interpret this as proof the location is safe.

The players can establish only the observed behavior. Building Safety, Weather and the activity owner retain authority for their own states.

### The Fans Are Running, the Room Is Closed

Visitors see equipment operating through a window and assume a public room has reopened.

The actual sequence is:
repair completed → equipment test → function verification → staffing check → public opening.

This seed reinforces the same provenance grammar used by the Electric Grid and Building Safety layers.

### Three Cool Places, One Address

Historical notices all name the same community center, but over decades the support activity moved between the main hall, an annex and a neighboring library room.

The current address makes old testimony appear contradictory.

Resolution comes from spatial scope and time, not deception.

### The Festival Went Inside and Never Fully Came Back Out

A festival moved several activities indoors during one hot year. Later editions restored most outdoor programming but kept one indoor performance because audiences preferred it.

This produces living cultural continuity and a reason for a venue relationship that predates the current player group.

### The Power Returned Before the Queue

A temporary support site lost power during a heat episode. Electricity returned first, cooling verification later, public access after that, and normal queue conditions last.

NPCs remember different “reopening” dates.

No one has to be wrong.

### The Water Point Is on the Map but Not in the System

A historical map marks a water point near a route. The structure still exists, but Drinking Water records show that its service role ended years ago.

The players must not infer potability from a block, basin or pipe model.

### The Farmhouse That Became a Known Stop

A remote household once helped stranded travelers during an unusual hot period. Later journeys began treating the location as an informal rest stop even though it never became a formal service.

This is inspired only by the structural lesson that remote ordinary places can become meaningful through repeated use.

Consent and household access remain authored.

### Two Heat Maps, Different Questions

One map shows observed weather. Another shows a later institutional impact assessment. Players initially think one has been falsified because boundaries differ.

The resolution is that the products use different inputs and purposes.

This can be solved entirely through provenance.

## Mysteries

### Five Times the Heat “Ended”

Five records contain different end dates:
1. the last unusually hot observed afternoon;
2. the first cooler overnight window;
3. a revised forecast;
4. closure of the temporary support hall;
5. the end of special clinic monitoring.

Narrative resolution:
The term “ended” referred to five different scopes. The episode record preserves all milestones and an authored closure basis.

Playable now:
Yes. No combat mechanics required.

### Four Open Buildings, Two Useful Rooms

Four buildings are physically open. Only two rooms are currently verified for the function needed during the heat episode.

Evidence:
operating windows, power sector records, maintenance verification, accessibility information and public notices.

Resolution:
Open-building state and cooling-function state were incorrectly treated as synonyms by a rumor chain.

### The Empty Trail at Twelve

A photographer has images showing the same trail crowded in the morning and empty at midday. A later image shows activity after sunset.

The players compare timestamps, local schedule changes, weather observations and guide notices.

No tactical hazard is needed.

## Exploration concept — The Courtyard Behind the Old Hall

Premise:
An old community hall was replaced years ago. Its former rear courtyard survives between newer buildings and contains mounts, faded shade anchors and signage fragments that do not fit the current map.

Exploration loop:
- compare historical heat-response notices;
- locate the former hall footprint through photographs and cartography;
- identify which courtyard survived redevelopment;
- inspect static remnants;
- interview residents about when the space was used;
- distinguish formal support use from later informal gathering;
- record the site's present status.

Current implementation-safe version:
All traversed geometry is static, assessed and authorized. No heat modifier exists. The puzzle uses chronology, aliases and spatial provenance.

Potential consequence:
The courtyard becomes a candidate public-space heritage or improvement proposal through the existing Civic/Public Works process.

## Faction and NPC candidates

All are NON-CANON role patterns.

### The Weather Archivist

Maintains old forecasts, station logs and notice revisions. Useful because they care about what was known at a specific time rather than claiming perfect hindsight.

### The Hall Coordinator

Knows the difference between building access, cooling function, staffing and public capacity. Can recur across ordinary community events as well as heat episodes.

### The Early-Market Vendor Circle

A loose group whose schedule changed during a past episode and remained changed for practical reasons. Not a formal faction unless later canon says so.

### The Route Guide

Tracks practical journey windows and rest stops. Their local experience creates observations and recommendations, not meteorological authority or PTU immunity.

### The Maintenance Pair

Two recurring technicians associated with a public building. Their work can finish before operational verification, allowing realistic continuity without making them exposition machines.

### The Neighborhood Check-In Team

Only usable if canon establishes a legitimate institution/mandate for welfare checks. Their records remain privacy-scoped and do not expose diagnoses.

## Longer arc — A Town Learns When to Move the Day

Phase 1 — Ordinary summer
The town already has markets, work schedules, travel habits, public buildings and weather observations. Nothing is introduced solely to prepare the plot.

Phase 2 — Small divergences
A guide shifts departure time. A vendor opens earlier. One known Pokémon changes its resting place. These remain observations and local decisions.

Phase 3 — A bounded heat episode
A canonized weather process records unusual conditions. Different institutions react independently. A public building may take on a temporary support role. Another service may continue normally.

Phase 4 — Secondary dependency
A power or maintenance issue affects one support site. The episode does not automatically cause that failure; the relevant owner investigates it.

Phase 5 — Uneven recovery
Observed conditions improve. Some measures end quickly. A clinic monitoring period continues. A market keeps early hours. A temporary shade site stays popular.

Phase 6 — Memory
Months or years later, old notices, a changed timetable or a reused courtyard create a new mystery or Civic/Public Works proposal.

Narrative value:
The arc changes daily life without requiring a villain, apocalypse or tactical climate simulator.
