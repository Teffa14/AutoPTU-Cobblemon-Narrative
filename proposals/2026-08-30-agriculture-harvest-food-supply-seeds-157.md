# Ouros Agriculture, Harvest & Food-Supply Seeds — Pass 157

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. None of these names, sites, institutions, species roles, regional products or histories are established Ouros canon.

These seeds demonstrate the agriculture/harvest/food-supply continuity layer introduced in Pass 157. They are designed to connect ordinary life, ecology, economy, infrastructure and travel while preserving provenance and battle-authority boundaries.

## The Quiet Harvest

Premise: the player spends part of an ordinary harvest day at a small production site. There is no emergency and no required battle.

Useful content:

- learn who works which area;
- observe an individual Pokémon performing an authored work role;
- see how produce moves from field to sorting area;
- hear an expected yield claim;
- record which road or local buyer normally receives the output;
- establish normal seasonal routines before later disruption.

Payoff: months later, changes in staffing, output, storage or route use are legible because the player saw the baseline.

Canon caution: no specific species, crop, ownership relationship or region is implied until adopted.

## Five Times They Said “The Harvest Failed”

Mystery premise: several sources use the same phrase while describing different stages.

Possible records:

- one field produced less than expected;
- a harvested lot spoiled before storage;
- a packing operation rejected part of a batch;
- a shipment missed its intended market window;
- a shop had none of the product when a customer arrived.

Resolution: the player reconstructs scope and chronology. Multiple statements can remain accurate within their speakers’ meanings.

Design value: teaches that production, post-harvest handling, distribution and retail availability are separate facts.

## The Orchard That Fed Two Towns Differently

Premise: one orchard historically supplies two nearby settlements through different routes and buyers. A disruption affects only one route.

Consequences:

- one settlement experiences a visible shortage;
- the other continues to receive ordinary deliveries;
- rumors accuse the orchard of favoritism;
- records show that the two supply paths were always separate.

Possible resolution: restore access, arrange a temporary redistribution, accept a short seasonal gap, or discover that an apparently shared dependency never existed.

No faction guilt is predetermined.

## The Full Storehouse and the Empty Market

Premise: residents report scarcity while a nearby storage site appears full.

Possible explanations remain open until evidence establishes them:

- the contents belong to a different supply chain;
- stock is reserved for a later commitment;
- the visible inventory record is outdated;
- custody prevents immediate release;
- transport from storage to market failed;
- only a portion of the stored material is usable;
- public perception of scarcity exceeds the confirmed scope.

Material Culture owns actual batch identity and custody. This proposal owns the supply episode around those records.

## The Berry Crop Everyone Blamed on Weather

Premise: poor market availability follows a severe weather event, and public explanation immediately blames the storm.

Investigation can produce several outcomes:

- field loss genuinely caused most of the shortage;
- harvest survived but post-harvest handling failed;
- route interruption created the visible shortage;
- several small losses combined;
- the effect was limited to one product or locality.

The weather event remains a fact. Causation remains a hypothesis until supported.

## The Temporary Crop

Premise: one product is limited for a season. Households, restaurants or market stalls adopt a substitute.

Long-term branches:

- old supply returns and substitution disappears;
- both remain available;
- one community keeps the substitute as a preferred seasonal dish;
- a later festival incorporates the adaptation and accurately remembers its recent origin;
- tourism later markets the practice as older than records support.

This seed can connect to Ritual/Tradition continuity without rewriting the substitution’s actual origin date.

## The Ranch Where the Pokémon Choose Different Work

Premise: several Pokémon associated with one workplace participate in different routines according to explicit individual histories.

Examples of authored roles may include movement between paddocks, carrying lightweight materials, accompanying a caretaker or simply living at the site without a work role.

Conflict: an outsider assumes species identity determines work ability and proposes reorganizing the site accordingly.

The story can be social and observational. No battle is required.

Hard boundary: species/type never creates a universal labor contract.

## The Route That Changed the Market Basket

Premise: a transport route changes for reasons owned by the transport or infrastructure systems. The region still produces the same food, but delivery patterns change.

Consequences can include:

- some products arrive less often;
- durable substitutes become more common;
- merchants change suppliers;
- a former roadside market loses traffic;
- another settlement becomes a transfer point.

This can create durable regional identity from logistical history without claiming a crop itself changed.

## The Granary Record Says Plenty

Premise: an inventory ledger reports enough stored food to cover expected demand, but field observation conflicts with that conclusion.

Investigation questions:

- when was the count made?
- did it refer to gross storage or releasable stock?
- were all lots the same product?
- was some stock already committed elsewhere?
- did a later loss occur?
- are current consumption assumptions wrong?

The case is about claim scope and chronology rather than a mandatory fraud plot.

## The Field With Two Calendars

Premise: two communities describe the same agricultural season with different customary markers. Their practical schedules overlap but do not use identical names or dates.

The player may initially think records contradict each other.

Payoff: local calendars encode different observations—flowering, rainfall, a civic date, market opening or another established marker.

Canon caution: no calendar, climate cycle or crop timing is created until separately approved.

## The Orchard's Missing Visitors

Premise: workers report that a familiar wild species stopped visiting during a production cycle. Output later changes.

Investigation should preserve several possible relations:

- the visits and output are causally related;
- both changed because of another environmental factor;
- the output change is unrelated;
- the remembered baseline was inaccurate.

Ecology owns any real species relationship. This proposal cannot silently declare pollination or pest-control dependence.

## The Workshop That Cannot Use This Batch

Premise: a processor rejects one lot despite a successful harvest.

Possible consequences:

- farmers still count the crop as harvested;
- the processor records unusable input;
- merchants report lower finished-product availability;
- another buyer may accept the material for a different use if canon permits.

The conflict shows why `HARVESTED` and `USABLE_FOR_THIS_PROCESS` require separate records.

## The Bad Season Without a Villain

Premise: several modest problems combine during one production season—late work, a local infrastructure interruption, one storage loss and a route delay.

No event alone is catastrophic. Together they change market availability and household behavior.

The player can improve some conditions without finding a mastermind. Recovery occurs over time through ordinary work and authored interventions.

## The Small Farm Everyone Calls Independent

Premise: local conversation calls a farm self-sufficient. Records show it relies on several external inputs or services.

The term may remain socially meaningful while being technically imprecise.

Potential dependencies:

- seasonal labor;
- a shared pump;
- replacement tools;
- a particular market;
- storage in another town;
- transport on one weekly route.

No dependency exists until explicitly authored or evidenced.

## The Produce That Arrived After the Festival

Premise: a seasonal celebration expects a fresh product, but a delayed shipment arrives afterward.

The festival still happens using preserved goods or a substitute. Later public memory compresses this into “the year without the harvest.”

Chronicle facts can preserve:

- harvest occurred;
- shipment was delayed;
- festival substitution occurred;
- product arrived later;
- memory simplified the event.

This seed connects agriculture to Public Memory and Ritual continuity.

## Long arc — The Region That Eats Differently After a Bad Season

Phase 1 establishes ordinary production through markets, work visits and travel. Different settlements depend on different combinations of local supply, storage and trade.

Phase 2 introduces several independent disruptions. Some sites have weak harvests. Another loses stored product. A route interruption affects one town. A processor has an unrelated problem.

Phase 3 produces substitutions. Merchants source differently, households change recipes, one institution reserves stock, and a temporary market becomes important.

Phase 4 follows recovery. Some production returns quickly. Storage takes longer to replenish. Distribution patterns partly remain changed because actors made new relationships during the disruption.

Phase 5 revisits memory. People describe “the bad harvest” even though the Chronicle shows a more complicated supply event.

Possible endings include ordinary normalization, permanent market diversification, a new seasonal practice, relocation of processing capacity, or accepted vulnerability without a single final resolution.

No famine, price spike, ecological collapse or political crisis is automatic. Those require their own evidence and canon authority.

## Encounter contract — Orchard Storm-Damage Perimeter

Full version intent: a post-storm orchard contains unstable branches, wet or obstructed terrain, active weather or residual hazards, frightened or territorial Pokémon, workers withdrawing and valuable access lanes.

Full dependency families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement if falling debris or constrained displacement matters;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle for changing hazards;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle as selected content requires;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- move-specific behavior, abilities, items and Trainer Features/perks by individual audit;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for withdraw/protect/route-control objectives;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback for authoritative hazard presentation.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: the storm has ended before initiative. Workers and produce remain outside BattleSpec. Geometry is static and verified safe for ordinary tactical movement. The battle can return only `IMMEDIATE_ORCHARD_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

Afterward, Ouros separately resolves inspection, salvage, harvest and production consequences.

## Encounter contract — Irrigation Gate Access Corridor

Full version intent: active water levels, current, a contested control point and changing access make the gate tactically meaningful.

Key blockers include terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, complete forced-movement semantics, lifecycle, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback.

Reduced version: water/service state is frozen before initiative. The gate and controls are non-targetable scenery. Technicians remain outside BattleSpec. AutoPTU may return `IMMEDIATE_IRRIGATION_GATE_ACCESS_CLEAR`.

`ACCESS_CLEAR` grants no `WATER_DELIVERED`, `GATE_OPERATED` or `HARVEST_SAVED` fact.

## Encounter contract — Harvest Cart Staging Perimeter

Full version intent: workers and carts are moving while opponents attempt to delay, divert or block departure.

Key blockers include object carrying, escort/vehicle semantics inside complete movement, lifecycle, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback. Hazards/reactions may also matter depending on map design.

Reduced version: carts, cargo and workers stay outside BattleSpec and stationary. The encounter occurs in a fixed staging perimeter with explicit combatants. Permitted result: `IMMEDIATE_HARVEST_STAGING_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

Transport and delivery occur later through their owning systems.

## Encounter contract — Ranch Holding-Perimeter Incident

Full version intent: a tactical incident occurs while noncombatant work Pokémon or other animals must be protected, redirected or evacuated through gates.

That version is BLOCKED when it requires escort/protect objectives, autonomous movement, dynamic gates, reactions or tactical AI that understands evacuation.

Reduced version is READY only if Ouros establishes before initiative that noncombatants have already reached a safe holding area. BattleSpec then contains only explicit combatants on static geometry and may return `IMMEDIATE_RANCH_PERIMETER_CLEAR`.

Tactical failure without a verified rescue/harm contract cannot establish injury, capture, escape, ownership change or death of off-spec Pokémon.

## Candidate recurring NPC/faction roles

These are archetypes, not canon identities:

- a field steward who remembers actual yields better than public summaries;
- a processor who sees quality variation but not field conditions;
- a seasonal worker who knows route and timing changes across sites;
- a storage caretaker who understands custody and release history;
- a market coordinator who sees availability but not production cause;
- a researcher studying an observed cultivation anomaly without claiming causal certainty;
- a family operator whose household, workplace and land history overlap;
- a transport contact whose view begins only when goods leave the production site.

No role receives automatic expertise, legal authority, ownership or mechanical bonuses.
