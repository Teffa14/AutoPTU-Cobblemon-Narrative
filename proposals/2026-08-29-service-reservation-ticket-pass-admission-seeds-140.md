# Ouros Proposals — Service Reservation, Ticket, Pass & Admission Seeds — Pass 140

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Nothing in this file becomes established Ouros lore without later approval.

These seeds exercise the proposed service-entitlement continuity layer while keeping transport, event, finance, identity, credential and battle authority with their existing owners.

## Seed: The Pass Still Works, the Train Does Not

A recurring commuter holds a currently valid regional pass. A power or infrastructure problem suspends the only service covered by it for part of the day.

The local dispute comes from language. One notice says passes remain valid. Another says service suspended. NPCs simplify both statements into "the pass stopped working."

Useful consequences:

- commuters improvise alternate routes;
- a café near the replacement stop becomes unexpectedly busy;
- a courier route changes temporarily;
- old complaints remain in records after service resumes;
- the pass never needed cancellation.

Core boundary: `VALID_ENTITLEMENT != SERVICE_OPERATING`.

## Seed: Three Islands, Then Seven

A coastal operator once issued limited passes covering only an inner group of islands. Years later, a broader successor pass was introduced without destroying the old network history.

An archive contains both pass designs. A resident remembers when the outer islands were outside normal service scope. Current passengers treat the difference as ancient trivia.

Possible uses:

- dating old photographs from pass design;
- explaining why an older terminal sign lists fewer destinations;
- finding a family record linked to an earlier network phase;
- preserving the old pass as material culture while its service right is superseded.

Core boundary: `SUCCESSOR_SCOPE_EXPANDED != OLD_HISTORY_ERASED`.

## Seed: The Ticket Is Valid for Somewhere Else

A traveler presents a genuine current ticket at a terminal. The issuer, holder and validity period all check out. The ticket applies to a different service boundary.

No fraud occurred. The traveler followed an outdated sign or a colloquial place name.

The resolution can connect Place Reference, Public Notices, Service Entitlement and Travel without villainizing the traveler or staff.

Core boundary: `VALID_ENTITLEMENT != VALID_FOR_THIS_SERVICE`.

## Seed: The Festival Has No Tickets

Visitors from a larger city assume every popular event needs advance booking. A village festival instead uses open admission and only reserves capacity for a few workshops.

The rumor that "tickets sold out" is false because general admission never used tickets. A workshop allocation did reach capacity.

This seed prevents the generator from universalizing urban administrative patterns across Ouros.

## Seed: The Reservation Exists, the Paper Does Not

A traveler loses the printed representation of a reservation. The local operator retains a registry record and has an authored replacement procedure.

The story can revolve around identity linkage, communications failure or a closed counter without treating the lost paper as automatic loss of the underlying right.

Alternate local version: another small operator uses bearer-only paper tickets and has no recovery record. Both models can coexist in different parts of Ouros.

## Seed: The Old Ticket Is Genuine

A collector brings an old admission ticket to an institution that reopened under a successor organization.

Experts can establish that the object is historically genuine. Nobody can establish that the current institution honors it.

Possible outcomes:

- accepted as an archival donation;
- returned with provenance notes;
- displayed in a local exhibit;
- current admission separately granted as goodwill by an authorized owner;
- current validity remains unresolved.

Core boundary: `HISTORICALLY_AUTHENTIC != CURRENTLY_VALID`.

## Seed: One Pass, Two Riders, No Fraud

A family or group entitlement legitimately covers more than one traveler. A new staff member assumes one visible ticket must equal one passenger.

The conflict is resolved by policy and record inspection. No forged ticket is required.

This seed supports group travel, school trips, club outings and community excursions without creating universal group-ticket rules.

## Seed: The Seat Changed, the Journey Did Not

A vehicle substitution or capacity change moves a passenger from one seat or cabin allocation to another while preserving the same underlying journey entitlement.

The old printed representation becomes stale before the new one is delivered.

Possible environmental storytelling:

- handwritten temporary seat list;
- old and new boarding slips clipped together;
- staff directing passengers by surname or group;
- one NPC sitting in the old assigned place before correction.

Core boundary: `ALLOCATION_CHANGED != ENTITLEMENT_CANCELLED`.

## Seed: The Event Moved Across the Plaza

An outdoor event relocates because its original site is unavailable. Admission remains valid. The venue reference changes.

Some people receive the message. Others arrive at the old gate because a public sign was not updated yet.

The resulting activity can involve Communications, Public Notices, Place Reference and Event Operations while admission records remain stable.

## Seed: The Refund Is Still Coming

An event cancellation correctly cancels admission rights. Finance has authorized refunds, but some transfers remain pending.

NPCs use "refunded" to describe several different stages:

- refund promised;
- refund authorized;
- refund sent;
- refund received.

The mystery is administrative chronology rather than theft.

Core boundary: `ENTITLEMENT_CANCELLED != REFUND_RECEIVED`.

## Seed: The Free Ferry Still Needs a Reservation

A public or community-funded crossing charges no fare during a temporary period, but vessel capacity remains finite.

A zero-price journey still needs a capacity allocation under the local policy.

This seed separates money from reservation and supports emergency, civic or festival transport without inventing a fare economy.

Core boundary: `NO_PAYMENT_REQUIRED != NO_CAPACITY_CONTROL`.

## Seed: The Pass Was Reissued Twice

A durable pass survives across two replacement representations: first after water damage, later after a station changes its issuing format.

Three physical artifacts exist in family storage. Only the current representation is accepted, but all three belong to one entitlement history.

Possible mystery: a photograph dated between the two reissues shows the middle card. The card helps date the photograph without proving where the holder traveled that day.

## Seed: The Invitation Is Not a Credential

A researcher, performer or guest receives admission to a restricted event. Their invitation allows entry under a narrow event scope.

It does not grant staff authority, professional certification, backstage access beyond the invitation, or permission to operate equipment.

Core boundary: `ADMISSION_ENTITLEMENT != PROFESSIONAL_AUTHORITY`.

## Seed: The Competitor Can Enter the Building but Cannot Compete

A battle venue uses spectator admission separately from challenge registration and eligibility.

A Trainer can hold a valid venue ticket while their roster or challenge registration is incomplete. Another Trainer can be competitively eligible but still need to complete the venue's ordinary entry process if local canon requires it.

Battle Institutions owns competition status. The service-entitlement layer never turns a ticket into battle legality.

## Seed: The Passenger Boarded, the Ticket Was Never Consumed

A small operator validates tickets visually and records boarding on a manifest. The ticket itself is not punched, scanned or destroyed.

Later, an NPC assumes the intact ticket proves it was unused.

The manifest and boarding event contradict that assumption without making the object counterfeit.

Core boundary: `PHYSICAL_TOKEN_UNALTERED != UNUSED`.

## Seed: The Ticket Was Scanned, the Journey Never Happened

A departure gate validates passengers before the service is canceled.

The resulting records contain successful validation events but no departure. A later investigation must avoid treating scans as proof of completed travel.

Core boundary: `VALIDATION_SUCCESS != SERVICE_COMPLETED`.

## Seed: The Wrong Date Was Right Yesterday

A reservation changed after a disruption. An older printed representation still shows the original date.

The paper is genuine but superseded. A screenshot, archive record or NPC memory can all be truthful while describing different versions of the same booking history.

## Seed: The Temporary Pass Became a Souvenir

A short-lived local shuttle or festival route used colored passes for one season. The service ended years ago, but residents kept the tokens.

Today the passes appear in homes, shops and a local history display. They carry social memory without current operational authority.

## Mystery: Five Times the Ticket Was "Valid"

Five witnesses use the word valid differently:

1. the issuer genuinely produced it;
2. its date had not expired;
3. it covered the network;
4. it covered the specific departure;
5. the holder successfully used it.

The investigation succeeds by separating authenticity, temporal validity, scope, allocation and use.

No forged object is necessary.

## Mystery: Four Tickets, One Entitlement

An archive box contains four representations that appear to duplicate one travel right.

Possible explanation:

- original paper issue;
- replacement after damage;
- updated itinerary representation;
- final reprint after station relocation.

The entitlement lineage explains all four without creating four simultaneous rights.

## Mystery: The Person Who "Was at the Festival"

A surviving admission record shows that an actor held a valid ticket. A gate log shows the ticket was validated. Neither fact alone proves where the actor spent the rest of the event.

The mystery intentionally stops short of using admission metadata as omniscient location tracking.

## Mystery: The Pass That Predates the Route

A historical pass appears to have been issued before the current route opened.

Possible non-fraud resolutions:

- the pass originally covered a predecessor route;
- the service retained its identity through a route redesign;
- the printed route name was adopted before physical opening;
- the pass is a replacement whose visible date differs from the underlying entitlement start;
- two services shared a colloquial name.

Place Reference, Transport and Entitlement provenance must be compared.

## Exploration: The Closed Ticket Hall

An old station wing still contains faded windows, queue rails, route diagrams and fragments of several ticket formats.

The current service uses a different entrance and no longer needs the hall.

Exploration can reveal service history through static scenery and records without any tactical ticket mechanic.

Reduced implementation: READY with existing world-state presentation.

## Exploration: The Island Pier With Three Pass Colors

A retired pier building contains paint marks corresponding to three historical service scopes. Local residents remember which colors belonged to which period.

The puzzle is chronological rather than combinatorial: establish which destinations were reachable during each network phase.

No invented Skill DC is required. Evidence can be ordinary records, NPC testimony and environmental clues.

## NPC archetype: The Validator Who Remembers Exceptions

A long-serving staff member knows the difference between routine validation and exceptional cases. They are useful because they remember old procedures, but their memory is evidence rather than automatic authority.

They can say, "we used to accept the blue cards here," while archival or policy records determine whether that memory applies to the disputed date.

## NPC archetype: The Commuter With Three Generations of Passes

A recurring passenger has lived through service redesigns, station relocation and pass-format changes.

Their possessions provide material continuity. Their habits provide human continuity. Neither grants them control over the transport system.

## NPC archetype: The Event Steward Who Cannot Issue Refunds

A steward can validate admission and manage the gate but has no financial authority.

This creates natural, non-antagonistic friction when a cancellation occurs. The steward can correctly deny a refund request at the gate while directing the holder to the proper owner.

## Faction / institution relationship: Operator, Issuer, Venue, Financier

A service can involve several institutions:

- one operates the vehicle or event;
- one issues reservations;
- one controls the venue;
- one handles payment or reimbursement.

Ouros should preserve those relationships rather than flatten them into a single "ticket company."

## Long arc: A Coast Learns What a Pass Covers

Phase 1 establishes ordinary travel between several towns using a mix of open services, reservations and a limited network pass.

Phase 2 introduces a new destination or restored route. The transport network expands before every old pass automatically gains the new scope.

Phase 3 introduces a broader successor pass. Old passes remain historically valid for their original scope until superseded or expired according to authored rules.

Phase 4 produces downstream differences. Old signs, family keepsakes, commuter routines, ticket-office architecture and merchant assumptions do not update simultaneously.

Phase 5 revisits the system during a disruption. Valid passes coexist with canceled departures, temporary free transport and rebooking.

Months later, the coast retains changed commuting patterns and visible ticket history. The arc can advance without villainy and without using battle as the authority for access decisions.

## Encounter concept: Boarding Gate Withdrawal

Narrative premise: passenger processing is underway when an independent tactical threat makes the immediate approach unsafe.

Full version requires:

- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement for escort/interception cases;
- full turn/round lifecycle for staged withdrawal;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for protected lanes or changing obstacles;
- AI tactical policy for PROTECT/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support for semantic gate and withdrawal states.

Reduced version: READY.

Passengers, validators, records and noncombatants leave the tactical grid before BattleSpec creation. Ouros supplies explicit combatants and static geometry. Victory can only clear the immediate gate approach.

`BATTLE_RESULT != ENTITLEMENT_VALIDATION`.

## Encounter concept: Ticket Office Access Chokepoint

Narrative premise: a disrupted service creates a temporary rebooking office while an unrelated threat blocks its approach.

Full version uses the same rich movement/lifecycle/hazard/AI/adapter families if civilians move dynamically.

Reduced version: READY.

Customers and staff are already safe. AutoPTU resolves a static encounter outside. The outcome can restore physical access but cannot process reservations, issue replacements or settle money.

## Encounter concept: Special-Admission Perimeter

Narrative premise: a remote activity requires both a broad service pass and a narrow special entitlement. A separate tactical encounter occurs outside the controlled area.

Full version becomes rich only if the encounter includes dynamic perimeter zones, reactions or escort behavior.

Reduced version: READY.

All admission decisions occur before BattleSpec. Combat may secure the exterior approach. It cannot satisfy either prerequisite.

## Encounter concept: Departure Change Corridor

Narrative premise: a departure relocates to another gate or berth after passengers already received an allocation. An independent encounter blocks the connecting public corridor.

Full version can require staged movement and objective-aware AI.

Reduced version: READY.

Passenger movement is resolved in world state first. Combatants fight on static geometry after the corridor has been cleared of civilians. Victory creates `IMMEDIATE_CORRIDOR_CLEAR` only.

`CORRIDOR_CLEAR != REBOOKING_COMPLETE`.
