# Ouros Myth & Archaeology Seeds — Pass 08

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. Names, cultures, institutions, locations, Pokémon and exact mechanics remain placeholders until canon and PTU/AutoPTU review.

## Seed CU — The Mountain With Two Names

Two neighboring communities consider the same mountain sacred for different reasons.

One tradition says the mountain marks a place where a protective Pokémon first appeared. The other says the mountain itself is the important subject and that later stories wrongly centered the Pokémon.

Both traditions preserve old routes, seasonal customs and place names that overlap physically.

Players may become involved through:
- a blocked pilgrimage route;
- a research survey;
- a disputed sign or memorial;
- ecological disturbance near a sacred area;
- an institution trying to publish one interpretation as definitive.

The system does not predeclare either tradition false.

## Seed CV — The Chamber Nobody Should Loot

An expedition reaches a sealed chamber containing old objects arranged around a central structure.

Nothing attacks.

The challenge is deciding what the chamber is.

Possible player actions:
- document it untouched;
- remove a loose object for stabilization;
- seek a steward or historian;
- compare the layout to another site;
- notice that wild Pokémon currently use the space;
- disturb the arrangement and create a new archaeological state.

The generator must not label every object treasure simply because it is old.

## Seed CW — The Ritual That Still Works Socially

A community performs a yearly ceremony believed to ensure safe migration through a dangerous pass.

Whether the ritual has any supernatural effect is unknown.

What is definitely true:
- people coordinate travel around it;
- volunteers inspect shelters beforehand;
- local guides gather route reports;
- visitors arrive;
- a protected Pokémon population is less disturbed during the closure period.

The ceremony has real consequences even if no magical mechanic exists.

A later quest can ask whether the ritual's practical function is being forgotten as the tradition becomes more commercialized.

## Seed CX — The Translation War

Two scholars produce incompatible translations of the same damaged inscription.

Neither is obviously dishonest.

One interpretation implies the site was a sanctuary.
The other suggests it was a warning or containment structure.

Players can find additional context through:
- another inscription;
- material layout;
- an old copied rubbing;
- oral tradition;
- comparison with a second site;
- environmental behavior around the structure.

The physical inscription remains the same while interpretation evolves.

## Seed CY — The Pilgrimage Road

An old route is still used by visitors traveling to a culturally important site.

The road crosses modern infrastructure and several Pokémon habitats.

Current pressures include:
- increased tourism;
- damaged shelters;
- route widening proposals;
- seasonal nesting;
- merchants wanting permanent stalls;
- residents who value quiet access.

Players can engage through repair, research, escort, negotiation, commerce or simple participation in the journey.

The route itself becomes a moving social space.

## Seed CZ — The Guardian Who Is Not Owned

A persistent Pokémon is closely associated with one settlement and appears during particular environmental conditions.

Residents regard it as a protector.
No record proves that anyone owns it.

A new institution wants to study it more closely after unusual behavior.

Possible tensions:
- researchers request tracking access;
- residents fear interference;
- younger locals are less protective than elders;
- the Pokémon begins appearing somewhere new;
- an outsider assumes it is simply a rare capture opportunity.

The story is about stewardship, observation and interpretation before capture.

## Seed DA — The Ruin Under the Road

Construction exposes an older structure beneath a heavily used route.

Stopping work completely harms transport.
Continuing immediately risks destroying evidence.

Possible stakeholders:
- builders;
- local residents;
- archaeologists;
- merchants;
- cultural stewards;
- conservation staff if Pokémon have moved into the exposed cavity.

Possible outcomes include rerouting, limited excavation, documentation before continuation, permanent preservation or a hybrid solution.

The site exists because the world changed, not because a quest marker spawned a dungeon.

## Seed DB — The Shrine Built Later

A revered shrine is assumed by tourists to be ancient.

Research shows the present structure was built much later than the event it commemorates.

This does not make the shrine fake.

The discovery can reveal:
- how the tradition changed;
- who rebuilt the site after a disaster;
- why one architectural style was chosen;
- which older location was abandoned;
- why current visitors misunderstand the timeline.

The story teaches that cultural importance and physical age are different facts.

## Seed DC — The Myth That Migrated

Three distant settlements tell variations of a story about a traveler guided home by a strange Pokémon during a storm.

The details differ:
- species description;
- route;
- season;
- whether the traveler survived;
- what moral the story teaches.

A researcher notices recurring motifs and asks whether the versions reflect an old migration corridor.

Players can investigate geography and records without ever proving that the original event happened exactly as any version says.

## Seed DD — The Artifact That Is Still Doing Something

A stone, machine or carved object has sat in an old site for centuries.

A recent disturbance reveals that moving or damaging nearby structures changes a measurable environmental condition.

The object may be:
- part of an old water-control system;
- stabilizing a seal;
- connected to a modern Pokémon nest;
- mechanically irrelevant while the surrounding architecture matters;
- genuinely anomalous.

The system records observations first. No magical explanation is generated automatically.

## Seed DE — The Festival That Changed the Myth

A recurring festival retells an old regional story every year.

Over time, performances simplified the story until one minor figure became the central hero and another community's role disappeared.

A new archaeological discovery reopens the question.

Possible consequences:
- next year's script changes;
- performers resist rewriting a beloved tradition;
- a museum adds context rather than declaring the festival wrong;
- another community asks to participate;
- tourists become interested in the debate.

Public memory and deep history interact without rewriting canonical ancient facts.

## Seed DF — The Cave of Borrowed Symbols

An underground site contains symbols associated with two different regions.

Initial theories assume one culture copied the other.

Further evidence suggests other possibilities:
- migration;
- trade;
- mixed community;
- later reuse of an older site;
- modern restoration combining fragments incorrectly.

The scenario is built around overlapping history rather than one lost super-civilization that explains everything.

## Seed DG — The Warden Without a Miracle

A local steward has spent years caring for a habitat associated with an important Pokémon but has never personally seen the creature.

Some outsiders quietly question the tradition because of that absence.

The steward's actual work still matters:
- habitat protection;
- route safety;
- recording reports;
- maintaining old shelters;
- guiding visitors;
- preventing disturbance during sensitive seasons.

The arc can explore duty without requiring a Legendary appearance as validation.

## Seed DH — The Excavation That Must Stop

An excavation team reaches a fragile layer during worsening weather.

They have enough time to do only one of several things:
- stabilize the exposed structure;
- document inscriptions;
- recover an at-risk object;
- evacuate personnel;
- protect a nearby Pokémon nest;
- seal the site and return later.

No choice preserves everything.

The result changes site state and future evidence availability instead of marking the mission simply success/failure.

## Seed DI — The Dream Is Evidence of a Dream

Several characters independently report unusual dreams after staying near an old site.

The dreams are recorded as experiences, not as historical truth.

Possible authored causes could eventually include:
- environmental stress;
- suggestion after hearing a local story;
- an actual governed Pokémon capability or Move;
- coincidence;
- an unexplained phenomenon.

The system must not turn dream content directly into canon facts unless a validated mechanic or authored event supports it.

## Seed DJ — The Missing Statue

An old photograph shows a statue that no longer exists at a heritage site.

Nobody agrees when it disappeared.

The search can branch into:
- old renovations;
- museum transfers;
- private collections;
- damage after a storm;
- a replacement mistaken for the original;
- deliberate removal during a political change.

The object becomes a provenance case connecting archaeology, public memory and material culture.

## Seed DK — The Quiet Sacred Site

Players reach a location that legends describe as dangerous and extraordinary.

Nothing happens.

The place is still useful because it contains:
- old architecture;
- a rare ecology;
- evidence of repeated visits;
- caretaker records;
- conflicting offerings from several traditions;
- a view of another important landscape.

This prevents sacred places from becoming vending machines for supernatural events.

## Seed DL — The Legendary Rumor Cascade

A distant silhouette, unusual weather and one damaged field recording produce a rumor that a Legendary Pokémon has appeared.

Different actors respond before the claim is verified:
- tourists travel toward the region;
- researchers ask for observations;
- opportunists sell fake maps;
- conservation staff restrict sensitive habitat;
- local residents become annoyed by attention;
- a faction uses the rumor for publicity.

The actual cause may or may not involve a Legendary Pokémon.

The story emerges from reactions to uncertain information.

## Seed DM — The Museum Returns the Object

A museum discovers that one displayed object entered its collection under an old arrangement whose terms were never clearly documented.

A community asks for its return.

Possible options:
- return the original;
- long-term loan;
- shared exhibition;
- replica display;
- further provenance research;
- leave the dispute unresolved.

The generator must not decide ownership law universally. The conflict depends on the authored norms and agreements of the involved institutions and communities.

## Seed DN — The Site With a Living Layer

An ancient ruin has become an important habitat over centuries.

Archaeologists want access to a buried section.
Ecologists warn that excavation timing would disrupt breeding.
Local stewards value both the old structure and the current Pokémon population.

Players may help create a seasonal access plan, find another entrance, document remotely, postpone work or prioritize one concern.

The old past and living present both matter.

## New implementation and canon questions

- Which Ouros regions will have authored historical cultures before procedural myth generation is allowed?
- Which Legendary/Mythical Pokémon are permitted to exist physically as persistent entities, and which remain lore-only until authored?
- How should Minecraft represent excavation stages without permanently destroying authored structures?
- Can the world bridge persist site-context metadata when blocks are removed or moved?
- Which PTU/Caelo Skills or capabilities are legal for archaeology, translation, special senses, dreams, Aura, tracking or Unown interaction?
- What stewardship, access and heritage norms belong to each region rather than a universal legal framework?
- How should oral-tradition versions be generated without producing hundreds of meaningless variants?
- Which myths are hand-authored anchors versus procedurally mutated local versions?
- How should the generator distinguish an anomalous phenomenon from a mechanically known Move/Ability/Capability effect?
- How should Legendary rumors affect spawning, tourism and factions without accidentally spawning the Pokémon?
- What human review is required before a mythic claim is promoted into established ancient canon?
