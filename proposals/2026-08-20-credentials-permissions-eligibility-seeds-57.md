# Pass 57 — Credentials, Permissions & Eligibility Seeds

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No source plot, dialogue, named character or protected prose is copied.

These seeds assume the systems defined in `design/credentials-permissions-eligibility-layer.md`. Any PTU/Caelo prerequisite remains unresolved until checked against the governing source.

## 1. The Pass That Still Works

A field researcher loses the physical card used at a reserve checkpoint. The institution's server record still shows an active grant, but the remote gate has been offline since a storm.

The story is about proving the current authorization without treating the lost card as either meaningless or magical authority.

## 2. The Pass That Should Not Work

An old physical badge still opens a storage door even though the role tied to it ended months ago. Nobody has to be malicious; the problem may be an outdated access-control system.

The players can report, document and repair the mismatch before it becomes a larger security failure.

## 3. Survey Rank, Wrong Valley

A highly ranked surveyor arrives at another institution and assumes their rank grants access. The host respects the experience but only recognizes part of the qualification.

A supervised route can let the expedition proceed while the recognition issue is reviewed.

## 4. The One-Use Challenge Ticket

A Trainer earns eligibility for a promotion battle and receives one challenge token. A schedule cancellation occurs before the match.

The interesting question is whether the institution reissues, extends or preserves the token, not whether the player must grind eligibility again.

## 5. Retired Ranger, Current Emergency

A respected former responder no longer holds active operational authority. During a crisis, the local team may issue a temporary emergency grant because the person has useful experience.

The temporary grant ends with the incident unless formally renewed.

## 6. The Sponsor's Invitation

A sponsor invites a Trainer to a restricted venue and publicly implies that access is arranged. The venue has received no valid grant request.

The conflict comes from mismatched expectations rather than corruption.

## 7. Uniform Without Access

A former employee still owns an old uniform. New staff repeatedly assume the person can enter controlled areas.

The visual-culture, infiltration and access systems can all participate without making the uniform itself authoritative.

## 8. Press Area Only

A journalist receives valid event media access. They later ask to inspect private medical records related to an injured competitor.

Their press credential remains valid while the additional request is denied or separately reviewed.

## 9. The Supervised Archive

A young researcher qualifies to inspect a sensitive collection only while a curator is present. The curator becomes unavailable halfway through the scheduled session.

The archive can pause access, arrange a handoff or reschedule without accusing anyone of wrongdoing.

## 10. The Name Changed, the Record Did Not

A Trainer's public identity record changed while an older credential still uses the previous name. The credential is genuine, but automated verification treats the records as two people.

Resolving the identity link becomes a small administrative mystery with privacy implications.

## 11. Temporary Nesting Permit

A field team receives access to a protected trail outside peak nesting hours. New observations show the nesting window started earlier than expected.

Stewards must decide whether to shorten the grant, add supervision or close the route entirely based on ecological evidence rather than rank.

## 12. The Apprentice Can Do the Work

An apprentice has completed the task-specific practical assessment but is not qualified to supervise others. A staffing shortage tempts the workshop to treat those roles as equivalent.

The best resolution may be to let the apprentice perform the work while preserving the supervision boundary.

## 13. Two Standards, Both Reasonable

Two research institutes accept different evidence for field certification. Neither system is fraudulent.

A joint expedition forces them to define exactly which parts of each standard they recognize.

## 14. The Old Guide Qualification

A local guide earned a route qualification years ago. The landscape has changed substantially since then.

The guide still has valuable knowledge, but a short route-specific reassessment may be sensible before leading visitors again.

## 15. Access During the Outage

A digital registry fails across a small region. Staff can verify some permissions through signed local caches and recent audit logs but not others.

The challenge is to maintain essential services without treating every physical token as unquestionable truth.

## 16. The Emergency Key

A waterworks keeps one physical emergency key for crisis use. Possession of the key can open a door, but the world record must still track who authorized its use and when it was returned.

The key is an instrument, not authority.

## 17. Partial Badge Recognition

A visiting Trainer presents a real League achievement from another region. The host circuit respects the record but does not automatically place the Trainer at an equivalent local stage.

A short placement assessment or specific challenge can bridge the systems without invalidating either region's history.

## 18. The Festival Vendor Pass

A local bakery obtains a stall permit for a festival. Visitors begin treating the vendor as an event official because of the visible pass.

The vendor can help with information but has no authority over event security or scheduling.

## 19. The Closed Mine Survey

Geologists have a valid survey permission for an old mine. A newly documented habitat closure now overlaps the access route.

Both records can be valid. The team needs an alternative route, amended scope or delayed survey rather than a winner-takes-all ruling.

## 20. The Clinic Specialist

A specialist visits a rural clinic for two weeks under a temporary assignment. The assignment permits specific treatments and records access but does not make the visitor head of the clinic.

The role expires normally at the end of the placement.

## 21. The Public Hero at the Locked Door

A famous Trainer reaches a controlled research annex and assumes recognition will help. The receptionist knows exactly who they are and still cannot grant access without the required project authorization.

The scene reinforces that reputation and permission are separate.

## 22. The Credential Under Review

An institution discovers a documentation gap in a group of credentials issued several years ago. It suspends only the affected activity scope while reviewing the records.

The story can reveal procedural weakness without declaring every holder unqualified.

## 23. The Reissued Card

A Trainer reports a stolen access card. The issuer invalidates the token and provides a new one while keeping the underlying permission continuous.

Later, the stolen card appears in someone else's possession and becomes evidence without granting access.

## 24. One Expedition, Mixed Permissions

A multiplayer party joins a scientific expedition. Different members have different qualifications and record visibility.

The group can travel together while only specific actors perform sample collection, restricted archive access or technical operations.

## 25. The Volunteer Who Stayed Too Long

A crisis volunteer's emergency grant reaches its end condition, but the person has become valuable to the recovery team.

The institution must decide whether to issue a normal role, a new temporary assignment or simply thank them and end the access.

## 26. The Exam That Was Never Logged

A student clearly remembers completing a practical assessment, and several witnesses agree, but the official result is missing after a data migration.

The institution can reconstruct evidence or offer a fair reassessment without accusing the student of lying.

## 27. The Wrong Expiry Date

A batch of credentials was printed with an incorrect expiry date while the authoritative records contain the intended date.

The problem spreads because shops, guides and local staff trust the printed card more than the registry.

## 28. The Former Partner's Badge

A retired institutional Pokémon still wears a historic service marker. Visitors assume the Pokémon is currently on duty.

The marker remains part of its history. It does not create current assignment, ownership or command authority.

## 29. Restricted Map, Public Trail

A public trail crosses near a sensitive research site. The trail map is public, but the detailed field map with nesting coordinates is restricted.

Access to one information layer never has to imply access to the other.

## 30. The Qualification That Expires Mid-Expedition

A long expedition begins while every participant's qualification is valid. One credential reaches its renewal date while the team is still in the field.

The institution needs an authored rule for safe continuity, provisional extension or task reassignment instead of abruptly making the expedition retroactively unauthorized.

# Long Arc A — The Open Route

A regional survey institute begins with only a few public routes and a small number of trained field staff. Across several seasons, players can complete practical training, assist supervised surveys, contribute maps and qualify for more difficult sectors.

The arc deliberately avoids a universal rank ladder. One new qualification may open a cliff survey, another may authorize sample handling, while protected nesting areas still require separate seasonal permission.

Later, infrastructure repairs create a public route into an area that previously required field authorization. The institution must decide which old access restrictions are no longer necessary and which remain important for research or conservation.

The long-term payoff is visible world change: more public trails, better orientation material, new staff, alumni mentors and a history of which qualifications mattered at each stage.

# Long Arc B — The Broken Registry

An institution modernizes decades of paper, physical-card and digital access records. The migration reveals duplicate identities, stale tokens, missing assessments, expired permissions still treated as active and valid credentials that local checkpoints no longer recognize.

No single saboteur is required. Players can help reconstruct provenance through archives, staff interviews, physical records, digital logs and field evidence.

Some errors have real consequences: a protected-area gate may have admitted visitors too broadly, while a retired technician may have been wrongly denied access during a past emergency.

The resolution should preserve corrected records and historical versions rather than rewriting the institution as if it had always been organized correctly.

# Long Arc C — Recognition Season

Three neighboring regions begin coordinating research, rescue and major public events. Each has legitimate but different qualifications, ranks and access practices.

The campaign follows a series of joint expeditions, exchange placements and incidents that expose exactly where reciprocal recognition works and where it does not.

Players can help build narrow recognition agreements: one region accepts another's field-safety course, another recognizes tournament records but not protected-area permits, and all three establish a temporary emergency-access framework for cross-border crisis response.

The final state is a network of explicit recognition links rather than one homogenized regional bureaucracy.

# Guardrails

No proposal in this file establishes a universal Trainer license, citizenship, police power, passport, legal profession or administrative code.

A credential never grants PTU Skills, Features, Moves, Abilities, capabilities or combat bonuses by itself.

Physical cards, uniforms, tickets and badges are presentation/provenance objects unless an authored rule gives them an additional mechanical role.

Rank, reputation, fame, friendship, employment, custody and permission remain separate state.

Any irreversible access or role change affecting a player character should be visible and attributable to an authored rule or explicit player action.
