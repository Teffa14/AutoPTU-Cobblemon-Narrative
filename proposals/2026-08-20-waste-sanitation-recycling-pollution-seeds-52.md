# Ouros Waste, Sanitation, Recycling & Pollution Seeds — Pass 52

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros concepts derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character, dialogue or exact puzzle has been copied.

## 1. The River That Looks Clean

A restoration campaign removed visible debris months ago, but new sampling still finds an unexplained water-quality problem downstream. Residents disagree because the river now looks healthy.

Useful systems: science, sanitation, media, ecology, conservation, public memory.

## 2. Night Shift at the Transfer Yard

Night workers repeatedly observe a different mix of wild Pokémon around the transfer station than daytime researchers record. The difference may come from schedule, lighting, food waste timing, traffic or another cause.

Do not infer nocturnal hostility or contamination.

## 3. The Refusal at Bay Three

A sanitation facility works with several Pokémon that routinely interact with sorted waste. One individual begins avoiding one specific bay. The refusal is observable; its cause remains unknown until investigated.

The system must not invent sickness, fear, abuse or dangerous material without evidence.

## 4. Festival Composition Shift

A seasonal festival does not overwhelm total sanitation capacity, but it changes the type and timing of waste enough to alter scavenger activity around the venue and nearby route.

## 5. The Old Dump That Became a Refuge

A disposal site closed decades ago. Vegetation and wild Pokémon now occupy much of it. A new containment project must protect the surrounding environment without assuming the current habitat has no value.

## 6. Repair Café District

Residents gradually build a repair/reuse culture around appliances, clothing, tools and containers. Over several seasons, the district produces less discard and develops new workshop relationships.

No automatic money or crafting bonuses.

## 7. The Logo in the Drain

Medical-service packaging is found in a storm drain. Public rumor blames the clinic. Provenance investigation must consider transport loss, theft, reuse, mixed collection and illegal disposal before assigning responsibility.

## 8. Market Compost Trial

A produce market pilots an organic-waste program. Researchers notice changes in nearby Bug- and Grass-associated activity, but they need enough observations to separate the program from season and weather.

## 9. Storm Drain Morning

A blocked drain causes localized flooding after ordinary rain. The blockage can be traced through route maintenance, litter, vegetation and service state rather than assumed sabotage.

## 10. The Missing Collection Route

Several streets accumulate waste while the rest of the town remains normal. The collection map, current road closure and vehicle dispatch records disagree about which route should have served them.

## 11. Reclaimed Stone

A demolition stream contains reusable stone and structural pieces. A public-works project can use them if provenance, condition and engineering approval support it.

Crafting legality remains separate.

## 12. The Quiet Sorting Line

A sorting facility is fully powered but operating below capacity. The problem is a combination of staffing, one unavailable downstream destination and material arriving badly mixed.

## 13. Kids and Trubbish

Local children have formed a routine around a group of Trubbish near a reuse yard. Nearby businesses have reasonable concerns about blocked access and odor. The conflict can be solved through site management and observation instead of declaring either side irrational.

## 14. The Clean-Looking Outfall

No obvious waste is visible near an outfall, yet fishers and researchers report changes downstream. Samples, maintenance history and upstream sources become the real investigation.

## 15. Broken Boom After the Storm

A debris boom fails during a storm. Recovery becomes a short expedition along several downstream sites while wildlife uses the same corridor.

## 16. The Workshop Scrap Exchange

Several workshops begin exchanging offcuts and spare parts instead of discarding them. A future shortage reveals that one shop had quietly become an important source for another.

## 17. Wastewater Bypass Notice

A treatment site publishes a controlled-bypass notice during repairs. Public discussion turns it into a claim of total treatment failure. The actual service state, environmental monitoring and communication history remain separate.

## 18. The Abandoned Appliance Lot

A legacy lot of discarded machinery attracts scavengers, tinkerers and possibly machine-associated Pokémon. Salvage, ownership, environmental condition and Pokémon activity all require separate evidence.

## 19. The Event Venue After Midnight

A major battle venue looks pristine during events because a large cleanup operation works after closing. Staff shortages one week reveal how much invisible labor keeps the public space functioning.

## 20. Upstream Legacy

A modern facility repeatedly passes inspection, yet downstream sediment remains contaminated because the source is historical material already deposited years earlier.

## 21. The Wrong Bin Problem

A new sorting program appears to fail because too much material enters the wrong stream. The cause may be confusing signage, visitor behavior, inconsistent containers or collection mixing.

## 22. Research Samples in the Wrong Place

Discarded sample containers appear outside a field station. The research team insists their disposal records are complete. Custody and transport records show a later handoff that was never documented correctly.

## 23. Closed Sewer, New Corridor

An old drainage tunnel no longer carries its original service and has become a route used by wild Pokémon. A proposal to reactivate part of the system creates a genuine infrastructure/ecology tradeoff.

## 24. Collection Day Learners

A group of wild Pokémon has learned the normal timing of collection vehicles and appears shortly before pickups. A schedule change alters the pattern and creates apparent disappearance until observers update their assumptions.

## 25. The Recycling Yard Fire Rumor

Smoke seen near a reuse facility creates a fast rumor of hazardous fire. The actual event is a separate nearby controlled process. The false association still affects visitor behavior and public trust.

## 26. Cleanup Finds History

Removing waste from a riverbank exposes an older object or structure of possible archaeological value. Cleanup pauses locally while custody, context and stewardship systems take over.

## 27. Second-Life Festival

A community festival showcases repaired and reused objects, workshops and art made from recovered materials. The event can affect public attitudes and local businesses without granting item bonuses.

## 28. Overflow Without Villainy

A combination of heavy rain, an offline pump and a delayed replacement part pushes a treatment site beyond capacity. Every contributing failure is ordinary, but together they create a serious downstream problem.

## 29. The Facility Pokémon Retirement

A long-associated sanitation Pokémon stops participating in its old routine as it ages or changes behavior. The institution must adapt staffing and process capacity without treating the Pokémon as replaceable equipment.

Any age/health interpretation requires evidence.

## 30. The New Facility and the Old Ecology

A modern transfer/treatment site improves service reliability but changes traffic, lighting, noise and food availability around an area used by wild collectives. The result is mixed rather than purely positive or negative.

# Long arc A — The River That Looks Clean

Year/phase 1: visible debris cleanup succeeds and public confidence rises.

Phase 2: science teams detect persistent water/sediment anomalies.

Phase 3: several plausible sources are investigated; one visually ugly site turns out to be contained correctly while a less visible historical source matters more.

Phase 4: source control, sediment/habitat work and sanitation upgrades occur across several institutions.

Phase 5: long-term monitoring decides which improvements actually held.

The arc has no required villain and can create lasting changes to fisheries, transport, tourism, conservation and settlement memory.

# Long arc B — Second Life District

A neighborhood begins with informal repair and reuse activity. Workshops, a market, collection services and a public-works program gradually build a circular material network.

Success creates new problems: storage capacity, safety questions, visitor traffic, provenance disputes, shortage of skilled staff and dependence on recovered-material streams.

The district becomes important because of accumulated state rather than a one-time upgrade tree.

# Long arc C — Invisible Backlog

Small sanitation delays begin appearing in different settlements. Each has a different local cause, but all depend on one regional transfer/treatment bottleneck.

Players can discover the network through route maps, staff handoffs, facility records, wild-Pokémon observations and public reports.

The resolution may be redundancy, distributed treatment, changed routes, staffing, reuse reduction or a new facility. No single answer is assumed correct before the world state is authored.

# Encounter contract B — Transfer Station Jam

Full intended version:

Workers and vehicles need a route cleared while waste-associated Pokémon approach, forage, withdraw and possibly defend material. Gates and piles can change access.

Reduced current version:

Use static blockers. Keep workers, vehicles and sorting out of the battle grid. Start a conventional battle only with Pokémon that actually become hostile.

The full version depends mainly on complete movement/interception, environmental/interactable support, tactical AI and adapter playback.

# Encounter contract C — River Boom Recovery

Full intended version:

Players recover a damaged containment boom while floating debris, current, weather and wildlife create changing spatial pressure.

Reduced current version:

Resolve boom repair, currents and debris recovery in overworld state. Use a stable bank/dock map for any battle. Do not create current, debris or contamination damage.

The full version is blocked by terrain/weather/hazards/zones, forced movement, objective-aware tactical AI and Minecraft playback.
