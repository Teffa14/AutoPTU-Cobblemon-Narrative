# Ouros Estuary, Tidal Wetland & Salinity Seeds — Pass 78

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, dialogue or distinctive character is transplanted.

## 1. The Creek That Runs Backward

Residents report that a familiar tidal creek sometimes appears to reverse direction. The observation is real; the explanation is open. Players can compare tidal stage, river flow, wind and old channel maps.

Use: investigation + cartography + estuary monitoring.

## 2. The Missing Mudflat

A low-tide feeding flat used for years by local Pokémon is suddenly much smaller. Possible causes include a shifted channel, sediment redistribution, dredging, storm deposition or a mapping error.

Use: science + fisheries + conservation.

## 3. Shellos Survey, Year Four

A community survey has four years of Shellos observations and detects a change in which form is most commonly reported in one reach. The research question is why; the answer is not preselected.

Use: long-term observation + public science.

Guardrail: form distribution does not create a salinity rule.

## 4. The Fisher's Line

Fishers say the productive water has moved upstream. Instrument records show a salinity shift, but the fishery response still needs verification.

Use: Fisheries + Science + local knowledge.

## 5. Salt in the Well

A coastal settlement's well shows rising conductivity before any visible flooding occurs. The investigation spans groundwater, pumping history, estuary levels and possible equipment error.

Use: Freshwater + infrastructure + science.

## 6. Boardwalk at Spring Tide

An old marsh boardwalk was designed for historical water levels. It remains safe most days but loses access during a growing set of high-tide windows.

Use: public works + tourism + accessibility.

## 7. The Inland Marsh

A restored marsh begins establishing vegetation farther inland than planners expected. The adjacent parcel was reserved for future housing.

Use: civic governance + conservation + demography.

No side is automatically villainous.

## 8. The Closed Mouth

Sediment partly closes a lagoon entrance. Fishers, researchers, ferry operators and wildlife observers all report different consequences.

Use: multi-stakeholder world-state problem.

## 9. One Storm, Three Salinities

Three monitoring stations produce very different readings after a storm. All three may be correct because they sampled different depths, reaches and times.

Use: science + data interpretation.

## 10. Stunfisk Flats

Stunfisk begin appearing in a mudflat used by school field trips. The issue is not automatically removal; the institution must understand where they occur and adjust routes safely.

Use: education + wildlife observation + access design.

Guardrail: mud does not create Static or ambush rules by itself.

## 11. The Salt Panne Bloom

A normally sparse high-salinity depression changes after an unusual wet season. Researchers debate whether the shift is temporary or the start of a longer transition.

Use: flora + seasonality + monitoring.

## 12. The Forgotten Tide Gate

A gate built decades earlier still changes drainage, but no current institution clearly owns its maintenance records.

Use: archives + infrastructure + civic responsibility.

## 13. The Nursery Reach

Juvenile aquatic Pokémon are observed repeatedly in one shallow reach during a narrow seasonal window. A shipping project is scheduled for the same period.

Use: conservation + maritime + calendar.

Guardrail: juvenile presence does not prove breeding location or parentage.

## 14. High Water, Empty Harbor

Harbor water levels look normal, yet several inner-estuary species are absent. The cause could sit upstream, downstream or outside the estuary entirely.

Use: ecological anomaly built from absence.

## 15. The Two Correct Maps

A fisher's chart and a public survey disagree about the main channel. Both are accurate for the dates when they were made.

Use: cartography + public memory.

## 16. The Marsh That Saved the Road

A storm hits harder than forecast, but a restored wetland absorbs part of the surge. The road still suffers damage; the outcome becomes evidence for future planning rather than a magical shield effect.

Use: crisis + conservation + infrastructure.

## 17. Freshwater Above, Salt Below

Surface samples remain relatively fresh while deeper measurements show marine influence. A simple map cannot represent the vertical structure.

Use: advanced field research.

## 18. The Dredging Window

A navigation channel needs maintenance, but the preferred work period overlaps with a known ecological window. Players can help gather enough current evidence to refine timing.

Use: maritime + stewardship + science.

## 19. The Old Rice Field

A former agricultural parcel is slowly reverting to tidal wetland after a control structure fails. Residents disagree about restoration goals because the field also has family and economic history.

Use: public memory + agriculture + conservation.

No real-world cultural practice should be copied into fictional regional tradition without deliberate authored work.

## 20. The Broken Salinity Logger

An automated station reports a dramatic shift. Manual samples contradict it. Investigation finds fouling, clock drift or sensor failure rather than an ecological crisis.

Use: technology + science + false-alarm handling.

## 21. The Creek Under the Warehouse

Historic maps show a tidal creek beneath an industrial district. Repeated water problems raise the question of whether parts of the old channel still influence drainage.

Use: architecture + archives + hydrology.

## 22. Marsh School

A local academy runs recurring field practice where students build a long-term record of tide, salinity, wildlife and vegetation. Different cohorts inherit previous datasets.

Use: education + science + legacy.

## 23. The Week the Ferry Stopped

A small ferry pauses because the channel shoaled, not because the vessel broke. The interruption changes commuting, visitor flow and market deliveries.

Use: travel + demography + economy.

## 24. Rain on the Coast

After days of persistent rain, normally coastal Gastrodon are reported far outside their usual observation band. The reports are real but may be temporary dispersal rather than range expansion.

Use: meteorology + Pokémon ecology.

## 25. Saltwater Garden

A community garden near the marsh begins showing uneven plant stress. Soil, irrigation source, groundwater and tidal influence all need to be separated before blaming the estuary.

Use: food/agriculture + soil + freshwater.

## 26. The Quiet Tidal Creek

A creek normally full of movement at low tide becomes unusually quiet. The system generates a research opportunity from deviation against a known baseline rather than spawning an arbitrary quest giver.

Use: observation + soundscape + ecology.

## 27. The Marsh Edge Photograph

A historical photograph shows the boardwalk beside open water. Today the same landmark sits behind marsh vegetation. The image becomes a spatial record, not proof of one causal explanation.

Use: photography + archives + marsh-edge history.

## 28. The Wrong Side of the Levee

A restoration improves tidal exchange on one side of a control structure but unexpectedly raises concern about a freshwater supply on the other. Monitoring must determine whether the feared connection actually exists.

Use: conservation + freshwater + civic planning.

## 29. The Visitor Tide

A famous wildlife observation brings a sudden wave of visitors precisely during a sensitive tidal-window event. The problem is flow management, information and access, not banning tourism by default.

Use: tourism + media + conservation.

## 30. The Estuary Atlas

Players, fishers, scientists and residents slowly build a multi-edition atlas containing channels, tide windows, salinity profiles, wildlife observations, old structures and revised boundaries. No edition is treated as permanently final.

Use: long-term regional memory.

# Long arc A — Five Years at Greywater Estuary

Year 1: players encounter a simple boardwalk-access problem and help replace a broken salinity logger.

Year 2: fishers report a shift in productive water while Shellos observations also change. No common cause is assumed.

Year 3: an upstream freshwater project alters seasonal inflow. Monitoring shows some expected and some unexpected effects.

Year 4: a storm shifts the estuary mouth and damages low infrastructure. A restored marsh reduces some exposure but also begins migrating toward land designated for future development.

Year 5: institutions must update maps, transport, conservation zones and water-supply planning. The final state should preserve both improvements and unresolved tradeoffs.

Design goal: demonstrate slow causal accumulation without a mastermind or single boss.

# Long arc B — The Salt Line

Several coastal wells begin changing at different times. Public discussion blames sea-level change, a pumping project, a harbor expansion and a supernatural cause in parallel.

Players accumulate well records, estuary profiles, groundwater evidence, rainfall history and infrastructure state. The eventual explanation may involve more than one pathway.

The arc should end with a revised model and practical adaptation, not necessarily a dramatic confrontation.

# Long arc C — The Moving Marsh

A tidal wetland restoration starts as a success story.

Over several seasons:

- vegetation zones shift;
- nursery use changes;
- the marsh edge moves;
- visitors arrive;
- a road experiences more frequent closures;
- a proposed housing area overlaps with future migration space;
- historic land-use records complicate the debate.

Players can participate as researchers, residents, battlers during isolated encounters, club members, planners or conservation volunteers.

The arc tests whether Ouros can let a landscape change without reducing every outcome to good/bad reputation.

# Encounter contract 1 — Tidal Creek Crossing

## Narrative premise

A field team needs to cross a tidal creek while a wild group is using the same corridor. The objective may be to leave space, observe, withdraw or cross safely rather than win a battle.

## FULL version

Desired behaviors:

- water-depth zones change according to a server-owned tide state;
- legal paths change during the encounter;
- wildlife AI values exit/safe-side objectives;
- players can choose to hold position, retreat, protect or clear a route;
- any current/displacement effect comes only from verified PTU rules;
- transcript records environmental transitions semantically.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement if any current or blocking behavior is mechanical;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle when a real status is involved;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

## REDUCED version

The overworld resolves tide stage before battle. One validated geometry is frozen for the whole encounter. Wildlife crossing/withdrawal occurs as world state before or after a standard battle. No dynamic water, current, mud, salinity effect or environmental status is simulated by AutoPTU.

# Encounter contract 2 — Marsh Boardwalk Breach

## Narrative premise

A boardwalk section fails during a monitoring visit. Visitors need to leave while adjacent wild Pokémon react to the disturbance.

## FULL version

Desired behaviors:

- failed tiles become unavailable;
- evacuation routes exist as explicit objectives;
- moving noncombatants are protected by objective state;
- wildlife can withdraw rather than attack to KO;
- optional battle actions interact with a stable semantic map, not raw block scripting.

Dependencies with special weight:

- complete movement/interception/forced movement;
- terrain/hazards/zones/reactions;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback.

All ordinary combat families remain required for any actual battle actions.

## REDUCED version

Evacuation resolves in the overworld first. Broken boardwalk tiles become fixed blockers. A legal static encounter may occur on a stable section. Repair and ecological consequences are written back afterward.

# Encounter contract 3 — Salinity Station Recovery

## Narrative premise

A remote station stops transmitting during an argument over whether the estuary is changing.

## FULL version

This is primarily an exploration/investigation scenario. If combat occurs, a mature engine could support reach/withdraw objectives and a tide-frozen environmental snapshot.

Dependencies:

- targeting/LoS — only if battle occurs;
- base movement — only if battle occurs;
- complete movement — only for richer reach/withdraw execution;
- action economy/initiative — battle only;
- lifecycle/damage/status/moves/abilities/items/Features — according to actual combatants;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — only if environmental mechanics are explicitly authored and verified;
- AI legal actions — battle only;
- tactical AI — for objective-aware retreat;
- adapter/playback — for final Minecraft integration.

## REDUCED version

Travel, station repair and sampling are pure world-state actions. A battle, if triggered by actual encounter state, uses a static arena. The recovered readings update evidence and cannot apply PTU effects.

# Mechanical guardrails

- `wetland`, `marsh`, `mud` or `estuary` labels do not create terrain penalties.
- salinity does not cause HP loss, status, type change or Accuracy modifiers without exact authority.
- visible water depth does not determine Swim legality by itself.
- Stunfisk presence does not create Static on the environment.
- Storm Drain does not represent real hydrology.
- juvenile observations do not create Eggs/breeding records.
- tide manipulation must not become a deterministic rare-spawn exploit.
- Minecraft rendering never owns PTU current, drowning, mud or environmental-status rules.
