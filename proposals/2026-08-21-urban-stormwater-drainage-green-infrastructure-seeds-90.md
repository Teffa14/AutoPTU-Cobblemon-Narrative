# Ouros Urban Stormwater, Drainage & Green Infrastructure Seeds — Pass 90

Status: Non-canon proposals. Requires review before promotion.

These concepts use Pass 90 stormwater architecture. They do not define PTU mechanics, legal authority, engineering formulas, Pokémon ownership, environmental damage or canon settlements.

## 1. The Corner That Floods First

A single intersection ponds during short intense storms while neighboring streets drain normally. Residents blame an old inlet. Inspection eventually shows several plausible contributors: curb reconstruction, a partially blocked grate and a downstream pipe near capacity.

Useful connections: Architecture, Road Ecology, Meteorology, Stormwater, Public Works.

## 2. The Basin That Became a Pond

A detention basin that normally drains within a day remains wet for several weeks after a sequence of storms. Wild Pokémon begin using it repeatedly. Maintenance work is still needed, but the site has acquired a temporary ecological role.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Conservation, Wild Collectives, Observation.

## 3. The Dry Tunnel Window

An underground stormwater tunnel is safely accessible only during a documented dry-weather window. Researchers use it to inspect old city infrastructure and Pokémon traces. A forecast revision can cancel an expedition without any villain or combat.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Meteorology, Credentials, Subterranean, Science.

## 4. The New Roof Problem

A large redevelopment adds roof and paved area faster than the downstream drainage network is upgraded. Nothing fails immediately. Several ordinary storms over the next year create increasingly consistent evidence of changed runoff behavior.

Useful connections: Architecture, Demography, Public Works, Stormwater.

## 5. The Rain Garden Nobody Maintained

A celebrated streetscape project initially performs well. Years later, sediment and neglected vegetation reduce one function while the area still looks attractive. The correct story is maintenance and institutional memory, not corruption by default.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Flora, Workplaces, Public Memory.

## 6. The Outfall After Market Day

A visible plume appears at an outfall after a storm following a major market event. Packaging, street residue, construction sediment and ordinary runoff are all plausible contributors. Evidence must be gathered before any business or vendor is blamed.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Food/Markets, Waste, Cases, Science.

## 7. Three Drains, Three Causes

Three neighborhoods report similar ponding on the same night. One has an inlet blockage, one has a pump outage and one is affected by high river level at the downstream outfall.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Freshwater, Infrastructure, Media.

## 8. The Culvert That Is Fine for Water

A road culvert passes stormwater successfully but repeated surveys show poor wildlife passage. Engineers and ecologists are both correct about different functions of the same structure.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Road Ecology, Conservation.

## 9. The Old Overflow Map

A municipal map marks an emergency overflow route that no longer matches the rebuilt street geometry. The map was correct when published. A later storm exposes the mismatch.

Useful connections: Cartography, Architecture, Stormwater, Public Memory.

## 10. Under the Station Plaza

A busy transit plaza sits above a large storage chamber. Most residents never know it exists until maintenance opens the site. Historical records reveal several expansions tied to earlier decades of city growth.

Useful connections: Travel, Archives, Architecture, Stormwater.

## 11. The Pump That Only Fails at High River

A pump station appears reliable in routine testing. It struggles only when heavy local rain coincides with a high receiving river. The failure requires two systems to align.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Freshwater, Technology, Meteorology.

## 12. The Shelter in the Channel

A small group of Pokémon repeatedly uses a normally dry concrete channel for shelter. A seasonal maintenance program needs to decide when and how to work without assuming the Pokémon own the channel or must be removed permanently.

Useful connections: Wild Collectives, Conservation, Workplaces, Stormwater.

## 13. The Street That Redirected Rain

A resurfacing project changes curb height by a small amount. During later storms, runoff begins entering an older side street that rarely flooded before.

Useful connections: Architecture, Public Works, Stormwater, Cases.

## 14. The Overflow That Wasn't Sewage

Residents report foul-looking water after a storm and assume a sanitary overflow. Sampling and network records show the water came from sediment-rich storm runoff instead.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Waste/Sanitation, Science, Media.

## 15. The Overflow That Was Sewage

A different event looks similar but this time an authored cross-connection or combined-system state is actually confirmed. The story exists only where the network configuration is canonically established.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Sanitation, Health Surveillance, Cases.

## 16. Green Roof Year Five

A district's green-roof program is reviewed after five years. Some roofs still perform well, some were modified by occupants, and some lost maintenance access. A citywide policy produces uneven physical outcomes.

Useful connections: Architecture, Flora, Urban Heat, Stormwater, Workplaces.

## 17. The Basement That Never Flooded Before

An old building develops basement water problems despite no major change in annual rainfall. Investigation compares street redevelopment, groundwater, neighboring excavation and drainage routing rather than choosing one cause immediately.

Useful connections: Architecture, Groundwater, Stormwater, Geology.

## 18. The Basin Birding Boom

A stormwater basin becomes locally famous after repeated wildlife observations. Visitor pressure then begins interfering with inspections and habitat use.

Useful connections: Tourism, Conservation, Stormwater, Media.

## 19. The Inlet Crew Knows First

Maintenance workers notice the same unusual debris appearing in several inlets before anyone else connects the pattern. Their operational knowledge becomes valuable evidence.

Useful connections: Workplaces, Cases, Stormwater, Material Provenance.

## 20. The Missing Sensor Night

A stormwater level sensor stops reporting during the most important storm of the season. The missing data becomes a research problem; it does not prove sabotage or system failure.

Useful connections: Technology, Science, Stormwater, Cases.

## 21. The Park That Stores Water

A public green functions normally as recreation space but temporarily stores runoff during major storms. A festival organizer, parks staff and drainage staff all need the same place for different legitimate reasons.

Useful connections: Public Space, Events, Stormwater, Civic Governance.

## 22. The Tree Trench Debate

A street redesign proposes tree trenches that could support shade, infiltration and public-space improvement. Underground utilities and loading access complicate the plan.

Useful connections: Urban Heat, Architecture, Technology, Stormwater.

## 23. The Disappearing Alley Puddle

A puddle that residents remember from childhood stops forming after a drainage upgrade. Years later, old photos of children and Pokémon using it become part of neighborhood memory.

Useful connections: Public Memory, Photography, Urban Public Space, Stormwater.

## 24. Former Partner at the Basin

A released former partner Pokémon is photographed using a detention basin during wet weather. The observation is logged without inferring that it returned for its former Trainer.

Useful connections: Pokémon Agency, Photography, Stormwater.

## 25. The Basin That Drains Too Fast

A restored basin no longer holds water long enough to support a temporary habitat that had developed during years of poor maintenance. Better engineering performance creates a new ecological question.

Useful connections: Stormwater, Conservation, Public Works.

## 26. Night Market Downpour

A sudden downpour occurs during a night market. Vendors, pedestrians and staff are moved before any tactical conflict. Later review shows that temporary stalls obstructed one overland relief path.

Useful connections: Public Space, Events, Stormwater, Crisis.

## 27. The Hidden Connection

Two drainage zones thought to be separate respond simultaneously to storms. Historical plans suggest an undocumented connection from an older construction phase.

Useful connections: Archives, Architecture, Stormwater, Cartography.

## 28. The Basin Beneath the Schoolyard

A schoolyard retrofit stores water below the surface during storms. Students later help maintain an observation program for rainfall and basin recovery without receiving invented academic or PTU bonuses.

Useful connections: Education, Science, Stormwater.

## 29. First Storm After Construction

A new district opens during dry weather. Its first major storm becomes a commissioning event that tests assumptions about drains, roofs, streets, transit and public communication.

Useful connections: Architecture, Demography, Infrastructure, Stormwater.

## 30. The Stormwater Atlas

Over years, multiple institutions build a versioned atlas showing drains, surface changes, flood observations, green infrastructure, wildlife use and known uncertainty. Each edition remains historically valid for its time.

Useful connections: Cartography, Archives, Science, Stormwater.

# Long-form arc A: Five Storms in Meridian Ward

Year 1: a short storm creates unexpected street ponding.
Year 2: redevelopment changes surface routing.
Year 3: a detention project opens.
Year 4: wildlife begins using the basin and maintenance practices change.
Year 5: a larger storm tests the revised network.

The arc can end with a better-understood district rather than a perfect flood-proof city.

# Long-form arc B: Beneath the Old City

Players gradually discover an underground network assembled across different construction eras. Some reaches are storm drains, some are abandoned channels, some connect to older infrastructure and some maps are wrong because later work changed them. Wildlife use, maintenance access and historical records become as important as dungeon exploration.

The central mystery can remain infrastructural and historical without requiring a hidden villain.

# Long-form arc C: The Green Streets Program

Several neighborhoods adopt different runoff-management projects. Years later the outcomes diverge because of soil, maintenance, canopy, utilities, public use and weather history. The player can help compare results and revise policy without the game reducing the program to one upgrade tree.
