# Pass 89 Proposals — Island Biogeography, Endemism & Dispersal

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Requires human review before promotion.

These concepts derive from the Pass 89 research layer and must preserve all PTU/Caelo and implementation guardrails.

## 1. The Island Where They Were Never Gone

A species thought locally extirpated is photographed again. Old surveys looked only at accessible lowlands. The new observation comes from a steep inland habitat never sampled well.

Use:
- science;
- cartography;
- public-memory correction;
- conservation;
- no automatic 'miracle return' framing.

## 2. The First Arrival That Wasn't

A population appears on a small island after a storm. Museum notes later reveal a vague century-old record of the same species.

Question:
new colonization, recolonization or bad historical documentation?

## 3. Three Islands, One Species, Three Schedules

The same species feeds at dawn on one island, at midday on another and mostly at dusk on a third.

The difference may reflect predators, resources, tourism, climate or survey bias.

Do not invent forms or stat changes.

## 4. The Empty Stepping Stone

A tiny island normally used during a seasonal crossing has no confirmed visitors this year.

The problem may lie on the stepping stone, the source island, the destination island, weather timing or observation coverage.

## 5. A Ferry Route Becomes an Ecological Hypothesis

A species begins appearing on islands connected by one ferry line.

The ferry is only a candidate pathway. Cargo, natural dispersal, storms and old undetected populations remain alternatives.

## 6. The Wrong Endemic

A famous guidebook calls a Pokémon population 'found nowhere else.' A new survey on a remote neighboring island finds what may be the same population.

The story becomes a scientific correction rather than a scandal by default.

## 7. The Last Known Meadow

One island supports the last confirmed patch of a resource used by a local Pokémon population.

The conservation value belongs to the habitat/resource relationship, not to an invented combat buff.

## 8. The Island That Only Matters for One Week

A nearly barren rock becomes a seasonal roost during a narrow migration window.

Outside that period it appears ecologically unimportant to casual visitors.

## 9. The Population That Returned by Itself

Years after habitat restoration, a species recolonizes naturally without a release program.

The correct response is monitoring, not claiming that the restoration project 'created' the population.

## 10. Two Maps of the Same Archipelago

A scientific atlas and a fishermen's chart show different 'important islands.'

Both are correct for their purposes: one maps breeding populations, the other temporary stopovers and safe crossings.

## 11. The Storm Passenger

After an extreme weather event, a single persistent Pokémon is found on an island outside its known range.

One displaced individual does not establish a population.

## 12. The Island With Too Many First Records

A newly funded research station reports many 'first island records.'

The pattern may reflect better survey effort rather than abrupt ecological change.

## 13. The Old Orchard Population

A species now associated with an abandoned orchard may descend from a historical introduction, natural colonization or both.

The investigation crosses Archives, Biosecurity, Flora and island occupancy history.

## 14. The Channel That Narrows Every Winter

Seasonal conditions make two islands functionally more connected during part of the year.

The server tracks the crossing window as world state. It does not automatically grant Swim or Sky travel to individual Pokémon.

## 15. The Island With No Harbor

A small island has high ecological connectivity for flying/marine Pokémon but low human accessibility.

This creates research and rescue challenges without equating human isolation with ecological isolation.

## 16. The Public Wants a Mascot

A town begins marketing a supposedly endemic population before the range assessment is complete.

Tourism, science, conservation and public memory may diverge without any actor being malicious.

## 17. The Population With a Different Call

Recordings suggest the same species vocalizes differently on one island.

Possible explanations include learned behavior, acoustics, social structure or deeper differentiation.

The narrative system stores the observation. It does not create a new form.

## 18. The Recolonization Nobody Saw

Repeated surveys jump from 'absent' to 'several individuals present.'

The missing part of the history becomes an investigation into survey gaps and dispersal routes.

## 19. The Island Refuge

A population disappears from larger inhabited islands but persists on one remote island.

Historical records suggest the remote site may have functioned as refuge rather than original center of distribution.

## 20. The Broken Chain

A small stepping-stone habitat is lost after a storm.

Neither major population disappears immediately, but future observations show reduced exchange and altered timing.

## 21. The Assisted Colonization Proposal

Researchers propose moving a highly restricted population to another island because its habitat is deteriorating.

The proposal must pass through Conservation, Biosecurity, custody, ethics and long-term monitoring. No outcome is predetermined.

## 22. The Island Form Rumor

Players repeatedly hear that an isolated population is a 'new regional form.'

Evidence instead shows only a behavioral or visual difference that has not been mechanically recognized.

The rumor can remain culturally important without becoming species truth.

## 23. The Former Partner Across the Channel

A released former partner is later observed on another island.

The event proves movement, not motive. The former Trainer gains no automatic command authority.

## 24. The Island That Gains a Population After a Port Closes

A human transport route shuts down. Surprisingly, a wild population becomes more common afterward.

Possible explanation: reduced disturbance rather than loss of dispersal.

## 25. The Rare Species That Was Common Somewhere Else

A Pokémon considered extremely rare on the main island is abundant on a small outer island.

Rarity remains spatially scoped. Encounter rarity should not be a global species property.

## 26. The Hidden Source Island

A population appears repeatedly after storms, but the presumed source island never contains enough individuals to explain the arrivals.

A smaller unsurveyed island may be the real source.

## 27. The Island Where the Survey Method Fails

A method that works well elsewhere systematically misses a nocturnal, canopy or cave-associated population on one island.

The correction changes scientific confidence without changing ecology retroactively.

## 28. The Old Crossing Returns

Restoration of a tiny freshwater source makes an island usable again as a seasonal stopover.

The chain is:
restoration -> resource returns -> observations change -> repeated use -> connectivity assessment revision.

## 29. The Same Pokémon, Different Food Web

The same species occupies similar habitats on two islands but interacts with different prey, plants or competitors.

This becomes a cross-island ecology project rather than a claim of new stats or typing.

## 30. The Archipelago Atlas

A multi-year project builds successive editions of island occupancy, routes, habitats and uncertainty.

Each edition preserves what was known at the time.

# Long arc A — Five Years Across Nine Islands

Year 1:
Players help establish comparable surveys across several islands.

Year 2:
Some populations appear stable while others are seasonal or poorly detected.

Year 3:
A storm creates several unusual observations and one probable natural colonization.

Year 4:
One stepping-stone site deteriorates while another is restored.

Year 5:
The region publishes a revised biogeographic atlas that changes conservation priorities and travel/research opportunities.

No single villain is required.

# Long arc B — The Species With Four Histories

Four island populations of one species have different local stories:
- one appears native and long-established;
- one may be a historical human introduction;
- one is seasonal;
- one may be a recent recolonization.

The campaign gradually reconstructs those histories using archives, surveys, behavior, biosecurity evidence and public memory.

The ending may preserve uncertainty.

# Long arc C — The Missing Stepping Stones

Several small islands that once linked larger populations stop functioning as reliable stopovers.

Possible causes differ by island:
- freshwater loss;
- coastal erosion;
- tourism pressure;
- invasive plants;
- storm damage;
- simple survey failure.

Restoring regional connectivity requires different solutions rather than one generic 'fix the habitat' quest.

# Encounter Contract 1 — Stepping-Stone Survey

Narrative premise:
Researchers need to verify whether a small island is still used during a seasonal movement window.

Reduced version:
- survey timing and arrivals resolve in world state;
- visitors/research staff remain outside AutoPTU unless directly involved;
- if battle occurs, use a fixed legal arena;
- observations after battle update the survey, not the population automatically.

Full version dependencies:
- complete movement including interception/forced movement if groups cross the arena;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if surf/wind/access change tactically;
- AI tactical policy for WITHDRAW/REACH_EXIT/PROTECT_SITE;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

# Encounter Contract 2 — Recolonization Shore

Narrative premise:
A population absent from records for years is detected again.

Reduced version:
- observation and population inference occur outside battle;
- battle, if any, uses static geometry;
- no battle result proves establishment or endemism.

Full version dependencies:
- complete movement for escape/withdrawal objectives;
- tactical AI that values leaving or protecting young/site use rather than KO;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if shoreline state matters;
- adapter/playback.

# Encounter Contract 3 — Channel Crossing Window

Narrative premise:
A recurring seasonal condition creates a short inter-island movement opportunity.

Reduced version:
- Seasonality/Open Ocean resolves whether the window exists;
- movement between islands remains world state;
- any combat occurs on a frozen snapshot before or after crossing.

Full version dependencies:
- complete movement;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- AI tactical policy for CROSS/WITHDRAW/PROTECT;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

# Mechanical guardrails

Do not invent:
- inter-island travel range;
- Swim/Sky endurance;
- migration Speed;
- island encounter bonuses;
- endemism bonuses;
- local-form stats;
- regional evolution triggers;
- capture restrictions;
- automatic Weather;
- stepping-stone buffs;
- group movement rules;
- colonization rolls;
- local-extinction dice;
- rare-spawn multipliers.

All concrete battle mechanics require PTU/Caelo authority plus matching current implementation evidence.
