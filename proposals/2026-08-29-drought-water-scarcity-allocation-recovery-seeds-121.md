# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Drought, Water Scarcity, Allocation & Recovery — Pass 121

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-29

These seeds use the Pass 121 continuity model without establishing a specific Ouros drought region, water authority, reservoir, aquifer, law, species role or scarcity history.

## Situation seeds

### It Rained, the Well Did Not Recover

A storm ends weeks of dry weather, but one settlement well remains low. Residents argue that the emergency should be over because rain has returned. The useful conflict is lag and evidence: weather improved, one source did not.

### The Reservoir Is Low, the Clinic Still Has Water

Visitors assume a low reservoir means every service is failing. The clinic continues operating through a separately verified source or temporary arrangement. The story demonstrates that source condition, service availability and institutional preparedness are linked but independent.

### The Old Road Came Back

Falling water reveals an earlier road alignment beneath a reservoir margin. Maps, property records and oral histories disagree about when it was abandoned. The exposed road creates an exploration and provenance problem before it creates any ownership or access right.

### The Market Changed Its Morning

A temporary allocation schedule shifts when vendors clean, cook, receive deliveries and open. Months later the schedule becomes a cultural habit even after supply improves.

### The Pokémon Returned Before the Stream

An individual or documented group resumes using a valley before visible surface flow returns. Researchers, residents and guides offer different interpretations. The observation matters, but it does not prove groundwater recovery or species-level drought sensing.

### The Drought Map Was Correct Somewhere Else

A regional assessment and a village report appear to conflict. The regional product described a larger geographic scope while the village relied on one spring and one well. Both can be accurate for their subjects.

### The Temporary Tank Became a Meeting Place

A canon-approved temporary water point becomes a regular social node. When it is scheduled for removal, the debate concerns the new public-space function rather than the original emergency purpose.

### The Farm Recovered Last

Household supply and town services return to ordinary operation, but a nearby agricultural system remains constrained for another season. Recovery therefore creates political and social pressure after the visible crisis seems finished.

### The Lake Exposed a Marker Nobody Recognizes

A low shoreline reveals a durable marker. Older residents remember a different name; an archive uses another location reference. The mystery connects cartography, public memory and possibly archaeology without assuming treasure or conspiracy.

### Two Causes Were True

A long dry period reduced source availability while a neglected conveyance problem worsened one district's shortage. The investigation closes with mixed causation instead of a single villain.

## NPC and faction candidates

A long-serving water-system operator knows which observations belong to which source but does not have authority over civic allocation.

A farmer keeps generational notebooks on springs, planting dates and animal behavior. The records are useful evidence with local bias and incomplete coverage, not omniscient truth.

A conservation worker tracks shrinking habitat use and argues that ecological recovery should not be inferred from human service restoration.

A market organizer rebuilt daily routines around temporary supply windows and now resists returning to the old schedule.

A young archivist recognizes that newly exposed foundations correspond to a retired settlement map rather than a hidden ruin.

An individual Pokémon has a documented work role locating leaks, carrying sealed supplies or assisting monitoring only if canon later establishes training and capability evidence for that actor.

## Mystery: Five Times the Drought “Ended”

Five dates circulate publicly. They refer to first significant rainfall, removal of one restriction, recovery of a major reservoir intake, closure of a temporary supply point and formal closure of the scarcity episode.

Resolution requires mapping each statement to its subject. None of the records needs to be fraudulent.

## Mystery: Three Dry Wells, Two Causes

Three nearby wells fail during the same season. Two share one aquifer response; the third has a separate local infrastructure problem. The case teaches that temporal coincidence does not prove one mechanism.

## Exploration: The Road Under the Reservoir

A low-water period exposes part of an abandoned route, foundations and old signs. The playable version uses photographs, maps, archive records, oral histories and a stable surveyed access strip.

Current implementation requires only static navigation and provenance. Unstable mud, sudden water return, collapsing banks or dynamic weather remain outside the reduced version.

## Longer arc: A Valley Learns What Recovery Means

Phase one establishes ordinary water use. Households, farms, habitats, businesses, tourism and public facilities depend on different sources and schedules.

Early signals then appear unevenly. One spring weakens, a stream changes, a reservoir edge retreats and residents disagree about whether the pattern is unusual. Monitoring coverage is incomplete.

Institutions adopt temporary arrangements and different restrictions. Some are inconvenient but effective. Others create new social geography. Several popular explanations compete until evidence improves.

Rain eventually returns, but recovery is staggered. Drinking-water service improves before some farms. One habitat remains stressed. Groundwater observations lag. A temporary pickup point becomes a community fixture. Exposed historical features generate their own side stories.

A later dry year can reuse all of that institutional memory, infrastructure history and local knowledge without resetting the region to a generic drought state.

## Encounter: Reservoir Margin Withdrawal

Full intent: noncombatants withdraw from a restricted shoreline while conflict unfolds. Dependencies include complete movement including interception/forced movement, lifecycle if withdrawal timing matters, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if shoreline cells or mud matter tactically, AI tactical policy for PROTECT/WITHDRAW and adapter/playback for semantic movement.

Reduced form: withdrawal and closure happen before BattleSpec. Only stable inspected terrain is used. Success secures the immediate perimeter and does not alter water state or access authorization elsewhere.

## Encounter: Exposed Causeway Perimeter

Full intent: conflict occurs around a newly exposed route with changing or unsafe cells. This depends on terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, complete movement where displacement matters, lifecycle for phased changes, exact damage/status rules for any environmental consequence, objective-aware AI and semantic playback.

Reduced form: a surveyed stable strip is authored before battle; all uncertain cells are excluded. The fight is conventional. Victory does not authenticate the causeway, grant public access or decide preservation.

## Encounter: Temporary Water Point Diversion

Full intent: civilians are actively rerouted while a conflict threatens access. Dependencies include complete movement, generalized reactions, lifecycle, AI tactical policy and playback. Any water custody, queue, capacity or service decision remains world state.

Reduced form: civilians and controlled supplies move before BattleSpec. AutoPTU resolves a static route-clearing encounter. Success clears that local obstruction only.

## Canon questions intentionally open

Where do recurring dry periods occur? Which settlements rely on springs, wells, reservoirs, canals or imported supply? Who has authority to allocate water? Which uses receive priority? What historical droughts shaped migration or infrastructure? Which temporary arrangements became permanent? Which Pokémon individuals have documented roles?

None of these proposals silently answer those questions.
