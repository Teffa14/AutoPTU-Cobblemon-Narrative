# Ouros Astronomy & Observatory Seeds — Pass 90

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-28

These candidates demonstrate the astronomy/observatory continuity extension without establishing any region, institution, technology, constellation, celestial body or cosmology as Ouros canon.

## Design intent

The emphasis is repeated observation, incomplete evidence, public/scientific interpretation, field follow-up and persistent places. Celestial events create opportunities and questions; they do not automatically create supernatural answers, Legendary encounters, tactical Weather or meteor hazards.

## The Night the Telescope Saw Nothing

An institution publicly predicts a strong observing window. The site opens, staff prepare, and the sky appears reasonably clear. The expected target is not detected.

Useful state:
- expected window remains historically recorded;
- instrument calibration is reviewed;
- outside observers may have independent reports;
- public disappointment and scientific value can coexist;
- the next observing window changes because of the non-detection.

Possible explanations remain open:
- the prediction was wrong;
- the event occurred outside the observed region;
- one instrument had a problem;
- the target was below the effective horizon;
- outside reports concern a different event;
- evidence remains insufficient.

No answer is forced merely because the quest needs closure.

## Public Night, Closed Dome

A scheduled public skywatch proceeds even though the main observing dome is unavailable after a maintenance issue.

The institution adapts with:
- outdoor interpretation;
- smaller instruments if available;
- historical images;
- guided naked-eye observation when culturally and technically appropriate;
- a reduced scientific program.

The public event can still be successful while the research session is degraded or cancelled.

This creates visible institutional resilience without pretending a broken asset still functions.

## The Old Ephemeris Is Off

A long-used prediction table no longer matches recent observations as closely as expected.

Gameplay:
- compare old editions and handwritten corrections;
- inspect who produced each revision;
- reconstruct which observations fed the table;
- check whether the discrepancy is systematic or limited to one subject;
- publish or circulate a correction if evidence supports it.

Possible outcomes:
- transcription error;
- obsolete method;
- wrong local time conversion;
- observation-site changes;
- genuine unexplained deviation;
- insufficient data.

The old table remains part of historical state even after correction.

## The Radio Detection With No Visible Counterpart

A specialist instrument records a signal during a night when visual observers report nothing unusual.

The investigation separates:
- equipment state;
- interference sources;
- exact timestamps;
- other stations or observers;
- weather and visibility;
- scientific interpretation.

The signal can remain unidentified for months. It does not automatically imply a Pokémon, spacecraft, dimensional anomaly or supernatural event.

Exact instrument technology remains subject to canon review.

## The Shooting Star That Became Three Reports

Three settlements report a bright transient event at apparently similar times. Public discussion treats them as one spectacular meteor.

Reconciliation may reveal:
- one event seen from three places;
- two separate streaks;
- one sky event plus a local electrical flash;
- timestamps rounded too coarsely to know;
- one report copied from another witness rather than independently observed.

The quest rewards provenance reconstruction rather than a predetermined cosmic revelation.

## The Town Changed Its Lights

Observers document that a growing nighttime lighting pattern is reducing useful sky visibility from an old observing site.

Astronomy can provide evidence. It cannot order the town to change infrastructure.

Potential stakeholders:
- residents concerned with safety or access;
- businesses using evening lighting;
- astronomers;
- wildlife researchers;
- event organizers;
- civic/infrastructure actors.

Possible outcomes:
- directional shielding;
- limited observing-night adjustments;
- moving some sessions to a secondary site;
- no change because other priorities win;
- a pilot program that later produces new evidence.

No custom `light pollution meter` grants Skills or battle penalties.

## The Amateur Saw It First

A local hobby observer records an unusual transient before the institutional system notices it.

The institution must decide how to:
- preserve the original timestamp;
- credit the observer;
- verify the record;
- compare it with later data;
- communicate uncertainty publicly.

The amateur's lack of institutional status does not make the observation false. Institutional acceptance does not make the interpretation infallible.

## Same Stars, Different Constellation

Two communities use different constellation mappings for the same visible region of sky.

A public exhibition or educational project initially tries to place both systems on one standardized diagram and creates confusion.

The better solution may preserve:
- both mappings;
- their distinct mythic claims;
- historical versions;
- scientific coordinate cross-references;
- who uses which naming system today.

The story explores translation and cultural continuity without deciding that one tradition is the objectively correct way to divide the sky.

## The Meteor Everyone Saw, Nobody Recovered

A bright event is witnessed by many people. Analysis narrows a possible ground-search corridor. Several searches find nothing that can be authenticated as related material.

The unresolved result persists.

Months later:
- erosion may expose a candidate object;
- another collector may present a rock with questionable provenance;
- improved trajectory analysis may shift the search area;
- the original assumption that material reached the ground may be revised.

The world does not conjure the missing meteorite into a chest.

## The Meteorite in the Wrong Collection

A museum or private collection contains an object catalogued decades ago as material from a famous celestial fall.

New review finds that:
- the collection date fits;
- the location label is ambiguous;
- chain of custody has a gap;
- old photographs show a different storage container;
- material analysis may or may not support the historical claim.

This connects Astronomy, Archives, Material Culture and Geology.

The resolution can remain `provenance uncertain`.

## Clouded-Out Observation

A school, club or community group travels to an observing site for a rare scheduled event. Clouds prevent direct viewing.

The evening can still create:
- relationships with staff;
- oral-history interviews about earlier events;
- maintenance/help tasks;
- data review from another station;
- a later invitation;
- public memory of an infamous failed observing night.

Failure to see the sky target is not failure to produce gameplay.

## The Observatory Without a Telescope

An old observing platform predates the current research institution and may have served calendar, navigation, ritual or community purposes.

Current actors disagree about its historical use.

Evidence can include:
- orientation;
- old records;
- sight lines;
- later alterations;
- oral tradition;
- nearby archaeological context.

The site should be handed to Archaeology/Myth for historical interpretation. Astronomy only contributes current sky-reference observations.

No ancient alignment proves supernatural knowledge.

## Four Images, Three Events

Mystery structure.

Four photographs from a single night are catalogued as four separate celestial events.

Investigation finds that:
- two images may capture the same track from different exposures;
- one image has a clock that was not synchronized;
- one may show a reflection or unrelated light;
- weather records leave a gap during part of the night.

Possible resolution:
- three distinct event candidates;
- two;
- four;
- unresolved.

The goal is source reconciliation rather than assigning a hidden truth score.

## Two Predictions, One Public Poster

An observing institution revises its event window, but a public poster still displays the older time.

Consequences:
- some visitors arrive early;
- others miss the useful period;
- staff believe the updated notice propagated when it did not;
- public complaints focus on the institution while the underlying scientific revision was correct.

This connects Astronomy, Communications and Public Notices without merging their authority.

## The Field Search That Finds Something Else

A team follows a meteor-fall search lead and instead discovers:
- an older geological specimen;
- evidence of historic land use;
- a wildlife monitoring device;
- unrelated debris with provenance implications;
- a new habitat observation.

The mission remains productive even if the intended meteor material is absent.

The discovered object or observation transfers to its owning system.

## Long arc — An Observatory Learns the Sky

Visit 1 establishes ordinary staff, instruments, night routines and public access.

Visit 2 introduces a modest prediction discrepancy rather than a crisis.

Visit 3 brings outside observations that force the institution to revisit calibration, archival records or assumptions.

Visit 4 uses a public observing event to expose operational pressure such as lighting, crowding, maintenance or communications.

Visit 5 produces a strong transient detection and a provisional field-search lead.

Visit 6 shows the limits of that lead: the field team may recover nothing or find material with uncertain provenance.

Visit 7 returns months later. The institution now has changed procedures, revised public material, better cross-site collaboration and one unresolved question that predates the players.

The institution develops through visible practices and records rather than an abstract progression level.

## Encounter — Ridge Observatory Withdrawal

Status: mechanically rich candidate.

Narrative premise:
A public or research observing night is interrupted by a local threat while people occupy an exposed high site.

Intended full version:
- visitors and staff move toward several safe exits;
- some access routes may become tactically relevant;
- Intercept/forced movement matters near restricted edges;
- environmental visibility/weather can influence battle only with authoritative support;
- autonomous opponents can prefer territorial withdrawal, route denial or escape;
- exact semantic events play back through Minecraft/Cobblemon.

Permanent capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced version:
The session is stopped first. Staff, visitors and fragile equipment are removed from the tactical state. Unsafe edges become static excluded geometry. Ouros selects combatants explicitly and AutoPTU runs an ordinary reviewed arena. Darkness, cloud, wind and sky effects stay presentational. Facility reopening is decided afterward.

## Encounter — Meteor-Fall Search Perimeter

Status: mechanically rich candidate.

Narrative premise:
A field team follows a provisional ground-search region derived from sky observations and encounters a separate local threat.

Intended full version:
- CLEAR_ROUTE or WITHDRAW intent;
- protected field-work zones;
- dynamic terrain/hazards only when authoritative;
- territorial or escape-aware AI;
- possible forced displacement around unstable ground;
- reviewed object interaction outside ordinary damage targeting;
- exact adapter playback.

Dependencies:
Same permanent capability families as Ridge Observatory Withdrawal, with especially strong dependency on terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, tactical AI and adapter support.

Reduced version:
Surveyors leave first. The suspected impact core is closed as world state. Any physical candidate object stays outside the tactical grid. AutoPTU resolves a static encounter on safe terrain. Survey resumes afterward. Winning does not create, authenticate or transfer a meteorite.

## Noncombat encounter — Amateur Observation Reconciliation

Status: runnable as narrative/investigation world state.

Several observers report a transient from different places.

Actions can include:
- checking timestamps;
- comparing sky direction or region;
- checking photographs;
- reviewing cloud windows;
- identifying copied testimony;
- checking instrument logs;
- preserving unresolved reports.

No tactical mechanics are required.
