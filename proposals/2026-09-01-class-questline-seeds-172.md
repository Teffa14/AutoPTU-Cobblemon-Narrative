# Interconnected PTU Class Questline Seeds — Pass 172

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS

These are original Ouros candidates built from existing project layers and the Pass 172 research scan. They are not PTU rules and do not establish canon factions or rewards.

## Shared World Arc Candidate — The Season of Empty Shelves

Premise: several settlements begin reporting shortages that appear unrelated: a kitchen cannot source a staple berry, a clinic cannot replace a common restorative ingredient, a route hostel changes its menu, and a research station reports declining yields in one watershed.

The event remains one shared world-state problem. Different class arcs expose different parts of it.

### Chef thread — The Dish That Changed Twice

Early episode:
A local kitchen asks the player to reproduce a regional dish after one ingredient becomes unavailable.

Middle pressure:
Substitutions become culturally contentious because an older version of the recipe preserved in a library differs from current practice.

Advanced dilemma:
A supplier offers a reliable substitute that would stabilize service but displace a small local producer.

Persistent outputs:
- recipe-history knowledge;
- supplier relationships;
- kitchen reputation;
- changed menu/service state;
- possible preservation of an endangered culinary practice.

Cross-class edges:
Researcher, Chronicler, Survivalist, Medic, Commander.

Mechanical note:
No food buff, Chef Feature effect or Item behavior is granted by narrative completion. Exact Chef mechanics require current authoritative build and Item/Feature support.

### Researcher thread — The Yield Curve Nobody Expected

Early episode:
Collect field observations from several sites that disagree about the cause of declining yields.

Middle pressure:
Different institutions clean and interpret the same data differently.

Advanced dilemma:
An early public explanation is politically convenient but weakly supported; waiting for better evidence has real costs.

Persistent outputs:
- dataset versions;
- competing claims;
- institutional trust/conflict;
- revised ecological knowledge.

Cross-class edges:
Chronicler, Druid, Chef, Survivalist, Medic.

### Survivalist thread — The Route That Still Works

Early episode:
Confirm which remote collection routes remain viable after local conditions change.

Middle pressure:
A route known to experienced travelers is safe only under specific seasonal conditions.

Advanced dilemma:
Opening a more reliable path may increase pressure on a fragile wild population.

Persistent outputs:
- route knowledge;
- access state;
- relationships with remote communities;
- ecological pressure hooks.

Cross-class edges:
Backpacker, Capture Specialist, Researcher, Druid.

### Chronicler thread — Three Famines That Were Not the Same

Early episode:
Locate records of previous shortages.

Middle pressure:
Public memory compresses several different historical events into one story.

Advanced dilemma:
Correcting the record weakens a faction's preferred explanation but also reveals a past institutional failure.

Persistent outputs:
- source provenance;
- public-memory conflict;
- revised historical interpretation;
- new contacts in libraries/archives.

Cross-class edges:
Researcher, Chef, Commander.

### Commander thread — Who Gets the Last Shipment

Early episode:
Coordinate delivery priority after several institutions request the same scarce supply.

Middle pressure:
Different settlements have legitimate but incompatible needs.

Advanced dilemma:
A temporary allocation policy may solve the immediate crisis while creating a durable political grievance.

Persistent outputs:
- institutional relationships;
- allocation decisions;
- public memory;
- faction pressure.

Mechanical note:
Narrative leadership does not grant Orders or Commander mechanics. If combat uses Orders, Trainer Features/perks and lifecycle must be audited.

## Shared Faction Arc Candidate — The Field Service Compact

Premise: several independent local organizations maintain routes, rescue services, field observation posts and emergency supply caches. A proposal emerges to formalize them into a regional compact.

This is deliberately not yet a canon Ranger organization.

Possible internal constituencies:
- local route wardens;
- researchers;
- rescue volunteers;
- couriers;
- community representatives;
- battle-trained responders;
- ecological advocates.

Conflicts:
- central standards vs local autonomy;
- emergency authority;
- funding and equipment custody;
- data sharing;
- capture ethics;
- who may speak for a settlement;
- whether battle competence should determine leadership.

Class affinities:
Capture Specialist, Survivalist, Backpacker, Commander, Medic, Researcher, Chronicler, Rider.

Class tensions:
Hunter may conflict with conservation priorities; Commander may conflict with local autonomy; Capture Specialist may disagree with researchers about intervention thresholds; Chronicler may contest secrecy policies.

Participation lanes:
member, contractor, outsider, investigator, critic, trainer, researcher, mediator, former member.

## Capture Specialist Arc Candidate — The Difference Between Catching and Taking

Stage 1 — A clean capture
A community asks for help safely removing one aggressive wild Pokémon from a high-traffic area.

Stage 2 — The returning population
Repeated incidents reveal that capture is treating a symptom rather than the cause.

Stage 3 — The disputed boundary
A land-use change has altered where a wild collective can move safely.

Stage 4 — The collector's offer
A private buyer offers resources in exchange for specimens that are legal to capture but ecologically important.

Stage 5 — The specialist's reputation
Different groups begin invoking the player's prior actions as evidence for or against future interventions.

Long-form question:
What does expertise in capture obligate the specialist to understand before acting?

Cross-class intersections:
Researcher provides evidence; Survivalist reads the landscape; Chronicler tracks prior incidents; Commander coordinates public access; Druid may frame ecological stakes; Hunter may agree on tracking while disagreeing on intervention.

Engine dependencies:
Full capture scenes may require exact capture mechanics, targeting/range/LoS, movement, Items, Trainer Features, status lifecycle and tactical policy. Reduced variants can separate investigation and world-state choice from a conventional audited battle/capture event.

## Ace Trainer Arc Candidate — The Team You Are Building

Stage 1 — The easy improvement
A respected trainer offers a simple training plan that produces visible short-term results.

Stage 2 — The partner who does not fit
One Pokémon on the team develops differently from the plan's assumptions.

Stage 3 — Competing schools
Two training institutions interpret the same competitive weakness differently.

Stage 4 — The inherited method
A mentor asks the player to teach a junior trainer using methods the player now questions.

Stage 5 — The public test
A formal battle makes the team's development visible, but the outcome alone does not answer whether the training philosophy was sound.

Long-form question:
Is mastery the ability to impose a system consistently, or to change the system for the individuals being trained?

Cross-class intersections:
Mentor, Style Expert, Stat Aces, Chronicler, Medic, Commander.

## Mentor Arc Candidate — The Student Who Outgrows the Lesson

Stage 1:
Help an inexperienced NPC achieve a concrete goal.

Stage 2:
The student begins copying the player's methods too literally.

Stage 3:
A second mentor offers conflicting advice.

Stage 4:
The student succeeds using an approach the player would not choose.

Stage 5:
The player must decide whether the relationship remains teacher/student, becomes peer-like, or ends.

Hard boundary:
No social resolution automatically grants Mentor Features, Skills or Edges.

## Coordinator + Dancer + Fashionista Intersection Arc — The Festival That Changed Its Audience

World fact:
A long-running regional performance event expands after broadcast coverage increases attendance.

Coordinator perspective:
Program structure, judging expectations, performer adaptation.

Dancer perspective:
Technique, rehearsal, tradition, physical performance and transmission between generations.

Fashionista perspective:
Costume provenance, material supply, presentation, imitation and cultural ownership claims.

Chronicler perspective:
How older performances are remembered and reinterpreted.

Cheerleader perspective:
Supporter groups and audience culture.

Possible faction conflict:
organizers, local performers, broadcasters, merchants and heritage advocates all want different things from the same event.

## Chronicler + Duelist Arc Candidate — The Rival Everyone Studied Wrong

Premise:
A successful competitor develops a public reputation for one signature tactic. Replays and records show that the reputation is incomplete.

Chronicler lane:
build a reliable technique/profile archive and separate public narrative from observed behavior.

Duelist lane:
prepare for a specific opponent while adapting when the expected pattern fails.

Shared payoff:
the rival becomes a persistent character rather than a one-battle obstacle.

Engine dependencies:
Conventional audited battle may be reduced-version READY. Full advanced replay-aware opponent policy remains dependent on AI tactical policy and verified move/ability/item/feature behavior.

## Multiclass Example — Chef / Researcher / Survivalist / Commander

The character enters `The Season of Empty Shelves` with four current classes.

The system may expose:
- culinary substitution choices;
- evidence collection;
- field-route options;
- allocation/coordination decisions.

The player may combine these approaches in one episode.

If the player later respecs and removes Chef:
- the kitchen still remembers them;
- prior menu changes remain;
- recipe knowledge remains if actually acquired;
- future Chef-mechanical actions are revalidated against the current build;
- Chef-only future eligibility may close while historical follow-up quests remain available.
