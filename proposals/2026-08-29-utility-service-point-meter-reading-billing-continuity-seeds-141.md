## The Meter Was Replaced Before the Storm

Premise:

A neighborhood experiences a short physical outage. After restoration, one business disputes a later utility record because the device serial shown in the newest documentation differs from older photographs.

Hidden structure:

The meter exchange happened before the storm. The outage did not cause the serial change. The old device's final observation, the new device's initial observation, the outage interval and the later bill all have different timestamps.

Possible player work:

- compare field photos;
- recover a meter-exchange notice;
- confirm which service point both devices belonged to;
- compare the outage timeline with the exchange timeline;
- leave the exact consumption question unresolved if no governing calculation exists.

Consequence:

The business learns that a genuine discrepancy can still have a mundane explanation. A later correction may be needed, but no one must be framed as dishonest.

Battle requirement: none.

## The Street Changed Name, the Service Point Did Not

Premise:

Two utility records appear to refer to different properties because one uses a former street name and one uses the current standardized name.

The building, endpoint and service point never moved.

Useful owners:

- Place Reference for address/name continuity;
- Utility Continuity for service-point identity;
- Archives for historical documents;
- Finance only if a monetary correction follows.

Possible resolution:

The records are linked for the relevant scope while both retain their original historical labels.

Battle requirement: none.

## Three Estimates and One Actual Reading

Premise:

A small workshop has three consecutive periods recorded from estimates. A later field observation produces a value that causes a large adjustment.

Narrative tension comes from uncertainty rather than guilt.

Questions:

- Why were observations unavailable?
- Was the device inaccessible, faulty, delayed or merely not transmitted?
- Which estimates were accepted at the time?
- Which later reading actually applies to which device association?
- Does the correction change one period or several?

A valid ending may be partial reconciliation rather than perfect certainty.

Battle requirement: none.

## Two Houses, One Old Label

Premise:

A property was physically divided years ago. An old exterior utility label still references the pre-division site description. Current service points belong to two distinct structures.

The mystery begins when a maintenance request is routed using the old label.

Design lesson:

`OLD_LABEL_MATCH != CURRENT_SERVICE_POINT_MATCH`.

Possible consequence:

The utility directory gets a new cross-reference while the old sign remains as historical material or is moved into an archive.

## The Bill Arrived After the Building Emptied

Premise:

A seasonal lodging site closes for the off-season. A later bill still arrives.

Potential explanations that remain independent:

- the billing period predates closure;
- a reading was received late;
- the bill used an estimate;
- maintenance kept limited service active;
- a later corrected reading changed the period;
- the account closure effective time differed from physical vacancy.

The generator must choose only explanations supported by authored records.

No assumption of theft or unauthorized occupancy is allowed.

## The Workshop Still Has Power, but the Name Is Old

Premise:

A family workshop changed operator but kept the same service point. A utility label or old account record still uses the predecessor's name.

The current business has physical service. The historical name does not prove the predecessor still operates the site.

Useful cross-system themes:

- business succession;
- human identity;
- place continuity;
- records lagging behind social change.

## The Old Pump Still Reports

Premise:

A decommissioned device continues to appear in a copied administrative export after a replacement was installed.

The old device is physically inactive. Its record remains because a downstream directory was not updated at the same time.

The quest is a provenance problem, not a haunted-machine story by default.

A supernatural interpretation may only exist if separate canon supports it.

## The Reconnect Order and the Field Crew Crossed Paths

Premise:

An authorized administrative reconnect request is cancelled or superseded while a field crew is already en route.

Request/Dispatch preserves what the crew was told at departure. Utility Continuity preserves the administrative action lineage. The physical owner decides what was actually executed.

Possible result:

The crew arrives, does not perform the work, and leaves a field note. Later NPC retellings compress this into “they came to reconnect us and refused.”

No deception is necessary.

## The Festival Kiosk Has a Temporary Service Point

Premise:

A recurring local festival uses a temporary utility endpoint each year.

Over time, the temporary point becomes socially persistent:

- vendors use it as a meeting landmark;
- old maps show it;
- event crews remember where queues used to form;
- a Pokémon waits near the sealed cabinet because that was once a familiar routine.

After the festival ends, the service point can be inactive while the place-memory remains active.

No species behavior is inferred mechanically.

## The Meter Room Is Older Than the Building Front

Premise:

A renovated commercial block has a modern frontage but an older rear utility room. The service point predates the current shop layout.

Exploration reveals several generations of labels and device mounts.

The story can teach players that building appearance is not the same thing as service-point chronology.

## The Customer Photo Is Real

Premise:

A disputed reading includes a genuine customer photograph. The image accurately shows the device display at that moment.

The unresolved question is whether that device was associated with the service point and billing period claimed in the later record.

`PHOTO_GENUINE != ASSOCIATION_PROVEN`.

This structure avoids the cheap reveal that every contradictory document was forged.

## The Utility Restored Service Yesterday; the Clinic Reopened Today

Premise:

Electric Grid or Drinking-Water Continuity verifies physical restoration to a clinic's service point. The clinic remains limited until its own downstream checks finish.

Utility customer records may update at yet another time.

The story exposes three independent clocks:

- physical utility restoration;
- administrative service/billing state;
- clinic operational readiness.

Players can see a lit building that is still not ready to provide every service.

## The Last Reading Belongs to the Right Meter and the Wrong Period

Premise:

A reading was captured after the nominal billing period but later attached to the wrong period during a manual correction.

The investigation is solved by timestamps and lineage rather than physical inspection.

Old panels, warning paint and conduit remain as environmental history.

## The Meter That Everyone Calls Broken

Premise:

Locals believe a meter is faulty because several bills were estimated.

Investigation shows that the device itself may be functioning. The missing link is data transmission or access.

Possible endings:

- device fault supported;
- data-transfer fault supported;
- access problem supported;
- evidence remains insufficient;
- several causes occurred in different periods.

`ESTIMATE_EXISTS != DEVICE_FAULT`.

## Mystery — Six Dates for One “Correction”

The phrase “the bill was corrected on Tuesday” appears in dialogue.

Records reveal six relevant moments:

1. discrepancy reported;
2. new evidence received;
3. reading accepted;
4. amended bill generated;
5. corrected bill delivered;
6. Finance adjustment settled.

Every speaker may be describing a real event.

The quest rewards scope clarification instead of selecting a single “true date.”

## Mystery — Four Devices, One Service Point

A century-old institution has used four successive observation devices at the same endpoint.

Historical records contain all four identifiers.

One archive description incorrectly assumes each identifier represents a different utility connection.

Players can reconstruct the succession without changing any original artifact.

## Mystery — The Building With No Current Account Name

A physically supplied public facility has a valid service point, but the customer-facing record does not map cleanly to a current human actor.

Possible authored explanations include:

- institutional rather than personal account relationship;
- recent office transition;
- data migration lag;
- shared administrative payer;
- unresolved linkage.

The system must not invent a hidden person to close the gap.

## Exploration — The Temporary Connection Nobody Removed From Memory

A marketplace once operated from a temporary utility point during reconstruction. The point is gone, but several current NPCs still use its nickname as directions.

Place Reference preserves the alias. Utility Continuity preserves that the technical episode ended. Public Memory explains why the term remains alive.

The reward is improved understanding of the district rather than money or combat power by default.

## Long arc — A Street Learns Which Meter Is Theirs

Phase 1: Normal routine.

A mixed residential/commercial street has stable utility service, several old buildings and different service-point histories. Players first encounter the system through ordinary life: shop opening, market preparation, maintenance or a landlord changing tenants.

Phase 2: Small discrepancy.

A meter replacement, building renumbering or delayed reading creates one localized record mismatch. No physical outage is required.

Phase 3: Multiple clocks become visible.

A later weather/outage incident affects the same area. Grid/Water restoration timestamps, device observations, account transitions and bill amendments diverge.

Phase 4: Social memory.

Residents remember “the day the power came back” differently because they refer to different buildings and services. One shop's meter was exchanged before the incident. Another account changed after it. A temporary kiosk existed only during recovery.

Phase 5: Persistent consequence.

Months later:

- a new service label exists;
- old labels remain in the alley;
- one business has a corrected record;
- one historical discrepancy is still unresolved;
- the temporary utility corner has become a local landmark;
- recurring field staff are recognized by residents;
- a future quest can use this history without retconning it.

The arc works without villainy and without a global utility simulator.

## Candidate recurring NPC/faction roles

All remain NON-CANON until localized:

Field reader or inspector: recognizes specific neighborhoods and builds long-term memory of access conditions and service-point history.

Customer-service records clerk: understands account and reading lineage but has no authority over Grid/Water technical state.

Meter technician: works on devices but does not decide bill disputes.

Utility liaison: coordinates between physical owner, account administration and affected institutions during disruptions.

Archive volunteer: preserves obsolete plans and old bill forms useful for historical linkage.

Property manager or shopkeeper: provides local evidence but does not become an institutional authority by being familiar with the site.

Trained partner Pokémon: may accompany a named worker in an authored role, but no utility capability is inferred from species or Type.
