# Ouros Proposal Seeds — Insurance, Coverage, Claims & Loss Adjustment — Pass 143

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.
Date: 2026-08-30

These seeds exercise the proposed Pass 143 continuity layer. None establishes that insurance, a particular policy type, legal regime, insurer, regulator, premium, liability doctrine or compensation entitlement exists anywhere in Ouros.

Each seed requires a canon gate before use: an authored risk-transfer, mutual-aid or compensation arrangement must already exist in the relevant location and institution.

## Seed: The Shop Reopened Before the Claim Closed

A small storefront suffers bounded physical damage during an incident. The owner uses temporary repairs and reopens quickly. Weeks later, the compensation process is still waiting on one estimate revision.

Useful continuity:

- physical service restored;
- repair partly complete;
- claim under review;
- customers assume the matter is finished because the shop is open;
- the owner does not need to be angry or financially ruined.

Possible player involvement:

Locate a pre-repair photograph or maintenance record needed to distinguish old wear from incident damage.

No combat required.

## Seed: The Covered Storm, the Uncovered Sign

A storm event falls within an authored agreement's scope. Several damaged subjects are claimed together. The building repair qualifies, while a decorative sign belongs to another actor or sits outside the covered subject list.

The conflict is about scope, not villainy.

Possible consequences:

- the shop repairs the building first;
- the old sign remains visibly damaged for months;
- locals remember the event through the sign long after service returns;
- a later donation or artisan project restores it through another system.

## Seed: The Claim Was Correct When Filed

A merchant reports a crate as lost after a transport disruption. The report is accurate based on information available that morning. The crate is recovered at a transfer yard two days later.

The claim history should retain:

- original good-faith loss notice;
- later recovery event;
- changed claim-item disposition;
- custody handoff;
- any settlement reassessment required by the authored agreement.

`LATER_RECOVERY != ORIGINAL_REPORT_FALSE`.

## Seed: The Old Damage Under the New Damage

A repair crew opens a wall after a recent incident and finds older deterioration behind it.

Questions split cleanly:

- Maintenance: what condition exists now and what must be repaired?
- Claim: which observed loss components belong to the covered incident?
- Case, only if relevant: is there any reason to investigate historical cause?

The default answer should not become fraud.

## Seed: Two Estimates, Both Honest

A field assessor and a repair specialist produce different estimates because they are measuring different scopes. One describes restoration to prior function; the other includes elective improvements or additional preventive work.

The quest is a document/provenance comparison:

- identify scope definitions;
- match line items to physical observations;
- separate repair, improvement and unrelated maintenance;
- preserve both versions.

The outcome can be `SCOPE_DIFFERENCE_EXPLAINED` without declaring either estimate wrong.

## Seed: The Previous Operator's Agreement

A business changes operator shortly before an incident. The premises, shop name and visible staff routines make continuity look obvious, but the relevant agreement was attached to the previous operator or another covered interest.

Potential world-state owners:

- Human Identity: who the people are;
- Commercial Services: who currently operates the shop;
- Land/Property: whatever ownership/use facts canon has established;
- Finance/Pass 143: who the agreement actually covers.

Do not infer ownership from operation.

## Seed: The Address Changed, the Building Did Not

A building's official street reference changed months before the loss, while an older risk-transfer agreement still uses the former address text.

Place Reference can establish that both descriptors resolve to the same persistent place. That does not itself confirm coverage; it only resolves the location mismatch.

Useful mystery structure:

The first three documents appear to describe three different places until the player reconstructs address history.

## Seed: The Building Changed, the Name Did Not

A business name moved to a new premises while an old document still refers to the same trading name.

The inverse of the previous seed prevents overreliance on names.

`SAME_BUSINESS_NAME != SAME_COVERED_LOCATION`.

## Seed: The Replacement Arrived First

A damaged piece of operational equipment is replaced quickly through a temporary loan, mutual-aid pool or emergency procurement before the claim decision is complete.

The replacement should keep its own identity, custody and eventual return/ownership state.

The later settlement must not retroactively turn the temporary replacement into the original asset.

## Seed: The Repair Was Authorized, the Road Was Closed

A covered repair has approval and funding, but the materials cannot reach the site because a route is unavailable.

Finance/claim state can be ready while Travel/Supply state blocks execution.

This creates a grounded dependency quest without turning the provider into an obstacle.

## Seed: The Claim Paid, the Roof Still Leaks

Finance records receipt of an authorized settlement. Maintenance still has an unresolved fault because repair work is waiting on materials, weather or specialist availability.

`SETTLEMENT_RECEIVED != REPAIR_COMPLETE`.

NPC dialogue should reflect both truths rather than using “the claim was paid” as shorthand for restoration.

## Seed: The Roof Was Fixed, the Claim Is Still Open

The reverse case. The household or institution repairs immediately using its own resources and seeks reimbursement afterward under an authored arrangement.

The repaired world state can become evidence of what was done without erasing pre-repair observations.

## Seed: Three Photos of One Window

Three images seem contradictory:

- one shows an intact window;
- one shows a cracked pane;
- one shows temporary boarding.

The timestamps reveal a normal sequence: pre-loss condition, post-loss damage, mitigation.

Photography provenance solves chronology. Pass 143 uses the observations but does not alter image authenticity rules.

## Seed: The Inspector Could Not See the Back Room

A claim site inspection is completed for accessible areas while one room remains restricted by Building Safety or another physical owner.

Claim state becomes `INFORMATION_REQUIRED` or equivalent for that component.

The inspector's visit remains complete as an event even though the full loss assessment is incomplete.

## Seed: The Claim With No Cause

A damaged asset is discovered after a period where no reliable witness exists. The agreement only requires proof that a defined loss occurred within scope; exact historical cause is not necessary for the claim decision.

The claim can close while cause remains `UNKNOWN`.

This protects a useful principle:

`CLAIM_RESOLVED != INCIDENT_HISTORY_FULLY_SOLVED`.

## Seed: The Cause Matters, but Nobody Knows Yet

A different authored agreement does require an event category that cannot yet be distinguished between two plausible causes.

The fair result is `INFORMATION_REQUIRED` or `UNRESOLVED`, not a random denial or fabricated certainty.

## Seed: The Denial That Nobody Lied About

A claimant accurately reports the event and damage. The claim is denied because the agreement did not cover that subject, date or event category.

The story explores disappointment, alternative repair routes and community support without making the claimant fraudulent or the provider corrupt.

## Seed: The Approval With an Excluded Item

Most of a multi-item claim is covered. One object is excluded because it belongs to a guest, another institution or a separate custody chain.

The claim becomes partially approved.

The excluded object's future is handed to its real owner system rather than deleted from the story.

## Seed: The Supplemental Crack

Weeks after repair begins, hidden structural damage becomes visible.

Maintenance records a new observation and determines whether it is linked to the same fault/incident. Pass 143 receives that evidence as a potential supplement rather than silently editing the original estimate.

The later revision preserves why the first estimate was reasonable at the time.

## Seed: The Temporary Counter Became Permanent

A shop relocates its counter during repairs. Customers prefer the new layout, so the business keeps it even after the claim and work order close.

The incident leaves a mundane but durable physical consequence.

This is useful environmental storytelling because recovery does not restore the exact old world.

## Seed: The Mutual-Aid Fund Has No Adjuster

A small community has no insurer-like business. It has an authored cooperative fund that reimburses specific shared losses after a local review.

Pass 143 can reuse the same chronology with local terms:

- request;
- evidence;
- scope check;
- community review;
- resource allocation;
- closure.

Do not force real-world insurance vocabulary into every culture.

## Seed: The League Venue's Equipment Pool

A competition venue has an authored internal risk pool for certain shared equipment used during sanctioned events.

A damaged piece of equipment creates a claim-like internal process, but the player-facing consequence concerns venue capacity and replacement timing rather than personal money.

No combat bonus, League authority or universal policy follows from the existence of the pool.

## Seed: The Festival Cancellation That Wasn't Total

Weather forces a temporary-public-event operator to cancel one evening session while the overall festival continues later.

If canon has an interruption arrangement, the claim needs exact event scope:

- one session cancelled;
- vendors partly operating;
- transport still running;
- some ticket entitlements rebooked;
- no assumption that the entire event was lost.

This can connect Events, Service Reservations, Finance and Weather while each owner keeps its state.

## Seed: The Cargo Was Damaged Before the Ferry

A cargo crate arrives damaged after a ferry crossing. Earlier warehouse observations reveal the same dent before loading.

The ferry trip remains historically real; the damage claim simply cannot attribute that particular condition to it.

The mystery works through chronology, not confrontation.

## Seed: The Cargo Was Fine at Loading

The inverse pattern. Storage photographs and loading records establish pre-departure condition, while arrival evidence shows damage.

This narrows the event window without automatically identifying responsibility or confirming coverage.

## Seed: The Pokémon Broke the Wall, but That Is Not the Whole Question

A Pokémon battle or wild incident visibly damages a wall.

Pass 143 explicitly refuses to jump from that fact to financial conclusions.

Separate questions remain:

- What exact damage occurred?
- Was the observed battle the cause of all of it?
- Does any authored agreement cover that event?
- Does liability doctrine exist here?
- Who owns or operates the wall/facility?
- Who repairs it and when?

This is a core Pokémon-world guardrail.

## Seed: The Battle Did Not Break the Wall

NPCs assume a recent fight caused damage because the timing is memorable. Maintenance records show the crack existed earlier.

The event can still have other consequences, but the wall claim must use the older chronology.

This creates a callback where public memory and physical provenance diverge without a conspiracy.

## Mystery: Five Times the Claim Was “Approved”

Five records use the word “approved” informally:

1. intake accepted the notice;
2. a reviewer accepted the evidence packet as complete;
3. a field assessor recommended a repair scope;
4. a decision authority approved coverage;
5. Finance authorized payment.

The player reconstructs which event each speaker meant.

The correct resolution may state that all five speakers used locally normal language while referring to different process stages.

## Mystery: Four Dates, One Loss

Documents show four apparently conflicting dates:

- incident date;
- discovery date;
- notice date;
- inspection date.

The mystery resolves when the player stops treating every date as “the claim date”.

Optional complication:

A fifth date is the agreement's effective-date boundary, which matters only if canon says so.

## Mystery: The Object Was “Paid For” Twice

A replacement appears to have been funded twice.

Possible legitimate explanation:

- one payment was an emergency loan or procurement advance;
- a later settlement reimbursed the institution that fronted the cost;
- neither event represents duplicate ownership of the object.

Finance provenance resolves the money path. Material Culture resolves asset identity.

## Mystery: The Missing Boat That Came Back

A small vessel or other asset was reported missing after severe weather and a claim process began. It is later found damaged at another location.

The recovery creates new questions:

- condition at recovery;
- custody;
- repair feasibility;
- claim supplement/reassessment;
- whether a replacement had already been ordered.

Wreck/Vehicle/Maritime systems retain physical identity. Pass 143 only updates the claim relationship.

## Mystery: The Claim That Refers to a Place Nobody Calls That Anymore

An old claim file references a historical district or street name no longer used on current maps.

Place Reference establishes the location history. Archives preserves the old document. Human witnesses may use either name depending on age/community.

The player can solve the location without proving every fact in the old claim.

## Mystery: The File That Cannot Be Finished

An archival claim from decades ago contains incomplete evidence and two equally plausible interpretations of one damaged object.

No surviving source can decide which interpretation is correct.

Valid outcome:

`ACCEPTED_AMBIGUITY`.

The archive preserves both readings and stops fabricating certainty.

## Recurring NPC archetype: The Field Assessor Who Knows Buildings, Not People

A technically competent recurring NPC can recognize repair scopes and physical conditions while refusing to speculate about motive, liability or personal honesty.

This archetype creates useful institutional character because expertise remains bounded.

No Trainer Skill or Feature is implied until PTU/Caelo/canon establishes it.

## Recurring NPC archetype: The Community Fund Clerk

A small-town clerk or volunteer knows local reimbursement procedure, remembers which documents arrived and can explain process state. They do not secretly control repair crews, civic authority or family finances.

Over time the NPC can become a familiar source of local history because claim files connect many past incidents.

## Recurring NPC archetype: The Repair Specialist Who Disagrees on Scope

A craftsperson or engineer may disagree with an adjuster's repair scope for coherent technical reasons while respecting the other person's role.

This gives disputes texture without default villainization.

## Faction/institution candidate: Regional Mutual Recovery Cooperative

Status: NON-CANON.

A cooperative could pool resources for authored categories of damage among participating members.

Potential differentiators from a commercial insurer:

- membership governance;
- resource or material settlement rather than only money;
- local assessors;
- transparent capacity limits;
- prioritization during region-wide disasters;
- reciprocal labor/material agreements.

Do not establish this institution without canon approval.

## Faction/institution candidate: League Venue Risk Pool

Status: NON-CANON.

A League-linked venue network might internally cover specific venue equipment or sanctioned-event interruptions without providing household or public insurance.

This can create institutional depth while preventing a single “Pokémon insurance company” from becoming universal.

No League power outside the authored agreement follows.

## Faction/institution candidate: Craft Guild Repair Guarantee

Status: NON-CANON.

A guild could warrant or compensate limited failures of work performed by participating workshops.

This belongs close to Procurement/Commercial/Repair systems and should not become general property insurance.

It creates stories about workmanship, materials and versioned work orders without inventing generic negligence law.

## Long arc: A District Recovers on Different Timelines

Status: NON-CANON.

Phase 1 establishes several ordinary premises and institutions before anything goes wrong.

Phase 2 a bounded event creates different physical losses across the district.

Phase 3 emergency stabilization and temporary service changes occur first.

Phase 4 financial recovery paths diverge because households/businesses/institutions have different authored arrangements or none.

Phase 5 players interact with evidence, deliveries, archives, route dependencies and repair choices. They do not become automatic claim judges.

Phase 6 some places reopen quickly, some rebuild slowly, one changes use, one closes for unrelated reasons, and one recovers without any formal claim.

Phase 7 months later, old notices, repaired facades, replacement objects, changed routines and unresolved records preserve the district's memory.

Narrative value:

Recovery gains social and historical texture without reducing every aftermath to a single quest-complete flag.

## Encounter seed: Recovered Cargo Handoff Chokepoint

Full version:

A controlled recovery/custody handoff is threatened while the object and staff are still present.

Rich semantics would need complete movement, protected zones/reactions, objective-aware AI and semantic adapter playback.

Reduced version — READY:

Remove cargo and staff from BattleSpec. Freeze custody state. Resolve explicit combatants on static geometry. Victory yields `IMMEDIATE_HANDOFF_APPROACH_CLEAR`; world-state owners perform any later transfer.

## Encounter seed: Temporary Repair Site Withdrawal

Full version:

Workers must exit an active repair area while a threat enters the site. Rich play may involve staged withdrawal, Intercept, restricted zones and changing worksite access.

Reduced version — READY:

Workers withdraw before initiative. Maintenance pauses the work order. Battle occurs in a safe static adjacent space. Victory yields `IMMEDIATE_REPAIR_WORK_AREA_CLEAR` only.

## Encounter seed: Archive Claim-File Access Perimeter

Full version:

A threat blocks an archive/service corridor during a scheduled records review. No private document should ever become a tactical objective whose pickup proves authorization.

Reduced version — READY:

Archive staff and records remain outside BattleSpec. The encounter clears the public approach only. Credentials/Archives later decide access.

`TACTICAL_VICTORY != RECORD_ACCESS_GRANTED`.

## Anti-false-completion rules

A battle victory never:

- creates a risk-transfer agreement;
- opens a claim;
- validates a loss notice;
- authenticates evidence;
- establishes cause;
- establishes liability;
- confirms coverage;
- approves a claim;
- determines valuation;
- authorizes repair;
- completes repair;
- transfers ownership;
- transfers evidence/cargo custody;
- pays a settlement;
- closes a claim;
- resolves a review request.

## Canon gates required before adoption

Any seed using formal coverage must specify:

- the institution/provider;
- agreement type;
- covered actor/subject/activity;
- scope;
- effective period;
- claim intake route;
- review authority;
- privacy;
- possible settlement forms;
- relation to Finance;
- relation to repair/recovery owners;
- whether a review mechanism exists.

Without those authored facts, use ordinary repair, aid, donation, procurement, relief or unresolved loss instead.
