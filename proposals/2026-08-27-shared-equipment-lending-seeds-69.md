# Shared Equipment & Lending Seeds — Pass 69

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-27

Every institution, location, procedure, item pool and cultural practice in this file is a candidate only. No seed establishes Ouros canon.

## Design intent

These seeds make reusable equipment part of persistent world state without turning Ouros into an inventory-management simulator. The same kit, counter or storage room can become familiar across several visits because its users, condition, location and availability change for traceable reasons.

Pokémon are excluded from the asset schema. Any Pokémon-assisted service must use Pokémon Agency plus its owning world system.

## The Last Survey Set

A field team has an approved assignment and a valid entitlement, but the final complete survey kit is already reserved for another team later that day.

The interesting choice concerns scheduling and scope rather than price. The players can finish early, narrow their work, use an approved substitute if one exists, coordinate a handoff, or postpone part of the survey.

Persistent consequences can include a changed reservation, a handoff location, a new routine between teams or a later callback when one group remembers that the other protected its time window.

No Skill bonus comes from holding the kit. Any actual mechanical survey capability remains governed elsewhere.

## Returned, Inspection Pending

The equipment is physically back on the shelf. A notice or staff member says it cannot be issued yet because the return has not passed its readiness check.

Players can investigate why the inspection is delayed: the responsible worker is covering another shift, a facility dependency is down, a component is missing, or the record itself is stale.

The return event stays true even while availability remains false.

This seed exists to prevent the common shortcut `returned = ready`.

## Reserved for the Morning Team

A rack appears full during the evening, yet several items are unavailable because they are already reserved for an early field assignment.

A later emergency request creates pressure to override that allocation. The extension records competing claims but cannot invent who has legal or institutional priority. The owning institution must have an established policy or make an authored decision.

A useful callback occurs the next morning: did the original team still receive what it expected, or did the overnight decision create a new dependency?

## The Kit That Came Back Different

A familiar hard case is returned with the expected model of instrument inside, but a repair mark or provenance detail does not match the item that was checked out.

Possible explanations include an innocent case swap, a transfer between field teams that was never recorded, a repair desk using the wrong outer case, or a genuine unresolved discrepancy.

The mystery should be solved through exact item identity, checkout events, handoffs, timestamps and repair history.

Do not assume theft. Do not create a hidden truth score.

## Borrowed for One Project, Needed by Another

An institution owns a specialized device that two legitimate projects need during overlapping windows.

Both projects can matter. One may be time-sensitive because of seasonality; the other may depend on an external visitor or transport slot. Players can gather actual constraints and help the institution construct a schedule.

The choice should persist into later project timelines. No side automatically becomes morally correct because its story hook appeared first.

## The Loaner Nobody Checked Back In

The register shows an asset still checked out to an actor who insists it was handed to another staff member days ago.

That second actor remembers receiving a bundle but cannot confirm the exact instance. A third record shows the equipment at a different node afterward.

The investigation follows custody transitions rather than accusations. It can end with a record correction, Found Property handoff, Courier link, unresolved location or Case escalation if independent evidence justifies it.

Lateness alone never establishes wrongdoing.

## Rain Gear at the Wrong Depot

A field assignment is still possible, but the approved shared protective gear is at a support node on the other side of a route disruption.

The gear itself is intact. The blocker is location.

Travel or Courier owns the movement problem. Weather owns the forecast/conditions. Pass 69 owns the fact that the pool cannot fulfill the local checkout until the resource arrives or an approved substitute is allocated.

This seed makes geography matter without inventing an equipment failure.

## The Replacement Is Similar, Not Equivalent

A requested item is unavailable and staff offer another model that appears suitable for the narrative task.

The group may accept it for a nonmechanical purpose if the institution regards it as suitable. If a PTU mechanical effect matters, similarity is insufficient: the exact item definition and implementation must be verified.

This seed teaches the adapter and narrative generator not to translate cosmetic similarity into rules equivalence.

## The Component That Stayed in the Field Case

A multi-part kit is returned and placed into INSPECTION_PENDING because one component cannot be reconciled.

The missing piece may still be inside a secondary pouch, have been handed to a teammate, be at the field site, or have been transferred intentionally.

The story can use a recovery trip, a call to a recurring NPC or a record comparison. The kit should remain unavailable until its owning process resolves readiness.

## Pickup at the Temporary Desk

A facility repair closes the ordinary equipment counter. The institution keeps limited service operating from a temporary room.

Reservations remain valid, but pickup location and handoff routines change. A regular user who follows yesterday's habit may go to the closed counter first.

The temporary arrangement can later become part of institutional memory even after the original desk reopens.

## The Asset with an Old Label

An object in the pool carries an older department or project label that predates the current storage arrangement.

The label is historical evidence, not ownership authority. Archive, Material Culture and workplace records can explain why the object moved.

The seed can reveal a previous project, retired team, relocation or organizational change without requiring a conspiracy.

## Reissue Morning

After several repairs or inspections, a small pool returns to service.

The playable content is the verification and allocation aftermath: which reservations can now be fulfilled, which project resumes first under established policy, who must be told, and whether the rack/notice state in Minecraft matches the authoritative pool state.

A visual repair alone cannot make the assets AVAILABLE.

## Mystery: Four Checkouts, Three Kits

Four checkout records appear in the same day, but the pool contains only three physical kits.

The apparent impossibility has several grounded solution families:

- one kit was returned and reissued before the day ended;
- one record refers to a component rather than a full kit;
- one checkout was cancelled but its display projection stayed stale;
- two records refer to the same exact item after an authorized handoff;
- one record has the wrong item-instance link.

Players reconstruct sequence, location and identity. The mystery does not require a culprit.

Useful evidence:

- checkout and return timestamps;
- exact item-instance IDs;
- handoff records;
- storage observations;
- repair or inspection events;
- route or workplace schedules;
- actor knowledge limited to what each person actually observed.

## Persistent arc: A Shared Workshop Learns Its Borrowers

Visit 1 establishes a small shared-equipment pool, its ordinary pickup location and several regular users.

Visit 2 creates a real allocation collision. Players learn that different teams have different time dependencies.

Visit 3 includes a return discrepancy. The issue is resolved through provenance or handoff history rather than accusation.

Visit 4 moves part of the pool temporarily because the normal room is under repair or another existing world-state blocker applies.

Visit 5 shows a revised routine. A label, storage position, reservation practice or handoff convention has changed because of prior experience.

Later visits can reuse those facts. The workshop should feel like the same place with accumulated operational memory.

No new institution or settlement is required.

## Noncombat encounter: Inspection Before Reissue

Premise:

A returned field kit is needed for an upcoming assignment, but it is still INSPECTION_PENDING. Players can help assemble the evidence required for the owning system to determine readiness.

Possible actions:

- compare issue and return condition observations;
- locate an expected component;
- fetch a repair record;
- confirm which actor performed the last authorized handoff;
- determine whether the actual blocker is technical, staffing or documentation.

Mechanical boundary:

No combat rule is required. Narrative observation cannot invent calibration, repair DCs, item bonuses or professional qualifications.

Minecraft can eventually show the rack and exact case, but persistent in-world projection depends on adapter/playback support.

## Capability-aware encounter: Field Kit Recovery Window

Narrative premise:

A field team has already withdrawn from an unsafe area, but one exact institutional kit remains at a known safe-to-approach position near a battle-capable territorial threat. Recovering it matters because another assignment depends on that exact resource.

Intended full version:

The full scene could support a CLEAR_ROUTE or RECOVER_AND_WITHDRAW objective, terrain and weather where legally mapped, territorial withdrawal behavior, interception or forced displacement near narrow terrain, objective-aware AI, and synchronized adapter playback that preserves the exact item location and custody transition.

Required capability families:

- targeting / footprints / range / LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement when used;
- core calculations;
- action economy / initiative;
- full turn / round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain / weather / hazards / zones / reactions when active;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items if any tactical item is used;
- Trainer Features / perks when present;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft / Cobblemon / Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced version:

Keep the kit outside the tactical grid. Confirm its world-state location before battle. Run a static reviewed encounter using only supported mechanics and no dynamic weather/hazard effects. After AutoPTU resolves the battle, perform recovery as a separate world interaction and update custody only then.

Winning the battle cannot automatically put the kit into inventory, mark it returned, verify its condition or make it AVAILABLE.

## Capability-aware encounter: Depot Access Lockdown

Narrative premise:

A small support depot contains equipment required elsewhere. Staff have evacuated because a local threat makes the access room unsafe. The goal is to restore safe access, then perform normal checkout or transfer.

Intended full version:

The richer version may use multiple access lanes, protected storage fixtures, dynamic withdrawal, interception, forced movement, reactions, hazards tied to validated PTU/Caelo rules, non-KO objective priorities and tactical AI that can defend territory or retreat.

Required capability families are the same permanent categories as Field Kit Recovery Window. Complete movement, active environment, tactical policy and adapter playback are especially important.

Reduced version:

Evacuate staff in world state. Isolate the equipment from tactical targeting. Freeze the room as a static legal arena away from the storage racks. Resolve the battle. Then let the facility/workplace decide whether the depot is safe to reopen and let Pass 69 execute checkout or transfer only after that decision.

Battle victory does not inspect equipment, authorize access, repair the depot or determine institutional priority.

## Canon questions deliberately left open

Ouros still needs reviewed answers for which institutions maintain shared pools, which equipment classes are reusable, how eligibility is proven, whether reservations or due windows are common, what return inspections exist, whether any fees/deposits/fines exist, how privacy works around checkout records and how much physical equipment state Minecraft should render.

No answer is established by these seeds.
