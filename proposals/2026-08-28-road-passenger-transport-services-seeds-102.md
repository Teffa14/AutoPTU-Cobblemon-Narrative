# Road Passenger Transport Services Seeds — Pass 102

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Requires canon review.

These seeds use the Pass 102 road-passenger-service architecture without establishing any Ouros operator, vehicle technology, species work role or transport law.

## The Road Is Open, the Shuttle Is Not

A familiar corridor remains fully passable, but its public service is suspended because one operational dependency failed. Travelers initially assume the road itself is closed.

Useful outputs: operator dependency, alternate journey choice, public-information correction, later restoration callback.

## The Stop Moved Across the Square

Construction or a temporary public event relocates one pickup point. Old signage remains visible and several travelers wait at the obsolete location.

No villain is required. The problem is reconciling effective dates, notices and stop identity.

## The Vehicle Is Here, Boarding Is Not

The service asset is physically present while the run remains DELAYED or SUSPENDED. Players can investigate the actual dependency rather than treating visual presence as proof of availability.

## The Last Run Served Only Half the Pattern

A run began normally, turned back or short-turned, and therefore produced several correct but incomplete passenger reports about where the service operated.

## The Temporary Shuttle Became the Regular One

A disruption creates an alternate limited route. Months later it remains popular enough that the operator considers retaining part of it after the original pattern returns.

This can affect businesses, lodging, visitor flow and neighborhood routines without using a transport-upgrade meter.

## The Pokémon Worker Returned, the Service Did Not

A known Pokémon associated with a public service is visible again after an absence. Residents assume operations have resumed, but verification, staffing or another dependency is still pending.

The exact individual remains an actor, not a species template.

## The Service Returned With Different Stops

An ecology, road or accessibility issue is resolved, but restoration uses a revised service pattern. Old maps remain historically correct for earlier dates.

## The Missed Pickup Changed the Expedition

A research team misses a limited road service and must choose between waiting, rerouting or changing its field objective. The missed run becomes expedition history rather than an arbitrary punishment.

## The Driver Everyone Knows Is Covering Another Route

A recurring operator is absent from their usual service because they are temporarily assigned elsewhere. The route remains functional, while the social continuity changes.

No hidden emotional inference is needed.

## The Old Stop Is Still a Landmark

A retired stop remains a common meeting point and local reference long after service moved. A later case, delivery or witness statement uses the historic name, creating a provenance problem rather than a navigation bug.

## The Shuttle Is Full in Every Story

Several witnesses say a service was “full,” but they may mean no seats, no boarding at their stop, a large visible cohort or a temporarily denied pickup. The investigation must avoid inventing capacity numbers.

## Mystery — Five Departure Claims, Three Runs

Sources include a timetable, operator log, passenger message, observation from a roadside shop and a stale public board. The solution comes from run IDs, effective pattern versions and timestamps. Multiple claims may be honest and still refer to different operational states.

## Mystery — Four Stops, Two Locations

Two temporary stop relocations occurred during a long works project. Locals kept using old stop names. The party must reconcile names, physical locations, effective dates and which run actually served each point.

## Long arc — A Route Learns Its Riders

Season 1 establishes a routine road service, its operators, several regular riders and businesses that depend on predictable stops.

A later disruption changes one dependency. The service becomes limited or rerouted rather than disappearing. Riders adapt. One temporary stop gains local importance. Another area loses incidental visitor traffic.

Restoration occurs in stages: road access, operator readiness, test run, limited public service and revised normal pattern. Months later the old temporary route, a recurring passenger, a transferred Pokémon worker or obsolete sign becomes relevant to a new story.

The arc persists through explicit service/run/stop history. There is no `transport_level`.

## Encounter — Stop Evacuation Withdrawal

Full intended form: civilians leave through several safe paths while participants protect the approach. Desired mechanics include complete Intercept/forced movement, withdrawal/protection objectives, generalized reactions, objective-aware AI and semantic playback. Live traffic or hazard zones require the terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions family.

Reduced form: service suspends before combat; passengers, staff, service assets and nonparticipant Pokémon leave the arena; Ouros selects combatants explicitly; AutoPTU receives a static roadside map. Victory only secures the immediate stop area.

## Encounter — Disabled Shuttle Perimeter

Full intended form: protect a work route while technicians reach a disabled asset, potentially requiring reactions, forced movement, zones/hazards and tactical AI.

Reduced form: Maintenance isolates the asset before combat. Workers and the service asset stay outside BattleSpec. Battle occurs nearby. Victory permits later diagnosis; it performs no repair and restarts no service.

## Encounter — Rerouted Pickup Conflict

Full intended form: a temporary pickup must remain clear while a territorial subgroup withdraws or is contained. Desired support includes reviewed terrain, route-protection objectives, reactions, AI tactical policy and playback.

Reduced form: all passengers wait elsewhere, the pickup is operationally suspended, and combat occurs on a static cleared section. The result is handed back to Road Passenger Operations for a separate safety/service decision.

## Persistent NPC/faction candidates

Potential roles, all proposed:
- route dispatcher who knows service history but not every road cause;
- recurring local driver/operator;
- worker responsible for stop information;
- Pokémon handler assigned to a public transport program;
- frequent commuter whose routine makes them a useful observer;
- small-town carrier connecting places too minor for a major terminal;
- accessibility advocate tracking whether temporary stops remain usable;
- business owner affected when a stop moves;
- maintenance liaison who can explain why `repair_complete` differs from `service_restored`.

These roles grant no PTU Skills, Edges, Features, authority or competence automatically.
