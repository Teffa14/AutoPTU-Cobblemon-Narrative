# Worksite Safety & Near-Miss Seeds — Pass 74

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Nothing in this file establishes Ouros geography, institutions, law, occupational policy, named settlements, Pokémon labor norms or mechanical rules.

These candidates are designed to reuse existing workplaces rather than create disposable quest locations.

## The Cart That Rolled Twice

Two shifts report that a loaded cart traveled farther than expected in the same corridor. Nobody was injured.

The easy story is “bad brakes.” The stronger version keeps several live hypotheses: floor condition, load distribution, equipment condition, route slope, procedure difference, or two superficially similar events with different causes.

Player contribution can involve comparing equipment custody, shift timing, maintenance observations and photographs. A restriction can remain even if no single cause is confirmed.

Long-term callback: the route or staging point changes visibly.

## The Barrier That Moves Every Morning

A temporary barrier is always found several blocks away from its documented location at first shift.

Possible explanations include cleaning routine, public access, wind/weather, another authorized work group, storage convenience or intentional removal. The scenario should not start from sabotage.

The important outcome is a better handoff and a control that matches how the space actually functions.

## The Same Warning, Different Meaning

Two workers use the same shorthand phrase in handoff notes, but they mean different physical conditions.

A new worker interprets the phrase according to the wrong team convention. A close call exposes the ambiguity before anyone is hurt.

Persistent change: the workplace adopts a clearer authored naming convention or physical marker.

No universal Ouros signage standard is implied.

## Stop Work, Event Still Open

A temporary public event is operating beside an active setup/teardown area. A safety observation stops one work task while the event itself can remain open using a different route.

This candidate connects Event Operations, Public Space, Notices, Staffing and Worksite Safety without treating every restriction as a crisis.

The player may help communicate the changed route or verify that temporary barriers match the current area state.

## The Tool Was Not There

Several people assume a shared machine caused a near miss. Equipment checkout history shows that exact item instance was being inspected elsewhere at the time.

The revelation narrows the investigation but does not identify the cause automatically.

This seed exists to make exact custody/provenance useful in ordinary stories.

## Rain After the Briefing

A work briefing was accurate when issued. Weather changes afterward and creates a condition that was never part of the morning plan.

The narrative question is how the workplace detects and responds to changed conditions, not whether the briefing “failed.”

Weather stays overworld state unless an exact PTU/Caelo tactical weather mapping is verified.

## The Pokémon Uses the Other Path

A worksite repeatedly sees wild Pokémon crossing a staging route at a particular time. Workers initially treat the presence as random interruption.

Repeated observations reveal a consistent local movement pattern. A route change may be easier than constant deterrence.

This seed should connect to Interspecies Ecology or Wild Collective Agency. It must not assume human ownership of the Pokémon, intent beyond observed behavior, or combat as the preferred solution.

## New Worker, Old Near Miss

A person joins a workplace after a close call occurred. The physical control is still visible, but nobody has explained why it exists.

The player can discover the history through handoff/briefing records rather than an exposition NPC.

The tension comes from institutional memory: a control can survive longer than the explanation that made it meaningful.

## The Temporary Fix Became the Route

A short-term reroute introduced during a repair remains in everyday use because workers prefer it. Months later, another project assumes the original route is still standard.

No misconduct is required. Two valid records belong to different operational eras.

The resolution may formalize the newer route, restore the older one or maintain both under different conditions.

## The Close Call Nobody Agrees Was One

A small event caused no injury and no damage. Some workers see it as ordinary variability; another actor believes circumstances were unusually close to harm.

The system records both claims and observable facts. It does not need a hidden risk score to decide who is “right.”

The useful output may simply be monitoring or a temporary control until more evidence exists.

## The Return Review Finds Something Else

A corrective action is complete and the original condition appears resolved. During return-to-work verification, a different unrelated condition becomes visible.

The first corrective action remains successful. The new observation opens a separate record.

This avoids the common quest pattern where completion is retroactively declared a failure just to extend the story.

## The Shift That Learned the Workaround

One shift developed a safe temporary procedure during a service disruption. Another shift never received the handoff because it worked a different schedule.

The player encounters the second group trying to resume the older routine.

The resolution is information continuity, not combat.

## Mystery: Five Reports, Four Events

Five reports seem to show an escalating pattern. Timeline reconstruction reveals that two reports refer to the same close call but use different area names and different shift timestamps.

Evidence sources:

- work assignments;
- stable actor IDs;
- area references;
- equipment custody;
- photographs;
- maintenance records;
- briefing revisions;
- witness/testimony provenance.

Possible resolution:

The duplicate is identified, but the remaining four events still reveal a real recurring condition. Deduplication must not become an excuse to dismiss the pattern.

## Persistent arc: A Worksite Learns Its Weak Points

Visit 1 establishes ordinary work: routes, staging points, staff rhythms, Pokémon presence, equipment and public-facing edges.

Visit 2 introduces a minor observation. A temporary control appears.

Visit 3 produces a near miss or repeated signal that makes several older observations relevant. Work pauses in one area while normal operations continue elsewhere.

Visit 4 focuses on assessment and dependencies. One explanation is narrowed or rejected. A corrective action changes the physical layout or procedure.

Visit 5 verifies the change. Some controls disappear; one remains because it proved useful independently of the original issue.

Later callback: a new worker asks why the unusual route or marker exists. The answer comes from persistent history rather than a reset world.

The arc can end without injury, villainy or catastrophe.

## Encounter: Scaffold-Line Evacuation

Premise:

An assessment team enters a restricted elevated work area after repeated movement observations. A separate wild Pokémon threat or authored disturbance makes withdrawal necessary.

Full version:

Workers and assessors move toward safe exits while route availability changes. Knockback/interception can matter near restricted edges. The environment may contain dynamic unsafe zones only if backed by PTU/Caelo rules. Wild AI may prioritize territory or escape. Minecraft playback must preserve exact movement and objective outcomes.

Required capability families:

VERIFIED: targeting/footprints/range/LoS; base movement legality; core calculations; action economy/initiative; AI legal-action infrastructure.

PARTIAL: full turn/round lifecycle; full stateful damage pipeline; status lifecycle; move-specific behavior; abilities; items; Trainer Features/perks.

BLOCKING: complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for dynamic work-area danger; AI tactical policy; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Work stops first. All workers/assessors leave the questionable section in world state. The area is excluded from the battle map. AutoPTU resolves a static conventional encounter in a safe perimeter. Assessment resumes afterward. Battle victory supplies no technical diagnosis and no reopening authority.

## Encounter: Loading-Bay Interruption

Premise:

A prior near miss is being reconstructed during a controlled closure. A Pokémon encounter interrupts the empty bay.

Full version:

Would support moving cargo, protected lanes, interruption-sensitive routes, possible forced displacement and objective-aware AI.

Reduced version:

Cargo, workers and machinery remain outside tactical state. AutoPTU resolves the Pokémon encounter on a static map. Investigators then continue reconstructing the original near miss from records and observations.

This keeps the story premise while avoiding fake escort, moving-object, hazard or AI rules.

## Encounter: Wildlife at the Temporary Route

Premise:

A temporary pedestrian/work route overlaps with a recurring wild Pokémon movement window.

Full version:

Could eventually support territorial/withdrawal goals, civilians clearing the route, changing access and ecological behavior.

Reduced version:

The route is closed and workers are removed before battle. If combat is appropriate, it occurs away from civilians on static terrain. The later world-state decision may reroute work, adjust timing, add observation or seek a coexistence solution through Ecology/Wild Agency.

Winning does not establish that the Pokémon were responsible for every earlier incident.

## Canon review questions

No seed should be promoted until the project decides which Ouros workplaces exist, who owns operational authority, what safety records or practices are culturally plausible, how workers can stop or challenge unsafe work, whether labor organizations exist, and what Pokémon participation arrangements are accepted in each relevant institution.
