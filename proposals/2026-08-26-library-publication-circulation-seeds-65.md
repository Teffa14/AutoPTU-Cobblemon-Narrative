# Ouros Library, Publication & Circulation Seeds — Pass 65

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. These are original candidates for review, not established Ouros lore.

## Design intent

These seeds use the publication/circulation extension without establishing specific Ouros libraries, publishing houses, laws, technologies or canonical books. They are written so existing settlements and institutions can adopt them later if continuity review approves.

The recurring principle is that a work, an edition and a specific copy can have different histories. Research can therefore create revisits and consequences without hiding all useful knowledge behind unique quest items.

## The Copy Everyone Thinks Is Missing

A reference work appears unavailable. Several regular users assume the only copy has been lost.

Investigation can reveal several mundane but consequential possibilities:
- the significant copy is under repair;
- it was returned but not re-shelved;
- a staff transfer never closed correctly;
- a reservation placed it in a temporary holding area;
- another edition is available but does not contain the exact section people expect.

The hook should begin from observed access state, not from an automatic theft allegation.

Persistent outputs can include corrected circulation state, a maintenance handoff, a staff-process improvement or a newly visible alternative edition.

## Two Editions, One Date

Two local reports cite the same respected title but give different dates for an event.

Neither reporter needs to be lying. One used an earlier edition. The other used a revision that changed the date after new evidence appeared.

Useful questions:
- when was each report written?
- which edition was accessible then?
- did the newer edition explain the revision?
- was an erratum publicly distributed or only incorporated into later copies?
- does either date now have stronger evidence, or is the dispute still open?

This can feed Science, Public Memory, a Case or Archaeology without allowing the printed correction to overwrite canonical truth automatically.

## The Handwritten Correction

A frequently consulted copy contains a handwritten correction beside a familiar passage.

The note may be:
- an informed correction;
- a reader's theory;
- a cross-reference;
- a later staff annotation;
- copied from another source;
- mistaken.

The story is about provenance first. Handwriting style, placement and apparent age are observations. Authorship and truth need evidence.

If the annotation becomes historically important, the copy can hand off to Archives for preservation while remaining a copy of the same edition.

## The Waiting List

A newly revised field guide or research volume becomes locally popular. The problem is access pressure, not a supernatural secret.

Possible world consequences:
- reservations accumulate;
- one reference copy becomes reading-room only;
- an older edition returns to active use;
- staff create a public summary of changed sections;
- a storefront expects a restock;
- another institution offers temporary access.

The hook can reveal what different groups actually need from the work. It should not create arbitrary scarcity merely to delay the player.

## The Book Returned to the Wrong Desk

A significant copy is physically back in the building, but its circulation record still shows it elsewhere.

This small discrepancy can matter because:
- another reader is waiting for it;
- a transfer was supposed to continue;
- a copy-specific insert remains inside;
- a condition report is missing;
- a research team assumes the source is inaccessible.

Resolution updates the handoff history. It does not require accusing staff of misconduct.

## A Photograph Between Pages

A loose photograph is found inside an old copy.

The copy's text is public and replaceable. The photograph creates the story.

Possible investigation:
- establish when this specific copy entered the collection;
- inspect custody/loan marks;
- identify what the photograph actually depicts before assigning meaning;
- compare it with archive or residential records;
- return it if a legitimate custodian can be established;
- preserve it if provenance becomes historically important.

Do not infer the photographer, owner, date or depicted relationship from proximity alone.

## The Author Changed Their Mind

A living author or institution releases a later work or revision that withdraws a confident earlier interpretation.

The previous edition remains in circulation and may still be quoted by NPCs who have not seen the correction.

Useful consequences:
- public discussion becomes temporally layered;
- a rival researcher can acknowledge the correction without erasing prior disagreement;
- an institution updates teaching material gradually;
- an old exhibit or local sign may still reflect the previous interpretation;
- a future anniversary forces a decision about which wording to present.

The retraction is itself an authored claim. The underlying world question may remain unresolved.

## The Reading Room That Reopens

A reading room or stack section has been inaccessible because of facility work, staffing or collection processing. The cause belongs to the appropriate owner system.

When it reopens, players can revisit earlier research blockers without the game pretending the information only now came into existence.

Persistent details may include:
- repaired shelving;
- new handling procedure;
- several returned collections;
- a staff member who now knows the room better;
- a backlog of waiting requests;
- an old edition whose condition prevented earlier use.

This seed works well after Pass 58 facility maintenance.

## The Manual Nobody Updated

A local operational manual still describes a process, route, facility layout or contact that changed months ago.

The outdated manual can explain repeated confusion without proving negligence or sabotage.

Investigation compares:
- current world state;
- edition/revision date;
- copies currently in circulation;
- whether a correction notice reached each workplace;
- which actors learned the new procedure through practice instead.

If a mechanical Move, Feature or battle procedure is mentioned, the narrative manual never overrides PTU/Caelo/AutoPTU authority.

## The Popular Abridgment

Most local readers know an abridged or simplified edition, while specialists cite the longer work.

The shortened text may omit uncertainty, competing explanations or historical context without being intentionally deceptive.

This creates useful public-memory friction:
- common sayings can come from the abridgment;
- experts can disagree about what was lost in simplification;
- a museum panel may inherit the abridged wording;
- a player can trace a popular belief back to editorial compression.

Do not make the full edition automatically correct or morally superior.

## The Copy That Traveled Further Than Its Text

A specific field copy has a long custody history across expeditions, temporary offices or homes. Its published content is available elsewhere.

The value lies in:
- marks of use;
- annotations;
- dated inserts;
- repair history;
- known handlers;
- links to past field sites.

This copy can become a bridge between Material Culture, Travel, Science, Residential and Archives without becoming a magical key.

## The Reference Shelf After the Expedition

A completed expedition produces several possible outputs rather than one reward screen:
- a field report;
- a revised guide section;
- a new bibliography;
- a donated copy;
- an unresolved-question notice;
- a public summary;
- material reserved for later verification.

When players revisit the institution, the changed shelf/notice state shows that the expedition entered public knowledge gradually.

Nothing is promoted to world truth solely because it was shelved.

## Mystery — Five Copies, Four Provenances

An institution lists five surviving copies of an old edition. Only four have coherent intake/custody histories.

The fifth is not automatically stolen or counterfeit.

Possible explanations include:
- a legacy catalog migration duplicated an identifier;
- one record refers to a facsimile;
- an uncatalogued donation was later folded into the collection;
- two institutions described the same transferred copy at different times;
- one physical copy was rebound and assigned a second local identifier;
- a genuine provenance gap remains unresolved.

The mystery resolves by reconstructing copy identity and handoffs, not by requiring a villain.

## Long arc — A Library Learns Its Readers

This arc reuses one approved institution across many visits.

Early state:
- common reference zones are established;
- a few staff specialties become known through observed interactions;
- players learn which materials are readily accessible and which require retrieval.

Middle state:
- players create requests based on their own projects;
- one copy goes into repair or transfer;
- an edition dispute changes what different readers quote;
- a field investigation produces material that returns to the institution;
- a public correction or revised edition appears.

Later state:
- staff referrals reflect prior requests;
- a previously closed room may reopen;
- an old edition remains available for historical comparison;
- the player's own report may be cited or catalogued if canon/institutional review allows it;
- another reader can independently follow a lead the players helped make public.

The institution gains memory without becoming a faction-reputation vending machine.

## Encounter — Closed-Stacks Evacuation

Narrative premise:
A Pokémon disturbance occurs while readers or staff are using a nonpublic storage/reading area. Human safety comes before collection handling.

FULL intent:
- narrow aisles and changing access;
- evacuation/clear-route objective;
- fragile/protected areas only with supported object/environment rules;
- interception/forced movement where legal;
- environmental hazard rules only from governing PTU/Caelo mechanics;
- AI that may prioritize escape;
- Minecraft playback preserving who evacuated and what remains inaccessible.

Capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when used
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

REDUCED version:
Evacuate all civilians first. Remove or secure the significant copy through world state before combat. Use a static adjacent arena with ordinary legal combat only. Afterward, facility/archive/circulation state determines inspection, reopening and copy condition. Victory cannot prove an annotation authentic or repair a collection automatically.

## Encounter — Special-Collection Transfer Interruption

Narrative premise:
A significant copy is between institutions when a route disturbance blocks the scheduled handoff.

FULL intent:
- moving transfer party;
- protect/escape/break-through objective;
- interception and forced movement;
- route terrain/weather only if PTU/Caelo mapping and engine support exist;
- objective-aware AI;
- synchronized battle plus courier/custody playback.

Capability dependencies are the same permanent families as above, with complete movement, environmental systems, tactical AI and adapter/playback currently BLOCKING; lifecycle/damage/status/moves/abilities/items/Trainer Features remain PARTIAL where relevant.

REDUCED version:
Keep the copy and custodian outside the battle grid. Freeze transfer state. Resolve an ordinary encounter at a static chokepoint. After the authoritative battle, Courier and Circulation decide whether the transfer continues, reroutes or returns. The copy is never an improvised target with custom HP.

## Noncombat encounter — Two Editions, One Citation

Two reports cite the same work and appear mutually exclusive.

The players can solve the discrepancy using:
- citation metadata;
- edition history;
- access/circulation dates;
- claim comparison;
- correction distribution history;
- actor-knowledge state.

This can run before any new tactical capability or Minecraft adapter family exists.

## Mechanical boundary

No seed here grants a mechanical reward because a character reads, owns, finds, annotates, publishes or restores a work.

A book naming a Move does not teach it. A field guide describing a species does not expose its private battle state. A Chronicler-related publication does not activate Chronicler mechanics. A rare copy is not automatically a PTU item. Research prestige does not alter Accuracy, initiative, damage or AI.

Any such effect needs exact PTU/Caelo authority and implementation evidence.

## Canon review questions

Before promoting any candidate, resolve:
- the actual institution/location;
- its collection mandate and access policy;
- relevant publication technology;
- whether the work/edition exists in approved lore;
- ownership/custody facts;
- named authors/editors only when continuity supports them;
- whether a correction became public;
- what Minecraft should materialize;
- whether any claimed mechanical knowledge has a rules-backed effect.

Until then every seed in this file remains PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
