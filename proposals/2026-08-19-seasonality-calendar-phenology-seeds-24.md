# Ouros Seasonality, Calendar & Phenology Seeds — Pass 24

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character or dialogue is copied.

These seeds assume the architecture in `design/seasonality-calendar-phenology-layer.md`. Exact PTU/Caelo mechanics remain pending validation.

## 1. First Thaw Survey

A mountain settlement does not reopen one route by calendar date. Local guides wait for a combination of snow retreat, stream level and Pokémon activity.

The first player-visible hook is a dispute between a printed reopening notice and current field observations.

Useful systems:
- phenology;
- travel;
- science;
- media/communications;
- settlement services.

## 2. The Empty Bloom

A valley's flowering period arrives on schedule, but the pollinator activity normally associated with it is absent.

The missing activity is an observation, not proof of population collapse.

Potential causes remain open until research establishes one.

## 3. The Late Caravan

A seasonal trading caravan has arrived within the same broad window for years. This year it is late.

Possible investigation branches:
- transport problem;
- route reroute;
- market decision;
- regional crisis;
- simple schedule change.

Do not declare foul play without evidence.

## 4. Winter Road Becomes a Shortcut

A wetland route that is awkward during warmer phases becomes easier to cross after a normal freeze.

Minecraft can change the route's visual/access state while AutoPTU uses ordinary static geometry until environmental battle mechanics are ready.

## 5. Autumn Access Archive

Fallen vegetation exposes an old maintenance path for only part of the year.

The path leads to records and infrastructure rather than treasure by default. Its return value can change after prior visits.

## 6. Nesting Detour

A well-used trail overlaps a nesting site during one recurring window.

The local transport service temporarily reroutes instead of treating the Pokémon as enemies.

Players may help map an alternate trail, communicate closures or investigate why the nesting window shifted.

## 7. The Season Research Station

A small field station only operates during two phases each year because its work depends on a local ecological cycle.

Former players' reports become historical datasets for later years.

This creates scientific continuity without requiring a massive permanent laboratory.

## 8. A Festival That Waits for the Pokémon

A town celebration is tied to the first confirmed arrival of a recurring wild collective rather than a fixed date.

The event can move earlier or later each year.

If the collective does not arrive, the community must decide whether to postpone, adapt or hold the social parts without pretending the ecological sign occurred.

## 9. The Wrong Calendar

An old regional guide uses a fixed date for an event now known to vary with ecological conditions.

Visitors arrive too early, creating pressure on transport and hospitality while researchers explain that the event has not started.

No villain is required.

## 10. Seasonal Trainer Circuit

A group of recurring Trainers travels between several settlements during different phases of the year.

Their presence creates social scenes, rematches and public memory without making them mandatory bosses.

Battle implementation still follows Battle Institution/Challenge contracts.

## 11. The Silent Marsh

A marsh is normally loud during one seasonal window. This year players notice the soundscape has changed.

The system records absence as evidence.

Investigation can connect to weather, pollution, migration, predation or ordinary variation.

## 12. Orchard Frost Watch

A farming community monitors an expected cold window because one unusual early frost could affect the harvest.

Players can contribute forecasts, shelter materials, transport or field observations.

No crop yield or frost damage math is invented by the narrative layer.

## 13. The Second Migration

Players witness the same collective migration in two different years.

The second edition references:
- prior route decisions;
- disturbances caused last year;
- new infrastructure;
- changed public knowledge;
- individual Pokémon previously identified.

This is a direct test of annual continuity.

## 14. Seasonal Ferry Schedule

A ferry route remains physically viable year-round but changes frequency during one storm-prone phase.

A missed ferry is usually a scheduling problem, not a quest. It becomes content only when the schedule intersects an expedition, emergency or personal objective.

## 15. Underground Summer

A cave network remains relatively stable while the surface changes dramatically.

During the hottest phase, more Pokémon and people use it as refuge, changing the social/ecological context without changing the dungeon identity.

## 16. The Frozen Entrance

An old ruin can be reached only when a shallow flooded chamber freezes or drains during a recurring phase.

The archaeology layer determines what may be disturbed. The season layer controls access timing. The battle layer remains separate.

## 17. A Market With Two Calendars

One market follows civic dates. Another follows harvest readiness.

A year with delayed crops causes the two schedules to diverge and creates cultural/economic tension without making either group irrational.

## 18. Out-of-Season Deerling Report

A researcher receives a credible report of a seasonal form outside the locally expected window.

The correct first response is verification, not declaring a supernatural event.

Possible results include misidentification, movement from another area, local microclimate or a genuine anomaly.

## 19. Storm Season Is Normal

A region experiences strong storms every year. Residents have infrastructure and routines for them.

The story appears when preparation fails, timing changes, infrastructure degrades or the storm exceeds the established normal band.

This keeps ordinary seasonal weather from becoming constant disaster quests.

## 20. First Snow Ceremony

A small community marks the first locally observed snow with a social ceremony.

There is no assumption that the ceremony creates mechanical Weather or invokes a Legendary Pokémon.

Its meaning comes from community tradition and previous editions.

## 21. The Long Evening League

A seasonal exhibition circuit uses longer evening daylight to hold public matches and social events outdoors.

The season changes scheduling and atmosphere. It does not change initiative, Accuracy or Move rules.

## 22. Winter Clinic Load

A care facility expects a recurring increase in specific categories of cases during one phase.

The care system tracks aggregate capacity and privacy-safe signals.

An unusual spike can become a research or infrastructure problem without exposing individual patient records.

## 23. The Missed Festival

A player returns after an annual event has already occurred.

Instead of silently deleting the content, the world provides:
- archived results;
- changed decorations/cleanup state;
- NPC recollections;
- consequences;
- a possible next-edition hook.

The player's personal progression remains intact.

## 24. The Unseasonal Heatwave

A heat event occurs during a phase where residents historically expect cooler conditions.

The anomaly exists only because the region has a known baseline.

Potential effects are initially narrative/world-state:
- altered schedules;
- water demand;
- wildlife observations;
- transport adjustments;
- research activity.

No PTU heat damage or status is invented.

## 25. Migratory Stopover Becomes Permanent

A group that normally uses a location briefly begins staying longer each year.

The change gradually affects:
- local ecology;
- tourism;
- public works;
- research;
- neighborhood behavior;
- food availability.

Players may disagree about whether intervention is desirable.

## 26. The Calendar Keeper

An institution maintains official event dates, historical seasonal records and route-opening archives.

Its records can contain errors, gaps and revisions. Institutional authority does not make every historical claim true.

## 27. Field Signs Before the UI

A region teaches experienced travelers to recognize an approaching phase through Pokémon calls, plant changes, wind patterns and service preparations.

The player UI may summarize the phase after discovery, but the world itself remains legible.

## 28. Seasonal Base Refit

A mobile expedition base requires different equipment/configuration in different regional phases.

The decision changes overworld preparedness and logistics. It does not grant arbitrary battle bonuses.

## 29. Festival Safety Review

A recurring event had a crowding problem in a previous edition.

Next year, organizers alter routes, schedules and capacity.

The event's history creates visible institutional learning instead of resetting.

## 30. The Fifth Year Dataset

After several in-world years, a research program finally has enough repeated observations to identify a meaningful timing shift.

The arc rewards long-term world persistence: the anomaly could not have been generated responsibly during year one.

# Longer Arc A — The River Calendar

## Premise

Several settlements along one river use different seasonal signals to organize transport, agriculture and public events. A sequence of unusual timing shifts causes those local calendars to disagree more than usual.

## Phase 1 — Normal variation

Players encounter small inconsistencies:
- one ferry opens later;
- one crop is ready early;
- a migratory collective has not arrived;
- a festival committee delays an announcement.

No central threat is declared.

## Phase 2 — Evidence accumulation

Researchers and residents compare records. Some differences are measurement errors; others are real.

Players can contribute through:
- field observation;
- route surveys;
- archive work;
- communications;
- transport support;
- social mediation.

## Phase 3 — Competing adaptations

Settlements propose different responses. One wants infrastructure upgrades. Another changes festival dates. Another protects a migration corridor.

The civic-governance layer handles decisions; the season layer supplies timing evidence.

## Phase 4 — Persistent outcome

The region does not return to one perfect schedule. It adopts new monitoring and more flexible institutions.

Future years can reference the changes.

No boss fight is required.

# Longer Arc B — Four Visits to One Pass

## Premise

A single mountain pass becomes a recurring landmark visited across four different seasonal phases.

## Visit 1

Players learn the normal route and meet local residents/wildlife.

## Visit 2

A seasonal change opens a temporary side route and reveals historical infrastructure.

## Visit 3

A migration overlaps the pass. The challenge is coexistence and route management, not automatically combat.

## Visit 4

A weather anomaly makes the expected seasonal layout unreliable. Players now understand enough baseline state to recognize the difference.

## Outcome

The pass gains history, public records and player memories without requiring four separate locations.

# Longer Arc C — The Festival That Changes Its Date

## Premise

A regional celebration was once scheduled by fixed civic calendar, but its original ecological basis was the arrival of a Pokémon migration.

## Early arc

Older residents and archives disagree about what the festival is actually commemorating.

## Middle arc

Scientists produce evidence that the migration timing has shifted over several years. Businesses prefer a predictable date. Conservation groups want the event to follow the animals. Organizers worry about visitors and transport.

No group is automatically correct on every issue.

## Player involvement

Players can participate through:
- historical research;
- ecological surveys;
- civic consultation;
- event logistics;
- communications;
- transport planning;
- optional exhibition/contest content.

## Possible outcomes

The event may:
- retain a fixed date but separate the ecological ceremony;
- adopt a flexible window;
- split into two related events;
- pause for one year;
- move location.

The chosen structure becomes part of future event-edition history.

# Encounter implementation notes

## Frostline Crossing

Full concept may require:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- complete movement if sliding or displacement matters;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced form:
- static legal terrain/blockers;
- seasonal visuals and route access handled in overworld state;
- standard encounter only when needed.

## Migration Corridor Bottleneck

Full concept may require:
- non-DEFEAT objective support;
- complete movement/interception/forced movement;
- AI tactical policy that understands escape/route goals;
- adapter/playback support.

Reduced form:
- collective routing handled outside battle;
- one ordinary encounter with a subgroup;
- world-state outcome combines battle result with prior overworld choices.

## Storm-Season Relay

Full concept may require:
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- full lifecycle timing;
- objective interaction;
- AI tactical policy;
- adapter/playback.

Reduced form:
- weather remains visual/world-state context;
- relay interaction stays outside grid;
- static standard battle if conflict occurs.
