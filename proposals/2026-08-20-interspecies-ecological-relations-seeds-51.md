# Ouros Interspecies Ecological Relations Seeds — Pass 51

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research structures. No external plot, prose or distinctive character is transplanted.

Date: 2026-08-20

## Design intent

These seeds exercise the new ecological-relation layer while reusing existing systems for observation, science, wild collectives, conservation, settlements, seasonality, travel and persistent Pokémon identity.

Every seed begins from a relationship or suspected relationship between organisms and asks what players can learn or change.

Predators are not automatically villains. Prey are not automatically rescue targets. A battle result does not automatically decide the ecology.

## Seed 01 — The Missing Followers

A coastal population normally appears with a smaller scavenging species nearby. This season the larger Pokémon are present but the followers are absent.

Possible explanations remain open:

- the food source changed;
- the smaller species moved its activity window;
- pollution affected one species differently;
- survey coverage missed the association;
- another resource became more attractive elsewhere.

Useful activities: photography, repeat survey, water testing, local testimony, comparison with historical records.

## Seed 02 — The Narrow Feeding Corridor

Road construction compresses two wild populations into the same feeding corridor. Conflict increases near one culvert.

The obvious solution is not necessarily to remove one population. Players can investigate route design, timing, alternate access, temporary barriers or habitat restoration.

Encounter contract: `Feeding Corridor Disturbance` in the design layer.

## Seed 03 — Scavengers After the Storm

After a severe storm, scavenger Pokémon gather around damaged orchards and roadside debris. Residents interpret the concentration as an invasion.

The actual story may involve fallen fruit, damaged storage, displaced wildlife and temporary opportunity rather than population growth.

## Seed 04 — The Predator Everyone Blames

A predator species becomes visible near a village at the same time that another species becomes harder to find.

Several residents conclude the predator caused the decline.

Survey data later shows that the supposedly missing species shifted to a new water source after construction changed runoff.

The predator may still be hunting normally. The causal accusation was wrong.

## Seed 05 — The Nesting Switch

A persistent collective moves its nesting site into an abandoned rail cutting.

Another species that previously used the cutting for shelter now appears more often near homes.

The quest is about indirect displacement, not a direct fight between the two groups.

## Seed 06 — Two Honest Food Maps

Two research teams publish different feeding-range maps for the same species.

Both are correct because one survey occurred during a dry season and the other after a wet year.

The conflict becomes a lesson in versioned ecology rather than research fraud.

## Seed 07 — The Orchard Night Shift

A nocturnal species begins feeding in an orchard after artificial lighting changes insect activity.

Farmers see the new Pokémon first. Researchers who only survey during the day initially miss the pattern.

This connects workplace schedules, infrastructure and ecology.

## Seed 08 — The Broken Association

Two species with a long-recorded local association stop appearing together.

Neither population seems to be collapsing.

The real hook is to discover what environmental or behavioral condition used to connect them.

## Seed 09 — The False Alpha Problem

Visitors repeatedly photograph the largest member of a mixed-species group and call it the leader.

Longer observation shows that movement decisions do not consistently originate from that individual.

This seed reinforces evidence-based leadership and association state.

## Seed 10 — The Parasite Study

A research institution studies a species with a biologically established host relationship.

Public discussion begins treating the relationship as a disease that players should cure.

The actual project asks a narrower question: what environmental conditions correlate with different observed states?

No treatment or status mechanic is generated without PTU/Caelo authority.

## Seed 11 — One Weak Individual

A camera network repeatedly records a predator following one visibly slower individual from another species.

The system records the observation but does not infer Injury, age, disease or impending death.

Players can investigate the individual's condition only through valid observation/care mechanics.

## Seed 12 — The Waterhole Rotation

Several species share one water source but arrive at different times of day.

A new tourist viewing platform shifts visitor traffic into one time window and changes which species remain visible.

The apparent disappearance is partly a schedule change.

## Seed 13 — Market-Day Foragers

A wild species learns that market cleanup leaves accessible food every week.

Another scavenger follows the first species rather than the humans.

A change in waste policy modifies both associations.

## Seed 14 — The Quiet Nest Defense

Surveyors need to cross near a nesting site during a short seasonal window.

The defending collective is not attacking the settlement. It reacts only to approach.

Players can reroute, wait, observe, deter or enter deliberately.

Encounter contract: `Nest Defense Survey`.

## Seed 15 — The Reef Clean-Up Paradox

A successful restoration project increases one habitat-forming population.

A species that feeds around that habitat also becomes more visible, leading visitors to claim restoration caused a new predator problem.

The correct response requires separating expected ecological recovery from actual risk.

## Seed 16 — The Empty Remains

Researchers find feeding remains in several locations but never observe the interaction itself.

Possible culprits overlap in range.

The relation remains a claim until stronger evidence appears.

This seed is designed for evidence graphs, camera traps and acoustic monitoring.

## Seed 17 — The New Shelter User

A species begins occupying culverts built for flood management.

Another species already uses those spaces during storms.

The result is intermittent shelter competition tied to weather, not permanent territorial war.

## Seed 18 — The Released Former Partner

A previously partnered Pokémon that was released months ago is observed joining ordinary feeding activity near a wild collective.

The former Trainer's history remains relevant, but the system does not assume the Pokémon wants to return or that it leads the group.

## Seed 19 — Fishing Pressure

A harbor's fishing activity changes the availability of one resource species.

Wild Pokémon that previously fed farther offshore begin appearing nearer breakwaters.

The problem connects workplaces, maritime policy, ecology and tourism.

No fishing yield or population formula is invented automatically.

## Seed 20 — The Temporary Truce at the Spring

During drought, two normally separated collectives repeatedly use the same spring without observed conflict.

Residents call it a truce.

Researchers record only shared resource use and low observed aggression. They do not infer an agreement between Pokémon.

## Seed 21 — The Scavenger Census Error

A sudden increase in scavenger sightings appears alarming.

The actual cause is improved detection after a new observation tower opened.

The population may be stable.

This seed tests survey-effort correction.

## Seed 22 — Habitat Engineer

A species repeatedly changes the physical environment in a way that creates shelter for other Pokémon.

A proposed infrastructure project would remove those structures.

The question becomes whether the secondary habitat function is important enough to change the project design.

No mechanical terrain creation is inferred from the ecological observation.

## Seed 23 — The Wrong Rescue

Players find a predator pursuing another wild Pokémon and intervene immediately.

Later evidence shows the interaction was ordinary local predation and no human-caused disturbance existed.

The consequence is not punishment. It becomes a monitoring question: did repeated intervention alter behavior around the route?

## Seed 24 — The Association That Only Exists in Winter

Two species appear together every winter and separately the rest of the year.

The shared factor might be shelter, food, temperature or migration timing.

The pattern becomes visible only after several years of calendar-linked observations.

## Seed 25 — The Industrial Edge

A factory's warm-water discharge creates a local feeding hotspot used by several species.

When the plant upgrades and the discharge stops, the wildlife pattern changes.

Nothing is mechanically wrong with the new infrastructure. The ecological relation was tied to an artificial resource that disappeared.

## Seed 26 — Competing Restoration Goals

One conservation program restores dense vegetation for a nesting species.

Another locally important species loses open foraging ground.

Both conservation goals are legitimate.

Players help compare zoning and phased restoration rather than choosing a good species and a bad species.

## Seed 27 — The Misread Battle Footage

Public battle footage shows one wild species attacking another near a road.

Viewers label the attacker a predator.

Field evidence later suggests the fight was territorial or protective rather than feeding behavior.

The visual-evidence layer preserves the footage while the ecological interpretation changes.

## Seed 28 — The Unfinished Food Web

A regional field notebook contains five well-supported ecological edges and twelve speculative ones.

A new research program asks players to improve confidence edge by edge instead of filling an entire encyclopedia at once.

This can become a long-running optional progression system with no combat requirement.

## Seed 29 — The Resource Pulse

A rare mass fruiting event creates a temporary concentration of several species.

Predators, scavengers, researchers, tourists and vendors all respond differently.

The event ends naturally. The region should not permanently retain the temporary encounter table.

## Seed 30 — The Return of the Old Route

A trail closed for years reopens after habitat restoration.

Wildlife has developed new movement patterns during the closure.

The reopening becomes a monitoring project rather than restoring the route's old encounter ecology from a saved template.

# Longer arc A — The Three-Link Valley

Year 1: a prey population shifts uphill.

Year 2: a predator begins appearing near farms.

Year 3: scavengers become more common along a transport route.

Public opinion treats the three changes as one worsening predator problem.

Players gradually discover that all three are connected to a changed water system, but each edge has a different mechanism and confidence level.

Possible outcomes include infrastructure revision, habitat restoration, changed survey practices and a stable new ecological equilibrium.

No boss is required.

# Longer arc B — The Coast That Learned New Habits

A coastal district changes over several seasons:

- new market waste attracts one forager;
- another species begins following it;
- tourism increases because the animals are easy to see;
- crowd pressure shifts the original species to night activity;
- guides misinterpret the change as disappearance;
- a revised waste and visitor-management plan creates another equilibrium.

The arc reuses tourism, media, workplaces, conservation and seasonality.

# Longer arc C — Field Guide Revision

An old regional field guide contains ecological relationships accepted for decades.

Players and NPC researchers gradually discover that some remain accurate, some were seasonal observations generalized too broadly, and some were correlations caused by historical land use that no longer exists.

The campaign goal is not to expose a fraudulent scientist. It is to produce a better versioned model of the living region.

# Encounter implementation notes

## Feeding Corridor Disturbance

Reduced implementation can run now with ordinary static movement/targeting and a conventional legal battle if one subgroup becomes hostile.

Full implementation needs:

- complete movement/interception/forced movement;
- terrain/zones/reactions if the corridor itself is tactical;
- tactical AI capable of CROSS, PROTECT and WITHDRAW priorities;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon playback for visible route use.

## Opportunistic Scavengers

Reduced implementation keeps arrivals and resource interaction outside battle.

Full implementation needs:

- lifecycle support for timed arrivals;
- complete movement for approach/withdrawal;
- tactical AI for resource investigation and retreat;
- hazards/zones only if storm debris matters mechanically;
- adapter/playback.

## Nest Defense Survey

Reduced implementation keeps the nest and protected young outside the grid and opens a static encounter only if needed.

Full implementation needs:

- complete movement/interception;
- zone control/reactions;
- tactical AI for defend/withdraw/pass-through goals;
- adapter/playback.

# Canon guardrails

None of these proposals establish:

- death or consumption from a battle result;
- hunger mechanics;
- predator/prey combat modifiers;
- morale/fear;
- pack bonuses;
- population mathematics;
- ecological morality;
- ownership of wild Pokémon;
- automatic conservation restrictions;
- species-specific AI behavior not backed by engine/rules evidence.

Promotion requires authored regional fit, species availability, provenance, PTU/Caelo mechanical review where needed and consistency with existing Ouros world state.
