## Long Arc — Five Years Around Market Square

Year 1 establishes ordinary urban wildlife observations and vendor routines. Year 2 introduces increased aggregation after a change in food-service practices. Year 3 produces public complaints, photographs and an intervention focused on attractants. Year 4 shows redistribution rather than disappearance. Year 5 forces institutions to compare what residents remember with the actual observation ledger. The arc can resolve through coexistence, altered routines and better public information without requiring a villain or boss encounter.

## Long Arc — The Electric Block

A neighborhood develops recurring Joltik sightings, minor outages and conflicting explanations. Technology records show some faults predate the sightings. Lighting, building age and utility maintenance change over several years. A small research program distinguishes which events correlate with Pokémon presence and which do not. The final answer may be mixed rather than singular.

## Long Arc — Rooflines of Meridian

A decade of renovations, new gardens, abandoned structures and transit construction changes rooftop and ledge habitat across one district. Different populations adopt and leave sites over time. Architecture, Urban Public Space, Light, Soundscapes, Diel Activity and Conservation all contribute evidence. The result is a readable urban-ecology history rather than a sequence of disconnected random encounters.
