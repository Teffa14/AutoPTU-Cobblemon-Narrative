# Ouros Wildlife Monitoring & Telemetry Seeds — Pass 79

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes a region, institution, technology, species practice, law, device type or historical event as Ouros canon.

These candidates are designed to exercise `design/wildlife-monitoring-tagging-telemetry-extension.md` while preserving the existing Science, Conservation, Photography, Pokémon Agency, Wild Collective and Cobblemon/AutoPTU boundaries.

## The Signal That Stops at the Same Hour

A monitoring program sees the same individual vanish from a receiver network at nearly the same time on several days.

Initial interpretations compete:

- the Pokémon leaves the monitored corridor;
- a local obstruction blocks reception;
- one receiver loses power during that period;
- the device changes orientation during a repeated behavior;
- the assumed route is wrong.

The player can inspect receiver uptime, direct observations, camera records and environmental context.

The satisfying outcome is a narrower explanation, not necessarily a dramatic revelation.

Persistence:

Once understood, the same daily behavior can become a reliable piece of regional ecological knowledge. If infrastructure changes later, the old gap pattern remains useful historical evidence.

## Tag Found, Pokémon Unknown

A detached device is recovered far from its last confirmed detection.

The device contains valid data up to a point, but no evidence proves exactly when or where it separated from the Pokémon.

The finder can return it through research/property custody. Researchers may then choose to:

- search for a visual re-sighting;
- inspect the device for failure;
- leave the individual unlocated;
- revise the deployment end time later if new evidence appears.

The proposal explicitly avoids the common quest shortcut where finding a collar means the animal is dead nearby.

## Two Individuals, One Old Identifier

A visible research mark is reported on two similar Pokémon in different places.

Possible explanations include:

- one observation was misclassified;
- an old identifier was reused improperly;
- two marking systems use confusingly similar notation;
- one visual record is too poor to support individual identification;
- historical documentation merged two subjects.

This is a provenance mystery rather than a fraud story by default.

Persistent consequence:

The research institution revises how marks are recorded or displayed. Older records keep their uncertainty instead of being silently rewritten.

## The Pokémon That Never Needed a Tag

A field team proposes artificial monitoring for a recurring wild individual, but existing photographs, natural markings and repeated route observations may already answer the research question.

The player can help compare methods.

Possible conclusion:

The program chooses a lower-disturbance method and redirects the device budget or equipment elsewhere.

This candidate exists to prevent “more technology” from functioning as the automatic superior solution.

## Receiver Four Is Quiet

A monitoring station stops producing detections during an important seasonal window.

The subject is not the first thing to investigate. The player checks:

- power/service state;
- physical access;
- device clock or data backlog;
- maintenance history;
- nearby visual/acoustic observations;
- whether other stations recorded expected traffic.

Possible outcome:

The station was offline and the migration continued normally.

Alternative outcome:

The station worked correctly and several tracked individuals really did use a different corridor.

Both outcomes preserve the usefulness of careful verification.

## The Route Nobody Expected

Several individually monitored Pokémon from one recurring collective begin appearing along a route that the collective was not previously known to use.

The first detection creates a question.

The second creates a pattern candidate.

Direct observation or several independent individuals may eventually justify a revised corridor hypothesis.

Possible downstream hooks:

- Travel reviews a planned route/service;
- Conservation reviews a corridor boundary;
- a settlement notices more wildlife near one edge;
- a public map remains deliberately approximate;
- a construction project now intersects an uncertain ecological route.

No single tracked Pokémon rewrites collective state by itself.

## The Public Map Is Deliberately Vague

Researchers publish that a monitored population uses a broad upland corridor but omit precise detections.

A visitor assumes the map is inaccurate after seeing a more detailed internal display.

The story becomes about information scope:

- what is public;
- what is current;
- what was intentionally generalized;
- who has access to exact coordinates;
- whether the reason for withholding detail is itself public.

No universal privacy or conservation law is assumed.

## The Old Deployment Is Still Sending Data

A device supposedly removed months ago produces a new detection.

The program cannot simply attach the event to the old subject.

The investigation checks:

- device custody history;
- whether it was redeployed;
- whether the removal record was wrong;
- duplicate serials;
- receiver parsing;
- whether the detection belongs to a test transmission.

This seed directly tests the subject/device/deployment separation.

## The Natural Mark Changes

Researchers have used a visible physical feature to identify a wild Pokémon for years. A new photograph no longer matches the historical description perfectly.

Possible explanations:

- ordinary growth or healing changed the mark;
- the new observation is a different individual;
- older photographs were interpreted too confidently;
- lighting/view angle changed apparent features.

Care may become relevant only if an actual health observation exists. The system must not diagnose injury from identity mismatch alone.

## One Receiver, Three Institutions

A monitoring tower or station supports several programs:

- migration research;
- acoustic ecology;
- protected-area management;
- perhaps a weather or communications sensor owned elsewhere.

Maintenance is delayed and every program describes the impact differently.

The gameplay comes from shared infrastructure and differing evidence needs, not from inventing one institution as incompetent.

Long-term consequence:

After repair, the station gains a clearer maintenance/handoff record and becomes a recurring regional landmark.

## The Re-sighting After Release

A previously partnered or captured Pokémon was released through an authoritative past event and later becomes part of a monitoring program.

Months later the player documents a re-sighting.

The old Trainer association remains historical.

The new observation does not automatically mean:

- the Pokémon wants to return;
- the release was a mistake;
- the Pokémon recognizes everyone present;
- the player can issue battle commands;
- ownership revived.

This candidate connects Pokémon Agency continuity with field ecology while keeping feelings and legal state evidence-based.

## The Device Returns Before the Data

A temporary archival sensor releases or is recovered normally, but its stored data cannot be read immediately because the receiving system, specialist or equipment is unavailable.

The physical recovery and the scientific result are different milestones.

Possible callbacks:

- the device waits in custody;
- the research question remains open;
- another expedition happens before analysis;
- later data contradict what observers assumed in the meantime.

## Four Sightings, Two Individuals

Four visual records appear to document one individual crossing a large area quickly.

Identity review finds that the records probably contain two similar Pokémon.

The correction changes:

- the inferred route;
- the apparent travel speed;
- the collective membership hypothesis;
- perhaps a public story already built around the “remarkable journey.”

The earlier interpretation remains part of research/public-memory history.

## The Monitoring Method Changes the Behavior

A program notices that a wild individual changes its routine shortly after researchers begin a more intrusive observation method.

Causality is initially uncertain.

The program can pause the method and compare:

- behavior before deployment;
- behavior during deployment;
- other individuals;
- direct observations;
- environmental changes occurring at the same time.

The arc can resolve with an improved method, unresolved uncertainty, or evidence that another factor mattered more.

No hidden “stress meter” is invented.

## A Valley Learns Its Individuals

Long-term arc.

Phase 1: baseline.

Researchers and local residents know that a species/collective uses the valley, but individual identity is mostly unknown.

Phase 2: first persistent subjects.

Natural markings, repeated photographs or an approved monitoring method establish a few durable individual records.

Phase 3: route assumptions.

Several observations create a plausible seasonal pattern.

Phase 4: contradiction.

A subject appears somewhere unexpected or a receiver gap undermines the original model.

Phase 5: method review.

The program adds, removes or relocates stations and may abandon one technique.

Phase 6: external decision.

Travel, Conservation, Public Works or a local institution considers a project that depends on how the valley is actually used.

Phase 7: revisitation.

Months later, some individuals return, some remain unobserved and perhaps new subjects appear. The old records make the difference legible without forcing every absence into a dramatic explanation.

This arc should feel like a place accumulating knowledge rather than a questline exhausting a location.

## Mystery — Six Detections, Five Real Events

A dataset contains six apparent receiver detections for one deployment.

The investigation compares:

- raw timestamps;
- receiver identity;
- duplicate ingestion;
- device deployment window;
- station uptime;
- direct re-sightings;
- data-system version history.

One detection may be duplicated or associated with the wrong deployment.

The correction can materially change an inferred route while still leaving the remaining five detections valid.

No saboteur is required.

## Encounter — Receiver Ridge Withdrawal

Narrative premise:

A field team reaches a receiver station to retrieve data during a narrow monitoring window. Wild Pokémon occupy the safe approach and respond territorially.

Intended full version:

Researchers withdraw through explicit exits while combatants secure access. The battle can include WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE logic, narrow geometry, interception/forced movement if selected, active terrain/weather only when mechanically supported, territorial AI and adapter playback.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when selected;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING when active;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

The field team exits the combat area first. The station is outside the grid and cannot be targeted. A static reviewed arena hosts an ordinary legal battle. After AutoPTU resolves the encounter, Ouros decides whether the route to the station can be reopened. No invented hazard, weather modifier, protection rule or knockback script is used.

World-state outputs may include:

- receiver_access_state;
- field_team_withdrawal_complete;
- retrieval_attempt_state;
- disturbance_observation;
- next_monitoring_window;
- coexistence_incident_ref.

## Encounter — Tag Recovery at the Waterline

Narrative premise:

A detached research device is located near a shoreline or wetland used by a monitored collective. Recovery matters for provenance and stored data; the original monitored Pokémon is not assumed present.

Intended full version:

The recovery point can function as an explicit objective while wild Pokémon defend or leave the area. Water/shore routing, weather, forced movement and territorial AI can matter only when the relevant capabilities are implemented and validated.

Capability dependencies:

The full profile can depend on the same families as Receiver Ridge, especially complete movement, environmental state, tactical AI and adapter playback.

Reduced version:

The device recovery is an overworld interaction before or after a conventional battle. The device never becomes tactical loot. Static geometry represents water safely. AutoPTU resolves only actual combatants chosen by Ouros.

Recovery does not prove the deployment end cause or current location of the monitored Pokémon.

## Encounter — Quiet Re-sighting

Narrative premise:

Players attempt to confirm whether a recurring wild Pokémon has returned to a known area.

Primary version:

No battle. Players gather visual, temporal and location evidence while choosing how much disturbance to create.

Possible consequences:

- supported identity match;
- rejected match;
- better photograph but unresolved identity;
- observation of new behavior;
- evidence that the assumed route is outdated;
- no detection during a valid observation window.

If combat becomes necessary due to a separate threat, Ouros instantiates a battle independently. The re-identification claim is never inferred from the battle roster.
