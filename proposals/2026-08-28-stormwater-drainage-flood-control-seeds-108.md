## Design intent

These situations make rain and flooding persistent social/infrastructure history instead of one-off spectacle.

They deliberately avoid:

- generic flood damage rules;
- automatic Water-type solutions;
- hidden culprit logic;
- global city flood meters;
- Minecraft water as world-state authority;
- assigning blame to nearby wild Pokémon;
- automatic road reopening after water disappears;
- tactical currents, drowning or contaminated-water effects without governing mechanics.

## Situation seed — The Street Is Dry, the Underpass Is Closed

A low underpass visibly drains after a storm. Residents assume the closure should end immediately, but the crossing owner is still waiting for a post-event inspection of a culvert and retaining structure.

Playable pressure comes from consequences rather than a secret villain:

- a road detour stays active;
- a shuttle changes stops;
- one shop sees less traffic;
- a courier route becomes longer;
- residents disagree about whether the closure is excessive;
- old photos show that a visually similar event once had a different cause.

Resolution can establish why the restriction remains, what inspection was completed and what evidence is still missing.

Mechanical profile: noncombat executable now. Any tactical scene should use the reduced Culvert Access Perimeter contract.

## Situation seed — The Drain Was Clear at Dawn

A maintenance record says an inlet was clear early in the morning. Later, the same corner floods and a resident claims the crew failed to do its job.

The investigation compares:

- inlet observation time;
- rainfall/weather observations;
- downstream link state;
- later debris observations;
- photographs;
- work records;
- neighboring catchment reports.

Possible outcomes include a later obstruction, a downstream constraint, evidence that the inlet was never the limiting point, or bounded uncertainty.

No outcome is preselected by the generator.

## Situation seed — The Pokémon Under the Grate

A particular wild Pokémon is repeatedly seen near one drainage access point shortly before several complaints of ponding.

The community develops competing explanations. Conservation records may eventually show that the Pokémon uses the space for shelter or food. Maintenance may find an unrelated obstruction elsewhere. Or evidence may remain incomplete.

Important constraint:

The Pokémon's presence is an observation. Species identity never establishes flood prediction, blockage, guilt or a maintenance capability.

Potential long-term consequence:

If the access point is redesigned, Conservation may need a habitat review while Stormwater remains focused on drainage operation.

## Situation seed — The Temporary Pump Became a Landmark

A temporary pump site operates for several weeks after repeated flooding. People begin giving directions using the pump enclosure as a landmark. Vendors and neighbors adjust routines around the temporary access restriction.

After normal drainage returns, the technical equipment can be removed while the place-name survives.

Later story hooks:

- an old message refers to “meet by the pump” after the pump is gone;
- Public Memory records the flood season differently from official operations logs;
- a future response team considers using the same site because historical records show it was accessible;
- the area has since become a small public space, creating a genuine civic tradeoff.

## Situation seed — The Basin Filled Exactly as Intended

After intense rain, a low landscaped area or authored detention asset holds water. Residents who do not know its function report that the park has flooded and failed.

Operations records instead show that the asset entered an expected authored storage state, while another nearby intersection experienced the actual service problem.

The story is about interpretation and communication, not proving that every filled basin is safe or correctly designed.

The exact facility type and operating regime remain canon questions.

## Situation seed — The Old Culvert Is Still on Every Map

A former drainage crossing was superseded years ago, but old maps, oral directions and maintenance notes continue using its name.

A new blockage report arrives under the old name. Different crews initially appear to contradict each other because they inspected different physical assets associated with the same historical label.

This can connect Cartography, Roads, Maintenance and Stormwater without a combat requirement.

## Situation seed — The Water Left One Building Last

A block drains unevenly. Most storefronts and residences return to normal while one building remains inaccessible.

Stormwater can establish that the public drainage sector is verified. Residential, Commercial Services, Maintenance or another building owner must determine the remaining local condition.

The quest teaches a durable rule:

public drainage recovery does not automatically restore every endpoint or property.

## Situation seed — The Outfall Is Open, the Habitat Review Is Not

A drainage outfall passes operational verification after repair. Wildlife observations downstream changed during the incident, so Conservation continues a separate review.

The system may operate while ecological interpretation remains open, or an authorized restriction may remain for another reason. Neither system overwrites the other.

This creates follow-up content months after the engineering problem is solved.

## Situation seed — The Debris Came From Somewhere Else

A culvert obstruction contains branches, packaging, sediment and unfamiliar material. Public rumor quickly identifies a nearby business or wild Pokémon group as the source.

Waste provenance, upstream observations and weather history show that material can move before it is observed.

The quest can end with:

- a supported source chain;
- multiple contributing sources;
- insufficient evidence;
- a service fix with the provenance question still unresolved.

No accusation is promoted merely because an object has a recognizable mark.

## Situation seed — The New Drain Changed the Quiet Corner

A targeted drainage improvement solves repeated ponding at one intersection. The street becomes easier to use during rain, which changes pedestrian routes, roadside waiting behavior and nearby wildlife observations.

The infrastructure fix succeeds while creating new ordinary world state.

Months later the changed foot traffic, not the flood itself, can seed another story.

## Mystery — Five Flood Reports, Three Actual Places

Five reports appear to describe a single escalating flood.

The records contain:

- an underpass name;
- an old neighborhood nickname;
- two street intersections;
- a photo without orientation;
- three different timestamps.

Investigation resolves stable location IDs and shows that some reports describe the same catchment point while others describe distinct downstream effects.

The outcome may be:

- three places affected at different times;
- two reports duplicated by reposting;
- one stale photograph attached to a current message;
- unresolved identity for one observation.

The system never forces one universal flood event if provenance does not support it.

## Mystery — Four Clearings, One Reopening

Different records state that the blockage was cleared at four times.

All can be legitimate milestones:

1. visible debris removed from the inlet;
2. downstream culvert access restored;
3. drainage path verified;
4. Road Operations lifted the crossing restriction.

The mystery is solved by scope, not by choosing which witness lied.

## Character archetype candidate — The Catchment Walker

A recurring municipal or cooperative worker knows where water usually appears first, which access points have old names, and which residents report quickly.

Their value is longitudinal observation and local memory, not supernatural detection.

They can become a bridge between Maintenance, Public Works, Weather and neighborhood NPCs.

No profession automatically grants PTU Skills, Features or flood-control mechanics.

## Character archetype candidate — The Shopkeeper at the Low Corner

A business owner has years of photographs and improvised marks showing previous water extents near the storefront.

The record can be useful evidence while remaining informal and location-specific.

Their commercial interest may bias interpretation, but the system should not assume dishonesty.

## Institution candidate — The Drainage Field Unit

A small operational team handles inspections, blockage response and verification for an authored district.

Possible recurring roles:

- field observer;
- maintenance liaison;
- pump/asset operator if canon supports it;
- public-contact worker;
- ecological liaison for sensitive sites.

Exact staffing, technology, authority and Pokémon participation remain undecided canon.

## Long arc — A Neighborhood Learns Its Low Points

Stage 1 establishes ordinary rainy-day behavior. Residents know which corners pond briefly, which routes stay reliable and which workers appear after storms.

Stage 2 introduces a meaningful but localized drainage problem. Reports disagree because different streets and timestamps are being compared.

Stage 3 activates a temporary solution: a pump, barrier, closure or alternate drainage path. The workaround changes travel, deliveries and public-space use.

Stage 4 separates physical drainage from verification. Water recedes before every asset and crossing can be cleared for ordinary use.

Stage 5 restores most services. One location retains a separate building, road or ecological issue.

Stage 6 removes or reviews temporary infrastructure. The neighborhood keeps changed habits, photographs, names and memories.

Stage 7 revisits the same catchment in a later season. Old observations now help distinguish normal behavior from a genuinely new problem.

The neighborhood accumulates history without a `flood_level` or `drainage_level` progression stat.

## Dungeon / exploration candidate — The Street Below the Street

Premise:

An old inspected drainage/service corridor has multiple access points and historical alignments. A current maintenance case requires mapping which sections still correspond to modern records.

The exploration version can run without battle mechanics:

- compare old and current map revisions;
- enter only authorized/verified dry sections;
- identify superseded links;
- photograph or record asset IDs;
- find where two neighborhood names refer to the same junction;
- discover that one old branch now serves another non-drainage use or habitat function.

Mechanically rich optional version:

A territorial encounter occurs on an adjacent service platform while the corridor itself remains protected.

Current implementation profile: REDUCED. Keep active flow, sudden water-level changes, collapsing debris, slippery surfaces and noncombat workers outside BattleSpec.

## Encounter candidate — Culvert Access Perimeter

FULL narrative intention:

A territorial group prevents inspectors from reaching a post-storm culvert access path. Full behavior would prioritize territory, withdrawal and route control.

Dependency classification is identical to Flooded Underpass Withdrawal at the permanent family level: VERIFIED targeting/base movement/core calculations/action economy/AI legal actions; PARTIAL complete movement/turn lifecycle/damage/status/moves/abilities/items/Trainer Features; BLOCKING terrain-weather-hazards-zones-reactions, tactical policy and adapter/playback.

REDUCED implementation:

Inspectors wait outside the scene. Flow, debris and culvert interior are not interactive. Resolve a conventional encounter on stable adjacent ground. Victory only permits later approach/inspection.

## Canon questions created by these seeds

Before promotion, decide:

- which Ouros settlements have dedicated stormwater networks, combined systems, open drains or mostly natural drainage;
- which institutions operate and maintain them;
- whether catch basins, culverts, detention/retention areas and pump stations exist in each region;
- what public access to underground service spaces looks like;
- which locations are known flood/ponding areas and why;
- how drainage infrastructure overlaps habitat and public space;
- which temporary mitigation practices exist;
- what technologies and maps are available;
- which individual Pokémon, if any, hold established drainage-related work roles;
- which old drainage assets survive as landmarks, habitat or service corridors.

## Mechanical questions intentionally unresolved

- exact flood-current and forced-movement behavior;
- drowning or suffocation;
- slipping/wet-surface rules;
- contaminated floodwater effects;
- debris collision;
- falling/collapse around culverts or washed-out ground;
- pump/technical equipment as tactical objects;
- Move/Ability/Item/Trainer Feature interaction with water removal or infrastructure work;
- rescue/carry mechanics;
- objective-aware WITHDRAW/PROTECT/SECURE_ROUTE policy;
- semantic Minecraft/Cobblemon playback for environmental changes.

No answer is invented by these proposals.
