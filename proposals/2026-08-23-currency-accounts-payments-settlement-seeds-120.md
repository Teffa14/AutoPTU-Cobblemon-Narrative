# Ouros Candidates — Currency, Accounts, Payments & Settlement — Pass 120

Status: NON-CANON proposals. All names, institutions, locations and rules below are placeholders until reviewed against Ouros canon, PTU/Caelo and implementation.

These candidates use the Pass 120 research and `design/currency-accounts-payments-settlement-layer.md`. They are designed to create consequences from actual world state rather than random economic drama.

## 1. The Payment That Never Became Final

A regional clinic believes a replacement pump was paid for. Procurement has a supplier confirmation. The payment rail shows authorization and a funds reservation, but no final settlement event exists because the service failed mid-processing during a storm.

Useful consequence:

The supplier did not steal the money and the clinic did not fail to pay deliberately. The quest can be about reconciling two systems and restoring the shipment path.

## 2. Cash Only After the Storm

A coastal market normally accepts account transfers and institutional credits. A communications outage disables both. Sellers revert to physical cash and signed local vouchers for one day.

Long-term consequence:

The event later justifies keeping an offline fallback procedure instead of becoming a one-off inconvenience.

## 3. The Old Festival Scrip

Construction beneath a plaza uncovers packets of event tokens from an observance that ended decades ago.

Possible truths:

- some are ordinary historic artifacts;
- some were never redeemed;
- an old institution may still have an authored promise to honor them;
- replicas also exist.

A museum, Finance and the payment layer can each answer a different question.

## 4. The Merchant Who Accepts League Credits

One remote merchant begins accepting a League-linked point system because many traveling competitors pass through the settlement.

The neighboring vendors do not follow.

This creates a visible local practice without declaring the point system universal currency.

## 5. The Rural Deposit Window

A small settlement has a weekly counter that only accepts deposits, withdrawals and basic transfers when a courier/communications route is active.

It offers no loans, investment, insurance or credit.

This gives the settlement financial infrastructure without importing a modern universal bank model.

## 6. The Missing Confirmation

A sponsor's public dashboard says an installment was paid. The recipient's system shows the incoming amount as pending.

The actual transfer is correct but the final confirmation message failed.

Media reports “funds missing” before reconciliation finishes.

## 7. Two Balances, One Club

A player-created club sees one balance in its public budget display and another in its treasury ledger because part of the money is reserved for a tournament venue deposit.

Nothing has disappeared.

The episode can teach shared-fund governance and available-versus-ledger balance.

## 8. The Refund Arrived First

A market purchase fails after payment because the item is placed on a quality hold before handoff.

The merchant issues a refund quickly, but the original transaction remains visible as settled in history.

A player reviewing only one screen believes they were charged twice until the new return payment appears.

## 9. The Till Difference

A café closes with less physical cash than expected.

Candidate explanations remain open:

- a refund was not posted;
- one sale used a different accepted token;
- a staff member counted incorrectly;
- an offline batch has not synced;
- actual loss occurred.

The system does not start with theft as the answer.

## 10. The Wrong Medium at the Auction

A visiting Trainer has enough total value but only in a payment medium the auction does not accept.

The story becomes logistical and social rather than a hidden wealth check: find an authorized conversion service, use another instrument, or decline the lot.

## 11. Emergency Relief Vouchers

After a regional flood, one civic institution issues temporary relief vouchers usable only for food, transport and emergency supplies at participating venues.

The vouchers expire after recovery and never become general currency.

Years later unused examples enter Archives/Public Memory.

## 12. The Conversion Window

An academy allows students to convert earned institutional credits into a regional transport balance during two periods each term.

The conversion is one-way and capped.

This allows institutional rewards to affect travel without inventing a floating exchange economy.

## 13. The Payment Rail Nobody Uses Anymore

An old network remains technically functional but almost all merchants stopped accepting it after a newer system became easier to use.

A historical research project discovers that old receipts cannot be interpreted correctly without understanding the former rail's settlement semantics.

## 14. The Account Survived the Building

A local financial service moves to a new building after a fire.

The account system remains the same institution and retains its ledgers despite the physical site changing.

This connects Architecture, Digital Systems and payments without tying account identity to one Minecraft structure.

## 15. The Building Survived the Institution

The inverse case: an old deposit hall still exists physically, but the institution closed years ago.

The building is now another use entirely.

Old signs and counters do not prove that financial services still operate there.

## 16. Three Prices, One Item

A merchant displays three legitimate ways to pay for the same item:

- ordinary currency;
- a smaller quantity of institution-specific points under a temporary promotion;
- an exchange request for a specific approved material batch.

The item has one mechanical identity. The different offers belong to Market/payment state, not PTU item mechanics.

## 17. The Offline Rail Test

A hospital, shelter and transit operator run a scheduled drill to prove they can still accept emergency payments if the normal network fails.

The drill finds that one site has outdated acceptance data.

No crisis is required for the world to improve preparedness.

## 18. The Cashbox From the Closed Station

Rail renovation uncovers an old locked cashbox from a station that closed decades earlier.

Its contents create questions of custody, provenance and archival value before anyone decides whether the currency remains redeemable.

## 19. The Duplicate Display

A client dashboard shows the same incoming payment twice because one line is the instruction and the second line is the final settlement record.

The actual ledger balance increased once.

The incident becomes a UI/data-quality issue rather than monetary duplication.

## 20. The Shared Treasury Vote

A multiplayer club has enough funds for either a field station upgrade or a tournament trip.

Both are valid uses, but the treasury's authored governance requires two approvals for large payments.

A single player cannot bypass the rule by reaching the Minecraft chest first.

## 21. The Player Who Paid Before the Road Closed

A Trainer prepays for a remote service. A landslide closes the route before delivery.

The payment is settled, but Supply Chains/Travel cannot fulfill the service yet.

Possible resolutions include wait, refund, reroute or substitution. Payment completion never teleports the service.

## 22. The Voucher With the Wrong Date

A relief voucher is authentic but issued under the previous emergency program revision and is no longer redeemable at normal vendors.

Its authenticity and present financial validity are different questions.

## 23. The Points That Cannot Buy the Important Thing

A Trainer accumulates a large institutional point balance through League or academy activity.

Most routine venues accept it, but a family-owned workshop declines because its supply costs require ordinary currency.

The workshop is not hostile or backwards; its acceptance profile reflects its actual operating dependencies.

## 24. The Historic Account Statement

An archive contains statements from an institution that changed accounting conventions years ago.

Modern readers initially misinterpret a reserved-funds column as debt.

Metrology/Archives-style version awareness solves the confusion.

## 25. The Payment System Returns Before the Network

A settlement service restores limited operation after a storm while public communications remain patchy.

Some payments can process even though customers cannot immediately receive confirmation messages.

This creates rumors of “vanishing payments” without a system failure.

## 26. The Tournament Prize Is Real but Pending

A competitor has officially won a prize. Battle Institutions establishes eligibility and Finance establishes the commitment.

The payment is still pending because identity/recipient details need correction.

The character is the winner even before the balance changes.

## 27. The Market Token Becomes a Collectible

A harbor market replaces physical tokens with a newer rail. Most tokens are redeemed and retired.

Years later collectors value surviving examples as Material Culture objects.

Their collectible value is a market claim, not their old face value or a PTU mechanical property.

## 28. The Wrong Account, Correct Institution

A grant payment is sent to the correct university but an obsolete project account.

The funds are not missing from the institution, yet the research team cannot use them until internal reconciliation moves/authorizes the balance correctly.

## 29. The Merchant Network Splits

A merchant association once used one shared payment service. After governance disagreements, half the members adopt another rail.

For several years signs and guidebooks must state which stalls accept which methods.

No faction hostility is required.

## 30. The Ledger That Saved the Reconstruction

After a major disaster destroys buildings and paper records, surviving settlement/account history helps reconstruct which repair invoices were actually paid, which remained authorized and which never progressed beyond a proposal.

The financial record becomes historical evidence without becoming omniscient truth about the physical work.

# Long Arc A — Five Outages at Meridian Exchange

Year 1: a short communications outage makes account payments unavailable at the central market. The fallback plan is improvised.

Year 2: the market formally adds a physical-cash and signed-voucher fallback. Most transactions remain routine.

Year 3: a longer outage exposes that one major supplier cannot use the fallback rail, causing a real stock delay even though consumers can still pay locally.

Year 4: an offline batch creates reconciliation discrepancies. No money is actually lost, but staff spend days rebuilding confidence in the ledger.

Year 5: another major storm occurs. Because the market now has versioned acceptance profiles, fallback rails and reconciliation procedures, payment disruption is shorter and does not become a major shortage.

Possible continuation:

The market may later retire one fallback method because the world changed. Institutional improvement does not require every system to expand forever.

# Long Arc B — The League Credit Years

Stage 1: a League-linked institution introduces a limited point system for event services and equipment.

Stage 2: a few nearby merchants accept it because competitor traffic is high.

Stage 3: acceptance expands along one transport corridor, while rural areas largely ignore it.

Stage 4: a conversion service appears between League credits and a separate institutional transport balance under a fixed authored rule.

Stage 5: public debate emerges over whether broader acceptance is useful, but no automatic answer is imposed.

Possible outcomes:

- the point system stays narrow;
- it becomes common only in tournament cities;
- a newer system replaces it;
- conversion remains possible but direct merchant acceptance declines.

The arc never invents a universal exchange rate or monetary policy.

# Long Arc C — The Treasury That Learned Governance

A new multiplayer club begins with one shared cashbox and informal norms.

A disputed purchase reveals that physical access is an inadequate authorization model.

The club creates a treasury account with spending scopes. Later it adds dual approval for major commitments, a public summary for members and private details for sensitive payments.

Years later leadership changes. The treasury survives because the account belongs to the institution, not the former treasurer.

A later crisis tests emergency-spending rules.

The arc can end with a mature governance model, a deliberate split into separate budgets or even dissolution with orderly closure. None of those outcomes is mechanically superior by default.

# Encounter Contracts

## Settlement Hall Outage

Full version:

A crowded market/payment hub has a live service outage while a Pokémon disturbance blocks access to an alternate terminal. Staff and civilians have movement objectives. Pending payments remain frozen at their actual state.

Requires:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement for staff/crowd lanes;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions only if a validated tactical hazard exists;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy for `CLEAR_ROUTE`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT`;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Evacuate customers in world state. Freeze all payment instructions in their current authoritative states. Use a static room/corridor battle only for the remaining threat. Reconciliation happens after battle. No payment becomes settled because the fight ended.

## Relief Voucher Depot

Full version:

A crisis depot is issuing temporary vouchers while wild Pokémon and evacuees need to cross the same space.

Requires complete movement/interception, objective-aware AI and playback; environmental families are required only if a real hazard exists.

Reduced version:

Resolve queues, voucher eligibility and wildlife movement before battle. If a confrontation remains, use a static nearby encounter. Voucher balances and acceptance never enter tactical resolution.

## Historic Token Reconciliation

Full version:

Old market tokens discovered during building work are being inventoried when a separate incident threatens the assessment area. Protect/withdraw objectives may be needed.

Reduced version:

Archive and Finance secure the token cache before battle. AutoPTU handles a separate static threat. Historic redemption status is resolved afterward from records and authored promises.

# Hard guards for all Pass 120 candidates

Do not generate:

- interest;
- loans or credit scores;
- overdrafts;
- debt collection;
- banking law;
- taxation;
- currency exchange rates;
- transaction fees;
- merchant discounts from Skills;
- payment-based battle buffs;
- wealth-based social bonuses;
- money duplication through backup restore;
- automatic fraud accusations;
- Pokémon collateral;
- Pokémon valuation;
- point-to-cash conversion without an authored rule;
- Pay Day or Amulet Coin behavior without PTU/Caelo/AutoPTU validation.

All candidates remain proposals until continuity, canon and implementation review.
