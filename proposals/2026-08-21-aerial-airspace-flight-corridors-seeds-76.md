# Ouros Aerial Airspace, Flight Corridors & Sky Ecology Seeds — Pass 76

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros material derived from high-level research patterns. No external characters, plots, prose or settings are imported.

## 1. The Missing Morning Flight

A normally reliable aerial passenger service fails to arrive at a mountain settlement. Weather was acceptable and the assigned service Pokémon later returns alone. The system records the missed arrival, the Pokémon's return location and operator reports without inventing injury, fear or sabotage. Investigation can reveal route, staffing, communication or ecological causes.

## 2. Lights Across the Orchard

A night service reports repeated bright flashes from farmland below. Farmers think the aircraft are distress signals; pilots think the ground lights are interference. The source may be Pokémon communication, machinery, a festival test or several unrelated sources.

## 3. The Old Sky Route

A corridor used for decades now passes close to a new residential district. Residents complain about noise and night lighting while operators argue the route predates the neighborhood. The story becomes a governance/transport problem rather than a villain plot.

## 4. The Corridor Nobody Uses

Maps still mark a formally open air route, but every operator quietly avoids it. The reason is not public. Players can reconstruct the route's incident history, weather behavior and wildlife observations.

## 5. Winter Braviary Count

Researchers expect a seasonal aerial arrival, but observations are late and incomplete. Aerial surveys, ground observers and photography disagree about numbers. No population change is asserted until enough evidence exists.

## 6. Rooftop Stopover

A dense urban block becomes a temporary stopover for migrating Flying Pokémon. The building owners want them removed before an event. Researchers want a short closure. Nearby businesses depend on foot traffic. No single interest is automatically correct.

## 7. The Valley's Two Altitudes

Two operators use the same valley but at different altitude bands. One reports turbulence and wildlife conflict; the other reports none. The difference can become a meteorology and route-design investigation.

## 8. The Beacon That Still Works

An obsolete navigation beacon continues operating after its route was retired. Wild Pokémon have begun using the structure. A modernization project must decide whether to remove, repurpose or preserve it.

## 9. The Last Survey Before Cloud Season

A research team has one practical flight window before seasonal cloud makes aerial observation unreliable. The pressure is information loss, not a guaranteed hazard or fight.

## 10. The Ferry Replacement

Aerial service temporarily replaces a ferry after infrastructure damage. The route works, but passenger demand exceeds capacity and cargo competes with medical referrals and urgent supplies.

## 11. The Flock Below the Approach

A large collective repeatedly gathers near an aerial-service landing approach. The group may be feeding at a new resource site rather than reacting to aircraft. The investigation connects ecology, waste, water and route planning.

## 12. The Silent Tower

A mountain relay stops transmitting route updates. The tower itself remains visible and powered from a distance. Players must determine whether the problem is communication, staffing, weather, physical access or equipment.

## 13. The Wrong Bird

Public reports blame one conspicuous Flying species for repeated route disruptions. Archived observations show that several different species use the corridor at different times.

## 14. The Aerial Map With No Ground Truth

A survey team mapped a remote area entirely from above. Ground expedition members later discover that some apparent clearings are canopy gaps, some roads are dry streambeds and one landmark was misidentified.

## 15. The Service Pokémon's New Route

An institutional Pokémon repeatedly approaches the same destination from a different direction than its historical route. The operator sees inefficiency. Researchers see a possible environmental signal. The system records behavior without assigning motivation.

## 16. Smoke Reroute

A wildfire is no longer threatening the service destination, but smoke aloft still makes the standard corridor unsuitable. The reroute crosses a seasonal migration zone and creates a second decision problem.

## 17. Stadium Night Conflict

A major event lights the sky around a city. Aerial traffic remains legal, but wildlife observations change at the same time. The story connects Performance, Lightscape, Migration and Transport.

## 18. The Unscheduled Courier

A courier Pokémon lands at a clinic carrying institutional material, but the clinic has no record of dispatch. Custody, provenance and communication records matter before anyone assumes theft or fraud.

## 19. The Mountain Pass From Above

An aerial survey suggests a blocked land route is clear beyond one ridge. A ground team still needs to verify slope, snow and access. Aerial knowledge reduces uncertainty without replacing ground exploration.

## 20. The Three Landing Pads

A settlement has three historical landing sites. One is now surrounded by housing, one is near protected habitat and one lacks current transport access. Choosing a new primary site becomes a public-works arc.

## 21. The Missing Stopover

A migration corridor remains active, but a traditional stopover wetland is nearly unused. Players must distinguish route change, food availability, disturbance, timing shift and observer error.

## 22. The High Route Rumor

Pilots claim a rare Pokémon is using an unusually high corridor. The claim becomes public before researchers can verify it, attracting tourism and unauthorized observation flights.

## 23. The Valley Closed for One Week

A route receives a short ecological restriction during a passage window. Businesses complain, but the closure is temporary and planned. The interesting part is whether mitigation and alternative transport actually work.

## 24. The Sky School Archive

An old training institution preserves flight logs from decades of service Pokémon and operators. The records become useful for climate, migration and infrastructure research even after the school closes.

## 25. The Wind Farm Debate

A proposed energy project intersects a known but poorly measured aerial corridor. The conflict requires better evidence, not automatic approval or rejection.

## 26. The Aerial Rescue That Never Became a Battle

A stranded field team is located from the air and extracted without combat. The mission is still meaningful because route knowledge, weather windows, communications and service capacity determined the outcome.

## 27. The Returned Partner in the Sky

A previously released Pokémon is observed repeatedly along a seasonal aerial corridor. The observation does not imply that it is returning to its former Trainer or asking to rejoin the party.

## 28. The Festival Balloon Problem

A regional festival adds temporary airborne decorations or platforms. Organizers discover that the event overlaps a service approach and bird/Pokémon movement zone. The story is solved through planning and timing rather than arbitrary combat.

## 29. The Broken Route Record

An institution's route history contains a five-year gap after a server migration. Old paper logs, public notices and resident memory conflict. Reconstructing the route becomes an Archives/Digital/Travel investigation.

## 30. The New Settlement Above the Ridge

A small elevated research outpost gradually becomes a permanent settlement. Its growth depends on supply routes, emergency access, weather, housing and service capacity. The air route becomes part of the town's identity rather than a menu button.

# Long arc A — The Flyway Years

Year 1: researchers identify a broad seasonal aerial corridor across several regions.

Year 2: a new transport service begins using part of the same airspace.

Year 3: incident reports rise, but causes differ by season and location.

Year 4: stopover restoration changes where groups gather.

Year 5: operators, conservation staff and settlements adopt a revised corridor network.

The arc preserves old route editions and observation datasets. No single Pokémon population needs to become an enemy.

# Long arc B — The Town That Learned to Fly

A remote settlement begins with irregular institutional flights. Over several years it develops a landing site, rescue access, scheduled passenger service, cargo links and aerial survey capacity. Growth creates new problems: housing near approaches, light pollution, service dependence and route congestion. The climax is not necessarily a disaster; it can be a regional review deciding what kind of aerial hub the town wants to become.

# Long arc C — The High Atlas

Players and NPC institutions gradually build a vertical regional atlas combining aerial photography, migration observations, weather, beacons, mountain passes and historical routes. Some early conclusions are wrong. Later editions preserve corrections rather than rewriting old data. The atlas becomes a durable research object and travel resource.

# Encounter contract 1 — Ridge Approach Interruption

Narrative premise: a service landing approach becomes unusable while a group of wild Pokémon occupies the safest corridor.

Full version:

- actual altitude bands inside the encounter;
- autonomous wild withdrawal routes;
- interception/avoidance behavior;
- wind or weather zones when mechanically verified;
- objective such as CLEAR_APPROACH_WITHOUT_KO or WITHDRAW;
- tactical AI aware of escape and route use;
- Minecraft playback of landing, ascent and retreat.

Required capability families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED, but not altitude-aware by itself;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED for existing movement primitives;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- abilities — PARTIAL if used;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL if used;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

The server resolves the approach conflict before battle. Wild Pokémon that remain hostile or otherwise enter combat are placed on a static validated arena. The service Pokémon/passengers remain outside the grid. The resulting battle can change whether the route reopens, but no altitude, wind or aerial interception rules are invented.
