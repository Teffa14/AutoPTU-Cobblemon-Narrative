# Ouros Music Traditions, Ensembles & Recordings Seeds — Pass 103

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from the research patterns in Pass 103. None becomes canon without continuity, originality, PTU/Caelo and implementation review.

## 1. The Missing Fourth Part

A town ensemble still performs a century-old four-part piece, but only three parts survive in notation. Older musicians insist the missing line was taught by ear. Three former students remember incompatible fragments.

Possible hooks:
- compare old recordings;
- locate former apprentices;
- identify which recording contains the missing performer;
- decide whether the next public version should reconstruct, improvise or leave the part absent.

No outcome is labeled the uniquely authentic version unless evidence supports that conclusion.

## 2. Three Towns, One Tune

Three settlements perform recognizable versions of the same melody and each has a different origin story. One claims it began as a ferry signal, one as a harvest song and one as a children's game.

The arc can connect migration, old maps, ferries, language variants and archives without requiring one community to be fraudulent.

## 3. The Bridge Without Its Bass

A layered public ensemble has played at the same bridge for years. Its lowest part disappears when one long-time musician moves away.

Possible outcomes:
- recruit a new player;
- rearrange the piece without that part;
- rotate several performers;
- let the local sound change permanently.

The ensemble remains the same persistent group unless its members actually choose a new identity.

## 4. The Instrument That Changed Hands

A handmade instrument has moved through a workshop, touring performer, school, private collection and museum loan.

The story follows provenance rather than magical power. Repairs, replacement parts, inscriptions and recordings gradually reveal the object's history.

## 5. Recording Before the Flood

An old community recording was made in a district before a major flood and reconstruction. Background echoes, bells and traffic reveal that the public space once functioned differently.

The recording can support Architecture and Public Memory research, but acoustic interpretation remains a hypothesis rather than direct geometric proof.

## 6. The Wild Countermelody

A recurring wild Pokémon call has become associated with a local evening tune because performers and wildlife usually overlap at the same hour.

One year the call is missing.

Separate possible explanations include:
- changed diel activity;
- migration;
- disturbance near the roost;
- the same individual no longer being present;
- simple detection failure.

The music tradition can continue even if the ecological component changes.

## 7. The Retired Gym Leader's Songbook

A retired Gym Leader left notebooks containing arrangements performed during public club nights. Fans assume the books hide battle strategies.

Most pages may simply be music.

Any actual battle notes must be separately evidenced. The songbook never grants hidden mechanical knowledge by narrative convenience.

## 8. New Hall, Old Repertoire

A historic ensemble moves into a newly built hall whose space, audience layout and technology are different from its former venue.

Rehearsals lead to new arrangements. Older recordings remain correct for the old hall rather than becoming obsolete errors.

## 9. Quiet Hours Rehearsal

A rehearsal space sits beside homes and a seasonal nesting site. Residents, performers and conservation staff all have legitimate concerns about timing.

The solution can be a negotiated schedule, acoustic treatment, relocation of some rehearsals or seasonal changes rather than shutting down one side permanently.

## 10. The Wrong Composer

A famous singer is widely credited with a regional standard because the oldest popular recording uses their name.

Archive research finds evidence that the work circulated earlier and may have emerged collectively. Public memory, performer fame and authorship claims diverge.

## 11. The School Version Became Standard

A teacher simplified a difficult local piece for students decades ago. That version spread so widely that most residents now consider it normal.

The older form remains documented but is not automatically superior.

## 12. The Revival Is Twenty Years Old

A repertoire once nearly disappeared. Students reconstructed it twenty years ago from fragments and recordings.

A new generation now treats the revival arrangement as its own tradition. A second reconstruction project must decide how to document both histories.

## 13. Two Recordings, One Night

Two archives hold recordings labeled as the same performance, but timings, crowd noise and one instrumental part do not match.

Possible explanations include:
- two shows on the same evening;
- a rehearsal and public performance;
- miscataloging;
- later edits;
- one clock being wrong.

The investigation should preserve each source rather than deleting the losing hypothesis.

## 14. The Borrowed Instrument

A valuable instrument is on legitimate loan for a festival, but weather closes its return route.

The problem becomes custody, transport and schedule management rather than theft.

## 15. The Song Everyone Knows Differently

Players begin collecting versions of a common tune from markets, schools, ferries, farms and clubs.

The content grows into a regional network map of transmission. There is no hidden purity score.

## 16. Street Ensemble Moves Indoors

A public-space ensemble becomes famous enough that crowds and weather make its original corner impractical.

Moving indoors improves some conditions and changes others. The original corner retains its history and may continue to host smaller performances.

## 17. The Recording Nobody May Broadcast

An archive holds a unique field recording. Researchers may listen under current institutional practice, but public rebroadcast has never been authorized.

The scenario distinguishes custody, access, publication and cultural consent without inventing a universal Ouros copyright code.

## 18. Toxtricity Without the Stage

A local instrument maker studies the acoustic character of Toxtricity and builds a new instrument family inspired by it.

A particular Toxtricity frequently visits the workshop but refuses the crowded public showcase.

The refusal is observable. The system does not invent fear, trauma or a Musician class for the Pokémon.

## 19. The Student Who Arranges Better Than the Teacher

An apprentice begins producing arrangements the teacher considers technically stronger than their own work.

The mentorship changes direction: the teacher remains the source of older repertoire knowledge while the student becomes the stronger arranger.

No private resentment or pride is assigned without evidence.

## 20. The Tune Under the Machine Noise

A decades-old workshop recording accidentally captured a machine cycle beneath the music.

Years later engineers use it as one source when reconstructing the history of an industrial system whose documentation is incomplete.

The recording supports a hypothesis; it does not prove machine condition by itself.

## 21. Former Partner at the Rehearsal Door

A released former partner appears repeatedly near a rehearsal room it once visited.

Possible player choices include observation, greeting, leaving it alone or inviting voluntary participation.

Presence does not restore ownership, command authority or partnership automatically.

## 22. The Ensemble With No Founder

A long-standing group has no reliable founding record. Several early members remember the beginning differently.

The ensemble can keep a shared identity without inventing a single founder for cleaner lore.

## 23. Seasonal Instrument Materials

A workshop depends on plant fibers, wood, reeds or other materials that are only available through specific seasonal supply chains.

A shortage changes repair schedules and instrumentation, not combat stats.

## 24. Last Player of the Old Form

Only one active performer still knows a regional variant that was never recorded fully.

They may choose to teach, document, partially share or let some elements remain private. Safeguarding is not treated as automatic public disclosure.

## 25. The Festival Commissions Three Versions

One observance commissions three different arrangements from different local schools rather than selecting a single official anthem.

Future editions may keep all three, rotate them or inspire additional versions.

## 26. Radio Made the Chorus Famous

A broadcaster repeatedly uses one section of a longer work. Visitors begin recognizing only that fragment.

Popularity changes Public Memory and tourism without transferring ownership or redefining the full work.

## 27. The Instrument Museum Still Plays

A museum preserves a historic instrument but occasionally loans it for carefully documented performances.

Each use adds custody, condition and recording history. The instrument is not frozen merely because it entered a collection.

## 28. The Band That Shares a Gym

A battle institution hosts a local music club during evenings. Challenge schedules, rehearsals, audience flow and maintenance share one building.

Neither institution controls the other's rules by default.

## 29. The Field Recording With a Second Mystery

Researchers record a traditional performance. Years later someone notices an unknown Pokémon call, warning signal or machine noise in the background.

The artistic document becomes evidence for an unrelated case while remaining incomplete and context-bound.

## 30. The Town That Stopped Singing It

A once-common local song gradually leaves ordinary use. There is no ban, villain or disaster; people simply stop choosing it.

Years later players may document memories, support a revival or allow the tradition to remain dormant. Dormancy is a valid world outcome.

# Longer arcs

## Arc A — Five Arrangements of Meridian Bridge

Year 1: four local performers establish the version most residents recognize.

Year 2: one member moves away and the group creates a reduced arrangement.

Year 3: bridge reconstruction changes the available performance space and soundscape.

Year 4: a younger ensemble adds a new part influenced by another region.

Year 5: an archive exhibit plays several versions side by side while residents disagree about which one feels most like home.

The arc lets one musical work carry Architecture, Demography, Public Space, Archives, Media and Social history without declaring one arrangement mechanically or culturally superior.

## Arc B — The Lost Repertoire Project

A regional archive identifies references to several works no longer performed.

Players, students and researchers collect fragments from:
- old recordings;
- notebooks;
- former performers;
- neighboring variants;
- instrument-specific memories;
- public event programs.

Some pieces may be reconstructed confidently. Others may end with two or three plausible modern realizations. The success condition is better provenance and living practice, not discovering a magic canonical score.

## Arc C — The Music Hall Generations

A hall begins as a neighborhood rehearsal space.

Over years it becomes:
- a public venue;
- a temporary crisis shelter;
- a school rehearsal annex;
- a recording site;
- a partially abandoned building;
- a restored intergenerational venue.

Its archive accumulates posters, instrument loans, recordings, lineup histories and changes in neighborhood use. The same room can anchor multiple generations without resetting after each campaign arc.

# Mechanically rich encounter candidates

## Encounter 1 — Rehearsal Hall Interruption

Premise:

A community ensemble is rehearsing when an external disturbance requires evacuation and temporarily threatens access to instruments and records.

FULL version dependencies:

VERIFIED foundations:
- targeting / footprints / range / LoS;
- base movement legality;
- core calculations;
- action economy / initiative;
- AI legal-action infrastructure.

PARTIAL if invoked:
- full turn / round lifecycle;
- move-specific behavior for exact Sonic Moves;
- abilities for exact Soundproof or other sound interactions;
- Trainer Features / perks for actual Musician Songs;
- full stateful damage and status lifecycle as used by the battle.

BLOCKING:
- complete movement including interception / forced movement for live civilian evacuation;
- terrain / weather / hazards / zones / reactions for an interactive damaged stage or building;
- AI tactical policy for PROTECT_EXIT, WITHDRAW and AVOID_CIVILIANS;
- Minecraft / Cobblemon / Craftics adapter and playback.

REDUCED version:

The rehearsal stops first. Civilians and instruments clear the tactical area. AutoPTU runs a conventional static battle. Musician Songs are unavailable unless a later exact Java parity slice verifies them. World state resumes the rehearsal, custody and repair consequences afterward.

## Encounter 2 — Missing Archive Recording

Premise:

A recording believed to preserve a rare arrangement is missing from its expected storage location.

FULL version dependencies:

Mostly overworld. If a pursuit or confrontation occurs, VERIFIED static combat foundations are enough for a simple battle. Complete movement and AI tactical policy become BLOCKING only if the scenario requires live pursuit, escort or a non-KO recovery objective. Adapter/playback remains BLOCKING for semantic object custody during battle.

REDUCED version:

Resolve catalog history, copies, permissions and custody outside AutoPTU. Secure the physical recording before any combat. If a confrontation follows, run a conventional static encounter whose result cannot silently transfer ownership or erase evidence.
