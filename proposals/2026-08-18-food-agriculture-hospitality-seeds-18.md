# Ouros Food, Agriculture & Hospitality Seeds — Pass 18

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from broad structural research, not adaptations of any single source.

## 1. The Orchard That Changed Visitors

A settlement restores an abandoned orchard. The immediate goal is economic, but flowering trees alter local Pokémon activity and bring new researchers, pollinators, pests and tourists. The important state change is not a universal encounter-table buff; it is a causal shift in who uses the place and why.

## 2. The Missing Harvest Window

A Berry-growing community expects a normal harvest period, but plants reach readiness unevenly. The first explanation is bad weather. Evidence eventually points toward a change in local water flow that began after earlier infrastructure work. The quest connects agriculture to settlement memory instead of introducing a random crop failure.

## 3. Last Table at the Ferry Inn

A small inn near a transport connection is overwhelmed after ferry delays strand travelers. Players may help with routing, supplies, sleeping space, information or food service. Combat is unnecessary unless another world-state problem already justifies it.

## 4. The Two Kitchens

Two long-established kitchens claim different versions of the same regional dish. Neither is automatically fraudulent. Interviews and old records reveal that the recipes diverged after migration split the original community. The resolution can recognize both traditions instead of selecting a winner.

## 5. The Traveling Canteen

A mobile kitchen follows long expedition routes and public events. Its schedule becomes a useful signal of route safety and field activity. When it stops appearing, players can investigate whether the cause is transport, staffing, supply or a deliberate route change.

## 6. Apiary Boundary

An apiary's production drops while nearby wild Pokémon activity increases. The beekeeper blames a new trail project; trail managers blame weather. Players observe movement patterns, flowering sites and human disturbance before recommending changes. Honey production itself remains governed by approved mechanics if it becomes an actual item yield.

## 7. The Recipe Nobody Owns

A celebrated festival recipe is widely described as belonging to one famous restaurant, but older evidence shows many families prepared related versions. The conflict concerns recognition and stewardship, not a mechanically unique recipe reward.

## 8. Kitchen Apprenticeship

A food-focused Trainer works under several specialists: one teaches logistics, another preparation, another ingredient identification and another hospitality. Progress is recorded through events and relationships. PTU Chef Features are never granted by narrative apprenticeship alone.

## 9. The Empty Market Morning

A market normally crowded with produce opens nearly empty. Several unrelated disruptions converged: a bridge closure, a greenhouse repair and a late fishing fleet. The scene is useful because the player can trace real supply dependencies instead of discovering a single mastermind.

## 10. Communal Meal After the Storm

Following a crisis, a communal meal functions as recovery content. Different NPCs return, rumors are corrected, missing people are accounted for, volunteers receive recognition and unresolved disagreements surface naturally. The meal creates social closure without awarding an invented healing bonus.

## 11. The Pokémon That Knows Closing Time

Urban wild Pokémon have learned when a market discards scraps. A schedule change causes them to appear at unusual hours and residents interpret the behavior as aggression. Observation reveals the food-source relationship.

## 12. Greenhouse Night Shift

A greenhouse employs both human workers and a small number of known Pokémon partners. When a heating system becomes unreliable, the obvious request is to use Fire-type Pokémon continuously. The actual question is whether those individuals have suitable legal capabilities and whether relying on them creates a sustainable schedule. Mechanical heating effects remain unresolved until capability review.

## 13. School Canteen Week

An academy rotates menus during a regional culture week. Students contribute family recipes and stories. The event can expose migration history, rival hometown traditions and character backgrounds while remaining primarily social.

## 14. The Fishermen's Quiet Bay

A fishery reports reduced catch. Instead of immediately spawning an aquatic boss, players compare seasonal patterns, wild collective movement, water quality and harvesting pressure. The eventual resolution may be a temporary closure or new stewardship agreement.

## 15. A Café That Became a Meeting Place

A modest café slowly accumulates importance because club members, researchers, transport workers and challengers all use it. Its role emerges from repeated events. Later renovations reflect who actually helped build its history.

## 16. Emergency Kitchen

A crisis overwhelms normal food services. A school kitchen, community hall or Gym can become a temporary distribution point. The challenge is coordination, capacity and delivery rather than cooking minigames.

## 17. The Famous Dish That Nobody Likes Anymore

A tourism board keeps promoting an old local specialty even though current residents rarely eat it. Players discover the gap between public memory and living culture. The town can update its identity without declaring the older tradition fake.

## 18. Orchard Rivalry Without Villains

Two growers disagree about land use. One wants higher output; another wants habitat buffers and diverse plantings. Both have legitimate concerns. The story changes future ecology and production depending on the compromise.

## 19. Ingredient Trail

A Chef wants to recreate a historically documented meal, but each ingredient leads to a different place: an orchard, herb garden, coastal supplier and archival record. The point is to learn the region through provenance, not to collect four glowing quest items.

## 20. Restaurant Recovery Arc

A damaged venue reopens over several stages: building repair, supplier reconnection, staff return, trial service and public reopening. Each stage can intersect with different existing systems. Revenue math remains abstract unless explicitly designed later.

## 21. The Unreliable Cookbook

A handwritten recipe collection contains contradictory notes from several generations. The mystery is not which page is "correct" but who wrote each layer and why preparation changed over time.

## 22. Festival Vendor Lottery

A public event has limited stall space. Selection creates legitimate conflict among old institutions, new chefs, traveling vendors and community groups. Players can help establish transparent criteria or uncover favoritism without the system deciding which cuisine is objectively best.

## 23. Berry Grove Stewardship

A grove used by both residents and wild Pokémon becomes too popular after a travel guide spreads. The response can include harvest limits, alternate routes, education or new cultivation elsewhere. Rare-spawn rewards should never be used to force the conservation outcome.

## 24. The Cold Storage Failure

A storage facility loses capacity shortly before a major event. The problem propagates into restaurant menus, care supplies and market availability. Exact spoilage remains abstract; named affected batches can be tracked when evidence or provenance matters.

## 25. The Pokémon Staff Member

A known Pokémon regularly assists at a venue because its actual behavior and legal capabilities support the task. Its participation is an individual character fact, not proof that every member of the species should work in kitchens.

## 26. Seasonal Caravan

A group of producers travels together to several towns during harvest season. Their route creates temporary markets, information exchange and social overlap. A disruption may redirect the caravan and shift economic activity elsewhere.

## 27. Public Tasting, No Contest

A town holds an open tasting where participants share food and stories. There are no points, judges or winner. The event exists to create social density and cultural exchange without turning every performance into competition.

## 28. The Supplier Who Retires

A respected ingredient supplier retires. Several venues must adapt, apprentices may compete or cooperate, and a regional recipe tradition may change. The consequence comes from a person leaving the economy, not a villain destroying the business.

## 29. The Farm That Became a Sanctuary

A small farm gradually shelters injured or displaced wild Pokémon after repeated crises. The place's identity shifts. This does not automatically become a breeding facility, Pokémon Center or ownership transfer site; those systems remain separate.

## 30. Kitchen Archive

A long-running venue keeps menus, supplier notes, photographs and staff records. The archive becomes a source for public-memory, case and cultural-history quests. Some records may be wrong or incomplete.

## 31. The Too-Successful Market

A restored market attracts enough visitors to strain transport, sanitation and nearby habitat. Success creates its own second-order problems. The solution may involve schedules, infrastructure or decentralization rather than shutting the market down.

## 32. Field Meal During Expedition

An expedition shares a meal at a staging site. This can trigger conversation, planning and Chronicle facts, but it should compress unless someone has a meaningful personal, cultural or logistical reason for the scene.

## 33. A Taste From Home

A migrant NPC prepares a dish associated with another region. The scene opens conversation about displacement, identity and memory. The dish's cultural meaning is independent from any PTU Food Buff.

## 34. Community Garden Governance

A shared garden needs decisions about plots, water, public access and wild Pokémon disturbance. The story revolves around governance and stewardship rather than maximizing yield.

## 35. Night Market Case

Several diners report similar symptoms. The case system tracks observations, purchases, ingredient batches, water sources and rumors. No venue becomes guilty until evidence establishes a source, and no PTU status is assigned from narrative reports alone.

## 36. Restaurant as Rival Hub

A recurring battle rival works or spends time at a venue. Some encounters are conversations, training talk, service help or mutual acquaintances rather than rematches. This keeps the rival's life broader than combat.

## 37. The Crop Nobody Expected

A plant resource begins thriving outside its assumed local range. Researchers, growers and conservationists have different explanations. The system stores each hypothesis separately until field evidence resolves it.

## 38. Relief Supply Choice

During recovery from a crisis, limited transport space forces a choice between several food or care shipments. The consequence should come from real capacity state and visible tradeoffs, not hidden morality scoring.

## 39. Cross-Regional Cooking Exchange

Two institutions exchange chefs/apprentices for a season. The resulting dishes and techniques reflect collaboration while recipe ownership and mechanical Chef access remain explicit.

## 40. The Restaurant That Refuses Trainers

A small venue temporarily bars battle participants after repeated property damage during crowded challenge days. The policy becomes a local social dispute linking battle institutions and hospitality without making the owner a villain.

# Longer arc candidates

## Arc A — From Grove to Table

A Berry-growing settlement, transport operator, market association and restaurant district form one visible supply chain. Early quests improve individual links. Later weather, ecological pressure or political decisions reveal that optimizing one link can damage another. The arc ends with a durable regional arrangement rather than a boss fight.

Potential systems:
- agriculture site state;
- wild collectives;
- travel/transport;
- material provenance;
- settlement services;
- public events;
- social bonds;
- cases/care if contamination occurs.

## Arc B — The Hospitality Quarter

A neglected district gradually becomes a gathering place through several independent venues: café, canteen, market, community kitchen and inn. Players influence which institutions survive, collaborate or specialize. NPC routines and visitors change physically in Minecraft as the district evolves.

## Arc C — The Cookbook Roads

An old collection of regional recipes becomes a reason to travel, but each entry points to living communities rather than treasure locations. Some traditions survived, some changed, and some names were misattributed. The arc becomes regional history through food without declaring one final canonical "original recipe" for every dish.

# Encounter implementation notes

Food/agriculture arcs should avoid unnecessary tactical combat. When combat is justified, use `design/food-agriculture-hospitality-layer.md` and `design/encounter-implementation-contracts.md`.

Three especially useful implementation profiles:
- Orchard Windbreak: FULL depends on weather/hazards, objective-aware AI and possibly forced movement; REDUCED uses fixed chokepoint battles and overworld evacuation.
- Cellar Spill: FULL depends on hazardous terrain/interactables; REDUCED uses static blockers and post-battle cleanup.
- Market Delivery Intercept: FULL depends on PROTECT/BREAK_THROUGH objective support, interception and tactical AI; REDUCED keeps shipment outside the grid and uses a standard route-clearing encounter.

Do not add custom food damage, crop HP, hunger clocks or improvised Chef effects to make these encounters work.
