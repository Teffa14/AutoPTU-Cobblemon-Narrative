# Pokémon Work Role Participation Seeds — Pass 83

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

These candidates are original Ouros material derived from the structures documented in `research/2026-08-27-pokemon-work-role-participation-scan-83.md`. Names, institutions, locations, technologies, labor practices and species choices remain provisional until canon review.

## Design intent

These seeds make ordinary workplaces legible through exact Pokémon individuals, schedules, observed competencies, supervision and persistent work history. They deliberately avoid type-based job assignment, universal labor rules, hidden productivity scores and occupational combat bonuses.

## The Pokémon Is Here, the Assignment Is Not

A familiar institutional Pokémon is physically present at a facility during a shift when it has no active work assignment. A visitor assumes the Pokémon can perform the same service it performed last week.

The scene exposes the difference between residence/presence and assignment state. The reason can be mundane: another task is scheduled later, the usual supervisor is absent, a work area is closed, or the service simply does not need that role today.

Persistence:

The NPC who made the assumption can later know that visible presence is not a reliable indicator of availability.

## Same Species, Different Work History

Two Pokémon of the same species are present in one institution. One has a long documented history with a specialized procedure; the other is new to the site.

A staffing shortage creates pressure to treat them as interchangeable. The correct review examines the task requirement, supervised experience and current authoritative capability evidence.

Possible outcomes include a narrower assignment, a supervised trial, postponement or choosing a completely different method.

The premise should never resolve through “same species means same skill.”

## The Handler Changes Shift

A Pokémon has an established routine with one supervisor. That person leaves early and another qualified worker takes over the shift.

The handoff becomes a small operational story: does the existing supervision scope permit the new worker to continue the assignment? Is a briefing enough? Does the task pause until the usual handler returns?

No outcome implies emotional attachment or disobedience unless separately evidenced.

## The Old Posting Says a Type

An old institutional posting asks for a Pokémon of a particular Type. Staff now realize the shorthand hides the actual task requirement.

The player can help translate the posting into explicit needs: route access, illumination, carrying a specific container, operating near water, producing a verified signal, or another real capability.

The physical notice can later change through the Public Notices layer, leaving the older version in institutional history.

## The Assignment After Evolution

A Pokémon evolves while holding a recurring noncombat role. Its identity and work history persist, but its physical form, movement profile or relevant mechanical state may have changed.

The institution performs a new suitability review instead of assuming the old assignment remains perfect or automatically terminating it.

Possible visible changes:

- different route through the workspace;
- larger resting/staging area;
- revised equipment;
- reduced or expanded task scope after evidence review;
- no change beyond appearance.

No change is guaranteed merely because evolution occurred.

## The Light Comes From a Known Method

A night inspection normally includes one individual Pokémon that has a specifically established, mechanically reviewed way to provide illumination or signaling support.

When that Pokémon is unavailable, the institution cannot substitute any Pokémon of a thematically similar Type. It uses lanterns, reschedules, borrows equipment, or performs a new suitability review on another individual.

This seed is primarily about visible institutional redundancy and source discipline.

## The Wild Helper Returns

A wild Pokémon previously assisted during one seasonal operation and later returns during the same kind of activity.

Staff recognize the individual from persistent observations. The institution can record another temporary cooperation window while the Pokémon remains wild.

No automatic capture, employment, Loyalty, ownership or permanent duty exists. If the Pokémon leaves, the operation adapts.

## The Work Route Became a Habitat Route

A repeatedly used work path begins intersecting a nesting, feeding or resting pattern documented by Ecology/Conservation.

The first response is not a battle. Staff compare schedules and reroute one part of the work process. A Pokémon already participating in the job may need its assignment changed because the route changed.

Later callbacks can show both routines coexisting, or a new conflict caused by seasonality.

## The Partner Stops at the Boundary

A worksite Pokémon repeatedly stops before entering one newly restricted area. The first record is only that observable behavior.

A review may discover:

- the area is correctly closed and the Pokémon is following a trained boundary;
- the assignment documentation was not updated;
- a new obstacle changed the normal route;
- equipment or supervision is missing;
- the reason remains unknown.

Do not generate fear, trauma or exceptional perception unless evidence supports it.

## The Relief Assignment Is Smaller

A recurring worker Pokémon becomes temporarily unavailable. Another individual can cover part of the role but not the full scope.

The service continues in LIMITED mode rather than pretending substitution is perfect or closing the entire institution.

This seed integrates with Workplaces service continuity and makes reduced service visible in Minecraft.

## The Tool Fits the Job, Not the Pokémon

An institution owns equipment designed for a work procedure. A proposed Pokémon replacement appears capable of the core task but has never been verified with the issued equipment.

The next step is an equipment compatibility review or supervised trial, not a species lookup.

Shared Equipment owns the asset. This extension owns the participation record.

## The Pokémon Who Is Retired From One Role

An important Pokémon stops participating in one recurring institutional role. The cause must be authored or evidenced; “retirement” cannot be generated from age or absence alone.

The work history stays visible through photos, routines remembered by staff, old equipment labels or Public Memory. The Pokémon may remain active in other contexts.

No battle capability changes are inferred.

## Five Assignments, Four Actual Workers

Mystery structure.

Five assignment records appear to imply five Pokémon participated during a busy week, but only four persistent Pokémon identities can be supported by the evidence.

Potential explanations:

- one assignment was superseded before it started;
- a temporary identifier and later permanent identifier refer to the same Pokémon;
- one handoff was recorded as a new worker rather than a continuation;
- a shift template was mistaken for an actual check-in;
- one record is incomplete.

The mystery reconstructs provenance. Fraud is possible only if evidence later supports it; it is not the required answer.

## A Workplace Learns Its Pokémon Partners

Long-form arc.

Visit 1 establishes the normal workplace routine and two or three exact Pokémon participants.

Visit 2 exposes a small substitution or handoff problem. Staff discover that the existing records describe species/type shorthand rather than the actual requirement.

Visit 3 introduces a pause, evolution, equipment change, safety restriction or supervisor absence. The institution creates a more precise capability/assignment review.

Visit 4 shows the new practice working during ordinary operations. The visible world changes through clearer staging, revised routes, different scheduling or better handoff surfaces.

Visit 5 introduces a different problem where the improved history prevents the old mistake from repeating.

The arc has no hidden “workplace level.” Improvement exists as records, practices, physical changes and reliable callbacks.

## Worksite Withdrawal With Partner Pokémon

Encounter concept.

Full intended version:

A routine operation stops while humans and working Pokémon withdraw from a contested area. Some exact Pokémon may become tactical participants only through explicit Ouros encounter composition. Safe lanes, Intercept, forced movement, withdrawal objectives and territorial AI can matter.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle as required;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if tactical environment is active;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items where relevant;
- Trainer Features/perks where relevant;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Reduced version:

Work stops first. Every human and non-selected Pokémon exits through Ouros world state. The work assignments are paused. AutoPTU receives only explicit combatants on a static reviewed arena. Worksite Safety and the workplace decide reopening after the authoritative battle. No crowd escort, work-role buffs, scripted Intercept or environmental damage is simulated in Minecraft.

## Service Route Partner Interruption

Encounter concept.

Full intended version:

A Pokémon participating in a survey, transport or delivery routine is present when a route becomes contested. The final design can include escape/clear-route intent, dynamic withdrawal, reactive movement and territorial opponent behavior.

Reduced version:

The service is paused outside battle. The working Pokémon stays outside tactical state unless Ouros explicitly selects it. If it participates, its occupational record gives no tactical bonus. AutoPTU resolves the battle and the owning service layer decides whether the route resumes.

## Assignment Suitability Review

Noncombat encounter.

The player or an NPC team reviews a task requirement against:

- exact Pokémon identity;
- current authoritative mechanical references;
- prior supervised work observations;
- equipment compatibility;
- current worksite constraints;
- available supervision;
- care/safety restrictions.

The interesting choice can be between a narrow assignment, a supervised trial, equipment substitution, rescheduling or using a human/technical alternative.

This concept requires no new battle capability unless the chosen trial itself enters mechanical resolution.
