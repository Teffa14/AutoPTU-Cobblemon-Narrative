# Marea managed strength and ecosystem feedback seeds — pass 223

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-03

## Scope

These seeds exercise pass 223 without assigning new species, predator-prey relations, authority jurisdiction, starter-zone boundaries or numerical PTU progression rules.

## Seed: The Route Is Busy for a Reason

A beginner-access route receives constant Trainer traffic. The local wild population does not stay weak because the map says so. Actual battle volume creates combat-exposure pressure, while captures, recruitment, migration and ordinary turnover continually change who remains.

Field observations begin showing a small upper tail of individuals performing noticeably better in legitimate battles. The useful question is not `why did the zone level up?` but `which members are becoming risky, and why are they still here?`

The authority response can be observation, warning, temporary restriction, deterrence, controlled relocation or another later-canonized measure. The population remains finite throughout.

## Seed: The Strong One Was Moved, Not Deleted

A persistent dangerous outlier is removed from a child-development area after repeated reports. Weeks later, another ecosystem records an unfamiliar strong individual.

If provenance confirms the relocation, both records describe the same Pokémon. Its strength and identity persist. The destination ecosystem receives its ecological and competitive pressure.

This can create a second-order institutional problem: making one place safer can alter another place.

## Seed: Fewer Predators, More Young Trees Eaten

Without canonizing a specific predator-prey pair, a future source-backed pair can demonstrate a trophic feedback.

A predator population falls or shifts away. Prey members spend more time in a vegetation patch and/or increase in abundance. Repeated browsing pressure accumulates. NPCs first notice fewer successful saplings, depleted forage and more tracks rather than receiving a UI message that `predation_pressure = -20%`.

The causal chain remains investigable and potentially reversible.

## Seed: Capture Season Changes More Than Count

A frequently captured population loses disproportionately visible or battle-prone members. Total abundance falls, but so can the composition of strength/life-stage/behavioral exposure.

Later encounters feel different because a real population changed, not because the encounter table silently rebalanced.

A field report can initially misread fewer strong sightings as population recovery or pacification. Later evidence may show selective capture instead.

## Seed: The Woods Learn the Pressure

Repeated population use of one habitat patch raises disturbance/resource pressure over several world windows. The manifestation is gradual: trails become clearer, forage thins, young vegetation is browsed, nesting or territorial signs concentrate, and some species shift activity elsewhere.

When pressure later falls, recovery is also gradual. The biome does not toggle instantly between two skins.

## Full simulation encounter: Managed Development Corridor

A future beginner corridor can combine:

- high native encounter availability;
- finite population ledgers;
- actual Trainer battle-result ingestion;
- abstract off-screen wild conflict pressure;
- capture and demographic writeback;
- strength-distribution drift;
- persistent dangerous outliers;
- management monitoring and response;
- neighboring ecosystem transfer effects;
- NPC observations and public warnings;
- visible vegetation/resource pressure.

The critical invariant is that safety is an institutional/ecological outcome, not a hard-coded wild Level ceiling.

## Long-term arc: A Safe Route Is Work

The player gradually learns that beginner regions are safe because generations of residents, Trainers and institutions maintain them.

Potential observations across time:

- battle volume rises during a youth-training season;
- stronger survivors become more common;
- captures and recruitment reshape the population;
- seasonal migrants alter the risk profile;
- an exceptional Pokémon forces temporary intervention;
- neighboring habitat receives relocated or dispersing members;
- vegetation and prey activity change as pressures shift;
- later NPC dialogue refers to the same history through reports and memories.

The route can therefore remain familiar while never being ecologically frozen.
