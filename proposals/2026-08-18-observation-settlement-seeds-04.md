# Ouros Observation & Settlement Seeds — Pass 04

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. Names, institutions, locations and exact mechanics remain placeholders until canon and PTU/AutoPTU review.

## Seed AE — The Nesting Window

A research group has evidence that a normally elusive species uses a particular valley for only a short seasonal nesting window.

The objective begins with observation rather than capture.

Players may:
- locate signs without disturbing the habitat;
- document group behavior;
- compare several nesting sites;
- identify an outside disturbance;
- escort another observer;
- intervene only if an immediate threat emerges.

Possible consequences:
- a protected route is proposed;
- a tourism faction wants controlled access;
- a poaching rumor gains credibility;
- a later migration pattern changes;
- the player earns access to better field information.

No species is assigned until regional ecology and PTU legality are reviewed.

## Seed AF — The Wrong Monster

A settlement believes a wild Pokémon is responsible for damaged food stores.

Several clues point toward it because residents have repeatedly seen it nearby. Direct observation reveals that the Pokémon is scavenging after the damage occurs rather than causing it.

The real cause may be:
- infrastructure failure;
- another species;
- an NPC theft;
- a supply-chain mistake;
- a faction testing access to storage.

Design value:
Observation can prevent an unnecessary battle and correct a rumor without making combat an invalid choice in every version of the scenario.

## Seed AG — The Field Notebook

A local research institution maintains a shared field notebook that players can expand through verified observations.

Entries are not generic Pokédex text. They record region-specific facts such as:
- where a population has actually been seen;
- behavior under local weather;
- relationships between species;
- signs of migration;
- dangerous interactions;
- known human disturbances.

As the notebook improves, it can expose new expedition questions and practical preparation advice.

The notebook should visibly preserve contributor provenance where appropriate.

## Seed AH — The Empty Workshop

A settlement has a working building but no specialist capable of operating it.

Players may encounter a potential resident elsewhere whose goals make relocation plausible.

The challenge is not `recruit NPC to unlock shop`.

The candidate may care about:
- available materials;
- safety;
- housing;
- professional independence;
- proximity to family;
- local demand;
- faction pressure;
- access to rare Pokémon or environments.

If the move occurs, the service appears physically in the Minecraft settlement and creates additional relationships and jobs.

## Seed AI — One Bridge, Five Stories

A damaged bridge affects several systems simultaneously.

For different players it creates different content:
- couriers lose a route;
- researchers cannot reach a habitat;
- merchants suffer supply delays;
- wild Pokémon begin using the abandoned road;
- a faction proposes financing repairs in exchange for influence.

Repairing the bridge does not simply restore the old state. It produces secondary effects such as increased traffic, changed encounters and new political leverage.

This seed is a template for infrastructure as a multi-actor narrative node.

## Seed AJ — The Three-Day Survey

A storm is expected to close access to a remote plateau.

The party has a limited number of field windows before conditions change.

Possible priorities:
- map a safe route;
- observe a rare behavior;
- retrieve equipment from an old station;
- investigate a distress beacon;
- establish a temporary shelter;
- collect evidence about an environmental anomaly.

Not every objective can necessarily be completed.

The clock is visible and weather evidence makes the pressure understandable. Missing an objective changes future state rather than resetting the expedition.

## Seed AK — Night at the Outpost

Players establish or inherit a temporary research outpost in a region with recurring threats.

During the day they can explore, gather information, repair systems or improve evacuation options. At night the surrounding world becomes more dangerous.

Preparation choices may alter:
- shelter availability;
- communications;
- medical support;
- escape routes;
- which NPCs can remain safely;
- where an AutoPTU encounter begins.

This is not intended as a universal survival loop. It is an authored scenario structure for specific arcs.

## Seed AL — The Village That Learned

A settlement has a recurring problem with one Pokémon population because residents misunderstand its behavior.

Players can gradually change the community response through:
- field observation;
- public demonstration;
- safer storage or infrastructure;
- training local responders;
- correcting exaggerated rumors;
- negotiating conservation or relocation measures.

The visible outcome is cultural and physical:
NPC dialogue changes, warning signs become more accurate, defensive structures may be removed or modified, and future encounters start under different assumptions.

## Seed AM — The Research Rival

Two field researchers investigate the same ecological mystery but disagree about interpretation.

Neither begins as a liar.

Each has:
- different prior observations;
- preferred methods;
- institutional incentives;
- blind spots;
- relationships with different local NPCs.

Players may share evidence with one, both or neither.

Later findings can vindicate one theory, reveal that both were incomplete, or expose a third cause. The rivalry may become respectful, hostile or collaborative based on conduct rather than a binary choice flag.

## Seed AN — The Town That Became Worth Visiting

A modest settlement begins with only a few reliable services and little reason for outsiders to stop there.

Across unrelated arcs, improvements accumulate:
- a repaired route increases traffic;
- a healer relocates after a rescue arc;
- a researcher establishes a field office;
- a festival becomes viable after local safety improves;
- a transport service adds the settlement to its route;
- nearby ecology becomes a reason for visitors to arrive.

The town becomes a stronger hub because of connected consequences, not because the player purchased `Town Level 2`.

Minecraft should express the growth physically through changed NPC presence, buildings, signs, activity and services.

## Seed AO — The Quiet Day Is Evidence

A route known for frequent Pokémon activity suddenly becomes unusually quiet.

Nothing attacks the player.

The absence itself is the clue.

Possible explanations include:
- migration;
- predator arrival;
- construction noise;
- pollution;
- weather pressure;
- a faction capturing aggressively;
- disturbance from a nearby dungeon.

The adventure begins because expected world behavior failed to occur.

This seed encourages the narrative system to detect anomalies in encounter ecology, including missing activity rather than only new threats.

## Seed AP — Shared Survey, Different Rewards

A multiplayer expedition sends several characters into the same region, but each carries a different purpose.

One may be documenting habitat use.
Another is testing route safety.
Another wants a capture opportunity.
Another is escorting an NPC.
Another is investigating faction activity.

They share travel and consequences without requiring identical objectives.

The expedition can produce a common Chronicle event plus player-specific knowledge and relationship outputs.

## Seed AQ — The Cost of Convenience

A proposed settlement upgrade clearly improves one service while creating a new pressure elsewhere.

Examples:
- brighter lighting improves night safety but disrupts nocturnal Pokémon;
- a faster ferry improves trade but increases coastal disturbance;
- a new road improves rescue access but fragments habitat;
- a larger market attracts visitors and pickpocketing;
- stronger flood control protects homes but changes a wetland.

The point is not to make all progress morally bad. Improvements should occasionally create realistic second-order effects that become future content.

## Seed AR — The Report Nobody Wants

Players produce credible evidence that an institution's preferred explanation for a problem is wrong.

The conflict is about what happens to the evidence.

Possible stakeholders:
- researchers protecting reputation;
- a merchant fearing economic harm;
- residents afraid of panic;
- a conservation group wanting immediate publication;
- a faction that benefits from delay.

The challenge can remain entirely social/investigative, or later create a protection encounter if someone attempts to seize or destroy evidence.

Knowledge has political consequences without becoming magical omniscience.

## New implementation questions

- Which existing AutoPTU data can expose Pokémon behavior-relevant capabilities without inventing biology not present in canon?
- How should observation events distinguish authored ecological truth from probabilistic encounter behavior?
- Should field reports be per-player, per-party or institution-shared in multiplayer?
- Which settlement services can be represented directly through Cobblemon/Minecraft NPCs and which require custom mod UI?
- How should regional clocks advance when no relevant player is online?
- Which clocks pause during server downtime versus continue in world time?
- How much settlement degradation is fun before maintenance starts feeling like chores?
- How should objective feasibility query actual Minecraft/Cobblemon state before a quest is offered?
- Which locations deserve dense repeated use, and which should remain one-off discoveries?
