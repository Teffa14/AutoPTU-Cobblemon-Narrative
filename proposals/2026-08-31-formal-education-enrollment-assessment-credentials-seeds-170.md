# Ouros formal education, enrollment, assessment & credentials seeds — Pass 170

Status: NON-CANON CANDIDATES
Date: 2026-08-31

These candidates are original Ouros material derived from high-level educational structures. They do not establish canon institutions, curricula, laws, grades or PTU progression.

## The Student Who Finished but Never Graduated

A learner completed every required component under an older programme version, but the institution changed its credential process before the final award was issued.

The mystery is administrative and historical rather than conspiratorial by default. The important records are the old curriculum, the learner's component history, the later credential rule and whatever transition policy actually existed.

Possible consequences:

A future employer accepts the evidence anyway.

Another school asks for formal recognition.

A retired instructor remembers the final project but cannot establish legal or institutional authority.

The player can help reconstruct provenance without deciding the answer through a battle.

## Five Times Someone Said “She Graduated Here”

Five speakers can mean different things:

she attended for years;

she completed the programme;

she received a certificate;

she took part in the graduation ceremony;

she became a famous alumna later.

The quest teaches the player that institutional memory compresses distinct events into one phrase.

## The Field Project That Became More Important Than the Course

A student team once mapped an ordinary wetland for a course assignment. Decades later the map is one of the few records showing a habitat before a major environmental change.

The academic mark is minor. The artifact becomes scientifically and historically significant much later.

This links Education to Cartography, Scientific Research, Archives and Ecology without changing any old academic result.

## The Academy With No Standard Age

A specialist institution accepts beginners, working adults and older returning learners into the same practical programme. Their social relationships matter more than age hierarchy.

The institution creates unusual cohorts: an experienced craft worker may be a novice in field ecology while a teenager already has years of practical Pokémon care experience.

No mechanical Skill Rank follows automatically from biography.

## The Temporary Student Tool

An institution issues a specialized device for one course. Alumni repeatedly misremember it as a graduation gift because some early cohorts were allowed to keep theirs under a discontinued policy.

The object can have separate Material Culture custody, Education issuance and Archive policy records.

## The Course That Was Renamed Three Times

A long-running field course changes title and curriculum as knowledge improves. Older alumni insist they “took the same class,” while the actual programme versions differ substantially.

The story can expose generational change in accepted ecological or historical knowledge without making earlier teachers foolish.

## The Transfer With Two Correct Answers

A learner moves between institutions. The first school records a component as completed. The second institution refuses to treat it as equivalent because its own programme expects a different learning outcome.

Both records can be internally correct.

The dispute creates a recognition episode rather than an automatic continuity error.

## The School Archive Fire That Did Not Erase the Students

A historical incident destroys part of an institution's central records. Personal notebooks, photographs, field reports, copies held by families and later alumni testimony reconstruct only part of the history.

Missing records remain missing. Narrative does not infer that a person failed to attend because the school lost the file.

## The Former Student Who Returns as Staff

An NPC introduced years earlier as an uncertain learner later returns as a laboratory assistant, groundskeeper, instructor, administrator or field coordinator.

The role transition should preserve the old enrollment episode rather than replace it with a generic “school NPC” identity.

## The Teacher Everyone Calls Professor

A community uses an honorific socially for a respected teacher. The title may have no formal relationship to any qualification or institutional rank.

This candidate exists to protect the distinction between public address, job role, academic credential and PTU Class/Feature.

## The Practical Exam Interrupted by a Real Emergency

A supervised field assessment is interrupted by an actual incident.

The rich encounter version would require escort/protection, lifecycle, hazards or reactions and tactical-objective AI if all students and evaluators remain tactical actors.

Reduced version:

The exam is suspended before initiative. Students and evaluators withdraw. AutoPTU resolves the immediate conventional threat. Narrative later decides whether the assessment is rescheduled, partially credited or invalidated according to the institution's authored rule.

Battle result never equals exam result.

## The Student Who Left and Returned Ten Years Later

A learner withdraws for work, family, travel, illness, relocation or another authored reason. Years later the institution still exists but the programme has changed.

The story can revolve around what prior learning is recognized, which requirements are new and which old relationships survived.

The system must preserve UNKNOWN rather than invent the reason for withdrawal if canon never established it.

## The Village School That Moves With the Season

A community has no permanent academy building. Instruction rotates through settlements or follows a seasonal route.

Persistent state lives in teachers, learner records, books, equipment custody, schedules and recurring meeting places rather than one Minecraft structure.

This is useful for remote areas where a giant institutional campus would be worldbuilding overreach.

## The Alumni Project Nobody Expected to Survive

A cohort builds or restores something small as part of a supervised project: a footbridge, observation hide, public notice board, garden, archive index or safety shelter.

Years later the structure still exists and successive cohorts maintain, modify or stop using it.

The artifact's maintenance history belongs to its physical/institutional domain; Education preserves why the original project existed.

## The Independent Study That Went Off Course

A learner proposes a self-directed project. The original question turns out to be wrong, but the fieldwork uncovers a different useful pattern.

The educational outcome, scientific conclusion and public importance can all diverge.

This supports stories where learning rewards correction rather than always validating the student's first theory.

## Long arc: One Institution, Four Generations

A modest regional school persists across several campaigns.

Generation one establishes the programme and records rough field knowledge.

Generation two inherits the institution but revises weak practices.

Generation three expands into new specialist tracks and stronger outside partnerships.

Generation four debates whether the school should remain local or become a larger regional institution.

Player actions can affect facilities, archives, staff careers, alumni networks and public trust without requiring the school to become the center of every campaign.

## Long arc: The Curriculum Catches Up With the World

A major ecological, technological or historical discovery makes an old lesson obsolete.

Teachers do not instantly update. Some materials remain in circulation. Students learn different versions depending on cohort and location. A later revision explains when the institution formally changed its programme.

This creates temporal texture and lets public knowledge evolve without rewriting the past.

## Long arc: The Alumni Network Nobody Designed

Former learners scatter across towns, research stations, clinics, workshops, Ranger-like services, battle circuits and local government.

Years later the school matters because those people share a history, not because the institution controls them.

The network can create introductions, conflicting loyalties, old project callbacks and archive discoveries. Alumni status never grants automatic trust, employment or authority.
