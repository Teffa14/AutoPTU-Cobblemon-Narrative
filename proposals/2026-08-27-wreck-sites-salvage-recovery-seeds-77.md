# Ouros Wreck-Site, Salvage & Recovery Seeds — Pass 77

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes locations, institutions, laws, wreck identities, technologies or historical events in Ouros.

These candidates are implementation-aware examples for `design/wreck-sites-salvage-recovery-preservation-extension.md`.

## 1. The Hatch That Was Never Opened

A wreck has been visited for years, but a recent condition survey reveals a hatch that older maps marked as inaccessible rather than opened.

The interesting decision is whether to intervene immediately or document the surrounding structure first.

Possible outcomes:

- the hatch opens to an ordinary service compartment whose contents correct an old historical assumption;
- the hatch remains sealed because movement around it is worsening the structure;
- a later archival source reveals why previous surveyors deliberately stopped there;
- a wild Pokémon group is using the adjacent void and access planning changes.

No outcome requires treasure.

## 2. Cargo Seen, Ownership Unknown

A partially flooded cargo space contains identifiable crates or containers still in place.

Multiple actors can make plausible claims:

- a former operator or successor institution;
- a recipient named in an old manifest;
- a current custodian of the wreck;
- a researcher who wants the contents left in context;
- a conservation actor concerned about ecological disturbance.

The scenario works even if nobody has enough evidence to authorize removal.

## 3. The Wreck Became a Nursery

Repeated surveys show that a section of abandoned infrastructure is now used during a recurring juvenile-care or nesting period by local Pokémon.

The old recovery plan is therefore no longer a purely technical question.

Possible handoffs:

- Conservation reviews seasonal access;
- Travel changes a route around the site;
- Archives document the change in site meaning;
- Maintenance evaluates whether loose structural pieces threaten the habitat;
- Public Memory later interprets the site as both industrial history and ecological refuge.

The story must not infer parental relationships or species-wide behavior from one observation.

## 4. The Survey Map Is More Valuable Than the Object

A conspicuous object draws attention, but mapping the compartment reveals more historically useful information than removing it.

The reward is a better world-state record:

- original placement;
- nearby structural modifications;
- maker marks;
- cargo grouping;
- later deposits;
- route into the compartment.

The object can remain in place.

## 5. One Wreck, Two Histories

Two archive sources describe the same former vessel or facility differently.

The site itself contains evidence supporting parts of both accounts.

The player reconstructs chronology from:

- modifications to the structure;
- repair marks;
- signage;
- cargo layout;
- later ecological occupation;
- recovered paperwork;
- witness or institutional records.

The resolution may be that the records describe different operational phases rather than one source being false.

## 6. Selective Recovery, Not Clearance

A recovery team wants one exact object because it is deteriorating faster than the surrounding site.

The task is intentionally narrow.

Success means:

- document context;
- reach the object;
- remove only the reviewed target;
- establish custody;
- leave the rest of the site unchanged;
- record the physical intervention.

This candidate is useful for teaching the player that recovery does not equal looting the whole area.

## 7. The Storm Exposed a New Section

After a world-state weather event, sediment or debris shifts enough to reveal a previously unknown entrance.

The discovery creates a survey opportunity, not an automatic dungeon unlock.

Later consequences can include:

- temporary closure while condition is assessed;
- a short window before sediment returns;
- new ecological observations;
- an object visible but unreachable;
- a new connection between two mapped zones.

Weather remains overworld state unless PTU weather mapping becomes mechanically verified.

## 8. Recovery Equipment Left Behind

An earlier intervention was interrupted. Some equipment remains at the site.

Later investigators must distinguish:

- original wreck material;
- recovery-team equipment;
- objects deposited by later visitors;
- current debris.

This becomes a provenance puzzle rather than a simple missing-item quest.

## 9. The Former Crew Returns as Visitors

A former worker, passenger or operator-linked actor visits the wreck after it becomes accessible.

Their memory provides claims and emotional context, but not omniscient truth.

Useful tensions:

- a remembered room layout differs from the current structure;
- they recognize a modification that archive plans omit;
- they ask that one object remain in place;
- they discover that wild Pokémon now occupy a space they remember differently.

No private emotion should be inferred beyond what the actor states or demonstrates.

## 10. The Public Route Is Too Successful

A stabilized wreck becomes a limited public-access site. Visitor pressure then creates new wear, litter, shortcut paths or disturbance around one zone.

The follow-up story concerns adaptation:

- reroute visitors;
- close one section seasonally;
- improve interpretation so people stop entering a sensitive area;
- add monitoring;
- reconsider which objects stay exposed.

The initial preservation success creates a second-order problem instead of ending the site's history.

## 11. The Component That Could Restore Something Else

A derelict structure contains a component that could help repair an active facility elsewhere.

That practical value conflicts with other reasons to leave the component where it is.

The player may need evidence about:

- whether the component is actually compatible;
- whether removal damages the wreck;
- whether another replacement can be procured;
- whether the wreck has historical or ecological significance;
- who has authority to decide.

Winning a battle cannot decide compatibility or ownership.

## 12. The Wreck That Might Sail Again

A vessel or transport structure once considered a permanent ruin becomes a plausible restoration candidate after a new survey.

The long arc separates:

- historical identity;
- current structural condition;
- ecological occupation;
- restoration feasibility;
- funding/procurement;
- public memory;
- eventual active-service record.

If restored, the same wreck-site history remains attached to the new operational asset.

## Mystery: Seven Objects, Four Contexts

Seven visually similar objects are recorded around a wreck.

At first they look like one cargo batch.

Closer provenance work shows four contexts:

- original cargo;
- equipment from an early recovery attempt;
- later visitor loss;
- one object whose origin remains unresolved.

The player uses position history, maker marks, material condition, old photographs, survey notes and custody records.

The conclusion can preserve uncertainty for the seventh object.

## Long arc: A Wreck Changes Meaning

### Visit 1 — Landmark

The site is simply a known obstacle or navigation marker. Only the exterior is mapped.

### Visit 2 — Survey

A bounded expedition establishes zones, blocked passages, condition and first ecological observations.

### Visit 3 — Historical Interest

A new archive record or recovered object makes one part of the site relevant to a broader history.

### Visit 4 — Competing Intervention Goals

Different actors want documentation, selective recovery, stabilization, ecological protection or eventual access.

No actor is automatically correct because of institutional status.

### Visit 5 — Physical Change

One reviewed intervention occurs. Minecraft reflects it visibly.

### Visit 6 — Consequence

A later season reveals new structural, ecological or social effects.

### Visit 7 — New Normal

The place now has a different role in the region while retaining its full prior history.

## Encounter: Flooded Compartment Withdrawal

Status: PROPOSED.

Narrative premise:

A survey team reaches a flooded interior zone and encounters defensive wild Pokémon. The immediate goal is safe withdrawal and later reassessment.

### Intended full version

Desired mechanics may include:

- water/underwater movement where legally defined;
- narrow footprint and LoS pressure;
- route-clearing or withdrawal objectives;
- interception or forced displacement;
- environmental visibility/current effects only if PTU/Caelo defines them and AutoPTU supports them;
- territorial AI that can withdraw;
- adapter playback in the exact wreck geometry.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING if used;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL where relevant;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if the flooded environment has tactical effects;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING.

### Reduced version

The survey team withdraws through world state before tactical combat begins. A static ordinary encounter happens in a reviewed accessible zone. Flooding remains visual/world state only. No drowning, current, pressure, visibility penalty or scripted water damage is invented.

After the battle, the site remains subject to a separate survey/access decision.

## Encounter: Selective Recovery Perimeter

Status: PROPOSED.

Narrative premise:

A team is recovering one deteriorating object while wild Pokémon use the surrounding remains.

### Intended full version

Potential dependencies include:

- PROTECT/CLEAR_ROUTE objective state;
- moving noncombatants;
- fragile zones;
- interception/forced movement;
- terrain/hazards if governing rules support them;
- territorial/withdrawal tactical AI;
- exact adapter synchronization.

The permanent capability map therefore touches the same families as above, with complete movement, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback currently blocking the rich version.

### Reduced version

Pause the recovery. Remove technicians and the object from the tactical grid. Resolve a standard static battle nearby. Afterward, perform the recovery as a separate authoritative world interaction, append provenance and transfer custody according to the owning system.

## Encounter: Exposed Deck Access Window

Status: PROPOSED.

Narrative premise:

A short-lived world-state condition makes a normally inaccessible deck reachable long enough for a survey or retrieval attempt.

### Intended full version

The rich form could involve changing access, weather, retreat timing and environment-aware AI.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement — BLOCKING if dynamic displacement/interception matters;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- lifecycle/damage/status/moves/abilities/items/features — PARTIAL as individually used;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING for active tactical weather/access change;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

### Reduced version

Use the access window only as an overworld timer/state gate before the battle. Once combat begins, freeze the arena into a reviewed static layout. If the world-state window closes, resolve that after combat rather than mutating the grid through unimplemented weather/hazard rules.

## Noncombat scenario: Context Before Collection

Players discover an apparently valuable object during a survey.

Available actions can include observation, photography, mapping, archive comparison, specialist consultation, selective recovery request or leaving it in place.

This scenario can be fully playable without tactical engine expansion because the meaningful choice is whether and how to intervene.
