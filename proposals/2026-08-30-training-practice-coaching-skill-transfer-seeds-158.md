## Design goal

These candidates make practice visible as ordinary life, relationship history and adventure material. None grants a Move, Feature, Edge, Skill Rank, stat change, Tutor Point, qualification or battle bonus unless a separate PTU/Caelo-authorized transaction exists.

## The Court With Chalk That Never Matches

A public practice court is redrawn by different instructors. Old lines remain faintly visible beneath new layouts. Players can learn which groups used the space and what they valued by comparing archived photographs, notices and current markings.

Useful hooks:

- a returning coach recognizes an obsolete exercise pattern;
- an old tournament layout explains why one barrier was moved years ago;
- two clubs both claim the same evening slot because their schedules use different revisions;
- a player can choose which instructor's version of the drill to try.

No chalk pattern grants terrain effects by itself.

## The Coach Who Changes the Question

A coach watches a learner practice switching and concludes that the larger issue may be information timing rather than switching technique. The plan is revised.

The interesting state is the revision itself. The first sessions remain historically valid. The coach's new interpretation is an attributed claim, not hidden truth.

## The Trainer Who Keeps Winning the Wrong Drill

A learner repeatedly wins a controlled spar while failing the stated practice objective. The session history therefore records victories and unsatisfied demonstration criteria at the same time.

This candidate protects a core Ouros principle: `BATTLE_WON != TRAINING_OBJECTIVE_SATISFIED`.

## The Student Who Demonstrated It Once

A novice executes a difficult positioning idea correctly during one session. Everyone remembers the moment. Later sessions show inconsistent reproduction.

The story can become encouraging, frustrating or simply realistic without a secret mastery percentage. The Chronicle stores the successful demonstration and later evidence separately.

## The Old Drill Became Bad Advice

A respected local exercise was designed around a challenge format that changed years ago. Older Trainers still teach it because it was genuinely useful in their era.

A research or social quest can reconstruct when the format changed and why the drill persisted. Nobody needs to be incompetent or dishonest.

## The Practice Partner Who Outgrew the Role

Two recurring peers have trained together for years. One eventually stops being the right partner for a particular exercise because their roster, goals or schedule changed.

The relationship remains intact. The practice thread changes partners.

`TRAINING_PARTNERSHIP_ENDED != FRIENDSHIP_ENDED`.

## The Gym Annex at Dawn

A formal battle institution uses one side court for early practice before registration opens. Staff, challengers, local workers and retired competitors overlap there in ways that never appear during official Gym battles.

Possible worldbuilding outputs:

- recurring morning NPC schedules;
- informal advice networks;
- old equipment with institutional provenance;
- learners who know each other long before meeting in a formal bracket;
- practice stories that later become public callbacks.

The annex does not need a unique battle mechanic to matter.

## The Specialist Who Refuses to Tutor That Move

A known instructor is a legitimate Move Tutor under the approved rules but cannot or will not teach every possible Move. Their legal capability, personal availability and target eligibility remain separate facts.

This produces a useful quest without inventing arbitrary mechanical access. The actual tutoring transaction must still pass the governing rules gate.

## The Lesson That Cost Nothing Mechanical

A retired Trainer spends an afternoon helping a younger Trainer review two battles. No XP, Feature, Move or item changes hands. Months later, the younger Trainer remembers a specific phrase or diagram because it altered how they think about a problem.

This seed exists to establish that meaningful training scenes do not require numerical rewards.

## The Retraining Conversation

A character is considering a build change that the adopted PTU rules may allow through Retraining. An NPC helps them think through what the change means in-world.

The conversation can precede, accompany or follow the mechanical transaction. It cannot execute the transaction by itself.

Potential consequences:

- an old technique becomes part of personal history rather than current mechanics;
- a mentor disagrees but respects the choice;
- a club needs someone else to cover an abandoned role;
- old battle reports remain accurate for the historical build.

## The Three Coaches Problem

Three instructors watch the same public battle and recommend different priorities:

- one focuses on spacing;
- one focuses on resource discipline;
- one thinks the team choice created the problem before the battle began.

All three can cite real evidence. The player chooses what to test next. Later results may narrow the disagreement without producing a universal “correct style.”

## The Empty Practice Ground

A familiar training venue is empty for an ordinary reason: schedule change, maintenance, seasonal event, staff reassignment or another institution using it. The absence becomes environmental storytelling because players know what normal looks like.

No quest is required unless another state intersects it.

## The Drill Nobody Finished

A session is interrupted by a power outage, evacuation, sudden weather closure, urgent call or unrelated world event. The training thread ends as `INTERRUPTED`, preserving the exact stage reached.

Returning later creates choices:

- resume the same exercise;
- revise it because conditions changed;
- retire it because the original goal no longer matters.

## The Novice Teaches Back

An experienced player supervises a beginner through a narrow exercise. At the end, the beginner explains the concept back in their own words or demonstrates it to another novice.

This creates a late-game social callback without automatically granting either character a mechanical teaching Feature.

## The Club With Ten Versions of the Same Drill

A long-lived battle club archives revisions of one exercise across generations. Each version reflects a different venue layout, challenge contract or coaching philosophy.

A current member can reconstruct the lineage and choose an older variant for historical interest. No version is mechanically legal until its selected combat content and arena contract are reviewed.

## The Partner Pokémon Changes the Routine

A Trainer's regular practice plan becomes inappropriate after their partner evolves, changes legal Move options through authoritative progression, or takes on a different role.

The plan is revised around observed current capability. Evolution itself remains owned by the Pokémon Evolution layer, and mechanical content remains authoritative elsewhere.

## The Coach Who Was Right for the Old Team

A coach's advice produced good results when the learner used an earlier roster. Months later, new teammates make the same advice less useful.

This seed supports staleness without retconning competence. The coach may still be an excellent instructor.

## The Scrimmage Everyone Remembers Differently

Several participants later describe the same practice battle differently because each was focused on a different learning objective. AutoPTU preserves the tactical result. Personal records preserve different observations.

Potential mystery:

A later journalist asks what “really happened.” The answer may be that the factual battle is clear while the lesson people took from it differs.

## The Practice Roster Leak

Someone sees a Trainer using a Pokémon during an informal drill and assumes it will appear in an upcoming official challenge. The sighting is real. The inference may be wrong.

Competitive Scouting can store the public observation if legitimately obtained. Battle Institutions still resolves the legal future roster separately.

`PRACTICE_ROSTER_SEEN != FORMAL_ROSTER_CONFIRMED`.

## The Instructor Who Only Watches

A coach spends an entire session observing without giving live commands. Their later feedback can be more useful because the learner's decisions were visible without intervention.

This supports training scenes where NPC mentors do not become tactical puppeteers or dominate the battle.

## The Practice Site Became Something Else

An old dojo annex becomes a clinic staging room, community hall, storage site or classroom after an institutional change. Former users still recognize floor marks, trophy hooks or wall repairs.

The site retains training history while its current function belongs to another system.

## Five Times Someone Said “They Trained There”

A provenance mystery distinguishes five claims:

1. the Trainer attended one public workshop;
2. the Trainer practiced there weekly for a season;
3. the Trainer was formally enrolled at the institution;
4. the Trainer received one private coaching session;
5. the Trainer used the location independently when it was open to the public.

All five speakers can be sincere while describing different relationships to the same place.

## Three Lessons, One Battle

An authoritative practice battle produces one tactical result. Three different observers record three different lessons:

- the coach records a spacing problem;
- the learner records hesitation before a switch;
- a spectator records an impressive recovery after an error.

The Chronicle preserves all three attributed observations without converting them into one truth score.

## Long arc — The Team That Learns in Public

Early phase:

A small group of recurring Trainers use an ordinary practice site. Sessions establish schedules, coaches, preferred drills and partner relationships before any major competitive arc.

Middle phase:

Formal battles create public records. Scouting and media begin to circulate interpretations. The group changes some training plans, rejects others and occasionally disagrees with coaches. One participant leaves for a different path; another begins teaching novices.

Later phase:

A new challenge format or institutional transition makes several old drills obsolete. The group can adapt, preserve some exercises as tradition, and reinterpret old victories without erasing them.

Possible payoff:

The final callback is not necessarily a championship. It can be a younger cohort using a revised drill whose provenance reaches back to the players' earliest sessions.

## Long arc — The Coach Who Keeps Revising the Notebook

A recurring coach maintains observations over years. Early notes are confident but incomplete. Later battles expose exceptions. The coach annotates, retracts and reframes earlier advice instead of becoming infallible.

This arc can connect Personal Records, Competitive Scouting, Rivalry and Social Bonds. The notebook remains a character artifact, not an AI policy file.

## Encounter concept — Pressure Drill: Protect the Marker

Full intended version:

A controlled exercise asks combatants to protect or deny a marked area while opponents adapt. Intended mechanics may include Intercept, forced movement, round-based objective changes, reactions and objective-aware AI.

Capability dependency:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED
- base movement legality: VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: PARTIAL
- core calculations: VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING for rich objective/reaction behavior
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL; selected content audit required
- abilities: PARTIAL; selected content audit required
- items: PARTIAL; selected content audit required
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL; selected content audit required
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support: BLOCKING

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version:

Use a static arena and explicit combatants. The marker is presentation/context only. AutoPTU resolves an ordinary audited spar. The coach records observed positioning events afterward. No engine claim says the learner protected the marker as a formal objective.

Reduced status: READY at narrative-contract level, subject to selected combat-content audit.

## Encounter concept — Positioning Labyrinth Scrimmage

Full intended version:

Practice barriers change between rounds, route-control decisions matter and forced movement/reactions can alter access.

Key blockers: complete movement remains partial; lifecycle remains partial; dynamic zones/reactions and tactical policy remain blocking; adapter playback remains blocking.

Reduced version:

Freeze all barriers before initiative. Use only static blockers supported by reviewed geometry. No moving walls, scripted displacement or hazard effects. The result is an ordinary battle plus post-session observations.

Reduced status: READY at narrative-contract level.

## Encounter concept — Partner Rotation Exercise

Full intended version:

Learners rotate partners during one structured exercise while the system preserves only the state the approved contract says persists.

Key dependencies: full lifecycle, complete movement where selected content needs it, stateful damage/status if state crosses stages, move/ability/item/Feature coverage, tactical AI, adapter playback.

Full status: BLOCKED for a single continuously mutating BattleSpec.

Reduced version:

Create separate BattleSpecs for each pairing. Ouros performs a session checkpoint between them. No HP, status, initiative, consumable or tactical state crosses the boundary without an explicit verified contract.

Reduced status: READY.

Full intended version:

An instructor creates a situation where the current authoritative Intercept path may legally trigger, allowing learners to review candidate discovery, positioning and outcome through semantic events.

Capability dependency:

- base movement legality: VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: PARTIAL despite current Intercept evidence
- core calculations: VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING as a generalized family
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL
- abilities: PARTIAL
- items: PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support: BLOCKING

Reduced status: READY.
