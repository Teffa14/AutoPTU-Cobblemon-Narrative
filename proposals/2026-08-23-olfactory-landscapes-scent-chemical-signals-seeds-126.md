# Pass 126 Proposals — Olfactory Landscapes, Scent & Chemical Signals

Status: NON-CANON candidates. These are design seeds, not established Ouros lore.

## 30 candidate concepts

1. The Bakery Before Dawn — a street’s morning smellscape changes after a bakery relocates, and nearby urban Pokémon shift their timing for reasons still under study.
2. The Missing Orchard Scent — a flowering orchard looks normal but its expected seasonal fragrance is absent; pollination, weather, pruning and cultivar changes remain separate hypotheses.
3. Three Smells at One Drain — residents report chemical, organic and sulfur-like odors at different times; later investigation shows three independent sources sharing one corridor.
4. The Stunky Detour — repeated defensive odor events coincide with wild Pokémon avoiding one path, but no one yet knows why the Stunky began using that site.
5. The Old Perfume Workshop — archived recipes, bottles and worker notes preserve a vanished district smellscape without proving exact historical composition.
6. The Slurpuff Shift — a bakery’s scent-sensitive partner detects a recipe drift before instruments or customers do; the result becomes a quality investigation, not a magical diagnosis.
7. The Spritzee Diet Year — one persistent Spritzee’s fragrance changes over several months after its food source changes, giving researchers a longitudinal record of identity plus context.
8. Smoke Over Flowers — wildfire smoke masks a migration-season scent cue at one stopover; the migration shift remains a hypothesis until repeated evidence exists.
9. The Marking Post Nobody Owns — several wild individuals use the same old post for scent marking; locals incorrectly treat it as one Pokémon’s permanent territory.
10. The Clean Room Smells Wrong — workers notice a new odor after maintenance even though sensors remain nominal; the source is eventually traced to an approved replacement material.
11. The Harbor Morning — fish market, ferry fuel, seaweed, bakery exhaust and tidepool odor overlap into a place identity that changes sharply on market-free days.
12. The Scent Map With Blank Areas — a research team learns that “no odor detected” often reflects wind, masking or observer limits rather than true absence.
13. The Skiploom Origin Claim — enthusiasts infer that a visiting Skiploom came from one valley based on fragrance; archival records later narrow the claim but never make scent alone definitive.
14. The Festival Perfume — a recurring civic event uses a seasonal fragrance blend; different editions preserve different recipes and public memories.
15. The Quiet Compost Year — a compost site loses its usual odor profile after a process change; wildlife use changes in parallel but causation stays under review.
16. The Tunnel Scent Boundary — a cave passage carries a strong outside scent after ventilation changes, altering how researchers interpret wildlife movement underground.
17. The Water That Smells Different — residents report a change at several taps while treatment data remain normal; the issue proves localized and does not become a universal contamination event.
18. The Former Partner’s Scent — a released Pokémon may have passed through an orchard; a scent-sensitive observer supports the hypothesis but does not restore Trainer authority or prove current proximity.
19. The Market Cleaning Night — a new cleaning schedule removes old food odors and changes urban wildlife visitation without any capture or relocation program.
20. The Wrong Source — an industrial site receives complaints for an odor event whose actual source is a seasonal wetland process farther upwind.
21. The Rain-Washed Trail — trackers lose an olfactory trace after heavy rain while physical prints remain; the two evidence channels diverge legitimately.
22. The Perfumer’s Archive — a museum preserves formula cards and sealed samples from several decades, allowing cultural history without claiming modern recreation is chemically identical.
23. The Nesting Corridor Smell — researchers observe a recurring seasonal odor profile near a colony but avoid publishing precise coordinates.
24. The Two Aromatisse Problem — two individuals share a public nickname but produce distinct observed scent profiles; identity records prevent false merging.
25. The Scent That Outlived the Shop — a storefront closes, yet old materials keep a recognizable odor for months, confusing visitors about whether the business reopened.
26. The Emergency Ventilation Test — responders use a controlled odor tracer to confirm airflow in an empty facility; no toxic or battle effect is implied.
27. The Worker Who Notices First — a machine operator reports a changed smell before a fault becomes visible; maintenance confirms a mechanical issue without turning the observation into a Technology Education bonus.
28. The Decomposition Baseline — a forest research plot builds a multi-year smellscape record around deadwood; one year’s anomaly triggers sampling, not an automatic disease conclusion.
29. The Smellscape Without a Quest — a district’s seasonal odor profile changes because vegetation, shops and transport changed gradually; Chronicle records it even if players never intervene.
30. The Olfactory Atlas — several institutions combine historical notes, worker reports, ecological observations and Pokémon-assisted surveys into dated smellscape maps with explicit uncertainty.

## Long arc 1 — Five Springs in Saffron Orchard

Year 1: researchers establish flowering and smellscape baselines.

Year 2: an unusually wet spring changes flowering timing and weakens one expected scent window.

Year 3: a Spritzee individual begins using the orchard regularly; its observed fragrance differs later in the season after diet changes.

Year 4: nearby development changes wind and visitor patterns. Researchers cannot tell whether altered Pokémon movement reflects odor, noise, light or simple access changes.

Year 5: the orchard adopts a new management plan. The final result is a richer longitudinal record, not a single solved cause.

Connections: Flora, Meteorology, Soundscapes, Light, Urban Development, Pokémon Agency, Science.

## Long arc 2 — The Old Market Smellscape

A market district changes across decades: fish sellers relocate, a rail terminal opens, waste collection improves, a bakery becomes famous, an old perfume shop closes and a wet-weather drain is rebuilt.

Older residents describe the place differently from younger ones because the smellscape genuinely changed.

Archive material, photographs, shop records and oral histories can reconstruct parts of the sensory history without pretending exact chemistry is known.

Connections: Markets, Rail, Waste, Public Memory, Archives, Food, Urban Wildlife.

## Long arc 3 — The Marking Route

A population uses a chain of scent-marking sites along a seasonal route.

Over several years one post disappears, a bridge opens, one location becomes crowded and a released former partner is observed near the route.

Researchers must distinguish:

- persistent route use;
- individual marking;
- shared sites;
- refresh frequency;
- learned route use;
- possible social transmission;
- infrastructure effects.

No single scent mark proves ownership, leadership or a culture-like tradition.

Connections: Tracking, Migration, Social Learning, Road Ecology, Pokémon Agency.
