# Pass 148 — Wayfinding / Route Guidance Candidates

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON

These candidates are designed to exercise persistent guidance history without declaring new Ouros canon.

7. The Long Accessible Route — a longer route is the appropriate documented option for some travellers even while locals prefer stairs.
8. The Burn Scar Reveals Old Markers — wildfire exposes a retired route-marking system under newer vegetation.
9. The Ford Moved — river migration leaves the named crossing downstream from the old sign.
10. The Landmark Was a Tree — a famous route description survives after its central tree dies and is removed.
11. Nesting Closure on the Pilgrim Road — a temporary conservation diversion crosses a historically important route without erasing either history.
12. The Ferry Breaks the Trail — a walking itinerary depends on a ferry window that changed seasonally.
13. Rail Changed the Footpath — a new station makes an old approach path quiet but still valid.
14. The Emergency Detour Became Popular — a temporary route remains socially used after the official route reopens.
15. The Old Topographic Guide — an accurate historical guide points to a road that no longer exists physically.
16. Former Partner at the Waypoint — a released former partner is observed at an old landmark; motive and route knowledge remain unknown.
17. Nothing Happened at Marker Seven — a routine inspection confirms the marker and route are exactly as expected.
18. Translation Lag — one language version of a junction sign still names a destination that the other versions have updated.
19. The Detour Board Never Came Down — a stale notice remains after the disruption ended.
20. Two Junction Numbering Systems — emergency services and hikers use different identifiers for the same physical intersections.
21. Winter Poles — snow buries summer signs while taller seasonal markers remain visible.
22. The Mining Blaze — an abandoned industrial marking is mistaken for modern trail guidance.
23. Follow the Bell — an old route description uses an audible landmark that no longer operates every day.
24. The New Tower Blocks the Old Tower — a familiar visual landmark becomes ambiguous after development.
25. The Unofficial Route Goes Viral — a popular guide spreads faster than the institution can verify it.
26. The Pilgrimage Stone Was Moved — road works relocate a historical marker while keeping the physical route open.

## Longer arcs

### Five Maps of Cloudstep Trail

Year 1 begins with a reliable regional hiking route and a familiar printed guide. A storm removes one bridge. Year 2 introduces a temporary detour; Year 3 shows that most users now prefer part of that detour. In Year 4 vegetation obscures several older visual landmarks while a new accessible alignment opens. By Year 5, five map/guidance revisions can all be historically legitimate.

The arc is about institutional memory, route choice and changing geography. No mastermind is necessary. A player who returns years later can recognize the place while discovering that their own knowledge is stale.

### The Route That Lost Its Landmarks

A mountain route was historically described almost entirely through trees, springs, a watchtower and a ferry landing. Over years, each landmark changes for a different reason: wildfire, groundwater decline, redevelopment and transport modernization. The physical corridor largely survives, but its narrative instructions become less usable.

Local elders, archived guidebooks, new surveyors and inexperienced visitors can all possess different correct fragments. The eventual outcome may be a redesigned guidance system rather than restoration of the old landmarks.

### Three Ways Across Meridian Pass

Three communities historically reach the same pass from different sides and maintain different naming/marker traditions. A new road and emergency service create a fourth standardized numbering system. During several years of Chronicle, the challenge is interoperability rather than choosing a single “true” tradition.

The arc can connect Languages, Emergency Services, Mountain Ecology, Cartography and Travel while preserving each system's authority.

## Encounter contracts

### Lost Hiker at Marker Seven

FULL version: a search team follows imperfect guidance while the missing actor continues moving or sheltering. The encounter may require dynamic search, escort/withdrawal, protected routes and optional confrontation with wildlife.

Dependencies: complete movement including interception/forced movement; AI tactical policy for SEARCH/REACH_ACTOR/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback. If a storm, avalanche, wildfire or other environmental effect changes tactical space, terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions is additionally required.

REDUCED version: route investigation and search resolve in overworld state. The missing actor is located and moved to a safe handoff point outside the grid. If an independent battle remains, AutoPTU receives a static legal arena with only actual combatants. Battle victory does not prove which marker was wrong or complete the rescue automatically.

### Rescue at the Vanished Ford

FULL version: a river crossing no longer matches the sign/map and actors must find or protect a viable crossing while water state can matter tactically.

Dependencies: complete movement; terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for dynamic/shallow/current water if those mechanics are used; AI tactical policy for CROSS/WITHDRAW/PROTECT; adapter/playback.

REDUCED version: Freshwater/Geomorphology and Wayfinding decide the current ford and safe approach before battle. The water and route are frozen for the encounter. AutoPTU receives a conventional static arena without invented current, drowning or mud rules.

### Wildlife Crossing at Temporary Detour

FULL version: a temporary public diversion overlaps a wildlife movement corridor. Wildlife needs non-hostile CROSS/WITHDRAW behavior while travellers are redirected.

Dependencies: complete movement; AI tactical policy; adapter/playback. Environmental zones are required only if a protected corridor has a real supported tactical effect.

REDUCED version: wildlife and civilians are rerouted in world state before any battle. If conflict remains, a static confrontation occurs outside the crossing path. Victory never makes the detour permanent or proves ecological connectivity.

### Signage Dispute

Primarily non-combat. Two route revisions, a historical map, maintenance records, translated signs and accessibility guidance disagree. The correct result may be a scoped conclusion or `UNRESOLVED` rather than a battle or Skill roll.

## Mechanical non-inferences

- Geometric LoS does not prove route awareness.
- Base movement legality does not prove navigation or pathfinding knowledge.
- Survival/Perception concepts do not authorize invented navigation DCs.
- A sign, cairn, blaze or landmark is not automatically a PTU Item or terrain feature.
- Being lost does not apply Confused, Slowed, Accuracy penalties, Speed loss or fatigue.
- A route marker visible in Minecraft does not prove it is current, official or correctly oriented.
- A Cobblemon entity walking a path does not prove the route is safe or authoritative.
