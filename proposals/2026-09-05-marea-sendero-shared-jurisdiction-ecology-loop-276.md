# Marea/Sendero shared-jurisdiction ecology loop — Pass 276

Status: PROPOSED. Fixture-grounded. No canon effect.

Premise

A local ecological concern crosses two human management scopes. One fixture-only body understands or monitors the habitat process. Another fixture-only body controls a route or operational surface affected by any access change. Neither scope silently supersedes the other.

Narrative purpose

Turn ecological governance into a playable investigation about mandates, tradeoffs and implementation rather than a single NPC issuing the objectively correct order.

Player-facing loop

The player first receives the same ecological evidence already available to both parties. The evidence does not identify who has authority to act.

At the site, the player can learn that the ecological process crosses an administrative or operational boundary. The habitat-side actor can propose a temporary access-window change but cannot implement the route change alone. The route-side actor can implement it but is not automatically owner of the ecological interpretation.

A meeting can occur with both present. Presence does not count as approval. The player may need to identify what each mandate actually covers and which approval, consultation and implementation roles apply to this action class.

If the required roles line up, the temporary measure can be authorized with a semantic review horizon. Its Minecraft presentation can be a changed schedule, notice, rope line, NPC instruction or route marker, but that presentation carries no PTU blocker, terrain or interception semantics by itself.

A capacity complication can then delay implementation even after authorization. The route-side actor may be occupied by a separate response. This creates a practical problem without inventing bad faith or ideological opposition.

A later fixture-only emergency can trigger a bounded temporary pause under an authored emergency mandate. The emergency action must expire or enter review and cannot expand that body's ordinary authority.

Quest consequences

A player who maps mandates accurately can unlock a coordinated temporary action without forcing either side into a fabricated consensus.

A player who assumes that the ecology-facing body controls all access can be corrected by world evidence rather than a hidden quest fail flag.

A player who treats attendance or silence as consent can discover that implementation still lacks authorization.

If an authority lacks capacity, the next useful task can be logistical rather than persuasive: obtain an alternate implementation route, reschedule work or wait for the declared semantic horizon. The system must not require endless errands when they cannot change the decision.

An emergency action can solve an immediate safety problem while creating a mandatory follow-up review. The later ecological outcome remains unknown until observation or recovery contracts support it.

Full version

A mechanically rich variant could involve players escorting monitors through the affected route, protecting a temporary field team, responding to wild Pokémon during an emergency closure or dealing with actors who physically dispute implementation.

Dependencies remain exact:

Therefore the reduced loop is the implementation-safe default. The narrative premise does not require tactical enforcement.

NPC/faction archetypes for later canon review

These are role patterns only, not new canon actors.

Field Steward: carries ecological observations and can propose habitat-facing actions within a narrow mandate.

Route Operator: controls schedules, maintenance or access implementation but does not own ecological truth.

Joint Coordinator: maintains the authority map, meeting record and review horizon without gaining automatic power to override either party.

Emergency Duty Officer: has a narrow temporary mandate triggered by explicit conditions and mandatory post-action review.

Community Observer: can contribute evidence or interests without automatically receiving formal approval/veto power.

Longer arc potential

Repeated cases can reveal that the same ecological population crosses several governance boundaries. The world can gradually accumulate coordination precedents, delegation arrangements and contested mandates. Those should remain explicit records, not a hidden reputation score.

If parties later negotiate standing access windows, cost sharing, restoration commitments or reciprocal duties, route those terms into `design/agreements-mediation-repair-layer.md` rather than expanding Pass 276 into a second negotiation engine.
