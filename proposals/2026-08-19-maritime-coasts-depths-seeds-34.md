# Ouros Maritime, Coasts & Underwater Depths Seeds — Pass 34

Status: NON-CANON proposals.

These concepts are original Ouros candidates derived from high-level maritime, PTU campaign and Pokémon exploration patterns. They do not copy external characters, plots, dialogue or location layouts.

## 1. The Wreck That Became a Reef

A decades-old wreck is now an important habitat. A salvage company has located valuable cargo that may still have a legitimate claimant. Removing parts could damage the habitat, while leaving everything in place could preserve property that someone still owns.

Useful systems: salvage custody, conservation, public memory, cases, material provenance.

## 2. Last Ferry Before the Swell

A public ferry announces an early final departure because sea conditions are expected to deteriorate. Players must decide whether to travel now, remain on the island, help move critical cargo, or investigate why one operator thinks the official forecast is too optimistic.

No tactical weather effect is implied.

## 3. The Quiet Harbor

A normally busy harbor has almost no wild Pokémon activity for three mornings. Fishers blame a new vessel route. Researchers suspect a water-quality change. Harbor workers report nothing unusual.

The absence itself is the hook.

## 4. Chart From Another Decade

A famous old navigation chart points to a safe channel that no longer exists after years of sediment movement and construction. The chart was correct when made. A recent accident turns the discrepancy into an investigation rather than a forgery case.

## 5. The Missing Buoy

One navigation marker disappears. It may have broken loose, been removed, been carried away, or been taken. Its absence reroutes traffic through a sensitive habitat.

## 6. A Cargo Crate Is Not Loot

Players find a sealed crate washed ashore after a storm. The contents could matter to a clinic, workshop, research station or private owner. The actual quest is identifying provenance and deciding custody before anyone opens or sells it.

## 7. Harbor Pokémon That Know the Schedule

A group of wild Pokémon regularly appears minutes before a specific ferry arrives because of long-term feeding opportunities around the dock. When the ferry timetable changes, their behavior changes too.

The system records learned pattern, not affection toward the operator.

## 8. Low-Tide Door

A stone entrance becomes visible only during a recurring low-water window. Locals know it exists but disagree about what it was used for. The real challenge is scheduling, documentation and deciding whether entry is responsible.

## 9. The False Salvage Boom

A blurry image leads to rumors that a historically important wreck has been discovered. Tourism, amateur diving and commercial interest arrive before researchers can verify the site.

The rumor can create real world consequences even if the identification is wrong.

## 10. Reef Passage Reopened

A restoration project makes a previously damaged passage usable again. Ferry operators, marine Pokémon, researchers and tourists all respond differently. Success creates new pressure rather than ending the story.

## 11. The Boat That Returned Empty

A small work boat drifts back to harbor without its operator. The story keeps PERSON_STATUS and POKEMON_STATUS unknown until evidence resolves them. Search-area claims can disagree without creating supernatural certainty.

## 12. Submerged Utility Tunnel

An old underwater service tunnel connects two parts of a coastal settlement. It is useful infrastructure, a habitat edge and a potential emergency route. A maintenance discovery reveals that different institutions have been using outdated maps of the same tunnel.

## 13. Two Harbors, One Lane

Two settlements share the only safe lane through a difficult coastal area. One wants heavier cargo traffic; the other depends on quieter water for tourism and local ecology. Neither side needs to be villainous.

## 14. Floating Clinic Day

A mobile clinic visits remote islands on a schedule. A delay in one harbor produces a backlog elsewhere. Players can assist with logistics, communications, supplies or travel without needing a medical-combat quest.

## 15. Diving Team With Unequal Access

A multiplayer expedition includes characters with different valid ways to reach a submerged site. Some can travel with a public service, some have their own capable partners, and some cannot legally/physically enter the same area yet.

The system must not equalize access by inventing capabilities.

## 16. The Harbor Under the Festival

A large coastal festival fills every berth and temporarily changes passenger flows, vendor demand, patrol schedules and wildlife presence. The harbor remains infrastructure first and event venue second.

## 17. Underwater Neighborhood Without Visitors

A submerged settlement is functional for residents but not yet prepared for outside visitors. Opening access becomes a civic/infrastructure arc involving rescue plans, communication, supplies and public-space design.

## 18. Storm Debris Corridor

Floating debris after a distant storm begins arriving along one coastline over several days. Some objects are harmless, some are lost property, and some may affect habitats or infrastructure. Do not turn every recovered object into treasure.

## 19. The Wrong Wreck

Researchers think a newly found wreck is tied to a famous historical disappearance. Early evidence is exciting but incomplete. Later analysis shows it is a different vessel whose own story has been overlooked because everyone wanted the famous answer.

## 20. Signal From Below

A coastal relay receives intermittent signals from an old submerged infrastructure node. Engineers suspect equipment failure. Researchers wonder whether environmental interference is involved. The signal alone does not prove an inhabited site or trapped person.

## 21. Public Mantine Route Equivalent

A community-operated Pokémon-assisted sea crossing is both transport and cultural activity. Its continued operation depends on individual Pokémon willingness/availability, staff, weather information and route conditions.

No species-wide Mountable rule is implied.

## 22. Fishing Ground Shift

A traditional fishing ground becomes less productive while a nearby site becomes unusually active. Competing explanations include migration, water temperature, vessel traffic, habitat restoration and measurement error.

## 23. Salvage Apprentice

An apprentice correctly identifies an object that experienced workers dismiss as junk. The object is valuable because of provenance and history, not because it has better mechanical stats.

## 24. The Harbor That Outgrew Its Rescue Plan

A small port becomes a transfer hub over several arcs. Passenger and cargo traffic increase, but emergency procedures still assume the old scale. The problem appears during a drill before a real disaster happens.

## 25. Unmarked Deep-Water Habitat

A research vessel repeatedly observes a rare aggregation away from known routes. Publishing exact coordinates could bring attention the habitat cannot tolerate. The science/media/conservation layers decide how information is shared.

## 26. Wreck Memorial Dispute

A wreck has become a memorial site for descendants and locals. A new investigation needs access to parts of it. The conflict concerns dignity, evidence and stewardship rather than simple ownership.

## 27. Island Supply Cascade

A ferry cancellation delays one workshop material, which delays pump repairs, which changes water availability, which affects a habitat and a settlement service. The narrative graph must preserve each causal link rather than jumping directly from storm to crisis.

## 28. The Sea Lane Nobody Uses Anymore

An old route remains physically viable but has fallen out of use after newer transport opened. Wild Pokémon behavior, informal travel and forgotten infrastructure have changed around it. Reopening it can create opportunities and conflicts.

# Longer arc A — The Archipelago Learns Its Own Map Again

Several island communities use charts produced by different institutions and decades. Infrastructure changes, sediment shifts and new conservation zones have made all of them partially obsolete.

The arc begins with minor route discrepancies and escalates through survey work, old records, conflicting local knowledge and one serious navigation incident. Players can help create a shared chart system without forcing every institution to surrender its own local terminology or knowledge.

Potential consequences:
- safer travel;
- newly documented habitat zones;
- discovery of neglected underwater structures;
- public disputes over disclosure;
- new routes and closed routes;
- future expeditions using player-generated data.

# Longer arc B — The Wreck Season

A sequence of storms exposes multiple wreck sites along a coastline. Commercial salvagers, historians, families, researchers, conservation groups and local governments all care for different reasons.

No single wreck is the whole plot. The arc tracks how provenance, custody, habitat, public memory and economic pressure interact across several sites.

The climax can be a public/institutional settlement about process rather than a boss battle.

# Longer arc C — City Beneath the Breakwater

Maintenance work near a major breakwater reveals evidence of a previously inaccessible submerged district or infrastructure complex.

The campaign progresses through verification, mapping, access planning, ecology study, structural assessment and debate over whether the site should become research space, public space, restored settlement, protected heritage or remain mostly closed.

If Ouros canon later supports a functioning underwater community, the same framework can support living residents instead of ruins. The system must not assume that outcome in advance.
