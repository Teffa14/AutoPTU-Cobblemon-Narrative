# Astronomy & Celestial Observation Seeds — Pass 63

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character, dialogue or facility is copied.

Date: 2026-08-20

These concepts use the systems contract in `design/astronomy-celestial-observation-layer.md`.

## 1. The Observatory That Lost the Horizon

A decades-old observatory can no longer see its historical eastern horizon because a growing district changed both the skyline and night lighting.

Useful systems: architecture, public works, dark-sky state, archives, civic governance.

The conflict does not require one side to be wrong. Housing, night work, public safety and research access can all be legitimate needs.

## 2. Three Clocks, One Shower

Three instruments record the same event at slightly different times. The disagreement appears astronomical until the team investigates calibration and synchronization history.

Useful systems: digital records, instrument maintenance, science, evidence.

## 3. The False Fall Site

A public bright-flash report triggers a search. The most dramatic recovered object is terrestrial industrial material, while the actual celestial fragment may never be found.

Useful systems: provenance, media, public belief, geology, scientific null results.

## 4. Last Night of the Old Dome

An obsolete telescope is scheduled for retirement shortly before a predictable observing window. Staff disagree about whether preserving one final observation justifies delaying replacement work.

Useful systems: workplace history, finance, archives, adaptive reuse.

## 5. The Fragment in the Drain

A small candidate meteorite is recovered from a stormwater grate after heavy rain. Its original landing position is lost, reducing scientific context even if later analysis proves the object genuine.

Useful systems: sanitation, hydrology, custody, geology, collections.

## 6. Moonless Festival

A town’s annual night festival is culturally fixed to a date rather than a useful observing phase. A new observatory wants lower lighting for several hours without claiming ownership of the tradition.

Useful systems: public events, agreements, visual culture, astronomy, governance.

## 7. The Hill Everyone Photographs

A night-sky viewpoint becomes famous after one striking image. The main problem is trail erosion, parking pressure and crowd concentration rather than damage to the astronomical site itself.

Useful systems: photography, tourism, transport, conservation, public works.

## 8. Silent All-Sky Camera

A camera records nothing during an expected event. The absence is scientifically useful only after operators establish that the instrument was actually working.

Useful systems: null results, instrument state, maintenance, data provenance.

## 9. Solrock at the Solar Farm

Workers report repeated Solrock activity around a solar installation. The pattern may correlate with sunlight, heat, maintenance schedules, habitat edges or something else.

The system records observations before creating a causal claim.

## 10. Lunatone Beneath the Quarry Archive

Old quarry photographs show Lunatone near a layer once described as meteorite-bearing. Modern researchers discover that the original geological labels were inconsistent.

Useful systems: archives, geology, photography, species observation.

## 11. School Night at Sector Four

Students using a wide-field visual survey notice a transient event missed by the professional telescope because the main instrument was pointed elsewhere.

The story rewards method and coverage rather than giving students implausibly superior equipment.

## 12. The Weather Balloon That Became a Star

A bright moving object generates rumors of a celestial anomaly. Meteorology records eventually identify a plausible weather-instrument explanation, but some observations remain inconsistent.

Useful systems: meteorology, media, scientific uncertainty.

## 13. Broken Calibration

An instrument upgrade reveals that several years of measurements share the same systematic offset. Old research is not deleted; it receives a correction history.

Useful systems: science, archives, digital versioning, institutional memory.

## 14. The Visitor Dome

A popular public telescope and a research telescope share the same site. A rare observation window overlaps with a fully booked outreach night.

Useful systems: tourism, education, staffing, scheduling, public access.

## 15. Dark-Sky Retrofit

A settlement tests shielded lighting in one district. The pilot improves sky visibility but creates maintenance, cost and resident-preference questions that need actual review.

Useful systems: public works, infrastructure, finance, accessibility, science.

## 16. The Fragment With Two Labels

A museum specimen and a laboratory record use different recovery locations for what appears to be the same fragment.

The discrepancy can come from clerical history, split specimens, an incorrect assumption or genuine provenance failure.

## 17. The Meteorite Buyer

A media leak creates private demand for fragments before classification is complete. Some sellers possess ordinary rocks, some possess genuine material, and some custody claims are unresolved.

Useful systems: illicit markets, cases, provenance, finance, museums.

## 18. Conjunction Route

A mountain trail receives temporary night access for a predictable celestial event. Route safety, transport, staffing and conservation capacity determine how much access is realistic.

No astronomical event grants a movement bonus.

## 19. The Empty Radiant

A historically reliable shower is unexpectedly weak. The correct immediate result is a documented null/low observation, not a replacement spectacle generated for player satisfaction.

Useful systems: long-term science, public expectations, media, tourism.

## 20. The Dockside Fireball Report

Night-shift workers at a port report a low bright object. Different viewpoints create inconsistent trajectories, and several cameras face the wrong direction.

Useful systems: workplaces, photography, evidence graphs, communications.

## 21. The Public Telescope Queue

A rare visible event produces a long line at a small observatory. An ordinary staffing problem becomes the main operational challenge while the sky event remains scientifically routine.

## 22. Old Star Chart, New Skyline

A historic chart uses now-obscured landmarks for orientation. Archivists, surveyors and astronomers collaborate to reconstruct how the old observing site worked.

Useful systems: cartography, archives, architecture, astronomy.

## 23. Eclipse Blackout Rumor

A power outage begins shortly before an eclipse and public rumor links the two. Infrastructure logs show the technical problem started earlier, but the coincidence becomes part of public memory.

Useful systems: infrastructure, media, public memory, evidence.

## 24. Night Market Light Budget

A seasonal market and nearby observing station negotiate a temporary lighting arrangement. The final plan preserves commercial activity while creating defined low-light windows.

Useful systems: agreements, commerce, event schedules, public works.

## 25. Contaminated Recovery

A fresh fragment is handled by many excited visitors before a research team arrives. It may remain valuable, but the handling history limits some later claims.

Useful systems: provenance, public outreach, science, museums.

## 26. The Roof That Points Somewhere

An archaeological structure appears aligned with a recurring celestial event. Several plausible architectural reasons exist, and the alignment alone cannot establish ritual purpose.

Useful systems: archaeology, myth, architectural history, scientific claims.

## 27. Observatory Turned Relay

A former observing station became a communications relay decades ago. A new astronomy program proposes returning part of the site to research while preserving the relay service.

Useful systems: adaptive reuse, communications, technology, governance.

## 28. The Regional Sky Notebook

Schools, clubs and observatories contribute standardized observations over years. Different coverage quality is preserved instead of averaging all reports into one truth score.

Useful systems: citizen science, education, multiplayer provenance, archives.

## 29. Dawn After the Viewing Party

After a successful public event, the meaningful follow-up is cleanup, transport recovery, resident feedback and checking whether nearby Pokémon activity changed.

The event does not need a boss encounter to have consequences.

## 30. The Observation That Arrived Late

A remote station uploads data days after a communications outage. The late record changes the interpretation of a regional event without rewriting what institutions reasonably believed at the time.

Useful systems: communications delay, temporal provenance, science, public record.

# Longer arc A — Thirty Years of Night

A regional observatory has records spanning decades. City growth, lighting, smoke events, instrument upgrades and changing public interest gradually alter what can be observed from the site.

The arc can support:

- restoration of historical data;
- public-works negotiations;
- relocation of some instruments;
- outreach programs;
- dark-sky pilots;
- comparison of old and new Pokémon observations;
- a final decision about whether the observatory remains, specializes or partly relocates.

There is no required villain and no requirement that the original site remain scientifically dominant forever.

# Longer arc B — Stones That Fell

Several fragments associated with one historical fall are distributed across a museum, university, private collection and an unknown location.

Years of inconsistent labels make it difficult to determine whether all pieces even belong to the same event.

The arc connects provenance, archives, geology, scientific replication, private collectors, public exhibits and possible illicit trading. Its climax can be a corrected history rather than acquisition of every fragment.

# Longer arc C — The Regional Sky Survey

A multiplayer network of schools, clubs, residents and observatories builds a long-running survey of meteor showers, night-sky quality and species observations.

Early seasons produce uneven data. Later years improve methods, reveal coverage gaps and create genuine baselines. Some expected phenomena fail to appear; those absences remain part of the record.

The network can eventually become an institution with its own archive, training program and public events, while individual contributors retain provenance for their observations.
