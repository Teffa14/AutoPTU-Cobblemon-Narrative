# Aging, Senescence, Retirement & Role Transition Seeds — Pass 134

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.
Date: 2026-08-23

These concepts are candidates only. They do not establish ages, lifespans, retirement policies, death rules, mechanical penalties or institutional canon.

## Thirty candidates

### 1. The Route He No Longer Finishes

A long-serving Pokémon partner still begins the same seasonal survey but has stopped taking the final ridge segment. The change could reflect age, an old injury, altered footing, weather, social preference or a new disturbance near the ridge. Investigation begins with the changed route, not an age diagnosis.

### 2. The Champion Who Retired From Only One Thing

A former Champion no longer enters sanctioned title competition but still spars, teaches and occasionally accepts exhibitions. Public profiles repeatedly shorten this to “retired,” causing confusion over what the person actually stopped doing.

### 3. The Twenty-Seventh Season

A club has complete records for one Pokémon's twenty-seven competitive seasons. The useful story is not a lifespan claim but a longitudinal archive of changing partners, team roles, venues and routines.

### 4. The Oldest Worker Is the Newest Member

An older technician joins a utility crew after decades in another profession. Coworkers wrongly assume long age means long tenure in this specific workplace.

### 5. The Retirement Photograph

A famous photograph is captioned “final battle,” but later records show several exhibitions afterward. The photograph remains authentic; the caption reflects what people believed at the time.

### 6. The Veteran Who Refuses the Mentor Role

A celebrated battler leaves competition and declines every request to coach. The world must allow retirement without converting the person into a quest-giver or mentor archetype.

### 7. The Mentor Who Never Competed

An older researcher becomes an excellent mentor despite never having had a famous battle career. This prevents “veteran” from meaning “former champion.”

### 8. The Torkoal With Four Name Tags

An old Torkoal carries accessories linked to several former Trainers. The tags provide provenance candidates, not proof of ownership, chronology or the reasons each partnership ended.

### 9. The Former Partner Still at the Door

A competitively retired Pokémon still waits near the training hall on match days. That behavior does not mean it wants to return, feels abandoned or expects to battle.

### 10. The Return for One Night

A retired performer agrees to one anniversary exhibition. Afterward the retirement status remains in force. The event becomes a historical exception rather than an automatic comeback arc.

### 11. The Role That Became Two Roles

A long-serving ranger reduces field patrols but begins reviewing maps and briefing younger teams. The old job has not simply been replaced; responsibilities have been divided.

### 12. The Successor Arrives Too Early

An institution begins training a successor while the current role-holder remains fully active. Rumors interpret the plan as proof of illness or forced retirement. Neither claim is established.

### 13. The Successor Arrives Too Late

A role-holder announces a planned departure, but the institution has preserved almost none of the operational knowledge needed by the next person. The challenge is documentation and handoff, not combat.

### 14. The Elderly Pokémon With the Largest Range

An old individual continues using a wider range than younger members of the same population. It becomes a standing counterexample against procedural age stereotypes.

### 15. The Smaller Range With Three Explanations

A persistent wild Pokémon gradually uses fewer sites. An old foot injury, changing food distribution and age are all plausible. The dataset is not sufficient to choose one.

### 16. The Route Memory After the Bridge Falls

An experienced Pokémon repeatedly approaches an old crossing after the bridge is gone, then uses a newer detour. Researchers disagree whether this shows memory, habit, navigation strategy or simple route geometry.

### 17. The Last Season Was Ordinary

A veteran completes a final work season with no crisis, farewell battle or revelation. The handoff happens because a scheduled choice was reached. Chronicle records ordinary closure.

### 18. The Retirement That Prevented a Quest

A staff member notices that continuing a role is no longer desirable and leaves before any failure occurs. Early planning prevents the emergency the generator might otherwise have manufactured.

### 19. The Competitive Health Misunderstanding

A public dashboard exposes the phrase “career health” from a sports system. Fans begin treating it as a medical diagnosis until staff clarify that it is a competitive workload metric.

### 20. The Active Elder

An elderly Gym official continues working at full scope while a much younger colleague takes a leave. Scheduling follows actual participation state rather than age.

### 21. The Younger Retiree

A relatively young Trainer retires from League competition after achieving a personal goal. There is no injury, scandal or failure.

### 22. The Return That Fails Eligibility

A former competitor wants to enter an event again, but a credential, roster or registration rule has changed since retirement. The barrier is current eligibility, not age.

### 23. The Old Partner and the New Equipment

A long-serving institutional Pokémon still performs its role, but equipment designed years ago no longer fits current practice. Updating the system is an Accessibility/Technology issue rather than evidence that the Pokémon must retire.

### 24. The Archive Knows More Than the Veteran

A respected retired worker remembers an incident differently from timestamped records. Both the testimony and archive remain valuable; seniority does not select truth automatically.

### 25. The Veteran Knows What the Archive Lost

A flood destroyed decades of logs, and a former worker remembers a discontinued valve, trail or storage area. The memory becomes a hypothesis to verify, not an automatic map update.

### 26. The Oldest Member Is Not the Leader

A wild collective contains a very old individual that does not lead movements or resource access. Age alone never assigns hierarchy.

### 27. The Seasonal Return From Retirement

A transport Pokémon no longer works year-round but voluntarily joins a short local service during one festival season. Participation is limited by role and schedule, not a full restoration of the previous career.

### 28. The Trainer Outlived the Institution

A former official remains alive decades after the organization that employed them closed. Interviews, objects and records preserve one perspective on the institution without granting ongoing authority.

### 29. The Pokémon Outlived Three Institutions

The same persistent Pokémon appears in records from a workshop, a research station and a sanctuary across different decades. The institutions changed; the Pokémon identity did not.

### 30. The Age Atlas With Blank Pages

A long-term research project records known or estimated ages for individually recognized Pokémon. Large parts remain unknown because absence of a birth record is a legitimate data state. The atlas improves over years without retroactively inventing dates.

## Long arc 1 — Five Roles of the Same Trainer

Year 1: a prominent Trainer still competes regularly and occasionally teaches.

Year 2: travel becomes less appealing, so the Trainer reduces circuit participation while retaining local exhibitions.

Year 3: the Trainer takes an advisory role at a Gym. Public media repeatedly calls this full retirement, although selected battles continue.

Year 4: a successor takes the operational role. The former Trainer remains available for historical questions but explicitly rejects management authority.

Year 5+: the institution discovers that some procedures existed only in the veteran's memory. A documentation project begins. Some recollections are confirmed, others corrected.

Possible outcomes:

- permanent advisory role;
- complete withdrawal;
- occasional exhibitions;
- return to a different circuit;
- no further public role.

The arc never requires illness or decline.

## Long arc 2 — The Partner Across Thirty Seasons

A persistent Pokémon joins a Trainer or institution early in Chronicle.

Over many seasons:

- its competitive workload is recorded separately from chronological age;
- it evolves without resetting identity;
- equipment and routines change;
- it accumulates former teammates and public records;
- Career mode may eventually mark it retired from competitive play;
- partnership remains intact unless separately changed;
- it may shift to demonstrations, local travel, sanctuary life, research participation or no formal role;
- later generations encounter the same individual with a long provenance trail.

The arc is compatible with an unknown biological lifespan. No exact age or death is required.

## Long arc 3 — The Knowledge Handoff Years

A regional route depends heavily on a few long-serving guides, Pokémon and technicians.

Season 1: normal operation.

Season 2: one veteran reduces scope. Documentation reveals gaps.

Season 3: a newer team shadows the route and discovers that official maps omit seasonal details known informally by experienced staff.

Season 4: infrastructure changes make some old knowledge obsolete while other historical observations become more valuable.

Season 5: responsibility is distributed across updated maps, archives, training and several actors instead of one irreplaceable veteran.

Success means the system becomes less dependent on a single person's memory. No actor needs to die for the legacy to matter.

A former competitor voluntarily accepts a single exhibition years after retiring from regular competition.

The battle itself can use ordinary verified/partial combat families. No age system is required. If the participant is mechanically legal, their authoritative battle state is used without age modifiers.

## Encounter contract — Handoff at North Watch

Narrative premise:

A veteran route watcher is completing a final scheduled season while another actor shadows the work. A disturbance interrupts the handoff.

FULL version primary dependencies:

- complete movement — BLOCKING for moving protection/withdrawal objectives;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if the disturbance changes tactical field state;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for PROTECT, WITHDRAW, CLEAR_ROUTE or REACH_OBJECTIVE;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback — BLOCKING;
- normal combat families retain their current VERIFIED/PARTIAL states.

REDUCED version:

Resolve both actors' route positions, handoff tasks and evacuation outside battle. Freeze a legal arena if confrontation remains. The battle result cannot appoint the successor, cancel retirement or prove age-related decline.

## Engine and canon guardrails

Do not create:

- age-based stat penalties;
- veteran stat bonuses;
- retirement as a Status;
- retirement as Fainted/death;
- `career_health` as biological HP;
- mandatory mentorship;
- forced succession at an age threshold;
- species lifespan tables without authored sources;
- age-based capture bonuses;
- movement loss based only on age;
- automatic healing penalties;
- cognitive decline rules;
- automatic elder leadership;
- automatic elder social withdrawal;
- retirement-based ownership transfer;
- Minecraft skins/animations that write age mechanics.
