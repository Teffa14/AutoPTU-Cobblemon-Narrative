# The Route That Turned Without Moving

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON

Pass: 318

## Premise

A surveyed route through a rocky service corridor begins producing contradictory navigation reports. Mechanical compasses deflect near one junction. A known landmark remains exactly where earlier surveys placed it. Route markers and physical distances still agree with the old map. Travelers nevertheless report that "north changes" near the same sector, and one field crew has begun avoiding the route.

The problem is not initially framed as sabotage, a supernatural event, or a Pokémon attack. The playable question is narrower: which observations are reliable, what changed, and what can each witness legitimately conclude from the evidence available to them?

## Core exploration loop

The player can compare several observation stations rather than follow a single objective marker.

A control station sits beside a surveyed landmark where prior measurements are considered reliable. A deflection station lies near the reported anomaly. A cross-check station has clear visual geometry but poor instrument agreement. A service spur approaches machinery or buried infrastructure that may be relevant without automatically being the cause.

Each station can yield separate evidence:
- visible landmark bearing;
- route-marker identity;
- mechanical compass reading;
- second-instrument comparison;
- maintenance or power state;
- recent work traces;
- species-specific Pokémon observation if a suitable Pokémon is present and canonically justified;
- testimony from NPCs who visited at different times.

The player should be able to discover that an observation is accurate while an interpretation built from it is wrong.

## Example mystery states

These are alternate authored explanations, not simultaneous canon facts.

1. A local mineral body deflects instruments, while the route itself never changed.
2. Service equipment produces a localized field only when operating.
3. An instrument used by the first survey crew became magnetized or damaged.
4. A Pokémon with established magnetic properties occupies or passes through the sector intermittently.
5. An old survey assumption was wrong even though current instruments are functioning normally.
6. Two causes overlap, causing different witnesses to report apparently incompatible results.

The final authored version can select one or combine compatible causes after regional geology, infrastructure, species ecology, and PTU/Caelo mechanics are approved.

## NPC and faction pressure

The conflict works without a villain.

A route custodian wants a defensible answer before changing signs or closing access. A maintenance crew wants to avoid being blamed for a phenomenon they may not have caused. A field researcher wants repeated measurements before drawing ecological conclusions. Travelers care about immediate safety and may spread a simpler story than the evidence supports. A local operator may have accurate knowledge of machinery schedules but no knowledge of Pokémon movement or geology.

Each actor receives only observations they personally made or were actually sent. A later correct explanation does not retroactively give earlier witnesses better information.

## Environmental storytelling

The corridor should remain navigable by physical evidence even while magnetic cues become unreliable.

Useful anchors include a distinctive ridge profile, watercourse, survey cairn, numbered service post, old retaining wall, or visible tower. Scratches around a maintenance hatch can show recent work without proving the work caused the anomaly. Replaced cable conduit can establish infrastructure change without proving harmful intent. An old map and current route markers can agree even when compass readings do not.

The place itself should let the player test hypotheses rather than merely collect dialogue.

## Controlled test

If infrastructure is a candidate source and the scenario permits a safe shutdown, the player can repeat the same observation before and after the state change.

A changed reading strengthens the association between the equipment state and the local field. It does not automatically prove who knew about the effect, whether it is dangerous, whether a Pokémon population was harmed, or whether every earlier navigation report had the same cause.

If a Pokémon such as Nosepass is eventually approved for the scene, its response can be another observation channel. Official franchise material supports Nosepass as a compass-related species. The scenario does not assume a PTU travel bonus, automatic success, exact magnetic sensitivity, or universal interpretation of its behavior.

## Reduced implementation version

The reduced version preserves the entire narrative premise without dynamic magnetic simulation.

The route graph remains authoritative and static unless changed by normal world-state logic. Between scenes, authored observation nodes can expose states such as:
- FIELD_BASELINE;
- FIELD_DEFLECTED;
- INSTRUMENT_DISAGREEMENT;
- LANDMARK_ROUTE_CONFIRMED;
- SOURCE_UNRESOLVED;
- CONTROLLED_TEST_CHANGED;
- CONTROLLED_TEST_NO_CHANGE.

These labels are evidence/world descriptors, not PTU statuses.

No magnetic radius is calculated. No compass item modifies movement. No actor is pulled. No electrical equipment fails through combat mechanics. No targeting changes. No Pokémon ability is invoked merely because the fiction mentions magnetism. The player moves through ordinary verified route geometry and gathers authored evidence with provenance.

## Longer-term arc potential

The first visit can resolve only whether the route is physically where the map says it is. A later infrastructure project can make the field relevant again. A seasonal Pokémon movement can generate a second dataset. A repaired or replaced machine can test the earlier hypothesis. A disagreement between archived and current survey records can become institutional rather than tactical.

This allows the same corridor to gain meaning across time without resetting after the quest.
