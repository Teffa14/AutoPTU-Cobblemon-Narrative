# Rivalry & Recurring Peer Seeds — Pass 59

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Nothing here establishes Ouros geography, institutions, characters, rules or mechanics.

## Design target

These candidates operationalize the recurring-peer model from `design/rivalry-recurring-peer-progression-extension.md` without inventing Caelo Rivalry mechanics, hidden scaling or guaranteed emotional outcomes.

## The Same Trail, Different Reasons

Two recurring peers arrive at the same route segment for unrelated but legitimate reasons.

One may be following an institution assignment, survey lead, competition schedule or public notice. The other may be traveling toward a different goal. Their overlap creates a choice: compare findings, travel together briefly, challenge each other, exchange information or continue separately.

Persistence outputs:
- shared-location event;
- updated legal knowledge packet;
- optional challenge invitation;
- route observation;
- no automatic friendship or hostility change.

The important rule is causal presence. The peer appears because agenda and route align, not because the player crossed a scripted checkpoint.

## Three Meetings, One Rivalry

A long-form arc uses three different contexts:

First meeting: direct competition establishes a meaningful comparison.

Second meeting: a local incident forces temporary cooperation or parallel work.

Third meeting: both enter a reviewed formal challenge or other competition after visible changes in their goals and capabilities.

The arc should preserve all three facts. Cooperation does not erase competition. A later battle does not erase prior assistance.

## The Rival Who Stops Battling

A recurring peer decides that formal battling is no longer their main goal.

Possible reasons must come from authored state or prior events: a career opportunity, changed interest, institutional role, research project, care responsibility, travel commitment or explicit personal decision.

The rivalry can transform into another domain such as field research, rescue performance, exploration, public problem solving or professional selection.

This candidate exists to prevent recurring characters from remaining battle dispensers forever.

No mechanical retirement, class retraining or progression change is inferred by narrative state.

## Public Record, Private Disagreement

Media or public spectators begin describing two Trainers as a famous rivalry after several visible encounters.

One or both participants dislike the framing, think it exaggerates hostility or believe it ignores cooperation between them.

The story can separate:
- public narrative;
- formal records;
- each actor’s authored statements;
- private relationship state;
- what unrelated NPCs believe.

No public nickname or media interpretation becomes canonical truth about internal motives.

## The Match That Does Not Gate Anything

A recurring peer asks for a battle during an ordinary period between larger goals.

The result is authoritative and remembered, but regional progression continues either way.

Possible consequences:
- rematch state changes;
- public record changes if witnessed;
- a new training question emerges;
- one peer reaches a later optional opportunity first;
- dialogue references the actual result;
- no consequence beyond historical record.

This is the default model for rivalry battles unless an approved challenge contract explicitly gates something.

## Same Mentor, Different Lesson

Two competitive peers independently work with the same mentor, instructor or specialist.

The mentor gives each a task appropriate to their current goal. They do not receive identical benefits or hidden buffs.

The peers later compare what they learned, disagree about the lesson or apply it differently.

Narrative state may record:
- shared mentor;
- separate tasks;
- demonstrated outcomes;
- observed changes.

Any actual progression remains governed by PTU/Caelo and implementation support.

## Peer Network Weekend

Three or four recurring peers converge at one public event, institution session, expedition staging window or tournament.

The player is one participant in a network rather than the center of every relationship.

Possible scenes:
- two peers compete without the player;
- one asks another for help;
- a shared mentor divides attention;
- one misses an event because another obligation wins;
- two people disagree about the player’s public reputation;
- pairings change between activities.

Off-screen PTU battle results must come from an approved authority source. The narrative generator may not fabricate exact dice outcomes or battle transcripts merely to populate the event.

## A Challenge Left Open

A peer issues an invitation that remains unresolved for weeks or months.

The challenge stays as state rather than spawning a forced encounter.

It may resolve when:
- both actors share a location;
- schedules align;
- an institution offers a legal venue;
- one participant withdraws the invitation;
- the format becomes irrelevant;
- another event transforms the relationship.

This gives absence and timing narrative meaning without treating delay as rejection.

## The Wrong Assumption About the Rematch

A player expects a familiar rematch based on the last battle.

The peer arrives with an authoritative current team or different approved format because their own life changed for reasons already recorded.

The surprise comes from continuity, not hidden counter-picking.

The scene should expose at least one traceable reason for the change before or after battle.

## Rivalry Changes Shape — recurring arc

This longer arc can span many sessions.

Phase A: Recognition. Two actors notice repeated overlap.

Phase B: Comparison. A competition, disagreement or shared evaluation creates a persistent competitive dimension.

Phase C: Separate Work. Each pursues goals elsewhere. Their worlds continue moving independently.

Phase D: Recontact. A plausible schedule, event or shared actor brings them together.

Phase E: Adaptation. One or both respond to things they actually observed or learned publicly.

Phase F: Divergence. Their careers, methods or priorities stop aligning.

Phase G: Convergence. A later crisis, institution, expedition or championship brings them back into contact.

Phase H: Transformation. Rivalry remains, softens, intensifies, changes domain or becomes dormant according to authored and observed state.

No phase requires friendship, romance, hostility, reconciliation or permanent opposition.

## Encounter candidate — Crossroads Rematch

Narrative premise:
Two recurring peers meet again during a legitimate route overlap. Both have changed since the previous encounter.

Intended full version:
- current authoritative legal teams;
- route terrain or weather enters combat only if those families are implemented;
- rival tactical priorities can use legally known information;
- public witnesses can affect record visibility without modifying battle rules;
- exact reveals from the battle feed later callbacks.

Capability dependencies:
- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when selected content needs it

- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED

- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL

- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL

Use a static legal arena and a deliberately restricted set of individually verified mechanics. Do not simulate tactical adaptation, route weather, dynamic terrain or unsupported features. The normal authoritative battle result still updates rivalry history and revealed-information records.

## Encounter candidate — Rival Team-Up Under Pressure

Narrative premise:
Two competitors temporarily fight on the same side because an immediate local problem gives them aligned goals.

Intended full version:
- shared tactical side;
- protect, escape or containment objective;
- interception and forced movement can matter;
- hazards or changing zones can alter priorities;
- allied AI coordinates around an objective while respecting actor knowledge limits;
- post-scene state records cooperation without assigning friendship.

## Noncombat candidate — Competing Field Claim

Two recurring peers independently investigate the same phenomenon.

Their disagreement may concern:
- interpretation;
- observation quality;
- chronology;
- attribution;
- whether evidence is strong enough to publish;
- which institution should review the finding.

The scene can operate entirely on current narrative evidence, timestamps, provenance and actor knowledge.

It must not invent:
- academic ownership law;
- publication priority rules;
- misconduct definitions;
- institutional authority;
- research rewards.

If the dispute becomes formal, hand it to the governing case, agreement or civic system.
