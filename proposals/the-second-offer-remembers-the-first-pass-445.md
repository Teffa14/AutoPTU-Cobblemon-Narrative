Premise

A specialist accepted a local inspection window. Access later failed. The specialist asked to reopen terms. The requester explicitly agreed and that answer reached the specialist. The specialist can now propose a different concrete window while the first promise remains part of the history.

Narrative value

The second offer can acknowledge changed reality without pretending the original agreement never existed. This supports recurring NPC relationships, accountability and believable schedule pressure. It also lets the site continue changing while people negotiate.

Reduced implementation version

The responder proposes a new complete window and, if useful, revised location, scope or alternative. The record preserves lineage to the old commitment and delivered reopening consent. Nothing else happens automatically. The requester still has to receive and decide on the offer.

This version needs private knowledge, semantic time, the existing information network and durable assistance ledgers. It does not require AutoPTU.

Example world evolution while the offer is pending

The closed approach may reopen, remain restricted, gain a temporary bypass, accumulate new environmental evidence or cease to matter because the underlying problem changes. Those are independent world facts. The proposal does not freeze them.

Mechanically rich version

If accepted terms later lead to an unsafe survey, escort, protection task, retrieval, containment or extraction, construct that scene from current world evidence. The narrative premise remains “fulfil revised assistance under changed conditions,” not “defeat every opponent.”

Design guardrails

The offer cannot rewrite the first promise. Delivered consent to negotiate cannot count as acceptance of the new terms. A replacement proposal cannot reserve travel or access. World conditions continue to evolve while messages travel. Hazardous mechanics remain owned by AutoPTU and adapter contracts rather than narrative code.

Open questions

The next owner should transport the replacement offer to the requester through the normal information queue. A later decision owner should distinguish acceptance, rejection and expiry. Only explicit acceptance should be eligible to create a replacement commitment. The old commitment should remain queryable for accountability even after a later obligation supersedes its future execution.
