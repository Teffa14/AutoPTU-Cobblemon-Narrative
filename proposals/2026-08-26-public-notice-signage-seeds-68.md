# Public Notice & Signage Seeds — Pass 68

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes Ouros geography, institutions, law, technology or canon. These are implementation-facing story candidates built from Pass 68 research and the existing Ouros systems.

## Design intent

Physical notices should make prior world-state work visible. They should not become universal quest dispensers or magical truth surfaces.

Each seed assumes that the underlying request, closure, service, route, map, event, forecast or maintenance record already belongs to another system. Pass 68 only controls what is displayed, where, when and with which provenance.

## The Notice That Stayed Up

A temporary closure ends, but one old placard remains on a secondary approach.

The main entrance shows the current reopening notice. Regular users who approach from the secondary side continue to avoid the area because they have not seen the correction.

Useful consequences:

- different NPC routines emerge from different information exposure;
- an apparent access conflict can be solved by reconstructing which notice each actor saw;
- Public Memory can preserve the stale notice after it is removed;
- no deception is required.

Owning systems: Public Space or Travel for access truth; Pass 68 for display state.

## Two Timetables, One Departure

Two timetable panels appear to disagree about the same service.

The discrepancy may come from different effective dates, a limited service pattern, a delayed update or one panel showing an older schedule version.

The player can compare:

- panel revision identifiers;
- posted-at times;
- service records;
- staff statements;
- actual departure observations.

The scene can become a small investigation without implying sabotage or incompetence.

Owning system: Travel/Transit owns actual service state.

## The Board Outside the Wrong Door

A public request remains correctly posted, but the office named in the notice has temporarily moved because of maintenance.

The player reaches the listed door and finds a second notice directing visitors elsewhere. A regular NPC still goes to the old location from habit.

This creates a useful chain across:

- request visibility;
- facility maintenance;
- temporary relocation;
- actor knowledge;
- physical signage.

The request itself never changes ownership.

## Closed Gate, Missing Placard

An entrance is legitimately closed, but the physical warning has disappeared.

Possible explanations include weather damage, maintenance removal, accidental displacement, an incomplete replacement cycle or deliberate removal only when evidence supports it.

The gate state remains closed even if the sign is gone.

This seed is valuable because Minecraft players often treat visible props as authority. The system should enforce the reverse: the prop reflects a restriction record.

## The Requester Withdraws the Request

A community request was posted publicly. The requester later withdraws it, but the board is not updated immediately.

A player who first sees the stale notice can still contact the requester. The requester can explain that the need has changed, been solved elsewhere or become private.

The system records:

- original request;
- withdrawal event;
- notice still visible;
- later removal or correction.

No quest should remain mechanically completable only because the old paper still exists.

## The Map Everyone Learned Before the Repair

A familiar public map shows a route configuration that was correct for years. A major repair or reroute makes one portion stale.

Some residents still navigate according to the old map because they memorized it long ago. New visitors follow the updated board.

This can generate:

- different wayfinding dialogue;
- different routine paths;
- historical map callbacks;
- a survey/correction task;
- a reason to preserve the old map in an archive.

Cartography remains the owner of map truth and editions.

## Reopening Notice, Work Order Still Open

A facility or public area reopens partially. A notice accurately states that public access has resumed, while a maintenance work order remains active for unfinished secondary work.

A player may incorrectly assume that “open” means “all work complete.”

The story tests a recurring Ouros rule: operational availability and physical completion can differ.

Facility Maintenance owns the work order. The relevant service/public-space system owns access. Pass 68 displays both when appropriate.

## Community Poster, Official Silence

A local club, research group or resident posts an unofficial notice about an observed issue before any institution publishes a statement.

The poster can be:

- accurate but unofficial;
- partly correct;
- cautious and evidence-based;
- outdated by later investigation.

Its visibility does not create authority. Its provenance can still make it useful evidence of what the community knew at the time.

This seed should never infer that an institution was negligent merely because it had not yet published a response.

## The Sign Nobody Updated After Market Day

A temporary market or festival changes pedestrian routing and uses temporary signs. Teardown removes most event infrastructure, but one directional sign remains.

The physical object becomes a visible trace of the prior event. Regulars ignore it. A visitor follows it.

Temporary Event Operations owns teardown completeness. Public Space owns ordinary-return state. Pass 68 records the leftover display.

## One Neighborhood Gets the Correction Late

A route, weather, service or event correction reaches several boards at different times.

The same town therefore contains different public knowledge for a short period.

The point is not a communications failure by default. Physical update cycles can simply be asynchronous.

Later dialogue can remain coherent because actor knowledge references the specific notice version each person observed.

## The Handwritten Addendum

An official printed notice receives a handwritten addendum from an identifiable operator or steward.

The addendum may be valid if the actor has authority, merely informative if they do not, or disputed until verified.

The system must store the addendum as a separate claim/provenance edge rather than silently altering the original notice record.

This supports believable local operations without assuming a universal signage standard.

## The Notice Nobody Can Read Completely

Weathering, obstruction or physical damage leaves only part of a warning readable.

The player can identify fragments such as a date, destination, issuer mark or route name, but not the complete instruction.

This can generate a lead toward:

- another copy of the notice;
- the issuer;
- archived photographs;
- a current service desk;
- a map or route record.

Partial readability must create partial actor knowledge, not auto-complete hidden text in the quest log.

## Noncombat mystery — Three Boards, Two Updates

Three public boards show versions of the same closure notice.

Observed facts:

- Board A has an early closure.
- Board B has a later extension.
- Board C appears to show reopening.

The player reconstructs the sequence from posting times, effective windows, issuer refs, physical condition and source-state history.

Possible resolutions:

- the reopening was issued, then superseded by a renewed closure;
- Board C was updated first while another route remained closed;
- one board carries an unofficial copied summary;
- one notice refers to a different entrance;
- the boards were updated on different cycles.

There does not need to be a liar, criminal or hidden villain.

The output should be a provenance-aware timeline and updated display state, not a hidden truth score.

## Persistence arc — A Board Learns the Town

The same local board appears across several visits.

First appearance:

- routine activity notice;
- service timetable;
- one public request.

Second appearance:

- the request is accepted or resolved;
- a route interruption adds a temporary notice.

Third appearance:

- the route notice is replaced by a correction;
- an older flyer is weathered but still recognizable;
- an NPC mentions acting on the earlier version.

Fourth appearance:

- the board receives an event notice;
- a maintenance closure changes where people approach it.

Later appearance:

- old notices with historical value have moved to Archives/Public Memory;
- ordinary clutter is compressed;
- current notices remain traceable to live world state.

The board becomes a low-cost persistent memory surface without materializing every anonymous resident.

## NPC archetype — The Board Steward

A recurring actor or role maintains one or more physical information surfaces.

Potential functions:

- replaces superseded notices;
- reports damage;
- knows normal update cadence;
- can explain which institution supplied a notice;
- notices unusual unofficial postings.

This archetype does not automatically possess censorship, police or municipal authority. Their mandate must be established separately.

## NPC archetype — The Regular Reader

A recurring local checks the same board at a predictable time.

They can become a useful witness to:

- when a notice appeared;
- whether a correction was present yesterday;
- whether the surface was damaged;
- what information ordinary users likely saw.

Repeated use does not make them an expert on the underlying service or policy.

## Encounter — Trailhead Update Under Pressure

Narrative premise:

An existing trail restriction changes. A stewarding team arrives to replace the visible guidance, but a battle-capable local threat makes the immediate trailhead unsafe.

Full intended version:

- workers/noncombatants withdraw through valid routes;
- a protected work position or CLEAR_ROUTE/PROTECT objective can matter;
- access lanes may change during the encounter;
- interception or forced displacement may occur;
- terrain/weather/hazard state may matter only when mapped from governing rules;
- wild or hostile AI may prefer territorial pressure, escape or denial instead of pure KO;
- adapter playback must preserve exact trail state and exact notice revision.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED;
- base movement legality: VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: BLOCKING when used;
- core calculations: VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING when active;
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL;
- abilities: PARTIAL;
- items: PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING.

Reduced executable shape:

Evacuate workers in world state. Keep the route restriction authoritative outside the battle. Run a static reviewed encounter near the trailhead using only supported mechanics. After AutoPTU returns its result, the owning world system decides access state and Pass 68 materializes the new notice.

Battle victory cannot reopen the trail by itself.

## Encounter — Station Notice Replacement

Narrative premise:

A timetable or service-status panel is stale during a transport interruption. Replacing it requires access to an area currently unsafe because of a battle-capable threat.

Full intended version:

- civilian withdrawal;
- access/protect objective state;
- dynamic barriers or route options;
- interception or forced movement;
- non-KO tactical priorities;
- semantic battle events for objective changes;
- synchronized adapter writeback for service state and displayed notice.

Permanent capability dependencies are the same as Trailhead Update Under Pressure when those mechanics are active.

Reduced executable shape:

Clear civilians. Freeze the service in the state owned by Travel/Transit. Resolve a static legal battle away from operational controls. Then perform the information-panel update as a separate world interaction.

A battle result cannot prove that the prior timetable was fraudulent, repair transport infrastructure or restore service.

## Optional implementation hook — Read Before You Leave

Before a player begins a known route, the adapter can surface nearby relevant notices generated from actual world state:

- active closure;
- schedule exception;
- event overlay;
- forecast warning;
- maintenance detour.

This should be informational only. It must not require the player to interact with every board before travel, and it must not expose facts the current surface does not contain.

## Canon questions

No answer is assumed for:

- which Ouros settlements maintain public boards;
- who is allowed to post official material;
- whether handwritten addenda are common or recognized;
- which symbols or languages appear on notices;
- whether any region uses dynamic electronic panels;
- how privacy works for public requests;
- who removes expired notices;
- what gets archived;
- how accessibility information is represented;
- how much physical text can be rendered legibly in Minecraft.

All names above are functional candidate labels, not canon place or institution names.
