# Ouros Case & Authority Seeds — Pass 06

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. Institutions, locations, laws, species and exact mechanics remain placeholders until canon and PTU/AutoPTU review.

## Seed BI — The Case That Belongs to Nobody

A damaged freight wagon is found on a route between two settlements. Cargo is missing, but the wagon itself crossed the administrative boundary before anyone discovered it.

One settlement argues the incident began outside its responsibility. The other insists the current scene is now somebody else's problem. A transport organization wants the road reopened quickly, while local residents care more about a missing courier.

Players can begin through several roles:
- witness the scene first;
- be hired by the transport group;
- search for the missing courier;
- document evidence for either settlement;
- ignore the institutional dispute and follow a physical trail.

The eventual cause could be accident, theft, wild-Pokémon interference, deliberate rerouting or several unrelated events. The truth is authored independently from the jurisdiction dispute.

Design purpose: teach that responsibility and truth are separate world states.

## Seed BJ — Evidence on Four Legs

A route incident has almost no reliable human witnesses. A wild Pokémon repeatedly returns to one stretch of road and reacts strongly to a particular scent, sound or object.

The Pokémon is not a talking clue dispenser.

Players must determine what can legally be learned through actual PTU/Caelo capabilities or careful observation. A capable partner may reveal a trail or location. Another player may reconstruct the same route through interviews, terrain evidence or records.

The resulting clue says where something went or what the Pokémon noticed; it does not identify the culprit automatically.

## Seed BK — The Missing Custody Tag

An object recovered during an earlier expedition disappears from an institution's secure storage before specialists can examine it.

The first question is not “who stole it?” but “what happened between discovery and disappearance?”

Players can reconstruct:
- who found it;
- who handled it;
- where it was stored;
- who was authorized to access it;
- whether its recorded description ever matched the physical object;
- which transfer was never properly logged.

Possible truths include theft, clerical error, mistaken transfer, deliberate substitution, emergency removal or an object/Pokémon that was never as inert as assumed.

The system must preserve the original Chronicle provenance even if the in-world evidence is lost.

## Seed BL — The Wrong Assignment

A response team is sent after someone accused of illegally taking Pokémon from a protected area.

Field evidence shows the accused person may actually have been evacuating injured Pokémon after an environmental hazard.

Players can:
- continue the original pursuit;
- pause and investigate;
- locate the relocated Pokémon;
- contact the requesting institution;
- protect evidence from a worsening hazard;
- negotiate a voluntary meeting.

The scenario works only if accusations remain claims rather than truth flags.

## Seed BM — The Fugitive Route

A person or organization of interest knows that several groups are looking for them and begins moving through the region.

Their route depends on plausible resources and relationships:
- safehouses;
- friendly settlements;
- transport access;
- wilderness knowledge;
- faction help;
- blocked routes;
- public attention.

Players can search, predict movement, interview contacts, intercept a route or attempt communication. The target may surrender, flee, hide, redirect pursuers or continue another objective.

World Pulse may advance the pursuit state, but cannot teleport the target for dramatic convenience.

## Seed BN — Handoff at Dawn

A local rescue group stabilizes a dangerous scene overnight. By morning, a regional specialist institution arrives because the incident may connect to a larger pattern.

Both groups contributed something real.

Conflict emerges around:
- who interviews exhausted witnesses first;
- whether a dangerous location remains closed;
- where recovered objects go;
- whether rescued Pokémon can be relocated immediately;
- how much information becomes public;
- who is responsible for follow-up.

Players can serve as bridge, ally with one group, remain independent or expose that the apparent jurisdiction conflict hides a separate factual disagreement.

## Seed BO — Three Thefts That Are Not One Crime

Three nearby settlements report similar missing supplies. Rumors quickly claim a coordinated group is responsible.

The incidents share surface details but are separately authored.

One may be theft.
One may be a delivery mistake.
One may be wild Pokémon taking accessible food.

Because the public combines them into one story, players can accidentally make the investigation worse by forcing evidence into a single theory.

The case system should support linked cases without requiring a shared culprit.

## Seed BP — The Responder Under Suspicion

A regional responder appears in the records of several recent incidents and becomes the obvious subject of public suspicion.

Their repeated presence has an innocent explanation: their role sends them to emergencies.

That does not mean they are automatically innocent of everything.

Players can compare:
- dispatch records;
- witness timing;
- travel feasibility;
- evidence custody;
- personal statements;
- other responders' accounts.

The story can clear them, implicate them in a separate mistake, reveal a process failure or leave one event unresolved.

## Seed BQ — The Old Evidence Room

A new case produces an object that resembles evidence archived from an unresolved incident years earlier.

Opening the old file creates several possibilities:
- the two cases are genuinely linked;
- the resemblance is coincidence;
- an old classification was wrong;
- a witness remembered a detail incorrectly;
- an institution publicly declared closure while privately preserving uncertainty.

This seed connects the Case layer to Public Memory and Legacy without retconning old Chronicle truth.

## Seed BR — No Badge for This

A Gym's staff are first on scene during a local emergency because their facility and trained people are nearby.

They can rescue, protect and stabilize according to whatever civic role Ouros eventually grants that Gym. They do not automatically become investigators with unlimited authority.

After the immediate danger passes, another institution may take over evidence or the underlying case.

This creates a natural boundary between civic reputation, emergency response and formal investigation.

## Seed BS — Shared Case, Split Roles

A multiplayer case unfolds simultaneously in two places.

Field group:
- follows physical evidence;
- searches a route;
- interviews travelers;
- protects a scene.

Settlement group:
- compares records;
- contacts institutions;
- interviews an injured witness;
- traces ownership or transport history.

Information becomes shared only through explicit communication or institution-shared case notes. Players can discover different pieces at the same time without duplicating the same investigation check.

## Seed BT — Surrender Is a Win

Players locate a dangerous wanted target in a tactically tense situation.

The target has reasons to avoid capture but also reasons not to fight to the end.

Possible encounter outcomes:
- voluntary surrender before combat;
- surrender after allies leave;
- negotiated release of another entity;
- target retreats and remains active;
- players secure evidence while the target escapes;
- tactical defeat followed by transfer if canon supports it.

The encounter should recognize resolution conditions other than unconsciousness. Exact surrender, restraint and custody mechanics remain undefined until PTU/AutoPTU design supports them.

## Seed BU — The Accusation Arrives First

A respected resident is publicly accused before investigators finish reviewing the evidence.

The accusation spreads because a visible witness sincerely believes it.

Players face two parallel problems:
- determine what happened;
- manage the consequences of an allegation already circulating.

Even if the resident is cleared, some public standing may not immediately recover. Conversely, protecting their reputation should not suppress valid evidence if they are responsible.

This seed explicitly links attribution events to witness/public-memory propagation.

## Seed BV — The Cross-Region Ledger

Several local cases involving missing equipment initially look unrelated. Each settlement resolves its immediate problem differently.

A specialist later notices that the serial records, transport path or supplier history connect the objects across regions.

The larger investigation emerges only after local cases have already generated Chronicle facts.

Players involved in an earlier case may be contacted because they hold unique knowledge. New players can enter through the later investigation without replaying the old missions.

## Seed BW — The Scene That Must Stay Open

A dangerous area contains evidence that will disappear if a natural process continues: floodwater rises, snow covers tracks, ash falls, machinery resumes, or migrating Pokémon disturb the site.

Players have a visible clock and competing priorities:
- rescue someone still at risk;
- document fragile evidence;
- stabilize the environment;
- recover an object;
- call specialist help.

Not all evidence needs to survive. Losing it changes the investigation state rather than resetting the mission.

## Seed BX — The Returned Pokémon Dispute

A rescued Pokémon is physically safe, but two parties claim responsibility for what should happen next.

Possible claimants:
- a previous Trainer;
- a rescue organization;
- a research program;
- a settlement that has cared for it;
- nobody, if ownership cannot be established.

The system must not decide ownership from possession alone.

Players may investigate records, relationships, the Pokémon's behavior and prior Chronicle events. Any actual ownership, capture or transfer rule requires explicit Ouros canon and PTU/Caelo review.

## Seed BY — The Specialist Who Refuses the Case

An institution requests help from a well-known expert, but the expert refuses.

The refusal may come from:
- conflict of interest;
- previous involvement with a suspect;
- lack of confidence in the evidence;
- fear of public misuse of their findings;
- an obligation to another crisis;
- personal history established in canon.

Players can seek another expert, solve the access problem or continue without specialist support.

The world does not treat every useful NPC as permanently available quest infrastructure.

## Seed BZ — Review After the Rescue

Players successfully resolve an emergency, but the aftermath reveals preventable operational mistakes.

Nobody has to be secretly corrupt.

Questions may include:
- why warnings were missed;
- why two teams received contradictory instructions;
- why equipment was unavailable;
- why a route stayed open too long;
- why a rescued Pokémon was sent to the wrong facility.

An institutional review can produce new training, coordination, policy, relationships and future hooks. Heroic success can still expose system weaknesses.

## New implementation and canon questions

- Which Ouros institutions actually possess investigative, rescue, conservation, transport, Gym or regional mandates?
- Is there any region-wide legal framework, or do settlements and institutions operate under different systems?
- How should ownership of Pokémon, eggs, fossils, artifacts and recovered items be represented?
- Which forms of temporary custody are valid for people, Pokémon and evidence?
- Which AutoPTU objectives can support interception, surrender, protection and escape without custom combat rules?
- How should evidence objects persist when Minecraft chunks unload or items are physically moved?
- Which Trainer/Pokémon capabilities can expose evidence routes without generating unsupported biological assumptions?
- How should case views prevent multiplayer information leakage?
- How should target movement interact with World Pulse while remaining legible and fair?
- What canon review is required before the generator labels an act illegal, criminal, unauthorized or punishable?
