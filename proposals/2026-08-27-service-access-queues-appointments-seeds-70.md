# Service Access, Queues & Appointments Seeds — Pass 70

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON. Nothing in this file establishes Ouros locations, institutions, policies, technologies or rules.
Date: 2026-08-27

These seeds exercise the service-access layer without assuming universal booking systems, medical triage, fees, identity documents or digital infrastructure.

## The Appointment That Survived the Closure

A recurring specialist service temporarily loses access to its normal room. The underlying service remains active at reduced capacity and moves to another approved location.

The player first encounters the closure notice, then learns that confirmed appointments were not deleted. Each allocation retains its original request, old location, relocation event and new check-in point.

Useful callbacks:

- a regular arrives at the old room because they saw an outdated notice;
- a later inspection reopens the original site;
- the temporary location remains associated with a different service after the move ends;
- an actor remembers which location they actually used, not the currently canonical one.

No battle is required.

## Two Names, One Slot

Two access records appear to occupy the same service window.

Potential evidence:

- original request timestamps;
- a reschedule that created a new allocation but failed to release the old display entry;
- a group booking whose second actor was rendered as an independent booking;
- an appointment transferred under an explicit policy;
- a stale printed schedule;
- a staff handoff note;
- a communications receipt.

The premise should never assume fraud. The investigation is about chronology and provenance.

Possible endings include a clean correction, an unresolved ambiguity, a legitimate shared allocation or a process improvement handed to the owning institution.

## The Walk-In Who Was Already Expected

An actor arrives through the walk-in channel, but the service has a callback/referral record for them from an earlier event.

The interesting question is not whether the actor is secretly important. It is whether the two records describe the same access need and what information is allowed to move between them.

The owning service decides eligibility and privacy. Pass 70 only links the access lifecycle.

## The Last Registration Window

A formal activity has one remaining registration window before its owning institution closes intake for the current cycle.

The player can continue another time-sensitive task or register now. World time advances either way.

The window may only exist if the relevant institution has an established schedule. Pass 70 does not invent a dramatic deadline merely to force urgency.

If the player misses it, the activity remains in the world. The consequence is an access-state change, not removal of the entire storyline.

## No-Show, Wrong Reason

A recurring NPC is recorded as a no-show. Other characters interpret this as unreliability.

Later evidence shows that the access record alone could not explain the absence. A route interruption, outdated relocation notice, communications failure or unrelated emergency may have prevented arrival.

The seed works as a small lesson in avoiding moral conclusions from operational records.

A corrected explanation should update Rumor/Testimony or Social state without deleting the historical `NO_SHOW_RECORDED` event.

## The Queue That Became a Social Scene

A service is operating normally but demand is temporarily high enough to create a meaningful wait.

Only a few waiting actors receive persistent identity: perhaps a recurring peer, a witness from another case and a worker whose shift begins soon. The rest stay aggregated.

The player may:

- wait and talk;
- leave and return if policy permits;
- abandon the request;
- discover that another actor is also balancing a deadline.

Nothing needs to go wrong. The scene exists because shared waiting time creates intersections between ongoing lives.

## Priority Without a Public Explanation

One request moves ahead of another through a valid but non-public decision or policy.

People in the waiting area form competing explanations. The actual access record contains a provenance edge but may protect private facts.

This seed tests three simultaneous truths:

- the priority decision may be legitimate;
- observers may reasonably perceive unfairness;
- privacy can prevent a complete public explanation.

Do not resolve the tension by exposing protected medical, personal or institutional information by default.

## The Specialist Comes Every Third Day

A specialist does not live or work permanently at the location. They provide a recurring service window while moving through an established route or set of workplaces.

The access layer stores requests against upcoming windows. Travel/Workplaces own the specialist's actual movement and availability.

A weather delay or route suspension can move the session without changing the service's long-term recurrence.

This creates repeat visits to the same waiting space and lets regulars become familiar without inventing a permanent specialist facility.

## Canceled Because the Room Is Fine

A room is open, clean and physically functional, yet its service session is cancelled.

Possible owning causes:

- qualified staff unavailable;
- required shared equipment still inspection-pending;
- prerequisite sample/material did not arrive;
- another dependency failed;
- the operator was reassigned by an existing emergency plan.

The purpose is to distinguish facility condition from service availability.

## Practice Slot, Competition Slot

The same venue supports practice and formal participation through separate access channels.

An actor who has a practice allocation cannot infer entry into the competition. A confirmed competition entry does not reserve the practice area unless a separate record says so.

This seed can support rivals who repeatedly overlap in practice without meeting in every formal event.

## The Queue Estimate Everyone Repeats

An early wait estimate circulates through the neighborhood even after demand changes and a later estimate replaces it.

Some actors decide not to visit because they heard the obsolete value. Others arrive expecting a shorter delay than the current queue can support.

The story can connect Public Notices, Communications and Rumor without making any estimate a guaranteed fact.

## The Check-In That Happened at the Wrong Desk

An actor reaches the correct facility but checks in at a desk for another service channel. Their physical presence is real; their intended request remains unconfirmed.

The problem can be solved by a staff handoff, clearer signage or a new access link. Do not treat walking into the building as authoritative check-in.

## The Callback After Closing

A service closes for the day while several unresolved requests remain. Staff later create callbacks for a subset based on explicit owning policy or case state.

The callbacks preserve the original request time and reason. They do not pretend service began before closure.

On a later visit, the player can see which cases were resolved elsewhere, withdrawn or still waiting.

## Four Check-Ins, Three Appointments

Mystery / noncombat investigation.

Four people have check-in events during a period when only three formal appointments were allocated.

Potential explanations include:

- one legitimate walk-in;
- one support person incorrectly rendered as a service recipient;
- one prior appointment rescheduled but still visible on a printed list;
- one referral that used a separate channel;
- a duplicate check-in event;
- incomplete records.

Required evidence:

- channel identity;
- timestamps;
- request/allocation refs;
- desk or service-point location;
- staff handoffs;
- schedule revisions;
- actor statements with provenance.

The correct ending may be “records insufficient to prove more.”

## A Service Learns Its Regulars

Long-term continuity arc.

First visit establishes the normal access pattern: which channel operates, where people wait and what demand looks like.

Later visits introduce ordinary variation. A staff shift changes. A recurring user now chooses a different window. One week uses a temporary room. A known peer is referred elsewhere. A new public schedule replaces the old one.

After a disruption, the service adopts a workaround. When normal operations return, not every habit returns with them. Some people keep the newer window; a second desk may remain useful; an outdated sign may become part of the place's history.

Regularity never creates hidden mechanical bonuses or secret priority. It creates familiarity, remembered interactions and better-grounded expectations.

## Encounter — Registration Hall Evacuation

Narrative premise:

Several people are checked in for a formal service or challenge when a battle-capable threat makes the public registration area unsafe.

Full intended version:

- queued civilians move toward multiple exits;
- actors can protect, withdraw or clear a route;
- access desks and waiting areas matter spatially;
- opponents may block escape or behave territorially rather than seek KOs;
- interception/forced movement may matter;
- changing safe zones, terrain, hazards or reactions may matter if the governing mechanics exist;
- playback preserves which appointments had begun and which remained pending.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting / footprints / range / LoS — VERIFIED
- base movement legality — VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when used
- core calculations — VERIFIED
- action economy / initiative — VERIFIED
- full turn / round lifecycle — PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL
- terrain / weather / hazards / zones / reactions — BLOCKING when active
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL
- abilities — PARTIAL
- items — PARTIAL
- Trainer Features / perks — PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft / Cobblemon / Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING

Reduced version executable with current safer boundaries:

Evacuate all noncombatants before battle through world state. Preserve every request/check-in/allocation record. Freeze a static battle arena away from desks and operational controls. Resolve the encounter with individually verified mechanics. The owning service decides whether the venue can reopen. Pass 70 then records rescheduling, cancellation or restored access.

Battle victory cannot complete appointments, establish priority, reopen the facility or create legal battle eligibility.

## Encounter — Mobile Service Stop Interrupted

Narrative premise:

A recurring mobile service has confirmed appointments plus a small walk-in cohort when a local threat interrupts the stop.

Full intended version:

- clients withdraw while staff protect a service point;
- a mobile facility or vehicle may be spatially relevant;
- PROTECT/WITHDRAW/CLEAR_ROUTE objectives may matter;
- active weather/terrain may affect approach only if authoritative mappings exist;
- interception, forced displacement or reactions may matter;
- opponents may prefer escape, territory or access denial over KO;
- playback must distinguish clients already in service from those merely waiting.

Permanent capability classification is identical to Registration Hall Evacuation. Any forced movement, active terrain/weather/hazards/reactions, tactical objective policy or adapter synchronization remains BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Transfer all clients and staff out of the tactical grid first. Preserve `IN_SERVICE`, `CHECKED_IN` and `WAITING` records independently. Run a static battle. The owning service then decides which sessions resume, refer or reschedule.

If this is a care service, AutoPTU battle outcome does not decide treatment urgency, clinical readiness or discharge.

## Noncombat implementation candidate — Two Names, One Slot

This is suitable for an early implementation because it only requires:

- stable access request IDs;
- slot/allocation IDs;
- timestamps;
- reschedule history;
- channel identity;
- communications/notice refs;
- provenance-aware observations.

It can run in text/UI before Minecraft adapter support exists.

## Canon questions

These seeds intentionally leave open:

- which institutions schedule appointments;
- which accept walk-ins;
- which services use formal registration;
- what technologies support booking;
- whether numbered queues or tickets exist;
- what identity checks exist;
- what priority policies exist;
- whether appointment transfers or proxies are allowed;
- consequences of cancellation/no-show;
- privacy of queue and booking records;
- how mobile services operate geographically;
- which waiting spaces physically exist in approved settlements.

No answer should be promoted from these proposals without canon review.
