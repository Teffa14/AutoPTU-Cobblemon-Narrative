# Ouros Crop & Plant Health Surveillance, Response & Recovery Seeds — Pass 129

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These are reusable candidate situations and must be reconciled with established region, institution, agricultural and Pokémon canon before use.
Date: 2026-08-29

## Design intent

These seeds apply the Pass 129 plant-health continuity architecture. They favor bounded evidence, competing explanations, persistent consequences and cross-system handoffs. None assumes a universal plant-health authority, disease list, quarantine power, pest classification or tactical environmental mechanic.

## The Row That Looked Worse From the Road

A highly visible orchard row appears badly discolored from the public road. Local rumor says the entire orchard is failing.

Closer inspection shows that the road-facing row differs in age, exposure or cultivation history from the rest of the site. The interesting question becomes why that row is different, not whether the rumor is malicious.

Persistent outputs can include a corrected public explanation, a new monitoring route and an archived comparison photograph.

## The Pokémon Was There Every Morning

Residents repeatedly see the same-looking Pokémon near damaged plants at dawn. The grower assumes feeding damage.

A good investigation separates individual identity, direct feeding observation, plant symptoms and timing. The Pokémon may be feeding, merely crossing the orchard, hunting another organism, using shade, or responding to the same upstream change affecting the plants.

A conclusion about one individual never becomes a species-wide pest label.

## Two Fields, One Symptom

Two farms report similar leaf discoloration in the same week. Their water sources, planting material and cultivation histories differ.

The first field eventually supports water stress. The second supports a different cause. Similar appearance created a useful comparison but never justified a shared diagnosis.

This seed teaches the player that visual pattern matching is a hypothesis generator rather than world truth.

## The Nursery Record Is Older Than the Trees

A nursery block contains young plants whose paperwork refers to a source lot older than the visible planting. A later propagation or relabeling event explains the mismatch.

The mystery lives in provenance: plant age, source material age, planting date and record revision are separate timelines.

No fraud is required.

## The Orchard Recovered Before the Harvest Did

New symptoms stop appearing and monitored plants look healthier. The season's yield remains poor because damage occurred earlier.

The episode therefore has several valid recovery dates: biological improvement, return to ordinary work, next successful flowering window and eventual normal harvest.

This can create a long-term financial or festival consequence without keeping the crop visually sick forever.

## The Temporary Footpath Became the Inspection Route

During a plant-health episode, workers create a temporary path so inspectors can reach one orchard block without crossing another. The episode later closes, but the path proves useful and remains.

Years later the path is ordinary local geography. Old markers and photographs reveal why its strange alignment exists.

## The Empty Trap Caught a Rumor

A monitoring device remains empty for several checks. Residents interpret this as proof that the suspected organism is absent.

The investigation reveals that an empty device means only that nothing was detected by that method at that place and time. The strongest story outcome may be improved uncertainty language rather than a dramatic discovery.

## The Sample From the Wrong Tree

A tagged sample and an orchard map disagree by one row. The sample itself can still be valid, but its source scope is uncertain.

The team must reconstruct the collection route from photographs, timestamps, marker positions and witness memory. A repeat sample may be needed.

The old result remains historical evidence with reduced scope instead of being deleted.

## The Water Changed, the Diagnosis Did Not

Irrigation was repaired during an open plant-health episode. Some symptoms improve, but a previously confirmed diagnosis remains valid for another bounded plot.

This creates a mixed-cause story where two explanations are simultaneously true. Repairing infrastructure does not erase biological evidence.

## The Fruit Stopped Disappearing After the Fence Broke

A grower expects a damaged fence to worsen fruit losses. Instead, losses decline.

The investigation may reveal a changed wildlife route, a new food source elsewhere, different harvest timing or another cause entirely. The counterintuitive observation opens a Coexistence/Ecology handoff.

No rule should force the final answer.

## Three Caretakers, Three Baselines

A family orchard changes hands temporarily during one season. Each caretaker describes the same tree differently because each compares it with a different period.

Historical photographs and harvest records clarify that the tree was already unusual before the current episode.

The seed creates character texture around knowledge without declaring anyone incompetent.

## The Orchard Map Changed After Lunch

Morning fieldwork occurs in two sectors. One team uploads records immediately. Another works offline and syncs later.

A midday interpretation map and an evening revision disagree. Both are legitimate products of the evidence available at their creation times.

The adventure focuses on provenance, not sabotage.

## The Block That Never Tested Positive

A nearby block is included in a precautionary affected scope because of proximity and incomplete coverage. Repeated samples do not detect the target within their test scope.

The plot may eventually be removed from the affected interpretation. Its inclusion was not a false accusation; it was an explicitly uncertain earlier scope.

## The Old Pruning Pile

Players discover old cut material at the edge of a field during a new episode. It could be ordinary seasonal work, evidence from a previous response, storm cleanup or unrelated debris.

Historical work records and plant age establish context. The pile itself never proves a past diagnosis.

## The Garden With the Missing Pollinators

A community garden reports reduced fruit set and unusual plant condition. Growers focus first on plant disease.

Ecology records show a change in pollinator activity. That may explain part of the production problem without explaining every visible symptom.

The story can end with multiple owner systems each holding a bounded part of the answer.

## The Greenhouse Bay That Improved When Nobody Treated It

One bay improves while an adjacent treated bay changes little.

Possible explanations include different starting conditions, ventilation, watering, cultivar, timing or statistical noise. The plant-health record should preserve the observation and reopen hypotheses instead of inventing a hidden treatment effect.

## The Festival Berry Was Cleared Too Late

A culturally important harvest is eventually considered usable by the appropriate owner, but the festival menu had already been changed.

The plant-health episode can close while the cultural consequence persists. The substitute dish may even become a tradition of its own.

## The Site Is Clear, the Nursery Is Not

An orchard's monitored scope reaches a recovery checkpoint, but planting material from a linked nursery remains under review.

Farm operations can resume locally while future replanting plans remain constrained by another site. One global “resolved” flag would lose the story.

## The Pokémon Work Role Became Formal

An individual partner Pokémon had informally helped a grower notice changes for years. During an episode, its observations are useful enough that the grower begins documenting the role and training routine.

This creates a Pokémon Work/Agency progression hook. It does not grant the species automatic diagnostic competence, and the individual still does not replace formal testing where testing is required.

## Mystery: Five Times the Orchard “Recovered”

The archive contains five apparent recovery dates.

The player reconstructs:

1. last newly observed symptom in a monitored row;
2. completion of a plant-removal action;
3. diagnostic recheck within a defined scope;
4. return to ordinary cultivation;
5. next normal harvest.

All entries can be accurate. The mystery teaches that recovery has multiple owners and scopes.

## Mystery: Three Brown Rows, Two Causes

Three rows display similar discoloration. Historical water records support one explanation for the first row. Diagnostic evidence supports another in the second. The third remains unresolved.

The strongest ending is allowed to be “two causes confirmed, one still unknown.” A tidy single culprit is unnecessary.

## Mystery: The Positive Sample Outside the Red Line

A later confirmed sample lies outside the previous interpreted affected area.

The question is whether the condition spread, the older delimitation missed the location, or the sample belongs to a separate episode. Revision history and survey coverage gaps matter more than dramatic accusation.

## Exploration: The Inspection Path Behind the Orchard Wall

A narrow overgrown path follows an odd line behind a modern orchard wall. Old photographs show temporary flags, sample stations and worker access from an episode decades earlier.

The path survived because later workers reused it. A repaired gate, abandoned marker posts and a changed irrigation line tell the history spatially.

Implementation-safe form: static geometry, records and environmental storytelling only. No crop hazard mechanics are required.

## Exploration: The Greenhouse With Two Numbering Systems

An old greenhouse complex has bay numbers painted over with newer labels. Historical sample records use the first system; maintenance and current cultivation use the second.

Players reconcile old maps, photographs and door positions to locate the actual historical sample site.

No hidden laboratory or villain is necessary.

## Long arc: A Valley Learns to Look Closely

Early season: establish ordinary farms, orchards, nurseries, irrigation, community markets and known individual Pokémon work roles.

First disturbance: small plant-condition reports appear at different sites. Most are mundane. A few remain unexplained.

Investigation: growers, local experts or a canon-authorized institution conduct bounded surveys. Samples and maps accumulate. A visible wild Pokémon becomes a popular explanation but evidence remains mixed.

Branching causes: one site hands off to Water. Another to Ecology/Coexistence. A third obtains a bounded diagnostic result. A fourth remains unknown.

Response: each site chooses or receives different actions based on its actual owner system. No valley-wide solution is assumed.

Recovery: sites improve on different schedules. One harvest is still lost. One nursery remains under review. A temporary cooperative inspection route and shared record practice remain after the episode.

Later season: a superficially similar symptom appears. The old archive helps, but the system explicitly prevents the old diagnosis from being copied onto the new event without evidence.

Full intended version:

Survey staff are still withdrawing through rows when hostile combatants enter. The tactical scenario can include protected lanes, Intercept, forced displacement, time-bounded withdrawal, crossing reactions and objective-aware AI. Any spore, pollen, heat, wind or crop-condition effect requires an exact PTU/Caelo rule plus implemented engine contract.

Dependency families:

- base movement legality: VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: PARTIAL;
- core calculations: VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING for dynamic protected lanes or environmental effects;
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL;
- abilities: PARTIAL;
- items: PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support: BLOCKING.

Reduced version: READY.

The survey pauses first. Staff, samples, tools and noncombatants leave the tactical space. Ouros selects explicit combatants and a static reviewed orchard perimeter. AutoPTU resolves a conventional battle. Winning can secure immediate access only. A later plant-health survey determines scope and cause.

## Encounter: Nursery Block Diversion

Full intended version:

A controlled planting-material area remains present as a protected objective while actors withdraw. This may need forced movement, protected-object semantics, zones, reactions, lifecycle and tactical policy.

Reduced version: READY.

The nursery block, samples and controlled material remain outside BattleSpec. The battle occurs on a stable access road or exterior yard. Battle outcome cannot clear, condemn, destroy, transfer or release planting material.

## Encounter: Pollinator Edge Reinspection

Full intended version:

Wild Pokémon use a habitat edge adjacent to a field under reinspection. A full scenario might require nonlethal objectives, moving noncombatants, reactions, terrain zones and AI that values withdrawal rather than defeat.

Reduced version: READY.

Ecology/Coexistence resolves wildlife movement before combat. The plant-health team withdraws. The subject Pokémon stays outside BattleSpec unless Ouros independently selects it as a combatant from world facts. AutoPTU receives static geometry. Winning does not prove causality or justify wildlife removal.

## Minecraft presentation opportunities

Once Ouros has decided the facts, Minecraft/Cobblemon can present changing crop visuals, inspection flags, temporary access paths, tagged rows, posted maps, different worker routines, repaired irrigation, replanted areas and recurring Pokémon at habitat edges.

Minecraft-native crop age, mob spawning, block breakage, potion particles or pathfinding must never decide diagnosis, affected scope, crop recovery, pest identity, custody, battle legality or narrative outcome.

## Canon review queue

These seeds remain proposed until regional canon answers enough of the following for a specific use:

- crop/site identity;
- operator/steward;
- relevant local institution or expertise;
- diagnostic technology actually available;
- authority for any compulsory action;
- privacy/publication policy;
- wildlife-management owner;
- individual Pokémon work roles;
- historical episode references;
- exact mechanical rule if an encounter uses environmental effects.
