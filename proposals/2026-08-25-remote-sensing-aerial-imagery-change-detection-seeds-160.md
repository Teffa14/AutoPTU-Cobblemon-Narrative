# Ouros Remote Sensing, Aerial Imagery & Change Detection Seeds — Pass 160

These candidates derive from the research/provenance in `research/2026-08-25-remote-sensing-aerial-imagery-change-detection-scan-160.md` and the systems boundary in `design/remote-sensing-aerial-imagery-change-detection-layer.md`.

The recurring design rule is that remote observation produces dated evidence with coverage limits. It does not create omniscient geography, causation, population truth or battle mechanics.

## 1. The Marsh That Changed Between Passes

Two dated acquisitions show the outer edge of a marsh in different positions. The change is supported, but the gap between observations is large enough that no exact date or cause can be assigned.

Potential handoffs: Estuaries/Wetlands, Freshwater, Climate, Science.

## 2. The Burn Scar That Wasn't

A dark patch in a derived product is initially flagged as a candidate burn scar. Field validation shows a mix of shadow, recently exposed soil and a small real burn that occupies only part of the polygon.

Design use: teach the distinction between classification and cause without making the institution incompetent.

## 3. Cloud Week

A monitoring program misses the exact week when a coastal breach forms because every planned acquisition is obscured. The best possible historical conclusion is an interval between the last clear image and the first clear image after the event.

Potential handoffs: Coastal Geomorphology, Meteorology, Archives.

## 4. The Old Image Solves a New Question

A decades-old image was collected for road planning. Years later it becomes the best evidence that a wetland expansion began before a restoration project that was publicly credited with causing it.

Design use: remote archives gain value without rewriting what people reasonably believed at the time.

## 5. Two Products, One Acquisition

Two institutions process the same source acquisition using different revisions. Their shoreline polygons disagree slightly. Neither source image changed.

Potential loop: compare processing provenance, inspect benchmarks, perform limited field validation, publish a scoped correction.

## 6. The Quarry Face Moved

A multi-year image series clearly shows extraction expanding across a quarry face. The final image also shows early reclamation on an older sector.

Design use: one site can contain extraction and recovery simultaneously.

## 7. The Forest Opening Predates the Storm

A public rumor attributes a forest opening to the latest windstorm. Archived imagery reveals that the opening already existed two years earlier.

The new storm may still have enlarged it. The remote record narrows the claim rather than replacing field ecology.

## 8. Eight Days Too Late

An institution's normal revisit cadence is too slow to see a short-lived flood peak. The acquisition after the event documents residual water and debris but not the peak itself.

Potential handoffs: Freshwater, Crisis, Metrology.

## 9. The Snowline Argument

Two mountain teams compare images from different times of day, viewing geometry and cloud conditions and initially treat them as equivalent. A comparability review reduces confidence in the apparent snowline shift.

Design use: uncertainty can prevent a false quest.

## 10. The Missing Island

A small river bar visible in several old acquisitions disappears from a later product. Field work discovers that it did not vanish entirely; it became attached to the bank and was classified differently.

Potential handoffs: Fluvial Geomorphology, Cartography.

## 11. The Protected Roost in the Public Product

A broad habitat product is publishable, but one detailed derivative exposes an exact roost location. The institution releases a generalized version and restricts the precise layer.

Potential handoffs: Conservation, Research Ethics, Media.

## 12. The Wrong Rare-Pokémon Headline

A low-resolution image contains several bright shapes. A public post labels them as a rare species before identity review. Later analysis shows the image cannot support species-level identification.

Design use: authentic imagery can carry a wrong caption without requiring fabrication.

## 13. One Visible Herd, Unknown Population

An aerial pass clearly records a large group in one meadow. The institution publishes the count as an observed aggregation, not a population estimate.

Potential handoffs: Wild Collectives, Demography-like wildlife estimates, Migration.

## 14. The Platform That Disturbed the Survey

A low-altitude survey platform repeatedly approaches a nesting shoreline. Later observations show route changes during the survey window, but causation remains under review.

Potential handoffs: Research Ethics, Airspace, Wild Nesting.

## 15. The Silent Tower

A fixed observation tower remains physically intact, but its acquisition program stops for a season after a sensor failure. The world keeps changing; the series gains a documented gap.

Potential handoffs: Technology, Metrology, Archives.

## 16. The Image Everyone Used

A historical emergency map was built from a product later found to have a small geometric offset. The old map remains part of the record because responders actually used it.

Design use: corrected evidence does not erase historical decision context.

## 17. The New Processing Revision

A reprocessing campaign improves alignment across twenty years of imagery and makes a slow shoreline retreat easier to see. The physical history is unchanged; confidence in the interpretation improves.

Potential handoffs: Metrology, Coastal Geomorphology, Climate.

## 18. The Reservoir Looks Larger

A new image appears to show a reservoir expansion. Investigation reveals that the previous acquisition happened under heavy shadow and partial cloud mask.

Result can legitimately be `INSUFFICIENT_COMPARABILITY` rather than a hydrological event.

## 19. The Urban Edge

Five years of acquisitions show a settlement gradually expanding toward an old industrial parcel. The change creates a future planning conflict with a brownfield restriction and a wildlife corridor.

Potential handoffs: Architecture/Urban systems, Contaminated Land, Road Ecology, Governance.

## 20. The Road That Appeared Before Approval

A faint corridor appears on imagery before any official road opening. Field validation shows it is a temporary construction/access track, not a public route.

Land Tenure and Travel remain authoritative for access.

## 21. The Heat Signature Rumor

A thermal-like product shows an anomalous warm area around an industrial site. Public speculation blames Fire-type Pokémon. The remote product can establish the spatial signal, not its source.

Potential handoffs: Technology, Urban Heat, Toxicology, Pokémon Agency.

## 22. The Orchard Rows Changed Color

A seasonal agricultural product flags a change across several orchard blocks. Field work finds different causes in different rows: harvest timing, irrigation stress and a newly planted block.

Potential handoffs: Agriculture, Irrigation, Plant Pathology, IPM.

## 23. The Bridge Interval

One acquisition shows an intact bridge; the next shows it absent. Dispatch records, storm reports and oral histories offer several possible event times within the interval.

Design use: different evidence types can narrow the timeline without remote imagery claiming exact causation.

## 24. The Archive With a Missing Year

A regional survey program is famous for its long history, but one year's products were lost during a storage migration. Later institutions preserve the gap instead of generating a synthetic replacement.

Potential handoffs: Digital Systems, Archives, Public Memory.

## 25. The Calibration Flight

A survey platform repeats a route over stable reference features to compare an updated sensor with an older one. Nothing dramatic happens, but the campaign improves comparability for future change detection.

Design use: routine technical work can become useful Chronicle state without a quest.

## 26. The Remote Craft Is Not Welcome Here

A protected area allows distant observation but restricts low-altitude approaches during a reproductive season. The scientific objective remains possible using lower-resolution coverage or a later field visit.

Potential handoffs: Conservation, Airspace, Research Ethics.

## 27. The Field Team Disagrees

Remote classification marks a shallow-water zone as dry. Field observers arrive weeks later and find water. The result remains unresolved until timing, masks and intervening rainfall are reconciled.

Design use: disagreement between remote and field evidence does not imply dishonesty.

## 28. The Map Was More Precise Than the Evidence

A public cartographic product draws a crisp boundary around a habitat derived from uncertain imagery. A later review keeps the original map for history but replaces the current product with an uncertainty band.

Potential handoffs: Cartography, Science, Conservation.

## 29. The Released Partner in Old Imagery

Years after a partnered Pokémon was released, an old remote image is found that may show the same individual at a distant site. Resolution is insufficient for identity confirmation.

Pokémon Agency keeps the identity claim provisional. No ownership or capture availability is inferred.

## 30. Nothing Changed This Pass

A scheduled acquisition covers the target region clearly and finds no change detectable at the program's scale. The result is archived as a valid baseline and creates no quest.

Design use: monitoring can succeed by documenting stability.

# Longer-Term Arcs

## A. Twenty Years Over Meridian Basin

A regional observation program begins as a modest aerial survey and accumulates repeated acquisitions across a river, reservoir, industrial edge, wetlands and expanding settlement.

Early years are mostly baseline work. A later flood changes the channel. A quarry expands and then enters reclamation. A marsh changes before anyone notices from the ground. A new processing revision improves comparison with older material. Eventually one historical product becomes central to a dispute over whether a public project caused an ecological change.

The arc can run for decades without a mastermind. Its narrative value comes from dated evidence, institutional memory and the fact that people make reasonable decisions using the products available at the time.

## B. The Missing Passes

A mountain/coastal observation program develops several gaps caused by weather, equipment failure, access restrictions and funding interruptions. Important landscape changes happen during some of those gaps.

Players can recover old field notebooks, repair observation infrastructure, conduct field validation and improve future monitoring, but they cannot reconstruct every missing day perfectly. The final archive remains stronger because it records uncertainty honestly.

## C. From Image to Ground Truth

A recurring research arc uses the same loop across different systems:

remote candidate change -> choose whether the site matters -> plan access -> field validation -> reconcile evidence -> update the owning domain -> preserve both old and new interpretations.

Over several years the institution learns which products are reliable for which questions, where field work remains essential and which apparent anomalies can be safely left unresolved.

# Encounter Contracts

## Remote Tower Retrieval

Narrative premise: a fixed monitoring site stopped transmitting after a storm. The remote-observation program needs the device or storage module retrieved before the next scheduled acquisition window.

FULL version:

The technician and/or retrieval actor must move through a route that can change tactically. Wildlife may withdraw rather than fight. Storm damage can create real blocked or hazardous sectors. The objective is `REACH_DEVICE`, `PROTECT_TECHNICIAN`, `WITHDRAW` or `CLEAR_ROUTE`, not simply eliminate all opponents.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED baseline;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED baseline;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING if actors/routes reposition dynamically;
- core calculations — VERIFIED baseline;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED baseline;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL if advanced multi-round objective state is modeled in battle;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL for ordinary damage completeness;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL only if an exact Status is invoked;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if storm debris, unstable ground or active weather changes tactical state;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL for exact Moves;
- abilities — PARTIAL for exact Abilities;
- items — PARTIAL if survey gear receives battle semantics;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL if an exact Feature is invoked;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED baseline;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for objective-aware movement/withdrawal;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING for semantic objective/world-state handoff.

REDUCED version:

World state closes unsafe sectors, moves the technician outside combat and resolves equipment access. If an independent confrontation remains, AutoPTU receives a static safe arena. After combat, the device retrieval is resolved outside battle.

## Change-Polygon Field Validation

Narrative premise: an imagery comparison detects a candidate wetland/forest/shoreline change. A field team visits the edge to determine whether the detected boundary is physical, seasonal or a processing artifact.

FULL version:

Wild Pokémon can `WITHDRAW`, `CROSS` or protect a route while investigators move between observation points. If water, mud, vegetation or unstable ground has real tactical effects, the battlefield changes with the environment.

Capability dependencies:

- VERIFIED: targeting/footprints/range/LoS, base movement legality, core calculations, action economy/initiative, AI legal-action infrastructure;
- BLOCKING: complete movement for crossing/withdrawal objectives, AI tactical policy, adapter/playback;
- CONDITIONAL BLOCKING: terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if the environmental boundary affects tactical rules;
- CONDITIONAL PARTIAL: exact status, move, ability, item or Trainer Feature families when invoked.

REDUCED version:

The field team completes observations outside battle. Wildlife movement and the environmental boundary remain overworld state. Any independent combat uses static geometry away from the measurement points.

## Sensitive Roost Publication Incident

Narrative premise: a detailed remote product accidentally exposes a sensitive roost or nest location just before a public event drives visitors toward the site.

FULL version:

Crowds, staff and wildlife may need to evacuate or reroute while keeping the protected site outside the primary confrontation.

Capability dependencies:

- VERIFIED baseline: targeting, base movement legality, calculations, action economy, AI legal-action infrastructure;
- BLOCKING: complete movement for moving civilians/wildlife, AI tactical policy for `EVACUATE`, `WITHDRAW`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`, adapter/playback;
- CONDITIONAL BLOCKING: environmental family only if the site has tactical protected/hazard zones;
- PARTIAL when exact Moves/Abilities/Items/Features are used.

REDUCED version:

Media/Conservation retract or generalize the product, Travel closes the route and civilians are redirected in world state. Combat begins only after the sensitive site is cleared.

## Processing Review Meeting

No battle dependency.

Two products disagree after a processing update. Metrology, Science, Remote Sensing and the owning environmental layer can conclude `SUFFICIENT_WITH_LIMITATIONS`, `REQUIRES_REPROCESSING`, `ARTIFACT_OR_PROCESSING_DIFFERENCE` or `UNRESOLVED` without any confrontation.
