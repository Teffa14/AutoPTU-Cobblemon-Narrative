# Local Sidequest Ecology Seeds — Pass 82

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

These are original Ouros candidates derived from the orchestration patterns in Pass 82 research. They do not establish locations, institutions, species, technologies or rules as canon.

## 1. The Street With Too Many Small Problems

Premise:

One familiar commercial street has several visible disruptions at once: one service closes early, a repair waits on a delivery, a public notice is outdated and foot traffic has shifted.

Hidden design structure:

All four facts trace to one transport-service suspension.

Foreground choice:

Surface one coherent investigation/coordination thread. Keep the other facts as stakeholder state rather than four separate quests.

Possible outputs:

- transport-service state clarified;
- storefront availability updated;
- maintenance timeline updated;
- notice corrected;
- pedestrian routine changes persisted.

No combat is required.

## 2. The Request That Never Becomes a Quest

Premise:

A resident casually mentions that a familiar wild Pokémon has stopped appearing near a public fountain at its usual time.

The player may:

- ask a follow-up question;
- observe the site later;
- ignore it;
- notice a related clue during another activity.

The game does not automatically create a formal mission.

Escalation condition:

Only repeated observations, conservation state, another witness, a route change or a player commitment can promote it.

Purpose:

Demonstrate ambient opportunity without quest-log inflation.

## 3. Three Requests, One Morning

Premise:

Three residents independently want help during the same limited morning window.

One needs a short observation.
One needs an item delivered within the district.
One needs someone present for a scheduled inspection.

The player cannot personally foreground all three at once.

Possible outcomes:

- choose one;
- delegate one through a valid institutional/helper route;
- reschedule where the owning system permits;
- let another actor handle one;
- accept that one opportunity expires.

No hidden morality penalty is assigned for choosing.

Purpose:

Make concurrent opportunity meaningful without introducing a downtime point economy.

## 4. The Same Bench, Different Story

Premise:

A public bench has appeared in several earlier scenes.

First visit:

A resident uses it during a normal routine.

Later:

A public event temporarily removes it.

Later again:

It returns in a slightly different location after repairs.

A recurring NPC now uses the old location to feed or observe Pokémon.

The hook is about the changed use of the space, not the bench as a magical quest object.

Purpose:

Use one ordinary prop as continuity evidence across several systems.

## 5. The Shopkeeper Already Solved It

Premise:

The player returns after hearing that a local storefront had a minor supply problem.

World Pulse has already advanced it.

The operator found a temporary substitute through an existing supplier relationship.

Player-facing content:

- the shop is LIMITED rather than CLOSED;
- a sign explains changed availability;
- the operator can mention what happened;
- a downstream consequence may still exist elsewhere.

No quest remains merely because the player did not arrive in time.

Purpose:

Prove that local problems can resolve without the protagonist.

## 6. The Failed Battle Does Not Respawn

Premise:

A local optional encounter ended in defeat.

The next visit does not instantiate the same opponent, same positions and same objective.

Possible state deltas:

- the wild group moved;
- the route is temporarily closed;
- a local specialist has gathered new observations;
- the player can approach from another direction;
- another actor already attempted a response;
- the original threat is gone but a consequence remains.

Purpose:

Operationalize the repeatability guard.

## 7. The Thread That Shrinks

Premise:

A situation first looks like a district-wide problem.

Investigation shows only one building and one delivery batch are affected.

The active thread becomes smaller instead of escalating for drama.

Purpose:

Reward narrowing scope and evidence quality.

## 8. The Thread That Absorbs Another

Premise:

A library asks about repeated late returns from one neighborhood. Separately, a courier service reports a pickup route is taking longer.

New evidence:

A temporary access diversion affects both.

The library-specific thread is absorbed into the larger route-access thread.

The library retains its own circulation consequences.

Purpose:

Show `hook_merge` without losing downstream state.

## 9. The Quiet Week

Premise:

The local cell has many active systems but no urgent foreground problem.

The game deliberately surfaces:

- one low-stakes conversation;
- one optional personal-project opportunity;
- one new visual change from prior world state.

It does not manufacture a crisis.

Purpose:

Preserve ordinary-life baseline and make later disruptions more legible.

## 10. The Newcomer Appears After Progress

Premise:

A previously unused room or workbench in a familiar district becomes occupied by a new recurring actor after an existing service expands.

The newcomer has their own routine, role and possible future hooks.

They do not appear because the player reached an arbitrary level.

Unlock provenance:

- facility expansion completed;
- staffing state created the role;
- transport/access permits arrival;
- housing/occupancy state provides a place.

Purpose:

Introduce new local cast through world causality.

## 11. The Requester Changes Their Mind

Premise:

An NPC asked for help with a low-urgency issue.

Before the player accepts it, new information arrives and the requester withdraws or reframes the request.

Possible reason:

- the problem resolved;
- a safer option appeared;
- they learned their original assumption was wrong;
- another institution took responsibility;
- they no longer want outside involvement.

Purpose:

Keep requesters agentic and prevent stale quest markers.

## 12. One Location, Four Systems

Premise:

A small courtyard simultaneously belongs to ordinary residential life, a storefront's delivery access, a recurring hobby routine and a wild Pokémon's observed movement path.

No single system owns the courtyard's entire meaning.

A temporary change in one use can produce a local thread only when it intersects another use in a meaningful way.

Purpose:

Stress-test location reuse and authority boundaries.

## 13. The Opportunity Nobody Notices Yet

Premise:

World state has created a valid problem, but no actor with plausible knowledge has observed it.

The hook remains latent.

Possible reveal paths:

- routine worker discovers it tomorrow;
- a player inspects the site for unrelated reasons;
- a maintenance sensor notices it if such technology is canonically supported;
- a Pokémon behavioral observation becomes evidence;
- another event exposes it.

Purpose:

Prevent omniscient quest generation.

## 14. The Wrong Marker Was Removed

Premise:

A public notice or quest marker disappears after a thread changes state.

Players later learn the underlying issue still exists in a different form.

The display was correctly retired because that specific request was withdrawn.

A new foreground thread appears only after new provenance exists.

Purpose:

Separate presentation lifecycle from causal state.

## 15. The Same Person Has More Than One Life

Premise:

A recurring NPC first appears as a shop worker.

Later they participate in a hobby.

Later still they become a witness in an unrelated investigation.

Their identity remains one persistent actor.

No new “quest NPC version” is created.

Purpose:

Make local cast feel like residents rather than interfaces.

## 16. Long arc — A Neighborhood Learns Its Own Rhythm

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Phase 1 — Baseline

Establish a small district in ordinary use:

- several residents;
- one or two services;
- a shared public space;
- routine delivery/access patterns;
- a recurring Pokémon observation;
- one quiet leisure routine.

Phase 2 — First overlap

A minor service disruption creates two or three valid hook candidates.

The orchestration layer surfaces only one foreground thread and keeps the rest ambient.

Phase 3 — Player history

The player's chosen response creates persistent changes:

- one relationship gains an observable history;
- one route or service changes;
- one location gets a visual delta;
- one resident's routine changes.

Phase 4 — Independent world action

World Pulse resolves another local issue without the player.

The player returns to evidence of what happened rather than an untouched quest.

Phase 5 — Revisit with a new cause

A familiar location becomes relevant again for a different reason.

The revisit reuses prior history as context but presents a different decision/activity profile.

Phase 6 — Saturation test

Several small opportunities become valid simultaneously.

Some merge. Some remain ambient. One waits. One expires.

Phase 7 — Ordinary return

After the busy period, the neighborhood visibly returns to a stable routine with a changed baseline.

Long-term output:

The player remembers the district because its residents and functions accumulated history, not because it produced an endless queue of disconnected tasks.

## 17. Mystery — Five Hooks, Two Causes

Premise:

Five local anomalies appear across one district:

- late opening;
- missing delivery;
- reduced foot traffic;
- an NPC routine deviation;
- a wild Pokémon appearing at an unusual hour.

The system initially stores five separate hook candidates.

Investigation may reveal:

- three come from one transport/access disruption;
- two come from a separate seasonal/ecological change.

The solution is not “everything is connected.”

Purpose:

Teach the system to merge only where evidence supports it.

## 18. Encounter — Market-Lane Clear Route

Status: mechanically rich candidate.

Narrative premise:

A temporary change in pedestrian flow creates conflict with a local wild-Pokémon use pattern near a market lane.

Intended full version:

- noncombatants actively withdraw;
- several exits remain relevant;
- CLEAR_ROUTE or WITHDRAW can matter more than defeat-all;
- narrow geometry makes Intercept and forced movement potentially relevant;
- some market fixtures should remain protected;
- wild AI may prefer territorial withdrawal over suicidal fighting;
- exact current environment may matter if later supported.

Permanent capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — required;
- base movement legality — required;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — required for the full spatial premise;
- core calculations — required;
- action economy/initiative — required;
- full turn/round lifecycle — required;
- full stateful damage pipeline — required;
- status lifecycle — required according to selected roster;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — required only if the environment becomes tactical;
- move-specific behavior — required according to selected roster;
- abilities — required according to selected roster;
- items — required according to selected roster;
- Trainer Features/perks — required according to selected participants;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — required;
- AI tactical policy — required for territorial/withdrawal objective behavior;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — required for final integrated presentation.

Reduced version:

- civilians leave before tactical instantiation;
- market goods and fixtures remain outside battle authority;
- Ouros freezes one reviewed static arena;
- explicit combatants are selected by Ouros;
- AutoPTU resolves a conventional approved encounter;
- route/public-space/ecology state decides reopening afterward;
- no Minecraft-scripted Intercept, escort bonus, knockback, destructible stalls or tactical crowd movement.

## 19. Encounter — Revisit at the Footbridge

Status: mechanically rich candidate.

Narrative premise:

A footbridge repaired during an earlier thread is now open. The new pedestrian pattern intersects a recurring Pokémon route.

The encounter is a revisit but not a repetition of the repair mission.

Intended full version:

- protected crossing/withdrawal space;
- Intercept or forced movement may affect chokepoint positioning;
- territorial/withdrawal AI;
- optional environment interaction if validated;
- writeback can alter route use rather than merely mark victory.

Reduced version:

- pedestrian rerouting occurs first in world state;
- noncombatants stay outside the grid;
- the bridge is represented as static geometry only;
- AutoPTU resolves selected combatants;
- later ecology/public-space state records whether the route remains shared, restricted or changed.

## 20. Encounter — The Optional Rival Is Busy

Status: low-mechanics competitive callback.

Premise:

A recurring rival is present in the local cell but has their own current commitment.

The player may speak with them, but a rematch is unavailable unless both schedules/conditions support it.

Later, if a battle occurs, it must use the normal Battle Institution/Rivalry/Scouting contracts.

Purpose:

Prevent persistent rivals from becoming stationary battle dispensers.

## 21. Cobblemon implementation notes

Potential safe presentation reuse:

- persistent NPC/Pokémon overworld entities;
- schedules/positions;
- signs and markers;
- animations and ambient behaviors;
- changed shop displays;
- temporary closure props;
- UI for optional threads;
- interaction callbacks;
- particles/sounds for environmental feedback;
- world geometry and blocks;
- network synchronization and persistence hooks.

Authority remains:

- Ouros chooses active/ambient/latent state and encounter participants;
- AutoPTU resolves all tactical state and results;
- Cobblemon displays the approved world and battle playback only.

Cobblemon internal BattleState, participant selection, HP/status, tactical AI and victory logic are never authoritative.
