# Ouros Dreams, Aura & Psychic Information Seeds — Pass 25

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. None of these are copied plots, characters, settings or dialogue.

## Design rule for this set

Every candidate preserves the separation between objective fact, subjective experience, interpretation, capability-limited observation and public belief. Psychic or dream information can open a path to investigation. It does not automatically finish the investigation.

## Candidate 01 — The Sleepless Block

Several residents of one neighborhood report poor sleep during the same week. The first reports blame a dream-associated Pokémon species because one was seen nearby.

The actual investigation should preserve multiple plausible causes: construction vibration, a damaged electrical system, environmental change, ordinary stress, deliberate interference, a wild Pokémon presence or a combination of factors.

Useful connections:
- care/recovery;
- science;
- media/rumor;
- settlement infrastructure;
- wild ecology.

No Legendary hypothesis is promoted without evidence.

## Candidate 02 — Black Mist at Dawn

A recurring dark-colored mist is observed outside a boarding house shortly before sunrise. Guests begin comparing nightmares and assume the mist records them.

The world state stores the mist as an observation. Dream content remains private unless voluntarily reported or accessed through a validated rule.

Possible outcomes include a harmless ecological explanation, a research opportunity, a service disruption or a true supernatural phenomenon. None is preselected by the seed.

## Candidate 03 — The Shared Stairwell

Three people independently describe a staircase in dreams. Their descriptions agree on the physical arrangement but disagree completely about the doors and what waits beyond them.

The common architecture becomes a `DREAM_REGION` hypothesis. Each door remains participant-specific perception.

A later physical discovery may corroborate part of the geometry without proving every dream image true.

## Candidate 04 — The Aura Misread

A respected Aura reader publicly reports a darkened reading around a person involved in a serious incident. The community begins treating the reading as proof of guilt.

The arc is built around correcting the category error. A limited mood/intention signal is not a confession, motive record or legal finding.

This can intersect with:
- case/evidence rules;
- media corrections;
- public memory;
- social trust.

## Candidate 05 — Dream Census

A research group asks volunteers to record recurring locations, colors, sounds and Pokémon appearing in dreams over several months.

The interesting play comes from consent, data quality and pattern interpretation rather than combat.

Independent observations can reveal stable dream landmarks, but the research program must distinguish copied rumors from genuinely independent reports.

## Candidate 06 — Residue Chain

After an incident, an actor carries mechanically validated psychic residue. Two institutions disagree about its source and significance.

The players can investigate:
- who had access;
- which psychic actions were actually possible;
- whether residue could have another source;
- what independent physical evidence exists.

A future Java implementation can use authoritative Psionic Analysis only after Trainer Feature support is ported.

## Candidate 07 — The Quiet-Mind Custom

A town with a long history of visiting psychic specialists has developed strong social customs around mental privacy.

The tradition may include explicit invitations, visible signals that someone does not wish to be contacted mentally, private consultation rooms and community expectations around disclosure.

These are social conventions unless later canon defines formal law.

## Candidate 08 — The Empty Dream

A person known for vivid recurring dreams suddenly reports none for several weeks. The absence itself becomes an observation.

Possible investigation directions include:
- changed sleep environment;
- medication or treatment;
- seasonal ecology;
- a vanished dream-associated Pokémon population;
- psychological interpretation;
- supernatural interference.

The system does not assume a pathology.

## Candidate 09 — Anchor Under the Old Station

A dream landmark repeatedly appears beneath an active transit station. Historical maps show an older structure once occupied the same ground.

The dream does not grant underground access. Players must investigate the physical site through ordinary travel, civic, archaeology and custody rules.

The dream may have preserved a memory, symbolized the location or reflected information heard elsewhere.

## Candidate 10 — Two Dreamers, One House

Two former residents of the same house describe it very differently in recurring dreams. One remembers warmth and safety; another perceives a threatening labyrinth.

The seed supports character history without asking the system to decide whose emotional memory is “correct.”

Shared architectural details can generate an ordinary historical investigation.

## Candidate 11 — The Volunteer Listener

A telepathic specialist runs an opt-in service for people and Pokémon who have difficulty communicating through ordinary means.

The service becomes useful for:
- care cases;
- rescue work;
- lost-person incidents;
- communication across disability or language barriers where PTU rules permit.

The specialist is not allowed to scan uninvolved people for convenient plot answers.

## Candidate 12 — Aura Trail to the Missing Rescuer

A missing response worker left the last known area under dangerous conditions. A participant with a validated Aura-related capability receives a limited directional or identifying signal within the exact scope of the governing rule.

The signal narrows search space. It does not provide exact coordinates, condition, thoughts or cause of disappearance unless the rule explicitly does so.

The final route can end in a normal static battle until richer objective support exists.

## Candidate 13 — Clinic Sleep Pattern

A care facility notices an unusual increase in patients reporting interrupted sleep. The clinic publishes only aggregated information.

The pattern can trigger:
- ecological fieldwork;
- infrastructure inspection;
- research;
- public-health communication;
- investigation of a possible psychic phenomenon.

Individual patient details remain private.

## Candidate 14 — The Returning Dream Pokémon

A wild dream-associated Pokémon repeatedly appears near the same sleeping shelter, leaves before sunrise and avoids capture attempts.

Players can observe behavior over time instead of treating it as a one-scene encounter.

The Pokémon’s motivation remains unknown until evidence supports an interpretation.

## Candidate 15 — Moonless Week

A recurring regional dream phenomenon normally appears during a known seasonal window but fails to occur one year.

The absence can connect seasonality, research and wild-collective movement.

The generator should require prior records before calling the absence anomalous.

## Candidate 16 — Telepathic Relay During an Outage

A communications outage isolates two groups. A validated telepathic capability can carry limited messages between actors who are actually in legal range or satisfy the governing rule.

This should not become a universal replacement for communications infrastructure.

The interesting question is which information can be transmitted and verified under pressure.

## Candidate 17 — Archive of Psychic Residue

An old research institution kept records of psychic traces from past experiments. Many entries lack modern provenance standards.

Players can compare old claims with current techniques and discover that some celebrated conclusions were overconfident.

The archive can later influence public memory and institutional reputation.

## Candidate 18 — Dream Region Return Visit

A previously explored dream region becomes accessible again after a major real-world event. The stable landmarks remain, but some symbolic features have changed.

The system records the changed features without automatically interpreting why they changed.

This creates meaningful return visits without cloning the original scene.

## Candidate 19 — The False Protection Market

After a public nightmare scare, vendors begin selling objects advertised as protection against psychic intrusion.

Some sellers may believe their claims. Others may exaggerate. A few products could have ordinary comfort value without any mechanical effect.

The seed supports economy, media, cases and cultural belief without inventing item bonuses.

## Candidate 20 — The Witness Who Heard a Voice

A key witness reports receiving a telepathic warning before an incident. No one else perceived it.

The report enters the case as testimony. It does not prove a telepathic sender exists.

Possible corroboration includes psychic residue, timing, independent witnesses, recorded movement or later communication.

## Candidate 21 — Dreams of a Closed Route

Travelers begin describing a route that has been physically closed for years. Their dreams share a landmark that no current map shows.

Potential explanations include old public memory, family stories, an undiscovered surviving section or a subjective construct.

The route remains closed until ordinary world-state evidence changes it.

## Candidate 22 — The Silent Aura School

An old institution associated with Aura traditions survives as a small community practice rather than a powerful combat academy.

Its members disagree over interpretation, ethics and how much supernatural information should be used in civic or investigative life.

This gives Aura culture internal diversity without assigning one doctrine as universal truth.

## Candidate 23 — Dream-Mist Conservation Study

Researchers discover that a dream-related resource is being collected faster than local Pokémon can sustainably produce or tolerate.

The conflict involves scientific uncertainty, artisan demand, cultural use and wild-Pokémon welfare.

Any actual Dream Mist production rules must come from PTU/Caelo, not this proposal.

## Candidate 24 — The Dreamer Who Refuses Interpretation

A recurring NPC voluntarily reports a vivid dream but explicitly asks everyone not to interpret it for them.

The narrative challenge is respecting that boundary while still investigating an external phenomenon linked to the same location.

This seed reinforces that access to information does not create ownership over another person’s meaning.

## Candidate 25 — The Memory Everyone Knows

A famous historical event survives in schoolbooks, songs and a recurring shared-dream motif. Archaeological evidence later contradicts part of the accepted story.

The dream motif remains culturally real even if it is historically inaccurate.

This connects myth, public memory and dream research without retroactively deleting community tradition.

## Candidate 26 — The Unwanted Signal

A psychic-sensitive NPC begins noticing a repeating mental signal near a busy public facility. The signal is intrusive but too vague to identify a sender.

Investigation must establish whether it is:
- intentional communication;
- environmental residue;
- a Pokémon behavior;
- equipment interference;
- a misunderstood ordinary stimulus.

Do not turn the vague signal into precise exposition.

## Candidate 27 — The Dream Door That Should Not Open

A persistent dream region contains a door associated with a real locked research archive. Some participants assume opening the dream door should unlock the physical archive.

It does not.

The dream scene can reveal a clue or participant-specific perception, while physical access remains governed by custody, authority and Minecraft state.

## Candidate 28 — Psychic Mediation Failure

Two groups agree to use a telepathic intermediary because ordinary negotiation has broken down. The intermediary can accurately relay statements but cannot guarantee sincerity or shared interpretation.

The arc explores communication limits rather than granting magical conflict resolution.

## Candidate 29 — The Pokémon That Dreams Nearby

A persistent Pokémon begins sleeping near a particular human settlement after a crisis. Residents interpret its presence as gratitude, protection or warning depending on their beliefs.

The system records behavior and public interpretations separately.

A later ecological explanation can coexist with the cultural meaning that developed around it.

## Candidate 30 — The Redacted Dream Study

A published research paper removes identifying dream content to protect participants. A faction claims the redaction proves the researchers are hiding a dangerous discovery.

Players can inspect methods, consent records and external evidence without demanding private participant material.

This creates a research/media conflict where privacy itself is not treated as suspicious behavior.

# Longer arc A — Sleepless Season

A region develops a measurable rise in sleep complaints during a recurring seasonal period.

Phase 1 establishes care-facility signals and ordinary infrastructure explanations.

Phase 2 adds ecological observations, dream-related Pokémon movement and conflicting public narratives.

Phase 3 produces targeted research, voluntary dream reports and possibly capability-limited psychic observations.

Phase 4 resolves the immediate cause or combination of causes while preserving unanswered secondary phenomena for later years.

No Legendary is required. If one ever becomes relevant, that is a separate canon decision.

# Longer arc B — The City That Remembers Wrong

Multiple dreamers describe architectural features from a neighborhood demolished generations ago. Public history says the area was abandoned peacefully. Archaeological and archival evidence begins to show a more complicated transition.

Dreams provide leads and emotional perspectives. They do not replace records, excavation or testimony.

The final question is less “which dream was true?” and more “how did different communities remember the same change, and what physical evidence survives?”

# Longer arc C — The Consent Accord

A serious psychic incident causes public argument about telepathy, Aura sensing and mental privacy.

Different groups propose norms for opt-in contact, emergency exceptions, research access and publication of psychic evidence.

The arc connects civic governance, cases, clubs, care, research and media. The system does not import modern legal doctrine automatically. Ouros-specific policy remains a canon-authored outcome.

The current safe baseline is ordinary static combat using VERIFIED targeting, base movement, core calculations and action economy/initiative. Full psychic Trainer Feature use remains blocked in Java. Sleep/status behavior, dream-related Moves, Abilities and Items remain PARTIAL according to live port evidence. Dynamic dream terrain, non-DEFEAT tactical policy and Minecraft private/shared dream presentation remain BLOCKING.
