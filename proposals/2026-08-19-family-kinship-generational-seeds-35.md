# Ouros Family, Kinship & Generational Seeds — Pass 35

Status: NON-CANON proposals. All names, institutions and outcomes are placeholders until reviewed.

These seeds are original structures derived from research themes. They do not copy external characters or plots.

## 1. The Name on the Gym Wall

A young Trainer arrives in a town where an older relative once held a respected League role. Locals assume they want the same career. The character may embrace, reject or ignore that expectation.

## 2. Two Siblings, Two Institutions

Two NPC siblings work in rival institutions: one in conservation, one in infrastructure. They care about the same watershed but disagree on priorities. Kinship does not collapse their objectives into one family agenda.

## 3. The Family Archive Has Three Versions

An old family archive contains three incompatible accounts of the same historical event. Public memory treats one as authoritative. Players can compare provenance rather than choosing the most dramatic version.

## 4. The Workshop Successor

A famous artisan retires. Their child is competent but uninterested; an apprentice wants the role; a distant relative owns part of the building. The institution must resolve succession through its own governance rather than bloodline default.

## 5. Reunion During Migration Season

A family reunion is scheduled at the same time as a major Pokémon migration. The hook is travel, accommodation, observation and ordinary logistical pressure, not catastrophe.

## 6. The Relative Nobody Recognizes

An NPC is related to a famous regional figure but uses a different surname and has no public career. The connection matters only when it is explicitly revealed.

## 7. Shared Parent, Different History

Two adult siblings remember their childhood town differently because they left at different ages. Their statements become separate evidence in an investigation of local history.

## 8. The Long-Lived Partner

A Pokémon once partnered with a deceased historical Trainer still lives near the old homestead. It does not automatically obey or belong to descendants. Players must treat it as its own persistent entity.

## 9. Old Badge, New Generation

A family displays an ancestor's League Badge and battle records. A younger member is proud of the archive but has never challenged a Gym. Public recognition must not become personal qualification.

## 10. The Guest Room That Became Permanent

A relative originally visiting for a season stays for years. Household state changes without the system inventing ownership, dependency or guardianship.

## 11. Conflicting Care Plans

Several relatives help an elderly NPC in different ways. The problem is coordination of transport, clinic visits and work schedules. No villain is needed.

## 12. A Child of Two Regions

An NPC has close family in two regions and feels connected to both. Interregional records, travel and cultural familiarity differ by place; the system does not force one region to be their "true" home.

## 13. The Family Business That Outgrew the Family

A small ferry service founded by one household now employs dozens of unrelated workers. Descendants retain symbolic prestige but no longer control daily operations.

## 14. Sibling Rivalry Without Hostility

Two siblings enter the same competitive circuit. They compare records and occasionally battle, but support each other outside competition. Rivalry state and family state remain separate.

## 15. The Inherited Map

A player receives an old route map from a relative. The map has provenance and emotional significance but is physically outdated. Following it reveals how the landscape changed.

## 16. Family Recipe, Public Restaurant

Several relatives disagree over which version of a recipe is traditional. Research shows all versions descend from different historical adaptations. The food layer handles production; the family layer handles provenance and claims.

## 17. The Missing Generation

A town has excellent records about a famous founder and modern descendants but almost nothing about the generation between them. The gap resulted from an archive relocation, not a conspiracy.

## 18. An Adopted Name

An NPC publicly uses a family name acquired through adoption. The world must not treat biological ancestry and family membership as interchangeable.

## 19. Guardian in Practice, Status Unknown

An older sibling has provided day-to-day care for years. Whether this creates formal guardianship remains unresolved until Ouros civic canon defines such status.

## 20. The Relative on the Other Side

Two relatives join opposing factions for sincere reasons. They may cooperate during emergencies while remaining politically opposed afterward.

## 21. Anniversary Without Consensus

A family-linked institution celebrates its founding date, but surviving records disagree on when the institution actually began. The event proceeds while historians investigate.

## 22. The Heirloom Nobody Owns Clearly

A historically important item has passed informally among relatives for decades. Custody is clear; ownership is not. Players can preserve, exhibit, research or help resolve the claim without treating possession as title.

## 23. Former PC Family Hook — Consent Locked

A future-generation NPC claims relation to a retired PC. This seed remains unusable until the original player's explicit approval defines the relationship and intervening life events.

## 24. Family Photo, Wrong Caption

A museum displays an old photograph with a confident caption identifying several relatives. A newly found source suggests two people were misidentified. Correcting the label changes public memory, not the photograph itself.

## 25. The Younger Relative's First Expedition

An experienced NPC accompanies a younger relative on their first field assignment. The older character can advise but cannot resolve every obstacle without erasing the younger person's agency.

## 26. Homecoming After Institutional Change

A character returns to a family-linked town after years away. The old Gym, clinic, workshop or school has changed staff and function. Personal nostalgia does not freeze world state.

## 27. Family Rumor Becomes Regional Myth

A small household story has been retold for generations until the public version contains supernatural claims. The archive, myth and public-memory layers can disentangle story lineage without declaring the family dishonest.

## 28. The Inherited Obligation That Was Never Formal

A descendant believes they must continue an ancestor's promise. Research shows the promise was personal, not legally or institutionally binding. The character decides what it means now.

## 29. Shared Pokémon Care, Separate Ownership

Several relatives help care for one Trainer's Pokémon while the Trainer travels. Care history must not change ownership or Loyalty by itself.

## 30. Family Event as Social Hub

A large celebration brings together relatives, neighbors, former coworkers, rivals and Pokémon partners from several systems. Different players receive hooks through food, performance, battle exhibition, research, travel or ordinary conversation.

# Longer Arc A — The Second Generation

Years after an earlier regional arc, a new group of PCs grows up among monuments, institutions and ecological changes created by the prior generation. Their story focuses on living with consequences rather than reenacting old victories.

Arc beats:
1. establish which earlier facts are public, private or mythologized;
2. show tangible benefits and unintended costs;
3. introduce descendants/relatives only where consent and canon allow;
4. let new PCs form identities independent of inherited reputations;
5. resolve at least one old problem differently from the prior generation.

# Longer Arc B — House of Many Paths

A well-known family is spread across League, science, conservation, business and civic institutions. A regional crisis exposes their disagreements. Outsiders assume the family acts as one bloc, but each member has independent goals and knowledge.

The arc is about institutions, public expectation and personal choice, not uncovering a secret evil family.

# Longer Arc C — The Archive and the Living

A family archive becomes crucial to a present-day investigation. Some records are public, some private, some incorrect and some emotionally significant. Players must work with living family members rather than treating the archive as loot.

Possible outcomes include restricted publication, corrected public memory, preservation without disclosure, or new research questions.

# Promotion guardrails

None of these seeds become canon automatically. Any PC-related family relation requires explicit player consent. Any inheritance, guardianship, property or custody consequence must use the relevant Ouros system once canon defines it. No proposal grants mechanical benefits because of bloodline, surname or family fame.
