# Ouros Community Health Surveillance & Cluster Investigation Seeds — Pass 117

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. These are story candidates only. They do not establish diseases, institutions, mechanics, laws or historical events in Ouros.

## Design rule

Every seed begins from bounded observations. A cluster may be infectious, noninfectious, several unrelated events, an operational failure, a product problem, an ecological clue or remain unresolved.

No Pokémon species, Type, Move, Ability, Item or Trainer Feature gains medical meaning from flavor alone.

## The Clinic Has Six, the Market Has Four

Three local services report apparently different counts of the same problem. The discrepancy looks suspicious until investigators discover that each source is using a different time window and inclusion rule.

Useful play:

- retrieve dated aggregate records;
- compare working definitions;
- reconstruct when each institution learned what;
- issue a correction that explains the scope change without exposing patient identities.

Possible consequence:

The town creates a shared reporting vocabulary while institutions retain separate records and authority.

No battle required.

## The Pokémon Left Before Anyone Felt Sick

A familiar group of wild Pokémon stopped visiting a communal water or feeding area days before several local health reports appeared.

Competing hypotheses:

- the Pokémon reacted to the same environmental change;
- their movement was seasonal or unrelated;
- infrastructure work displaced them;
- the health reports have another cause entirely;
- later rumor incorrectly treats the species as an infallible warning system.

Investigation handoffs may involve Ecology, Water, Pollution, Care and Public Notices.

Canon guardrail:

No disease sensing, prediction or immunity is granted to the species.

## The Temporary Desk Never Closed

During an old cluster investigation, two clinics created a temporary joint information desk in a community hall. The event closed, but residents kept using the desk because it made referrals and service navigation easier.

Years later the desk is a permanent local institution with staff who remember the old investigation differently.

Narrative value:

A temporary response creates durable social infrastructure without requiring the original cause to remain relevant.

## The Same Building, a Different Cause

A new cluster appears in a building associated with an older health event. Residents immediately revive the old explanation.

Evidence can instead support:

- a different source;
- an unrelated set of cases;
- a changed building layout that makes old maps misleading;
- a monitoring gap;
- a new tenant or activity;
- no common cause at all.

The old investigation file remains useful because it preserves renovation history and prior observations, not because history must repeat itself.

## The Batch Everyone Blamed

Several affected actors bought the same product, so the product becomes the dominant rumor.

Batch Traceability later shows that:

- some affected actors received different production lots;
- several unaffected actors used the suspected lot;
- one early report never used the product;
- the apparent link came from a shared event or location instead.

Alternative ending:

The product remains a plausible contributor for only a subset of cases and the original cluster splits into two investigations.

The health layer never performs the recall itself.

## No Reports Between Midnight and Dawn

A dashboard appears to show the cluster abruptly ending overnight.

Investigation reveals that the reporting link was offline during the exact same window.

Quest material:

- recover local records;
- determine the outage interval;
- reconcile delayed reports;
- correct the timeline;
- preserve the original dashboard snapshot as historical evidence rather than deleting it.

The mystery is provenance, not sabotage unless separate evidence supports sabotage.

## The Center Saw a Pattern First

A Pokémon care facility notices several similar observations before any other institution realizes they may be related.

The facility can legitimately create an aggregate signal while keeping patient records protected.

Possible story branches:

- the signal matches a wildlife observation elsewhere;
- it maps to a shared route;
- it disappears when the working definition becomes more specific;
- it identifies a service problem rather than disease;
- no sufficient common cause is found.

Mechanical boundary:

Care observations do not create status conditions or diagnoses by narrative inference.

## The Notice Was Correct Yesterday

A public advisory from the previous day remains posted after the investigation scope changes.

Nobody forged anything. The notice accurately describes the evidence available when it was issued.

Player-facing problem:

- find which notices are still active;
- identify superseded versions;
- help Communications publish a correction;
- explain why older screenshots still circulate;
- preserve a public archive of the revision history.

This seed reinforces versioned truth instead of binary true/false documents.

## The School Closed for the Wrong Reason, but the Closure Helped

Status: requires canon review because school institutions and public-health authority vary by region.

A local institution temporarily suspends an activity under uncertainty. Later evidence weakens the original suspected source.

The temporary action still changed contact patterns, schedules or service demand in a useful way.

Narrative question:

How does the community evaluate a precaution that was reasonable under uncertainty even though the original hypothesis was not confirmed?

No automatic reputation score should decide the answer.

## The Wild Clinic and the Human Clinic Do Not Use the Same Words

Two institutions describe what appears to be the same regional pattern using different observation vocabularies.

One records feeding, grooming, mobility and behavior. The other uses human care observations. Neither can simply translate its records into the other's categories.

Useful structure:

Players help build a shared timeline and location map without forcing equivalence between symptoms across species.

Potential outcome:

A common environmental exposure becomes plausible, or the two patterns remain unrelated.

## The Route Is the Only Thing in Common

Affected actors have no shared workplace, food, household or product. Their only obvious overlap is a travel corridor.

Possible hypotheses:

- a location-specific environmental exposure;
- one stop along the route;
- a recurring event held near the corridor;
- reporting bias because one clinic serves that route;
- coincidence.

This can pull Roads, Transit, Water, Pollution, Workplace or Ecology into the investigation without letting the health layer decide their truth.

## The Pokémon Nobody Asked About

A long-running cluster investigation focuses on human reports. Months later a wildlife researcher notices archived observations from persistent Pokémon actors in the same area.

The observation does not prove cross-species transmission.

It may instead reveal:

- shared environmental timing;
- displacement;
- different exposure pathways;
- unrelated seasonal behavior;
- a previously unnoticed infrastructure event.

The value comes from connecting archives, not granting Pokémon a diagnostic role.

## Five Definitions, One Investigation

Mystery structure.

Definition v1 is intentionally broad to find possible cases.

Definition v2 excludes a common background presentation.

Definition v3 adds a location/time criterion.

Definition v4 splits Pokémon observations from human observations.

Definition v5 is the final historical summary.

NPCs quote different counts because each remembers a different revision.

Resolution requires identifying which version each claim used.

No character needs to be lying.

## Three Clusters That Became Two

An investigation begins with three geographic clusters.

Later chronology shows:

- two locations share a common event and merge;
- the third has a different cause or remains unresolved.

This creates a graph that changes shape over time while preserving every prior scope revision.

## The Field Tent Became a Research Station

A temporary observation site is installed during an investigation. After closure, its location proves valuable for unrelated ecological and community monitoring.

Over several seasons it becomes a small permanent station.

Long-term hooks:

- old equipment in storage;
- local volunteers who trained there;
- an old access path now used by hikers;
- a recurring Pokémon that learned the site's routine;
- archived measurements relevant to a new mystery.

## The Clinic Ledger That Changed the Map

Exploration concept.

An old clinic aggregate register lists neighborhood aliases that no longer appear on current maps. Cross-referencing it with a market calendar and route timetable reveals that affected actors overlapped at a temporary event ground later redeveloped.

Current executable version:

- explore archives and current locations;
- inspect old signs and photographs;
- compare effective dates;
- interview actors;
- identify the former event footprint.

No disease mechanics required.

Future richer version could add a separate tactical incident around one archive/service location, but the health mystery itself remains world-state investigation.

## Clinic Annex Access Perimeter

Encounter proposal.

Premise:

An unrelated battle threat blocks an external service entrance while the clinic is under increased demand.

Full version wants:

- protected staff withdrawal;
- clear-route objective;
- reactions/interception;
- temporary access lanes;
- tactical policy that understands service access rather than pure KO optimization;

## Records Transfer Diversion

Encounter proposal.

Premise:

A courier carrying protected records or an authored scientific sample must pause because a battle threat blocks a route.

Full version wants ESCAPE/PROTECT/CLEAR_ROUTE behavior, multiple paths, Intercept and custody-aware semantic playback.

Reduced version:

The protected material is handed to a secure off-grid custodian before combat. The tactical fight only clears the route. Courier/Science/Cold Chain decides the later transfer and condition record.

## A Town Learns How to Compare Notes

Long-term arc.

Season 1 establishes ordinary routines at clinics, markets, workplaces, schools or analogous institutions, transport nodes and wildlife sites.

A small number of health observations appear but remain local.

A legitimate pattern causes several institutions to compare aggregate evidence. The first working definition is intentionally broad. Players can contribute through records, field checks, route reconstruction, service restoration or social scenes rather than combat alone.

Later evidence changes the scope. One prominent hypothesis weakens. Another owner system receives a stronger handoff. Temporary arrangements alter everyday routines.

The investigation eventually closes with one of several valid outcomes: supported common source, multiple causes, no common cause or insufficient evidence.

Recovery removes temporary measures system by system. Some cross-institution relationships remain useful and become ordinary town infrastructure.

A later season introduces a new signal. Characters who remember the old event have better archives and relationships, but no automatic certainty that the same cause has returned.

## Faction and NPC archetypes

NON-CANON roles suitable for later generation:

The cautious aggregator: collects only enough information to show a pattern and refuses to overclaim.

The field verifier: knows locations and routines better than institutional records.

The privacy advocate: supports investigation while challenging unnecessary disclosure.

The revision keeper: maintains superseded definitions and notices so history remains intelligible.

The operations liaison: converts an investigation question into a bounded request for Water, Food, Facilities, Ecology or another owner system.

The rumor amplifier: repeats an early plausible explanation after evidence has moved on; this role does not need malicious intent.

The institutional skeptic: remembers a prior false association and demands stronger evidence this time.

The local bridge: trusted by communities that do not normally share records with the same institutions.

None of these archetypes automatically receives a mechanical Feature, Skill rank, legal mandate or social modifier.

- Who can create or receive aggregate health signals?
- How is privacy handled for Trainers, other humans and Pokémon?
- Can wild-Pokémon observations enter formal surveillance, and through whom?
- Are any conditions formally reportable?

- Are isolation/quarantine powers part of any region's institutions?
- How are public notices authorized and corrected?
- Which historical events are actually canon?
- What regional vocabulary is used instead of imported real-world public-health terminology?
