# Ouros Death, Loss, Mourning, and Memorial Seeds — Pass 154

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS
Canon effect: NONE
Pass: 154

These concepts are original Ouros candidates derived from the high-level structural lessons in the paired research file. Names, locations, institutions, customs, and characters are placeholders until reviewed against canon.

## Design goals

Use loss to create durable world continuity without making tragedy a mandatory content generator.

Preserve uncertainty when evidence is incomplete.

Allow memorial stories with no combat.

Keep survivors as independent actors.

Never use a tactical defeat, Minecraft despawn, visual corpse, grave marker, rumor, or public belief as automatic death authority.

## 1. The Marker With No Grave

Premise:

A roadside marker remembers a surveyor and their partner Pokémon lost decades ago during a winter crossing. A roadworks crew needs to move the marker several meters because the slope is failing.

Complication:

Local residents disagree over whether the marker indicates the actual resting place. The oldest surviving route plan shows the marker was installed after the road was widened once before.

Possible discoveries:

- the marker has already moved at least once;
- the original installation record says `memorial marker`, not `burial site`;
- an old photograph shows a different alignment;
- a family story places the final camp farther uphill;
- no source currently confirms where remains were located.

Possible outcomes:

- relocate the marker with a complete provenance record;
- stabilize the slope and leave it;
- install a temporary marker while the original is restored;
- revise public signage to distinguish memorial from grave;
- leave the historical location question unresolved.

Combat:

None required.

## 2. The Bell Nobody Rings Alone

Premise:

A small hill settlement has a remembrance bell used after the loss of local working Pokémon and community members. The bell has developed a crack and is unsafe to use.

The interesting problem:

Three groups want different repairs.

- older residents want the original bell preserved;
- younger volunteers want a safe replacement that sounds different;
- a metalworker proposes preserving the old bell as an archive object and casting a new one.

World-state hooks:

- material provenance;
- community meeting;
- workshop capacity;
- transport of the bell;
- local tradition variant;
- public memory.

Potential resolution:

The tradition can continue even if the physical object changes. The old bell's history remains.

No mechanical blessing or buff is attached to ringing.

## 3. The Pokémon That Waits at Closing Time

Premise:

A surviving Pokémon returns every few days to the shuttered workshop of its deceased Trainer.

Important boundary:

The Pokémon's behavior is observed. Its private emotion is not assigned by the system.

Possible player actions:

- learn who currently maintains the building;
- arrange food/water without claiming ownership;
- locate the Trainer's former coworkers;
- retrieve a care record if access is lawful;
- identify whether the Pokémon has another current caretaker;
- help reopen a side room used for shelter;
- leave the Pokémon alone while continuing observation.

Long-term consequences:

The Pokémon may later choose a different routine, remain linked to the workshop, join an existing caretaker through an explicit event, or move elsewhere.

Forbidden shortcut:

The player cannot catch or inherit the Pokémon merely because its Trainer died.

## 4. The Memorial Everyone Thinks Proves the Accident

Premise:

A stone monument names five members of an old expedition and states they were lost during a bridge collapse.

Research reveals:

- the monument was erected eighteen years after the expedition;
- the collapse was public knowledge;
- only three members were directly confirmed at the bridge;
- one had separated from the group the previous day;
- one person's final location remains unknown.

Mystery structure:

The monument is evidence of public memory and commemoration, not a perfect incident report.

Possible resolution:

The bridge collapse still happened. The inscription may be historically overbroad. A correction can be proposed without disrespecting the memorialized group.

## 5. The Flowers Keep Moving

Premise:

Fresh flowers repeatedly appear at an old Pokémon memorial, but the caretaker never sees who leaves them.

Possible explanations:

- a former Trainer visits before dawn;
- several unrelated people use the same memorial for private remembrance;
- a nearby school has adopted maintenance of the site;
- a delivery worker leaves flowers on behalf of clients;
- one set of flowers is actually part of a local seasonal practice.

Anti-twist rule:

No supernatural answer is generated unless canon already supports one.

This is primarily an observation/provenance mystery.

## 6. Two Names on One Collar

Premise:

A preserved collar in a community archive is labeled as belonging to a famous rescue Pokémon. A newly donated photograph shows the same collar being worn by a different Pokémon years earlier.

Potential explanations:

- the collar was reused;
- the archive label is incomplete;
- the object was inherited within a working team;
- the photograph date is wrong;
- the famous Pokémon's memorial story simplified the object's earlier history.

No outcome needs fraud.

The object can legitimately have several association periods.

## 7. The Trainer Who Came Back

Premise:

A memorial plaque was installed for a Trainer presumed dead after a remote expedition. Months later the Trainer returns alive after having been stranded beyond communications coverage.

Persistent consequences:

- the plaque remains a real historical object;
- public notices need correction;
- insurance/legal questions remain out of scope until canon defines them;
- coworkers have changed schedules and roles;
- a replacement expedition leader may now hold the position;
- family and friends have made choices during the presumed-loss period;
- the returned Trainer may or may not want the plaque removed.

Best outcome shape:

No reset. The world keeps the entire sequence.

## 8. The Garden for Pokémon Nobody Could Name

Premise:

A rescue organization maintains a small garden for wild Pokémon that died during a historical disaster and could not all be individually identified.

Current conflict:

A new ecological survey suggests the original estimated species list was partly wrong.

Choices:

- update the educational display;
- preserve the original list as a dated historical document and add current evidence;
- leave some entries as unknown species;
- separate confirmed records from later community recollection.

Theme:

Respect and epistemic humility can coexist.

## 9. The Last Delivery

Premise:

A courier finds a package addressed to an NPC who has recently died. The sender had no knowledge of the death.

The package is not automatically inherited by anyone.

Quest lanes:

- return to sender;
- hold through the courier system according to future canon policy;
- contact an explicitly authorized recipient if one exists;
- investigate whether the address belongs to an institution rather than the person;
- preserve chain of custody.

Potential emotional content comes from actor choices, not system labels.

## 10. The Empty Seat at the Dojo

Premise:

A long-serving instructor dies between seasons. The dojo's schedule still contains their weekly class.

System consequences:

- recurring schedule requires update;
- mentorship links become historical;
- current students need reassignment decisions;
- the organization chooses whether to continue, rename, retire, or restructure the class;
- personal teaching notes may enter archives if custody permits.

No automatic successor is chosen based on friendship, combat strength, or seniority unless canon says so.

## 11. The Route That Became a Memorial

Premise:

After a fatal historical accident, travelers began leaving small stones at a safe turnout overlooking the old route. Decades later the route itself closes for habitat restoration.

Question:

Does the memorial practice move to the new road, remain reachable only by foot, or become archival?

Connections:

- Travel;
- Conservation;
- Tradition;
- Public Memory;
- Accessibility;
- Cartography.

No supernatural property attaches to the stones.

## 12. The Photograph Taken After the Funeral

Premise:

An archive photograph is dated three days after an NPC's recorded funeral, and the person appears to be visible in the background.

Possible explanations:

- photograph date was entered incorrectly;
- funeral date was copied from an announcement rather than the held event;
- the figure is another person;
- the photograph was reproduced later and its print date confused with exposure date;
- death record or identity requires review.

Investigation rule:

A visual resemblance is a lead. It is not resurrection proof.

## 13. The Rival's Old Partner

Premise:

A recurring competitive NPC has an old memorial card for a Pokémon that died before the player met them.

Use:

The player can learn about the earlier partnership through ordinary conversation, a tournament archive, or a memorial visit. The rival's current style may have changed after the loss, but only authored observed behavior establishes that relationship.

Avoid:

- hidden grief debuffs;
- sudden supernatural return;
- using the deceased Pokémon as a cheap motivation reveal;
- forcing the player into counseling dialogue.

Long payoff:

Years later, the rival may coach a young Trainer whose Pokémon uses a tactic associated publicly with the old partner. This is a memory callback, not reincarnation unless canon explicitly establishes it.

## 14. The Care Case With No Final Page

Premise:

A historical clinic file ends during an emergency evacuation. Local tradition says the patient Pokémon died that night.

Evidence:

- admission record exists;
- treatment notes stop abruptly;
- evacuation list contains a similar species but no persistent identifier;
- a later memorial names the Pokémon;
- no direct confirmation record has yet been found.

Correct state:

`DEATH_REPORTED`

Potentially:

`MEMORIALIZED_WHILE_STATUS_UNRESOLVED`

Do not promote to `DEATH_CONFIRMED` without authority.

## 15. The Memorial Was Vandalized Twice

Premise:

A memorial plaque is damaged. Public discussion immediately blames the same faction accused of an earlier attack years ago.

Investigation finds:

- current tool marks differ from the previous incident;
- several witnesses repeat the same online claim;
- camera coverage does not include the actual plaque;
- a later storm also caused damage.

The mystery can end with:

- current vandal identified;
- accident identified;
- mixed causes;
- unresolved perpetrator;
- public rumor corrected or still persistent.

Combat is optional and should not authenticate the accusation.

## 16. The Service Pokémon With Three Caretakers

Premise:

A deceased worker had a Pokémon partner that also worked with a municipal team. Three parties now claim responsibility for its care:

- a family member;
- a coworker;
- the institution.

The Pokémon's own observed preferences and existing custody records matter, but Pass 154 creates no universal ownership law.

Quest focus:

Resolve current safe care and document claims without letting the player seize the Pokémon as a quest reward.

## 17. The Memorial Ledger Has Six Versions

Premise:

A town has moved its burial/memorial records through six institutions over a century.

Each ledger preserves different identifiers:

- nickname only;
- species plus Trainer;
- family name;
- marker number;
- photograph;
- modern persistent ID for recent entries.

Adventure:

Players help reconcile which marker corresponds to an old requested restoration.

Possible result:

`RELATIONSHIP_UNCERTAIN` remains correct for one or more records.

## 18. The Pokémon That Inherited Nothing

Premise:

Public rumor says a wealthy Trainer “left everything to their Pokémon.”

The actual known facts are narrower:

- the Pokémon still lives at the property;
- staff continue caring for it;
- a public statement said its care would continue;
- legal ownership of the property is outside current canon.

This seed exists specifically to prevent the narrative system from converting colorful public language into a legal fact.

## 19. The Expedition Memorial Without a Final Battle

Premise:

A long arc investigates why a historical expedition disappeared.

Potential final answers:

- weather and route choices;
- equipment failure;
- environmental hazard;
- faction interference;
- split party;
- several causes;
- partial explanation with one missing member unresolved.

The finale can be archival, archaeological, environmental, or social. A boss fight is optional.

## 20. The Memorial Tournament

Premise:

A local battle event is held annually in memory of a former Trainer.

Separation:

- Battle Institutions owns match contracts/results;
- Tradition/Public Memory owns the recurring event meaning;
- Pass 154 links the event to a memorial subject;
- no mechanical advantage is granted for “fighting in their spirit.”

Complication:

A new organizer wants to change the format, and some participants claim the old format was part of the deceased Trainer's wishes. Archive evidence is incomplete.

The conflict can resolve through records, discussion, and institutional decision.

## 21. The Name Added Too Soon

Premise:

After a disaster, a public temporary memorial includes a missing person's name before death is confirmed.

Weeks later the person is found alive.

Persistent history:

- temporary memorial episode remains;
- correction event occurs;
- news/media records preserve both states;
- the recovered person's later choices remain theirs;
- no one needs to have acted maliciously.

## 22. The Smallest Memorial in the Region

Premise:

An elderly NPC maintains a single weathered tag tied to a fence post for a Pokémon partner that died many years ago.

The tag has no tourist significance and no public institution behind it.

Adventure options:

- replace the string;
- preserve the original tag in a box and install a replica;
- photograph it for the family archive;
- leave it untouched;
- hear one short story and move on.

Purpose:

Ouros should support intimate continuity at the same level as monumental lore.

## 23. Five Times Someone Said “They Died There”

Mystery/provenance seed.

Five sources use the same phrase for different claims:

1. last seen at the site;
2. death reported by a witness;
3. memorial installed there;
4. remains reportedly recovered nearby;
5. official record lists the site as place of death.

The investigation maps each statement to its actual evidence. They may converge, partially agree, or remain inconsistent.

## 24. Three Graves, Two Pokémon, One Nickname

Premise:

A common nickname appears on three old markers. Local lore assumes all three refer to one famous Pokémon.

Research may show:

- two different Pokémon shared the nickname;
- one marker commemorates a later working team named after the original;
- one date was misread;
- the legend compressed multiple lives into one story.

No villain required.

## 25. The Memorial Garden After the Flood

Premise:

Flood damage displaces stones, destroys labels, and makes paths unsafe.

Full story lanes:

- crisis response;
- conservation;
- archive reconciliation;
- material restoration;
- visitor access;

No tactical combat is required.

- abilities — PARTIAL, individual audit required

- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL, individual audit required
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support — BLOCKING

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version:

- memorial objects remain outside BattleSpec;
- any remains/custody state remains frozen outside BattleSpec;

## Encounter contract B — Cemetery Visitor Withdrawal Perimeter

Intended full version:

Visitors are moving toward exits while hostile combatants create pressure, route denial, Intercept opportunities, and potentially hazards.

Full dependencies:

- complete movement — PARTIAL;
- lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- reactions/zones — BLOCKING if used;
- tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Full status: BLOCKED.

Reduced version:

Ouros first resolves visitor extraction only when it can do so from verified world facts without tactical simulation. The BattleSpec then contains only actual remaining combatants and static geometry.

Allowed result:
`IMMEDIATE_CEMETERY_PERIMETER_CLEAR`.

Forbidden inferences:

- `ALL_VISITORS_SURVIVED_PREVIOUS_INCIDENT`;
- `DEATH_CONFIRMED`;
- `MEMORIAL_PROTECTED_FOREVER`.

Reduced status: READY when pre-battle extraction is valid.

## Encounter contract C — Memorial Archive Recovery Chokepoint

Intended full version:

A fixed archive approach is occupied. Different actors may want to delay, capture, destroy, steal, or escape with records.

Full status: BLOCKED because semantic object-carrying/protection and objective-aware tactical policy are not globally verified.

Reduced version:

The records remain outside BattleSpec. The battle clears only the immediate physical approach.

Allowed result:
`IMMEDIATE_ARCHIVE_APPROACH_CLEAR`.

Archives/Investigation then determines document custody and meaning.

Reduced status: READY.

## Encounter contract D — Survivor Rescue From Memorial-Site Incident

Intended full version:

A living survivor must be reached or extracted under ongoing dynamic danger.

Full status: BLOCKED if it requires escort, rescue movement, dynamic hazards, reactions, or objective-aware AI.

Reduced version:

Use only when Ouros can validly place the survivor outside the tactical slice before initiative. Then resolve a static confrontation.

If safe extraction cannot be represented without missing mechanics, the encounter remains blocked.

This rule prevents battle failure from silently becoming a death generator.

## Long arc — The Names That Stayed After the Road Moved

Act 1:

The player repeatedly passes a small cluster of markers beside an old road. They are ordinary scenery with occasional visitors.

Act 2:

A transport upgrade and slope-stability project proposes moving the road and several markers. Archive records are incomplete.

Act 3:

Players can help reconcile records, speak with current communities, inspect prior relocation evidence, and identify which markers are symbolic.

Act 4:

A severe weather event accelerates the decision. The challenge is practical rather than metaphysical: access, documentation, material preservation, safe routes, and continuity of records.

Act 5:

The new road opens. Some markers move, some remain, one is replaced, and a digital/public archive records the old alignment. Several historical questions remain uncertain.

Months later:

Visitors use both the new memorial area and the old overlook for different reasons. The world remembers the transition.

Potential combat:

None required. A reduced static perimeter encounter may occur if an independently motivated antagonist or wild threat intersects the project.

## Long NPC arc — The Partner Who Kept Working

Start:

A public-service Trainer dies through an authored canon event outside this proposal. Their Pokémon partner survives.

Phase 1:

The Pokémon remains under safe temporary care while custody and work roles are reviewed.

Phase 2:

Coworkers disagree about whether the Pokémon should return to service. The Pokémon's observed choices matter. No human gets automatic ownership.

Phase 3:

The Pokémon may:

- continue with the institution through an explicit new relationship;
- retire to a sanctuary/household;
- work in a changed capacity;
- stop participating;
- remain temporarily undecided.

Phase 4:

A memorial event occurs months later. The surviving Pokémon may attend or not based on current World Agency state.

The arc focuses on continuity and agency rather than replacing the deceased Trainer.

## Long mystery — The Expedition With Four Endings

A historic expedition has four commonly repeated endings:

- killed by a storm;
- attacked by wild Pokémon;
- betrayed by a guide;
- vanished after discovering a ruin.

The player can eventually establish:

- when each version first appeared;
- which sources copied each other;
- where the team split;
- what weather occurred;
- which members were later seen;
- which memorials were built before reliable records surfaced.

A valid final answer may be:

- three deaths confirmed;
- one member found alive years later;
- one final location unresolved;
- no evidence for the betrayal story;
- ruin discovery supported but causally unrelated to the deaths.

The campaign gains closure without forcing certainty where none exists.

## Canon review checklist for any seed

Before promotion, confirm:

- source of any death fact;
- identity certainty;
- whether cause is known or only claimed;
- survivor state;
- Pokémon custody/ownership boundaries;
- memorial-site authority;
- local tradition status;
- public/private information scope;
- any relevant institutional procedure;
- all combat capability dependencies;
- reduced version when rich mechanics remain unavailable;
- no inferred supernatural effect;
- no imported copyrighted character/plot material.
