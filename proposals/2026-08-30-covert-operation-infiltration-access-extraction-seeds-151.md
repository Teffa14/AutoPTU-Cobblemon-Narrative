# Ouros Covert Operation, Infiltration, Access & Extraction Seeds — Pass 151

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. All names, organizations, sites and events below are candidates only.

## Design intent

These concepts exercise the covert-operation continuity layer while preserving Ouros authority boundaries. They emphasize conflicting objectives, scoped cover, observer-specific exposure, persistent access changes and aftermath.

No proposal establishes a universal police force, criminal law, disguise system, hacking system, stealth meter or security technology.

## 1. The Night Delivery

A public-facing facility receives legitimate late deliveries through a service entrance. A temporary organization using rooms deeper inside the building is believed to be moving records during the same window.

The player can enter through a genuine delivery relationship if current world state supports it, but that access only covers the loading area and designated corridors.

Possible objectives:

- verify which organization currently occupies the restricted rooms;
- observe where a specific container is taken;
- keep the actual delivery intact;
- avoid exposing the worker who provided scheduling information.

Persistent outcomes:

- service access remains legitimate but additional escorts are added;
- the target relocates;
- a worker remembers the player positively or negatively based on actual observed conduct;
- the container is later linked to another site.

## 2. The Badge That Still Works

A former organization left a building months ago. One old credential remains technically accepted by a door system, but the current occupant is a different successor or unrelated tenant.

The central question is not whether the door opens. It is what the credential now means.

Possible outcomes:

- the badge opens only an obsolete internal corridor;
- the player learns the building changed hands;
- the current staff treat the old badge as evidence of prior affiliation;
- the credential is finally revoked and becomes a historical object.

This seed should reference Organization Lineage and Material Culture.

## 3. The Meeting Above the Workshop

A legitimate workshop shares a building with a private upstairs meeting room used by several groups at different times.

A contact can provide lawful workshop access but cannot authorize entry upstairs.

Possible routes:

- observe arrivals from the public street;
- speak to the workshop staff;
- learn whether the same group returns regularly;
- enter upstairs only if another independently supported access path exists.

The scenario can resolve through information without combat.

## 4. The Wrong Branch

The party believes a site belongs to the main body of an adversarial faction. Operation evidence instead indicates a semi-independent branch with different leadership and methods.

The operation's local success can therefore change the campaign without proving anything about the parent organization.

Possible payoff:

- a presumed antagonist becomes a potential witness;
- a previously minor lineage dispute becomes strategically important;
- the parent group denies responsibility truthfully or deceptively depending on authored state;
- public memory initially collapses both groups together.

## 5. The Alias Known by One Person

An NPC encountered during an earlier operation recognizes the player's old alias but never learned the real identity.

Years later, that NPC appears in another institution.

The reappearance tests observer-scoped cover state:

- the NPC may greet the alias warmly;
- another actor may overhear it;
- the old identity may become suspicious without being resolved;
- the player may choose to explain, preserve ambiguity or withdraw.

No automatic exposure occurs merely because the alias was spoken.

## 6. The Record Room With Two Objectives

A target archive contains one record the player needs to read and another record an allied faction wants removed.

Those are different objectives with different consequences.

Possible choices:

- inspect only;
- copy information where technology/canon allows;
- refuse removal;
- remove the second record and create a custody/evidence problem;
- discover that the requested record belongs to another institution entirely.

The scenario should make objective ownership visible in the operation ledger.

## 7. The Alarm Nobody Received

A local alert is triggered during an operation, but a communications outage already exists in world state.

The site itself reacts. Remote leadership does not automatically know why.

Afterward:

- local staff file reports;
- delayed messages reach other actors later;
- contradictory accounts create an investigation;
- the target organization's attention increases only when actual information arrives.

The scenario tests Communications integration and forbids tactical omniscience.

## 8. The Door Opened During the Fire Drill

A scheduled safety exercise temporarily changes normal circulation through a large institution.

The operation can use only changes that actually exist in authored event state.

The player may observe an otherwise hidden route while participating in the public exercise, but emergency access does not grant continuing authorization after the event.

This seed can later connect to building-safety continuity without importing real-world bypass procedures.

## 9. The Mobile Laboratory

A field laboratory relocates between research sites. The same organization, staff and equipment persist, but the physical layout changes.

An earlier operation provides useful knowledge about personnel and equipment identity but stale route information.

Possible objectives:

- verify whether a specific instrument traveled with the lab;
- learn who currently supervises it;
- establish whether the lab belongs to the same organizational branch;
- leave without disrupting legitimate research.

## 10. The Contact Who Will Introduce You, Nothing More

A recurring companion or professional contact agrees to introduce the player to a restricted institution under a legitimate pretext.

Their participation scope is explicit:

- they attend the introduction;
- they do not enter restricted rooms;
- they do not lie for the party;
- they do not become a combatant;
- they leave at an agreed point.

The seed tests Pass 150 companion continuity together with Pass 151 covert state.

## 11. The Extraction Without the Object

The party reaches the target object but learns that removing it would destroy provenance, trigger an unrelated crisis or harm an innocent custodian.

They can leave with information instead.

The operation can therefore be locally successful even though `OBJECTIVE_ITEM_IN_PLAYER_CUSTODY` never occurs.

This seed reinforces that observation, custody and extraction are separate.

## 12. The Object Without the Extraction

The reverse situation.

A specific item instance is acquired, but the planned route closes because of a world event or target reaction.

The tactical scene may clear an immediate exit corridor. Travel then determines whether the group actually reaches the rendezvous.

Battle victory cannot commit full extraction.

## 13. The Site That Wants to Be Found

An adversarial actor deliberately leaves evidence that points toward one facility because it wants observers to discover a controlled portion of its operation.

The site is real. The evidence is real. The conclusion it encourages is incomplete.

The party can detect the manipulation only from conflicts between evidence, organization lineage and actor knowledge.

Do not create a hidden mastermind retrospectively. The deception must already exist as an actor plan.

## 14. The Empty Headquarters

The party finally reaches the expected leadership site and finds it largely abandoned.

The operation has still produced useful state:

- departure timing;
- inventory traces;
- messages;
- staff recollections;
- evidence of which branch left;
- a possible destination.

The story advances without manufacturing a boss battle.

## 15. The Public Floor

A high-stakes target operates inside a building with a large public ground floor.

The player can enter legally and observe public behavior indefinitely. Restricted access is a separate problem.

The seed encourages social observation and information gathering before any infiltration attempt.

## 16. The Guard Who Is Not a Member

A contractor or unrelated security worker protects a location used by an adversarial organization.

Organization Lineage records that the worker is not a faction member.

Consequences differ if the player treats every person at the site as an enemy.

This seed is especially useful for preventing faction dungeons from becoming rooms full of morally interchangeable combatants.

## 17. The Evacuation Route

A crisis forces multiple occupants of a restricted site into a common evacuation path.

The player can learn identities, relationships and route structure during the event.

The crisis itself remains owned by the Crisis layer. The generator cannot create a fire, flood or other emergency merely because infiltration needs an opening.

## 18. The Shortcut They Remembered

Months after an operation, the party returns to the same facility for a completely different reason.

A previously opened internal shortcut may still exist, may now be closed, or may have become public circulation after renovation.

The outcome comes from Construction/Facility state, not from a permanent videogame unlock flag.

## 19. The Burned Credential

A valid credential is linked to suspicious activity and retired after the operation.

Later stories preserve:

- the physical old card as an item instance;
- the revocation event;
- the actor who remembers it;
- the reason given publicly;
- whether the holder's identity was ever confirmed.

The credential can become evidence or museum/archive material without becoming usable access.

## 20. The Quiet Success

No battle happens.

The party enters through valid scoped access, observes the needed event, leaves before conditions change and sends the information through a functioning channel.

The value of the mission lies in preparation, actor knowledge and restraint.

This seed should remain possible even when the engine eventually supports richer tactical stealth-adjacent encounters.

## Provenance mysteries

### Five Times Someone Said “They Got In”

Five records use the phrase differently:

- entered the public lobby;
- obtained service access;
- reached the restricted floor;
- reached the target room;
- extracted with the objective.

The mystery asks what each source actually establishes.

### The Alarm Before the Intrusion

An alert timestamp predates the suspected operation.

Possible explanations include a routine test, unrelated incident, clock mismatch, earlier actor, delayed logging or mistaken chronology. The system must not choose one without evidence.

### Three Badges, One Door

Three credentials are reported as having opened the same door at different times. Building and organization history later show that the access policy changed twice.

### The Person Everyone Remembers Wearing Red

Multiple witnesses remember a distinctive visual detail, but their accounts differ on face, voice, direction of travel and companion presence.

Public repetition can strengthen the cultural memory of the detail without increasing its evidentiary reliability automatically.

## Longer arc — The Operation That Changes What the Player Thinks the Organization Is

Stage 1:

A small information operation targets what appears to be a routine faction facility.

Stage 2:

The party discovers internal roles and branch boundaries that contradict public assumptions.

Stage 3:

A later operation has multiple competing objectives: preserve one source, identify leadership, recover evidence and avoid causing an unrelated institutional shutdown.

Stage 4:

Exposure produces adaptation rather than instant war. One branch tightens access. Another distances itself. A third uses the event for internal leverage.

Stage 5:

The player eventually understands that “the organization” was never a single synchronized mind.

Possible endings:

- one branch reforms;
- another dissolves;
- a successor emerges;
- a case becomes public;
- evidence remains insufficient for the strongest allegation;
- the player's former cover identity survives in public memory;
- the campaign moves on while old access changes remain visible in the world.

## Tactical encounter 1 — Record Room Exit Corridor

Full-version premise:

Combat begins after the party has already obtained information. Opponents try to block withdrawal while different actors value capture, delay or protection differently.

Full dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions if doors/zones change during combat;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

Ouros fixes the alert state and explicit combatant roster before initiative. Records and noncombatants leave BattleSpec. Geometry is static. AutoPTU may resolve an ordinary battle and return `IMMEDIATE_EXIT_ROUTE_CLEAR`. Extraction is evaluated afterward.

## Tactical encounter 2 — Loading Bay Observation Window

Full-version premise:

The party needs a short observation window while multiple actors move cargo and hostile combatants may contest lines, routes and access zones.

Full version: BLOCKED where it depends on dynamic objectives, moving noncombatants, zones/reactions and tactical AI.

Reduced version: READY.

The loading operation pauses before BattleSpec. Cargo and workers are removed from tactical state. AutoPTU resolves explicit combatants in fixed geometry. A reviewed result may be `OBSERVATION_WINDOW_CREATED`. The Observation layer then resolves what can actually be seen from the established world state.

## Tactical encounter 3 — Compromised Cover Perimeter

Full-version premise:

Some opponents suspect the player's cover while others do not. The party wants to withdraw without converting every observer into a hostile combatant.

Full version: BLOCKED because observer-relative suspicion and objective-aware tactical participation cannot be delegated to generic battle AI.

Reduced version: READY.

Ouros decides before BattleSpec exactly who becomes a combatant. Noncombatant observers remain outside tactical state. AutoPTU resolves only those explicit participants. Afterward, Communications and Exposure state determine what information spreads.

## Tactical encounter 4 — Mobile Site Boarding Chokepoint

Full-version premise:

A moving facility or vehicle creates a narrow boarding or exit contest.

Full version: BLOCKED if it requires moving-platform semantics, dynamic geometry, timed arrival, environmental displacement or tactical extraction AI.

Reduced version: READY.

The vehicle is stationary for the tactical slice. World state determines whether it is present and accessible. AutoPTU may clear an immediate approach. Travel/vehicle systems determine departure afterward.
