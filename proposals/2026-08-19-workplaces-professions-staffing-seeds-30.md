# Workplaces, Professions & Staffing Seeds — Pass 30

Status: NON-CANON Ouros candidates. Original concepts derived from high-level research patterns.

## 1. The Missing Morning Shift

A transport office opens late because three different staff absences overlap for unrelated reasons.

The initial rumor is sabotage.

The actual situation may involve ordinary reassignment, illness, training or a delayed inbound route.

Useful systems:
- staffing capacity
- communication
- transport
- public rumor
- work backlog

## 2. The Apprentice Who Knows the Old System

A junior worker is the only person who has recently practiced a legacy procedure because senior staff considered it obsolete.

When an older subsystem fails, that narrow training suddenly matters.

No free PTU Feature or Skill gain is implied.

## 3. Night Crew Ecology

Workers on a late shift consistently observe Pokémon behavior that daytime researchers have never recorded.

Their observations can create a scientific lead without turning workers into formal researchers.

## 4. The Reliable Substitute

An NPC repeatedly covers shifts across several services and becomes widely known without holding a prestigious title.

Their cross-institution knowledge eventually makes them valuable during a regional disruption.

## 5. Too Many Orders

A successful workshop receives more commissions than its staff can complete.

The problem is growth, not failure.

Players may encounter:
- delayed orders
- hiring needs
- training pressure
- supplier strain
- quality-control choices

## 6. Specialist Out of Town

A clinic, archive, laboratory or machine shop remains open but cannot provide one specialized service because the relevant worker is temporarily elsewhere.

The reduced service state should remain playable rather than closing the entire institution.

## 7. Two Jobs, One Person

An important NPC holds two legitimate roles whose schedules collide during a public event.

The conflict is about commitments, not deception.

## 8. Institutional Memory Gap

A long-serving worker retires or transfers after properly documented approval.

The manuals remain, but certain practical details were never written down.

The follow-up story asks whether the workplace trains replacements, redesigns the process or keeps calling the former worker.

## 9. The Pokémon That Stops Volunteering

An institutional Pokémon that previously joined a routine task begins avoiding the staging area.

Allowed observations:
- it does not approach;
- it leaves when equipment starts;
- it still behaves normally elsewhere.

Not allowed without evidence:
- it hates the job;
- it is abused;
- it is injured;
- it is afraid of a specific person.

## 10. Festival Staffing Pulse

A recurring festival temporarily pulls workers from restaurants, transport, media and public services.

The festival succeeds, but some ordinary services operate on reduced capacity.

This creates a believable region-wide demand pulse rather than random closures.

## 11. The Handoff Nobody Read Yet

A shift ends with a complete handoff note, but the incoming worker is immediately pulled into another incident.

The information exists.

It simply has not reached the relevant decision-maker yet.

## 12. Seasonal Crew

A route, farm, harbor or research station hires temporary workers during a predictable seasonal peak.

Returning seasonal staff can become recurring NPCs with their own histories.

## 13. The Vacant Role

A workplace has an official position that has remained unfilled for months.

Staff have informally redistributed its duties.

The vacancy itself becomes part of the institution's identity.

## 14. Training Day

A normally efficient service operates more slowly because experienced staff are training new workers.

The slowdown is a planned investment rather than a crisis.

## 15. Mutual Aid Roster

Several neighboring settlements maintain an informal list of workers who can temporarily reinforce another service after storms, festivals or equipment failures.

This can connect crisis, travel and public works systems.

## 16. The Former Gym Trainer

A retired or reassigned Gym Trainer now works in an unrelated profession.

Their battle history remains part of their past, but their present identity is not forced back into competition.

## 17. The Second Career

An NPC develops a new profession over several arcs because repeated events gave them experience, contacts and interest in another field.

The transition must be authored from accumulated state rather than generated from nowhere.

## 18. Workplace Rivalry Without Battle

Two teams compete over turnaround time, quality, public trust or innovation.

Their rivalry may remain friendly, tense or unresolved without becoming a Trainer battle.

## 19. The Unofficial Expert

A worker has no prestigious title but repeatedly solves a narrow problem because of long experience.

An institution may trust that expertise without the narrative engine inventing formal certification.

## 20. The Job Posting Nobody Wants

A legitimate but inconvenient role stays open because the location, schedule or conditions are unpopular.

The world should not invent a villain to explain the vacancy.

## 21. Worksite and Wild Route

A public maintenance crew must regularly work inside an active Pokémon habitat.

Over time the workers develop observed routines that reduce disturbance.

This can connect workplace culture with conservation without inventing mechanical bonuses.

## 22. The Overbooked Courier Network

A major event creates more delivery demand than the network can handle.

Priority decisions affect shops, clinics, researchers and private recipients differently.

The system records actual priorities rather than assuming all packages are equal.

## 23. The New Supervisor

A promotion changes authority and workload but does not automatically change friendships, competence or ideology.

Coworkers may react differently based on their existing relationship history.

## 24. The Field Assignment

A normally stationary worker joins an expedition because their expertise is required.

Their normal workplace temporarily loses capacity while the expedition gains an expert.

## 25. The Service That Quietly Improved

After several months of training, maintenance and better handoffs, a service develops fewer backlogs.

The improvement becomes visible through shorter queues and broader availability, not a magical settlement level-up.

## Long arc A — The People Who Keep the Harbor Running

A harbor begins as background infrastructure.

Across several arcs, players encounter dispatchers, mechanics, pilots, inspectors, warehouse staff, couriers and Pokémon partners.

A sequence of ordinary pressures exposes weak handoffs and specialist bottlenecks.

The eventual story is not about defeating a mastermind. It is about how an institution learns, trains replacements and becomes more resilient.

Possible lasting outcomes:
- cross-training program
- new deputy role
- revised emergency roster
- different service hours
- stronger mutual-aid ties
- one worker choosing a new career

## Long arc B — The Apprenticeship Year

Several junior workers enter different institutions during the same season.

Their trajectories diverge according to actual assignments, mentors, mistakes, successes and player interactions.

One may stay.
One may transfer.
One may discover another profession.
One may become technically capable but dislike the role.

No outcome is predetermined.

## Long arc C — The Regional Staffing Crunch

Several unrelated events create simultaneous demand:

- festival season;
- route repairs;
- migration surveys;
- clinic referrals;
- construction work;
- transport maintenance.

The region has enough people overall but not enough specialists in every place at once.

Players see institutions negotiate coverage, postpone low-priority work, call mutual aid and train temporary staff.

The arc tests networks and priorities rather than combat power.
