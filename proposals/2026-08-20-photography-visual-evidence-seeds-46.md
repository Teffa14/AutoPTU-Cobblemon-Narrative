# Photography & Visual Evidence Seeds — Pass 46

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros material derived from structural research only.

## Seed 1 — The Empty Crossing

A camera station beside a known migration corridor records nothing for several nights. The mystery begins only after the team verifies that the station was powered, aimed correctly and operating during the expected window.

Possible causes remain open: route change, weather, disturbance, seasonal timing, equipment bias or a real population shift.

## Seed 2 — Same Mark, Different Valley

Two research teams independently photograph a Pokémon with a distinctive visible mark in valleys far apart.

The system creates an identity-match hypothesis, not a confirmed persistent entity merge.

Future play can seek better angles, tagged records or direct observation.

## Seed 3 — The Cropped Headline

A newspaper publishes a legitimate photograph that was tightly cropped for layout. The crop removes a second actor who changes how readers interpret the event.

The image is not fake. The publication is not automatically malicious. Context was lost.

## Seed 4 — Winter Camera, Summer Map

A camera trap is recovered months later after seasonal access returns. Its stored images document a route that no longer exists in the current map edition.

This can connect cartography, seasonality and public works.

## Seed 5 — Photograph of a Building Nobody Remembers

An old family photograph shows part of a structure behind the subjects. Current maps contain no record of it.

Possible future directions:
- demolished service building;
- old transport stop;
- temporary festival structure;
- mislocated photograph;
- structure now hidden by landscape change.

## Seed 6 — Behavioral First

A field photographer records a wild Pokémon using an object in a way no local study has documented.

The observation becomes a research lead. It does not immediately create a new species capability or battle mechanic.

## Seed 7 — The Misclassified Night Frame

A low-light camera image is confidently classified as a rare species by several volunteers. A specialist notices one anatomical detail that challenges the identification.

The classification history stays preserved after correction.

## Seed 8 — Harbor Camera Blind Spot

A port authority relies on cameras for loading-area records. One angle has been blocked for weeks by temporary construction.

A later case contains a gap that looks suspicious until maintenance records explain the blind spot.

## Seed 9 — Photographer Rivalry

Two field photographers repeatedly document the same region with different methods.

One prioritizes patient distance and baseline coverage. The other pursues rare moments and close observations.

Their disagreement can remain professional and productive rather than becoming villainy.

## Seed 10 — The Uncaptioned Archive Box

A museum receives a box of historic photographs with dates but almost no labels.

Players can connect architecture, clothing, Pokémon presence, landscapes and public records to restore context.

## Seed 11 — The Photo That Arrived Before the Rumor

A remote station transmits one image successfully before communications fail. The image reaches researchers before public reports of the event reach the same region.

Knowledge spreads unevenly through the existing communications layer.

## Seed 12 — Camera Trap Disturbance

A study detects that Pokémon behavior near one station differs from behavior at control sites.

The research team must consider whether the monitoring equipment itself changed the behavior it was intended to observe.

## Seed 13 — Public Battle Archive

A League-adjacent institution maintains public photographs and authorized battle records from important matches.

Images preserve cultural memory. AutoPTU transcripts preserve mechanical truth.

A photo can become iconic without becoming the rules record.

## Seed 14 — The Wrong Date

A famous photograph has been displayed for decades with the wrong year.

Correcting the date changes which historical event it can plausibly depict but does not change the image itself.

## Seed 15 — Sensitive Nest Coordinates

A researcher captures strong visual evidence of a rare nesting site.

The research archive stores the original location. Public derivatives remove precise coordinates to reduce disturbance.

The redaction is documented, not hidden manipulation.

## Seed 16 — The Familiar Stranger

A released Pokémon is photographed near a settlement months later. Several residents believe it is a well-known former partner.

The agency layer requires corroboration before the system confirms identity.

## Seed 17 — Restoration Timelapse

A damaged wetland is photographed from the same marked point every month.

The resulting series becomes evidence for recovery, public memory and later scientific comparison.

A visually greener site does not automatically prove full ecological recovery.

## Seed 18 — The Missing Negative

An archive has several prints from a historical incident but lacks the original source record or capture metadata.

The images remain valuable, but their provenance quality is lower.

## Seed 19 — Festival Photo Wall

A recurring festival maintains a public wall of photographs from prior editions.

Players can see venue changes, former performers, retired staff, old fashions and recurring Pokémon.

The wall becomes a low-cost physical form of public memory.

## Seed 20 — Camera Station Adoption

A school or club adopts maintenance responsibility for one ecological camera station.

The story value comes from long-term data quality, training, handoffs and discoveries rather than daily chores.

## Seed 21 — Visual Alibi, Limited Scope

A case photograph proves that an actor was present at one place at one time.

It does not prove where that actor was before or after the frame, what they intended or what occurred outside the image.

## Seed 22 — Photo Exchange Between Regions

Two research institutions compare image archives of a migrating species.

Different camera methods and classification practices make the datasets initially difficult to combine.

The project becomes a scientific and interregional collaboration arc.

## Seed 23 — A Camera Damaged From Inside

A sealed observation enclosure contains a camera with damage on an unexpected side.

The visual-evidence system records the physical facts without deciding whether the cause was Pokémon behavior, equipment failure or human action.

## Seed 24 — The Photographer Who Never Battles

A recurring NPC builds regional importance through field documentation, publications, museum work and ecological discoveries.

Their career can intersect major arcs without requiring a combat build.

## Seed 25 — Before and After the New Road

Researchers possess visual records of wildlife use before a road opened. New images suggest changed movement patterns.

The project can feed civic review and conservation without pre-deciding whether the road was a mistake.

## Seed 26 — The Battle Photo Misread as Strategy

A public photograph appears to show a Trainer preparing a specific tactic.

A rival builds a plan around that assumption. The photo was real, but the tactical interpretation may be wrong.

AI opponents may only use information that their actor could plausibly access.

## Seed 27 — Annotated Copy Becomes Famous

A researcher marks arrows and notes on a copy of a field photograph. Years later, the annotated version is reproduced so often that the public begins treating the annotations as part of the original image.

Archive provenance can later separate them.

## Seed 28 — The Camera Network Goes Quiet

Several stations stop reporting at once.

The first investigation is technical: power, relay, weather, access and maintenance. Only then does the system escalate toward ecological or hostile explanations.

## Seed 29 — One Scene, Four Pictures

Four people photograph the same public incident from different positions.

Each image is authentic and incomplete.

The case or public-memory system can use all four without forcing one image to be the definitive story.

## Seed 30 — Photodocumentary of a Returning Species

A species absent from a restored area begins appearing again in scattered photographs across several seasons.

The long arc is built around accumulating confidence, not one dramatic frame.

## Long Arc A — The Liminal Archive

A regional archive gradually accumulates photographs from routes, festivals, battles, construction projects and ecological surveys.

Years later, researchers discover that several unrelated images contain the same background landmark in progressively different condition.

The landmark becomes a thread connecting settlement growth, habitat change and an old infrastructure project. The arc emerges from accumulated records rather than a pre-written conspiracy.

## Long Arc B — Eyes on the Corridor

A conservation program deploys a small camera network along a migration corridor.

Season 1 establishes baseline.

Season 2 produces missing detections.

Season 3 reveals route changes and pressure from new infrastructure.

Season 4 tests mitigation and records whether the animals use the restored path.

The campaign can involve science, public works, transport, wildlife, clubs and media with no required villain.

## Long Arc C — The Photograph Everyone Knows

A single historic image becomes one of the region's most reproduced cultural objects.

Different institutions use it for different stories: courage, disaster recovery, institutional prestige, family memory or regional identity.

Later archival work reveals that the common caption contains several errors. Correcting those errors creates debate without requiring the image to be forged.
