# Investigation Inference, Hypothesis & Revision Seeds — Pass 152

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS. Every named location, actor, institution, event and historical detail below requires later canon approval.

Date: 2026-08-30

## Design objective

These seeds exercise the proposed Investigation Inference & Hypothesis Revision layer. They are written to generate mysteries that can survive wrong theories, partial answers and later recontextualization.

No seed depends on a hidden “correct answer meter.” Evidence has provenance and scope. The server owns canonical world facts.

## The Footprints That Belong to Two Stories

A set of tracks crossing a service route appears to document one journey. Measurements of depth, overlap and weathering later support a different sequence: two passages happened at different times.

Initial plausible theories can include one traveler doubling back, two travelers moving together, or two unrelated crossings.

The later evidence resolves sequence more strongly than identity or motive.

Useful continuity writes:

- preserve the original track observation;
- preserve each theory proposed before the overlap was understood;
- add a recontextualization event rather than rewriting the first record;
- leave identity open unless independent material resolves it.

## Three Witnesses, One Exact Phrase

Three people in different places describe an event using nearly identical wording.

The tempting conclusion is coordinated deception. Another possibility is that all three heard an earlier public announcement. A third is that two copied one source while the remaining witness had independent knowledge.

The mystery shifts toward information lineage.

Resolution can establish a shared transmission source without resolving whether the underlying event claim is true.

## The Broken Gauge and the Correct Reading

An unusual measurement becomes controversial because a later inspection finds the instrument damaged.

The easy theory says the old reading was bad.

Calibration and maintenance provenance from Pass 145 may instead show that the device was within tolerance during the relevant observation window and was damaged later.

The instrument theory can be disproven narrowly while the cause of the unusual reading remains unresolved.

## The Map That Was Accurate Last Spring

A traveler follows an old route map and reaches a blocked or altered path.

Residents call the map wrong. An archived survey and seasonal records show that it accurately represented an earlier state.

The map becomes stale evidence, not false evidence.

This seed can connect Cartography, Weather, Infrastructure or ecological change without requiring any of them to be the explanation by default.

## The Badge in the River

A credential belonging to a known organization is recovered downstream from an incident site.

It establishes that the physical credential reached the water system.

It does not establish:

- that the listed holder was present;
- that the badge was valid at the time;
- that the organization authorized anyone;
- that the holder committed the incident;
- how the object entered the water.

The resulting investigation can branch into credential history, upstream access, lost-property records, organization lineage and water-flow evidence.

## The Battle Everyone Calls the Confession

An accused antagonist attacks or challenges the party shortly after an accusation becomes public.

Public memory quickly compresses the event into “they confessed by fighting.”

The Chronicle must preserve the actual sequence:

- accusation;
- knowledge received by the antagonist;
- confrontation;
- battle result;
- later interpretations.

Combat proves only what AutoPTU actually resolved. Motive and guilt remain separate questions.

## The Missing Page Is a Lead, Not an Answer

A copied report has one page absent.

NPCs immediately assume the missing page contained the decisive information.

The investigation can pursue edition history, circulation copies, archival custody, author correspondence and later references to the report.

Finding the page may reveal that it was mundane, while the reason it disappeared is still interesting. Alternatively, the page may remain lost and the case can still progress through other routes.

## The Rumor That Solves Location and Nothing Else

A long-running local rumor places an unusual Pokémon near a disused structure.

Independent observation confirms that the Pokémon was indeed there during one period.

That does not confirm the rumor's claims about why it was there, whether it caused nearby incidents or whether it still uses the location.

This seed directly tests narrow confirmation.

## The Empty Nest

An abandoned nesting site is interpreted as migration.

Later observations show the species uses the site only under a narrower seasonal or resource condition.

The absence becomes evidence about current use only after the observation window and detection assumptions are reviewed.

Possible final states include a seasonal explanation, disturbance, relocation, multiple contributing causes or accepted ambiguity.

## The Pokémon That Returned to the Wrong Place

A repeatedly observed Pokémon returns to a site that locals consider irrelevant.

Competing theories include territorial memory, food availability, nesting history, an old human relationship or a route used by a wider group.

The investigation should support several observation cycles. A single return does not choose the explanation.

The payoff can be ecological, social, archaeological or mundane.

## The Clock That Everyone Trusted

Several records agree that an event happened before another because all timestamps line up.

Later, maintenance records show the local clock was offset during that period.

The records themselves remain authentic. Their chronological interpretation changes.

This is a clean demonstration of an assumption record becoming challenged and then revised.

## The Photograph With the Right Person and the Wrong Date

A photograph clearly contains an identified person at a known site. The attached date comes from a later catalog entry rather than the original capture event.

Photography provenance can establish image content while archive provenance challenges the date.

Identity, location and time therefore resolve independently.

## The Door Was Locked From the Inside

Several witnesses repeat that a room was inaccessible because its door was locked from inside.

Inspection shows the mechanism can reach the same visible state from several causes: normal locking, mechanical failure or an earlier puzzle/mechanism state.

The locked state is evidence. The cause remains a question.

## Five Times Someone Said “We Know What Happened”

Five records describe the same investigation as solved:

1. a witness says they recognized someone;
2. an investigator says one theory now fits best;
3. an institution says it has enough information for a narrow operational decision;
4. a newspaper reports a simplified conclusion;
5. a later historian says the event is still disputed.

All five records can be authentic.

The mystery is understanding what “solved” meant in each context.

## Two Independent Reports That Were One Report

Two distant settlements submit similar descriptions of a threat.

At first this looks like independent corroboration.

Communications provenance reveals that both reports relied on the same courier's earlier account.

The event may still be real. The confidence based on source independence must be revised.

## The Correct Theory for the Wrong Reason

A player or NPC proposes the eventual correct answer very early, but supports it with weak or invalid reasoning.

Later evidence independently establishes the conclusion.

The Chronicle should preserve that the guess was early, while the investigation only became justified later.

This prevents retrospective omniscience.

## The Wrong Theory That Found the Right Place

Investigators pursue a mistaken theory but it directs them to a location that contains genuinely relevant evidence for a different reason.

The old theory can be rejected while its investigative consequence remains part of history.

The generator should not punish the party by declaring the entire branch meaningless.

## The Lead That Expired Overnight

A clue correctly identifies where an actor was staying.

By the time the party arrives, the actor has left for ordinary causal reasons already represented by World Agency or Travel.

The lead becomes stale. It was never false.

New traces may point onward, but only if the location state supports them.

## The Silence After the Storm

An expected sound, signal or wildlife behavior is absent after a storm.

The absence is initially treated as evidence of damage.

Before drawing that conclusion, investigators need to establish whether wind, power loss, changed operating hours, displacement or observation conditions would also explain the silence.

This seed can connect Soundscape, Weather, Infrastructure and Ecology layers.

## The Artifact Everyone Identified Too Quickly

A recovered object resembles a known historical type.

An early identification spreads publicly. Later material analysis or archival comparison shows that the object is later, repaired, reused or assembled from parts of different ages.

The artifact remains real. Its classification changes.

No ancient conspiracy is required for the mystery to matter.

## The Last Message Was Not the Last Event

A communication timestamp becomes the assumed endpoint of an actor's activity.

Later travel, service or observation records establish activity after the message without revealing why no later message was sent.

The timeline expands while the communication record remains valid.

## Mystery structure: Seven Answers to One Question

A long regional investigation begins with a broad question such as “What happened at the abandoned station?”

Over several local arcs, the investigation splits into narrower questions:

- who was physically present;
- which equipment was operating;
- what witnesses could actually observe;
- what occurred before the shutdown;
- which organization controlled the site at that date;
- what environmental condition changed afterward;
- which later story caused the public version to diverge from the surviving records.

Each local adventure should answer at least one narrow question while creating or revising others.

The final state does not need total certainty. The campaign can close with a robust chronology and one unresolved motive, or a known mechanism with disputed responsibility.

## Long arc: The Investigation That Changes Its Name

Phase 1 begins as a missing-property case.

A recovered object reveals a route anomaly, turning attention toward transport history.

The transport question reveals a historical organizational split.

The organization records reveal that the object was never the central issue; it was evidence of a decades-old handoff whose public interpretation shaped a modern faction dispute.

The same case container can preserve linked subquestions while the narrative label changes over time.

The payoff is continuity of reasoning rather than a surprise villain.

## Encounter contract: Witness Separation Perimeter

Narrative premise:

A witness or informant must leave an escalating confrontation while several groups have different interests in reaching or delaying them.

Full version depends heavily on complete movement, Intercept/reactions, lifecycle and AI tactical policy.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

The witness leaves tactical state before initiative. Ouros records their actual location and availability. The battle may clear the immediate perimeter. It cannot decide what the witness later says, whether they are truthful or whether their account confirms a theory.

## Encounter contract: Reconstruction Site Chokepoint

Narrative premise:

Investigators are reconstructing an event at a site whose machinery, hazards or environmental state can change during confrontation.

Full version dependencies include dynamic zones, hazards, reactions, movement, full lifecycle and adapter playback.

Full version: BLOCKED.

Reduced version: READY.

The reconstruction activity pauses before BattleSpec. Its observations and assumptions are frozen. The encounter uses static geometry. After combat, investigators may resume and separately assess whether the interruption changed the site's evidentiary value.

## Encounter contract: False Lead Ambush

Narrative premise:

An adversarial actor learns which lead the investigators are following and prepares an ambush at the expected destination.

The ambush itself can be an ordinary static battle if roster, geometry and timing are authored before BattleSpec.

Reduced/current form: READY, subject to the exact Moves, Abilities, Items and Features selected for combatants.

Important boundary:

The existence of the ambush does not prove that the lead was false. The adversary may have learned about a valid lead, exploited a stale lead or planted information through the existing rumor/covert-operation systems.

AutoPTU resolves the fight only.
