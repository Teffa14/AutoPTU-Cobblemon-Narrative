# The Yes Before the Handoff — Pass 342

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-07

## Premise

A field team needs one specialized instrument for a short survey window.

Another worker currently holds the instrument. The team sends a legitimate request. The holder agrees to make it available.

The request has been accepted.

The instrument has not moved.

Nobody has established whether the requester is expected to collect it, whether the provider intends to bring it, or whether a third actor has authority to carry it between sites.

The conflict comes from a real gap between consent and fulfillment rather than a villain.

## Why this belongs in Ouros

Passes 338–341 already allow the world-agent layer to represent a needed physical resource, reservation windows, technical readiness and a recovery option aimed at a known holder.

Pass 342 allows the acceptance itself to become durable world state without granting possession.

The case turns that systems distinction into playable continuity.

## Setup

The exact region, institution, NPCs, instrument and work site remain unapproved.

A canon-safe binding should reuse an existing organization, workplace and route after review rather than invent a new institution merely to stage this case.

Required world facts:

- a requester has a real obligation with a meaningful time window;
- a specific resource is required;
- another persistent actor is the current known holder/provider;
- the resource is currently serviceable under the relevant readiness owner;
- the requester can reach the provider through an established communication channel;
- no physical transfer has occurred when the provider accepts.

## Opening state

The requester has a blocked work intent.

Pass 341 derives `REQUEST_RESOURCE` toward the current holder.

The request enters the Pass 342 ledger and travels through the normal communication stack.

The provider receives it and accepts before the request deadline.

The requester later receives the acceptance.

At this point the world contains evidence for:

- a legitimate work need;
- a submitted request;
- provider consent;
- current holder identity;
- current resource location;
- no transfer event.

## The misunderstanding

The requester interprets acceptance as “it is coming.”

The provider interprets acceptance as “you may collect it when I finish.”

Both can speak sincerely.

Pass 335 belief-aware dialogue can keep those beliefs separate. Pass 336 grounding can determine whether pickup responsibility was ever jointly established. Pass 337 audience state can preserve which actors heard any clarification.

The system does not infer fault from the misunderstanding.

## Player-facing investigation / intervention

The player can resolve the situation by reconstructing narrow questions:

- Was the request delivered?
- Was the response delivered?
- What exact resource was accepted?
- Did the provider offer the original unit or an alternate?
- Where is the unit now?
- Who holds it?
- Was pickup responsibility ever stated?
- Did both actors ground the same meaning?
- Is a travel route currently available?
- Does another actor have proxy/transfer authority?
- Is the survey window still open?

No single “find clue” flag answers all of them.

## Branches

### Requester collects

If the world later establishes pickup authority and travel is feasible, the requester can travel to the provider.

Pass 342 still does not perform checkout or handoff. A later transfer owner must record the possession change.

### Provider delivers

The provider may replan around their own obligations and travel toward the requester.

That travel competes with real schedule commitments rather than teleporting the resource.

### Alternate resource offered

The provider may accept while explicitly offering another capable unit.

The requester then needs to determine whether that alternate satisfies the world-level capability requirement and, if it maps to a PTU Item, whether exact mechanical behavior is supported.

### Acceptance withdrawn

Before handoff, new readiness evidence or another obligation may make the unit unavailable.

The provider creates a later withdrawal event. The earlier acceptance remains historically true.

### Survey window missed

The work can fail operationally without anyone acting maliciously.

The aftermath can create a reschedule, a documented process change, a relationship consequence based on actual conduct, or a later request for clearer handoff procedures.

## Consequences

Possible non-canon consequences after the immediate case:

- recurring pairs of workers establish explicit pickup/delivery conventions;
- an institution begins recording `PICKUP_EXPECTED`, `DELIVERY_EXPECTED` or `TRANSFER_OWNER` in future requests;
- a late survey changes which observations exist in the world;
- a missed work window affects later schedules without deleting the original obligation;
- trust changes only when supported by the actual behavior and existing social-state rules;
- an accepted-but-unfulfilled request becomes discoverable historical evidence in a later review.

None of these consequences are promoted to canon by this proposal.

The case can be resolved entirely through semantic world events and deterministic dialogue options.

## Rich version

A later transfer can intersect with a route incident or field encounter.

The narrative premise remains the same: the instrument was requested and accepted before the structured event.

Possible structured objectives include reaching a handoff point, protecting the carrier, withdrawing with the instrument, or finishing a limited field observation without damaging equipment.
