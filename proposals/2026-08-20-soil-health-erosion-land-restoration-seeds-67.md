# Ouros Soil Health, Erosion & Land Restoration Seeds — Pass 67

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, character, place or prose is imported.

## 1. Bare Patch Isn't Dead

A conservation volunteer reports a bare patch beside a wetland trail as degradation. Older photographs show the same patch appearing seasonally for years. Players compare soil observations, visitor pressure and plant cycles before anyone closes the area permanently.

Possible outcomes include seasonal management, targeted monitoring, a real restoration project or simply correcting the public interpretation.

## 2. The Road That Compacted the Orchard

A temporary freight detour sends heavy traffic past an orchard. Months later, outer rows show ponding while interior rows do not.

The story can connect transport decisions, soil compaction, irrigation, crop planning and compensation without assuming the road operator acted negligently.

## 3. First Rain on the Burn Scar

A wildfire ended weeks ago. The first strong rainfall creates new rills on one slope while nearby unburned patches remain stable.

The immediate fire crisis is over; the new question is erosion, downstream sediment and which locations need stabilization first.

## 4. Three Generations of Soil Maps

A farming cooperative owns three surveys from different decades. All are internally consistent, but each divides the same valley differently.

The players reconstruct land-use history and discover that field boundaries, drainage and sampling methods changed between editions.

## 5. The Digging Pokémon Scapegoat

Farmers blame a local burrowing population for uneven ground. Historical records reveal that subsurface utility work and old drainage channels also cross the affected field.

The correct resolution may involve protecting infrastructure, changing farming access, monitoring Pokémon behavior or accepting several simultaneous causes.

## 6. Swinub and the Warm Patch

Swinub repeatedly forage around a patch of winter ground where snow melts earlier than elsewhere.

The behavior generates a geothermal investigation. The Pokémon are evidence of a pattern, not magical survey instruments and not proof that a hot spring exists.

## 7. Zarude Vine Study

Researchers document nutrient changes where naturally shed vine material accumulates in one forest.

The study must separate localized observations from species-wide claims and avoid turning the forest into a farm for harvesting vines.

## 8. Eldegoss Seed Drift

A farming district notices seedlings appearing farther downwind after a seasonal Eldegoss movement.

Researchers, growers and conservation staff compare seed dispersal, soil condition and land use. No automatic fertility bonus or spawn boost is created.

## 9. Hillside Footpath Closure

A popular walking trail develops deepening ruts after a wet season. Tourism wants quick reopening; conservation staff want a longer closure; residents need the path for everyday travel.

A small reroute may solve more than a total ban.

## 10. Quarry Dust on the Lower Fields

Farmers report a new surface crust after a quarry expands operations uphill. The timing is suspicious, but weather records also show an unusual dry/windy period.

Sampling and provenance determine whether the quarry is actually responsible and whether the observed layer affects soil function.

## 11. Festival Overflow Lawn

A town festival uses a public green as overflow space for vendors and visitors. Weeks later the lawn has bare lanes following exactly the highest-traffic paths.

The next edition must decide whether to relocate stalls, rotate use, install temporary walkways or accept a longer recovery period.

## 12. The Abandoned Road Heals Unevenly

A bypass removes traffic from an old road. Some sections rapidly revegetate while compacted shoulders remain almost unchanged.

The route becomes a long-term restoration corridor and a visible record of past infrastructure.

## 13. Riverbank Gully

A small gully appears above a river after repeated storms. Sediment reaches the water only during certain rainfall events.

The story can connect Soil, Freshwater and Meteorology without treating every rainstorm as a combat hazard.

## 14. Wetland Trampling

A newly popular photography site concentrates visitors around one observation point. The habitat remains visually green, but repeated foot traffic changes surface structure and drainage.

A boardwalk, timed access or moving the viewing point may reduce pressure while keeping the site open.

## 15. Research Plot Blind Spot

A famous long-term agricultural experiment has excellent data but no measurements from the strip where vehicles always turn around.

A new researcher notices that the missing area may explain several unexplained drainage observations.

## 16. Orchard Roots and the Utility Trench

A utility repair crosses an old orchard. The trench is filled and the service restored, but several trees later show different rooting behavior along the disturbed line.

The infrastructure repair can be fully successful while still creating a separate land-restoration problem.

## 17. Tunnel Spoil Heap

Excavation from a transport tunnel creates a large spoil pile. It is geologically ordinary material, but its placement changes runoff and gradually becomes colonized by plants and Pokémon.

The site can become a disposal problem, restoration project, research site or future habitat depending on what players and institutions do.

## 18. Guardian of the Fields Claim

A region celebrates a traditional story that a Legendary once made its fields abundant. A modern soil survey shows real differences between districts but cannot establish the legendary cause.

The cultural tradition and scientific work can both remain meaningful without forcing either into the other's role.

## 19. Visitor Trail Creep

Visitors repeatedly leave a marked trail to reach a scenic overlook. Several informal paths form gradually.

The story is about accumulated small decisions rather than one vandal. Restoration may include better wayfinding, closing redundant tracks and moving the overlook.

## 20. Snowmelt Rill Network

After a winter with unusual snowpack, meltwater cuts many tiny channels across a meadow.

Cryosphere explains the timing. Soil records the surface response. Freshwater records where the water goes. No mud or ice battle mechanic is assumed.

## 21. Ashfall Soil Study

A volcanic ashfall covers several farms. One year later, the fields show different responses because their previous soils, drainage and management differed.

The ash event is shared; the land outcomes are not.

## 22. Nursery Yard Compaction

A busy Pokémon nursery expands without expanding its outdoor service yard. Staff notice ponding in the same high-traffic areas used for deliveries and animal handling.

The institution must redesign operations without treating the Pokémon as the cause merely because they use the yard.

## 23. Construction Topsoil Stockpile

A public-works project correctly saves the site's topsoil before construction. A schedule delay leaves the stockpile exposed through a storm season.

The later question is whether the material still meets the project's restoration needs and what evidence supports that assessment.

## 24. The Garden Above the Archive

Erosion beside an old memorial garden exposes fragments that may belong to an earlier structure. The site is simultaneously a soil-restoration problem and a heritage-context problem.

Players must stabilize the area before investigation destroys the very context it wants to study.

## 25. School Garden Comparison

Students maintain several small plots using different practices. The project becomes valuable because it produces repeated observations across years, not because one method gives a magical crop bonus.

The garden can seed future science, food, education and community arcs.

## 26. The Old Plow Pan

A field with good surface cover still ponds after heavy rain. Historical farming records suggest a compacted layer below the surface.

The discovery challenges the town's habit of judging land health by appearance alone.

## 27. Windblown Sediment at the Rail Line

Fine material repeatedly accumulates along a rail cutting during dry months. Maintenance crews clear it efficiently, but nobody has identified the source.

Mapping wind, exposed land and seasonal work patterns produces a cross-system investigation.

## 28. Rewilding the Former Field

An agricultural field is retired and allowed to recover as habitat. Residents disagree about whether its untidy appearance means failure.

Long-term monitoring tracks soil cover, infiltration, vegetation and Pokémon use while public expectations evolve.

## 29. Muddy Trail, Different Cause

Two trail sections look equally muddy. One is compacted and poorly drained; the other is temporarily saturated because groundwater is unusually high.

Applying the same intervention to both would be a mistake.

## 30. Soil Biology Baseline

A research institution begins a soil baseline program before a new road opens. For several seasons nothing dramatic happens.

Years later, those quiet records become critical for distinguishing a genuine change from normal variability.

# Long arc A — The Hill That Keeps Moving

Year 1: a hillside road develops small ruts after heavy traffic.

Year 2: drainage changes and one section begins eroding faster.

Year 3: a wildfire removes surface cover from part of the slope.

Year 4: a major rain season sends sediment into a lower stream.

Year 5: the region has enough observations to redesign the road, stabilize selected areas and leave other sections to recover naturally.

The arc has no mandatory villain. Different decisions can be reasonable at different times because evidence changes.

# Long arc B — Three Fields, Three Histories

Three neighboring farms show similar crop problems during one season.

Field A has deep compaction from old machinery traffic.

Field B has a water-distribution issue with otherwise stable soil.

Field C sits on recently deposited material whose behavior is still poorly understood.

Players who assume one regional cause will struggle. Repeated investigation, archives, Freshwater state and practical knowledge reveal three histories.

Later seasons can show different recovery trajectories and new agricultural choices without assigning invented yield bonuses.

# Long arc C — From Ash to Orchard

A severe disturbance leaves a patchwork of ash, exposed soil and surviving vegetation near a settlement.

Early years focus on stabilization and monitoring.

Later years introduce experimental planting, wildlife return, visitor pressure and a debate over whether part of the area should become productive land again.

If an orchard eventually exists, it is the result of many world-state decisions and observations. Volcanic/fire material never becomes automatically fertile because the narrative wants a hopeful ending.
