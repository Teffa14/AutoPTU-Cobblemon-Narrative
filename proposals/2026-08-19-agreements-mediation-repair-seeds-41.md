# Ouros Agreements, Mediation & Repair Seeds — Pass 41

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from abstract research patterns. None of these establish law, institutions, prices, PTU mechanics or permanent setting facts.

## Design intent

These seeds exercise disputes where investigation, negotiation, temporary cooperation, repair or explicit refusal are meaningful outcomes. They should connect to existing case, faction, civic, custody, media, finance, conservation and world-state systems.

No seed assumes that compromise is always correct. Some should end with one party refusing terms, an agreement expiring normally, evidence resolving the factual dispute, or a conflict narrowing without disappearing.

## 1. The Ferry Window

Two rival transport operators both need the same damaged landing during storm recovery. Neither wants a permanent partnership. They may agree to alternating emergency windows until the second dock is restored.

Callback: months later, residents remember that the temporary schedule worked better than the old one, creating a separate civic proposal rather than silently extending the truce.

## 2. Coordinates Under Embargo

Two research teams independently confirm a sensitive nesting site. They disagree about publication timing but can draft a temporary coordinate embargo while the season is active.

The agreement concerns human publication behavior. The wild Pokémon are not parties to it.

## 3. The Rematch Terms

Two recurring rivals want a public rematch after a disputed previous event. They negotiate venue, public information, timing and which prior footage may be used for scouting.

Agreement does not imply friendship. The battle itself remains governed entirely by the battle-institution and AutoPTU contracts.

## 4. The Borrowed Fossil

A family collection and a regional museum both have legitimate interests in an important fossil. They negotiate a temporary loan, conservation work, display wording and a fixed return date.

Custody changes during the loan. Ownership does not change unless separately established.

## 5. Repair Instead of Payment

A workshop damages equipment belonging to a field team. Both parties prefer that the workshop repair the equipment and provide a replacement during the repair window rather than exchange money.

The outcome creates service commitments, not an invented debt formula.

## 6. The Referral That Did Not Happen

Two clinics have an established referral arrangement. A patient transfer fails because the ferry route closes unexpectedly.

The first public rumor accuses one clinic of breaking its promise. The case becomes a useful test of `nonperformance != bad faith`.

## 7. Evacuation Corridor

Two hostile organizations agree to leave one route untouched while civilians and injured Pokémon evacuate during a crisis.

The ceasefire ends when the corridor closes. Returning to normal rivalry afterward is not a breach.

## 8. The Sponsor's Name

A sponsor and event organizer disagree over how prominently the sponsor should appear after the event's scope changes. They revise visibility terms without giving the sponsor control over competition rules.

The agreement can be fulfilled even if public opinion about the sponsor remains negative.

## 9. Correction Before Retraction

A newspaper publishes an inaccurate description of a research incident. The affected institution does not demand deletion of the historical article; instead both sides agree on a correction, source disclosure and a linked follow-up.

Public memory may still contain both versions.

## 10. Quiet Season Access

A conservation body, local guides and a transport operator negotiate a seasonal closure around a sensitive route.

The closure includes review dates and alternate transport information. No wild Pokémon is treated as a signatory.

## 11. The Apprentice's Revised Hours

An apprentice and workshop agree to change training duties after seasonal workload makes the original schedule impossible.

The old commitment remains in history. Renegotiation is not treated as failure.

## 12. Shared Nursery Custody Plan

Two human custodians and a nursery need an explicit care schedule for an Egg whose final ownership is unresolved.

Every human commitment needs explicit consent. The Egg itself is not treated as property merely because custody is scheduled.

## 13. Publication After Excavation

An archaeology team, site steward and museum negotiate how long a newly found inscription remains under limited publication while context is documented.

The terms can cover human access, copying and publication. They cannot make a historical interpretation true.

## 14. Bridge Maintenance Rotation

Two settlements rely on the same bridge but historically expected the other to handle maintenance. A new arrangement defines inspection duties, supply responsibilities and a review after one season.

A later failure should check whether the responsible task was actually due before assigning blame.

## 15. Festival Quiet Hours

A festival organizer, nearby residents and wildlife stewards negotiate performance hours around a known evening roost.

The arrangement can preserve the festival while changing schedule and venue layout. No custom Sonic or morale mechanics are created.

## 16. Shared Staging Ground

Two rescue groups with different procedures need the same open field during flood response. They agree on zones, radio contacts and handoff procedures for one crisis.

The agreement ends when the emergency phase ends unless deliberately renewed.

## 17. Clubhouse Calendar

Two clubs both have historical claims to a shared training hall. Instead of deciding who 'owns the culture', they create a seasonal booking plan and rules for public events.

Members can still dislike each other.

## 18. The Case Handoff

A local institution investigates an incident until evidence points outside its authored scope. It negotiates a handoff with a regional specialist body, including what evidence can be transferred and what remains private.

Transfer of responsibility does not erase the first institution's case history.

## 19. The Lost Instrument

A borrowed research instrument disappears during an expedition. The borrower offers replacement service and temporary equipment while ownership and responsibility are still being investigated.

The repair plan can proceed without requiring a premature finding of fault.

## 20. The Old Compact

An archive reveals a century-old agreement between two institutions. Both still cite it, but neither can prove that the current organizations inherited the old authority.

The story centers on whether they draft a new arrangement rather than pretending the old document automatically controls the present.

## 21. Agreement Fulfilled, Rumor Alive

Two factions complete every term of a limited resource-sharing arrangement. A distant settlement still believes the agreement collapsed because its newspaper never received the correction.

The problem becomes information propagation, not renewed breach.

## 22. Outage or Breach?

A service provider misses an agreed communication window. One party alleges deliberate withdrawal. Technical records show a relay outage at the relevant time, but some questions remain.

The agreement can move into review without assuming either perfect innocence or bad faith.

## 23. The Closed Route Clause

A supply commitment becomes impossible after a landslide closes the only legal route. The parties can suspend, substitute or extend the term.

The quest tests dependency-aware obligations instead of arbitrary deadline punishment.

## 24. Refusal at the Table

A proposed settlement is reasonable but one party still refuses because it does not meet their core interest.

The content remains successful if players understand the conflict better and prevent immediate escalation. No mandatory compromise ending exists.

## 25. The Mediator Steps Away

A respected mediator discovers that their organization benefits from one proposed outcome. They disclose the conflict and recuse themselves.

Finding a replacement becomes a social/institutional problem. The disclosure itself does not prove corruption.

## 26. Truce Completed Exactly

Two rival groups agree not to interfere with each other during a one-day joint survey. Both comply perfectly. The following day they resume competing for the same contract.

NPCs who expected permanent reconciliation misinterpret the result; the Chronicle records the narrower truth.

## 27. The Returned Specimen

A research institute agrees to return a collected specimen after analysis, but discovers that the original storage site is now unsafe.

The parties must decide whether to delay return, choose a new custodian or create a temporary conservation arrangement. The object is not silently retained.

## 28. Restoring the Market Stall

A public disturbance damages a long-running vendor stall. Several participants agree to rebuild it, replace specific documented goods and correct a false public accusation that followed the incident.

Repair produces visible Minecraft change without generating friendship or forgiveness labels.

## 29. The Temporary Guide Pact

A local guide and an outside expedition distrust each other but need each other's capabilities for one crossing. Terms cover route knowledge, safety responsibilities, publication of sensitive landmarks and the point at which the guide's duty ends.

The guide can fulfill every term while continuing to oppose the expedition's larger project.

## 30. The Reopened Agreement

An old seasonal access agreement worked for years until climate and migration timing changed. Neither side technically breached it; the underlying assumptions became obsolete.

The next arc is renegotiation based on new observational evidence.

# Longer arc A — The River Compact

Several settlements, farms, conservation groups, transport operators and research institutions depend on one river system. Early disputes concern maintenance and access rather than abstract ownership.

First phase:
Players investigate which services actually depend on which structures, distinguish measured water conditions from public claims and identify agreements that already exist.

Second phase:
Parties create small bilateral arrangements: maintenance windows, emergency access, seasonal monitoring and data sharing. Not every party signs the same document.

Third phase:
A dry season exposes dependencies the first arrangements missed. Some commitments are fulfilled, some delayed and some impossible under current state.

Fourth phase:
The region can build a revised compact from recorded performance rather than inventing a grand solution. The final state may still contain unresolved disputes.

World consequence:
Future crises, agriculture, transport, ecology and civic projects can read the actual commitment history.

# Longer arc B — Three Winters of Truce

Two organizations have a persistent conflict over a mountain corridor. Every winter, dangerous conditions force some degree of practical cooperation.

Year one:
A narrow evacuation ceasefire is created and ends normally.

Year two:
Both sides agree to share weather observations and rescue signals while still competing elsewhere.

Year three:
A new generation of local actors asks whether repeated limited cooperation should become a permanent corridor-management arrangement.

Possible endings:
- permanent narrow agreement;
- continued seasonal truces;
- breakdown after a verified breach;
- no agreement but safer communication rules;
- institutional handoff to another steward.

No ending requires the factions to merge or become friends.

# Longer arc C — The Repair Ledger

A district has accumulated many small unresolved harms after years of construction, festivals, business turnover, route changes and public incidents. None is large enough to justify one regional villain arc.

Players help institutions reconstruct provenance: borrowed equipment, damaged signs, blocked access, incorrect public records, unfinished restoration, unpaid-but-unverified claims and old service promises.

Each resolution creates a small concrete state change. Some are repairs. Some are corrections. Some claims are unsupported. Some parties refuse proposals.

Over time the district visibly changes in Minecraft through restored spaces, corrected notices, returned objects and reopened services. Public trust can improve as an emergent consequence of observable performance, not through a global 'good reputation' meter.

# Encounter contracts

The detailed capability contracts for `Relay Station Ceasefire`, `Waterworks Boundary Agreement` and `Restitution at the Market Gate` live in `design/agreements-mediation-repair-layer.md`.

All three preserve a REDUCED version that keeps agreements and obligations in world state while AutoPTU runs only conventional static combat. FULL versions remain blocked wherever they require interception/forced movement, hazards/zones/reactions, objective-aware tactical AI or Minecraft adapter support.
