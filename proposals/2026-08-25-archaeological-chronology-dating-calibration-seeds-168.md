# Archaeological Chronology and Dating — Ouros Seeds 168

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Nothing here establishes Ouros history, institutions, technology, calendars, ruins or archaeological truth.

Authority note: these candidates extend `design/myth-archaeology-sacred-sites-layer.md` through `design/archaeological-chronology-dating-calibration-protocol.md`.

## 30 worldbuilding and quest candidates

### 1. The Beam Older Than the Hall

A timber in a ceremonial hall dates much earlier than the rest of the structure. The explanation may be deliberate reuse, salvage, inheritance or an earlier building phase. The timber's age is real; the hall's date remains a separate question.

### 2. The Inscription Came Later

A famous inscription sits on a wall widely believed to be ancient. Tool marks and context indicate the wall predates the text by centuries. The inscription can remain culturally important without being contemporary with the building.

### 3. Two Dates, One Hearth

Two samples from the same hearth produce non-overlapping intervals. The team investigates mixed fuel, old wood, intrusion and sampling history instead of accusing a laboratory of failure.

### 4. The Famous Year Was Always an Estimate

A town celebrates the supposed 1,000th anniversary of a ruin. Archive review shows the original publication used a broad estimate that later tourism simplified into a single year.

### 5. The Sample With No Date

A carefully recovered specimen is unsuitable for the available method. The project records `NO_USABLE_DATE`. The failed attempt becomes useful because it prevents false certainty.

### 6. The New Calibration Moves the Festival

A reference revision shifts the estimated interval for an event associated with a local observance. The festival does not automatically change. Cultural chronology and scientific chronology continue side by side.

### 7. The Burrow Through Three Centuries

A Pokémon-made burrow cuts through several archaeological layers and carries older material upward. The objects are genuine, but their new positions no longer directly date the upper context.

### 8. The Coin in the Old Floor

A comparatively recent object is found inside a structure believed to be ancient. Investigation asks whether the floor was repaired, cut later, contaminated during excavation or incorrectly reconstructed.

### 9. Three Labs, Three Intervals

Three institutions analyze different samples from the same phase. Their results overlap only partially. A chronology review must explain sample choice and event linkage rather than average everything into one number.

### 10. The Tree-Ring Sequence Stops Short

A preserved beam has clear rings, but the regional master chronology does not extend far enough to place it on the calendar. The sample still offers internal growth information and may become datable years later when the reference sequence expands.

### 11. The Repair Phase Became the Founding Date

An old excavation report dated a prominent repair and later summaries treated that result as the site's construction date. New fieldwork separates the two phases.

### 12. The Redeposited Shrine Stone

A carved sacred stone appears in a much younger flood deposit. The stone may be ancient while its deposition at the current site is recent. Sacred Sites, Fluvial Geomorphology and Archaeology all retain separate authority.

### 13. The Pottery Style Lasted Longer Here

A ceramic style used as a chronological marker persists locally after it disappeared elsewhere. Cross-dating remains useful but becomes regionally calibrated rather than universal.

### 14. The Charcoal Was From an Old Tree

A hearth sample is older than the activity archaeologists hoped to date because the fire burned long-dead wood. The discrepancy becomes a lesson in what the method actually dated.

### 15. The Archive Sample Gets a Second Life

A sample collected decades earlier was stored but never analyzed. New technology or a new reference framework allows a dating attempt without reopening the site.

### 16. The Context Was Excavated Before Provenance Standards

A museum owns hundreds of objects from an early dig with poor layer records. The objects are authentic, but modern chronology can only use them cautiously.

### 17. The Ruin With No Absolute Date

A large site has an excellent relative sequence and no secure numeric dates. The campaign can know which phases came first while still arguing about centuries.

### 18. The Quarry Stone Is Older Than Everything

A building material is geologically ancient, but the structure made from it is recent. Public discussion repeatedly confuses material age with construction age.

### 19. The Reused Doorway

A decorated doorway was moved from an older building into a later one. Its style correctly reflects the older phase while its current masonry context reflects the later phase.

### 20. The Dated Nest Inside the Ruin

Organic material from a modern or recent Pokémon nest is sampled accidentally while investigating an ancient chamber. The result is valid for the nest, not the architecture.

### 21. The Flood Mixed the Sequence

A historical flood eroded several layers and redeposited material downslope. The new mixed deposit becomes an event worth dating in its own right.

### 22. The Calendar Translation Problem

An inscription contains a local era name and a numbered year. Languages can translate the words, but converting the date into the modern Ouros calendar requires a separate chronology model.

### 23. The Observatory Date and the Tree Rings Disagree

A historical claim ties a construction to a celestial event. Dendrochronology suggests a different interval. The conflict can remain unresolved while both evidence chains remain intact.

### 24. The Wall Was Built in Stages

One apparently uniform fortification contains several construction episodes. A single `site_age` would hide the actual history.

### 25. The Excavation Solved the Puzzle but Damaged the Date

A team opens a sealed chamber through an undocumented cut. The chamber becomes accessible, but some stratigraphic information is permanently lost. The success has a durable cost without needing villainy.

### 26. The Public Plaque Is Thirty Years Out of Date

A heritage plaque still displays the best estimate from an older research generation. Updating it becomes a public-memory decision, not an emergency.

### 27. The Same Sample Under Two Curves

A radiometric-style measurement is interpreted under two reference revisions. The raw result is unchanged while the calendar interval shifts. Both published versions remain in Chronicle.

### 28. The Ancient Object Was Buried Yesterday

A genuinely old artifact is deliberately reburied during a modern ceremony, restoration or emergency. Future investigators who ignore context could make the wrong inference.

### 29. The Scholar Who Was Mostly Right

A deceased researcher proposed the correct phase order but assigned the wrong calendar centuries. Later work can honor the contribution without preserving every conclusion.

### 30. Nothing Changed This Season

A field season produces no new dates and no dramatic discovery. Better context drawings, sample rejection and one confirmed relative relationship make the chronology more reliable anyway.

## Long arc A — Five Chronologies of Meridian Ruins

Year one produces a simple sequence based mostly on architecture. Year two adds several samples and a provisional numeric range. Year three discovers a reused timber that forces a phase split. Year four recalibrates an older result. Year five publishes a chronology with narrower claims and more explicit uncertainty than the original version.

The arc can connect Archaeology, Museums, Languages, Public Memory, Metrology and Science without requiring an antagonist.

## Long arc B — The Building With Six Dates

One persistent structure accumulates six legitimate chronological statements: quarrying of stone, growth/felling of reused timber, original construction, major repair, later inscription and modern restoration. The story teaches players to ask what each date actually refers to.

## Long arc C — The Regional Master Sequence

Several institutions gradually assemble a long reference chronology from living trees, historical buildings and preserved wood. Early gaps remain unresolved. A later sample finally bridges two sequences, changing the dating potential of dozens of archived objects without changing any object's physical history.

## Encounter concept — Sealed Context Evacuation

FULL: a newly exposed chamber must be secured while researchers, custodians and possibly wildlife withdraw through contested space. Complete movement is required for evacuation/interception; AI tactical policy is required for `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_RESEARCHER`, `CLEAR_ROUTE` and `REACH_EXIT`; Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics playback is required for authoritative handoff. Terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions is additionally required if collapse, unstable flooring, dust or protected zones have tactical effects. Standard targeting, base movement legality, core calculations, action economy/initiative and AI legal-action infrastructure can use VERIFIED support. Lifecycle, damage, statuses, Move behavior, Abilities, Items and Trainer Features remain PARTIAL whenever invoked.

REDUCED: Archaeology closes the context, moves researchers/custodians out through world state and freezes a safe static arena. AutoPTU resolves only a separate confrontation. The battle cannot date the chamber.

## Encounter concept — Sample Transfer Through Relic Passage

FULL: a custody-sensitive sample must move between an excavation and field lab while access changes around it. Complete movement and AI tactical policy are required for a moving objective and protective behavior; adapter/playback remains BLOCKING. Environment enters only if the route itself changes tactically.

REDUCED: Postal/Material Culture/Custody resolves the sample handoff outside combat. A battle, if any, occurs after the sample is already secured or before transfer begins.

## Encounter concept — Stratigraphic Collapse After Storm

FULL: storm damage exposes and destabilizes several layers while people and Pokémon must leave without trampling sensitive areas. This depends on complete movement, environment/hazards, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback. If specific Moves or Abilities interact with the environment, those exact PARTIAL families must be validated separately.

REDUCED: Crisis/Archaeology closes the trench, documents the exposed profile and evacuates all noncombatants first. Any fight uses an adjacent stable arena. No custom collapse, falling, dust or Rough Terrain mechanics are invented.

## Non-combat investigation — Which Date Belongs to the Temple?

Players compare a reused beam, a foundation deposit, a repair inscription and an older publication. The correct result may be several dates attached to several phases rather than one answer. No battle is required.

## Canon gates

Future canon must decide which dating technologies exist, what regional reference chronologies are available, which institutions can sample sacred/restricted sites, which calendar conventions appear in old records and how much numerical precision should be visible to players.
