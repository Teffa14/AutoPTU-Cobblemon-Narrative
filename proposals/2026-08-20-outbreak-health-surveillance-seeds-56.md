# Ouros Outbreak & Health Surveillance Seeds — Pass 56

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros structures derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot is copied.

## 1. The North Trail Cluster

Three clinics independently record similar symptoms among Pokémon that recently crossed the same hiking route.

The route link is real. The cause remains unknown.

Possible directions:

- shared water source;
- seasonal plant exposure;
- unrelated cases amplified by reporting;
- contamination from a damaged utility;
- a wild-population event.

The first quest is establishing a usable case definition, not finding a villain.

## 2. The Clinic That Noticed First

A small rural Pokémon Center sees a pattern several days before the regional institution does.

The story focuses on whether local observations are trusted, how data is shared and what information can be published without exposing patients.

## 3. False Outbreak, Real Problem

A suspected contagious cluster turns out to contain three unrelated causes.

The investigation is still successful because it discovers:

- a sanitation problem;
- one ordinary seasonal illness;
- one mechanical injury pattern caused by damaged terrain.

## 4. The Empty Waiting Room

A clinic suddenly receives fewer wild patients from a normally active area.

Possible explanations include:

- population movement;
- route closure;
- avoidance of humans;
- survey failure;
- actual health decline.

Absence becomes an investigation hook only because a baseline exists.

## 5. A Case Definition Changes

An investigation initially groups every Pokémon with the same visible symptom.

Later evidence shows that the definition is too broad.

Old classifications remain in history while the current version excludes several early cases.

## 6. The Nursery Pause

A nursery voluntarily pauses new arrivals after several unclear health signals.

The pause is precautionary.

No Pokémon is declared infected and no Trainer is blamed.

The story can become logistics, communication, care capacity or research.

## 7. Two Valleys, One Signal

Wildlife observers in two disconnected valleys report the same unusual sign.

Players investigate whether the events share:

- migration;
- weather;
- food source;
- trade/transport;
- coincidence.

## 8. The Festival Aftermath

A large festival produces a spike in clinic visits.

Possible causes may include heat, stress, food exposure, crowded transport, ordinary injuries or a transmissible condition.

The game must not select one cause without evidence.

## 9. The Ferry Exposure Graph

Several cases share one ferry trip.

The ferry becomes an exposure node, not proof of transmission.

The investigation may discover that every affected passenger also visited the same harbor market before boarding.

## 10. The Wild Sentinel

A ranger team observes a change in one common wild species before any clinical cases appear.

The observation becomes a sentinel signal and opens targeted sampling.

The species is not automatically the source of the problem.

## 11. The Water Sample That Frightened Everyone

A sample from a public water source tests abnormal.

The finding is real.

Whether it explains the health cluster is still unresolved.

Media/public-memory systems can track how certainty becomes exaggerated.

## 12. The Negative Result

A high-profile suspected exposure source is tested repeatedly and evidence does not support it.

The negative finding changes the investigation and may clear an institution or location that had already suffered reputational damage.

## 13. Missed by the Map

Public dashboards show no cases in a remote district.

Players discover that the region has almost no observation coverage.

The correct state becomes `unknown`, not `healthy`.

## 14. A Researcher Changes Their Mind

An investigator publicly supported one hypothesis early.

New evidence weakens it.

The researcher issues a correction rather than becoming defensive or secretly villainous.

## 15. The Old Clinic Ledger

Archive staff discover decades-old care records that resemble the current cluster.

The comparison is useful, but old diagnostic language and missing metadata create uncertainty.

## 16. The Reopened Investigation

A health event was closed after cases stopped.

Months later, a new cluster shares enough features to reopen the old case.

The original evidence remains intact and versioned.

## 17. The Supply Bottleneck

A clinic has beds and staff but lacks one routine consumable because a route is closed.

The gameplay is logistics and prioritization, not a new disease mechanic.

## 18. The Mobile Clinic Route

A mobile clinic begins visiting three settlements because their case counts are individually small but collectively unusual.

The route itself becomes a persistent service and future source of baseline data.

## 19. The Transfer Pause

Two institutions temporarily pause Pokémon transfers while investigating a shared exposure.

The decision affects breeding, competitions, research and transport without implying the affected Pokémon are sick.

## 20. The Wrong Species Blamed

Residents blame a visible wild species after several health events.

Evidence later shows the species was also avoiding the actual exposure source.

The resolution can repair public perception and improve ecological knowledge.

## 21. A Clinic with Too Much Success

A well-known specialist becomes overloaded because reports from outside its intended service area increase.

The resulting backlog can produce referral, staffing and transport stories without any worsening disease.

## 22. The Shared Batch

Several cases share a food or material batch.

The batch becomes one hypothesis node.

Ownership, provenance, storage history and distribution are checked through existing material/food layers.

## 23. The Storm Complication

A storm interrupts an active investigation.

Power loss, route closure and wildlife movement make data collection harder.

The weather event does not automatically make the disease worse.

## 24. The Quiet Wild Collective

A monitored collective changes its normal gathering behavior.

The change may indicate disturbance, weather, food movement, visitor pressure or health problems.

Investigation must compare ecological alternatives.

## 25. The Public Notice Error

A notice correctly warns about an investigation but incorrectly names the affected route.

The correction reaches some communities and not others.

Health state, message state and public belief diverge.

## 26. The Volunteer Reporting Network

Residents and Trainers can submit structured health observations.

The network gains value over time but must handle duplicates, uncertain identifications and uneven geographic coverage.

## 27. The Case That Wasn't Infectious

A cluster is traced to a shared environmental exposure.

The story still uses outbreak-style surveillance because the initial pattern was correctly detected even though no transmission existed.

## 28. The Condition Nobody Can Name Yet

Several observations remain consistent over months, but no reliable cause or diagnosis emerges.

Ouros preserves the uncertainty instead of generating a fake scientific answer.

## 29. The Recovery Lag

Clinical cases decline, but tourism and transport remain depressed because public belief updates more slowly.

The post-event story is about recovery, corrections and institutional trust.

## 30. The After-Action Archive

After an investigation closes, institutions compare:

- what they knew at each stage;
- which warnings arrived first;
- which hypotheses were wrong;
- where data was missing;
- which measures helped operationally;
- what should change before the next event.

This creates durable institutional memory.

# Longer arc A — The Three Rivers Investigation

Three river valleys report different combinations of wild-Pokémon behavior, clinic visits and water-quality changes.

Early play treats them as separate investigations.

Later evidence may show a shared upstream infrastructure dependency, seasonal environmental driver, several unrelated local causes, or a mixture.

The arc can connect:

- science;
- sanitation;
- conservation;
- clinics;
- transport;
- local government;
- media;
- public trust.

No single causal answer is required until authored evidence supports it.

# Longer arc B — A Year of Baselines

A research programme begins with an apparent lack of dramatic content: clinics, rangers and players collect ordinary observations for one full regional cycle.

Months later those baselines make several subtle anomalies detectable.

The payoff is systemic: earlier quiet work becomes valuable evidence rather than filler.

# Longer arc C — The Second Wave That Wasn't

A region previously experienced a confirmed health event.

A year later several similar signs appear.

Public memory rapidly labels it a return.

The investigation discovers that the new cluster has a different cause.

The arc explores how previous experience improves readiness while also creating confirmation bias.

# Encounter contract 1 — Trail Clinic Supply Run

Full version:

Players protect moving medical cargo through a disrupted route while maintaining access for retreat and responders.

Exact dependency families:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement/push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL when selected legal effects require it;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING when route conditions affect combat;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Resolve cargo movement and clinic delivery outside the grid. If combat occurs, use a static legal battle on a safe route segment. The cargo has no invented HP, movement or protection rules.

# Encounter contract 2 — Wildlife Sampling Perimeter

Full version:

Researchers hold a temporary safe observation area while wild Pokémon can approach, withdraw or move toward escape routes. Victory is not defined as defeating every Pokémon.

Additional blockers:

- complete movement/interception — BLOCKING;
- zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Sampling occurs outside battle. If defensive combat is necessary, use only currently present combatants and a static arena. Battle success does not equal sample success.

# Encounter contract 3 — Treatment Plant Access

Full version:

Players reach controls or sampling points while active facility zones change the tactical space.

Additional blockers:

- terrain/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING;
- complete movement — BLOCKING if machinery or flows move actors;
- lifecycle — PARTIAL for timed transitions;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING;
- adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Keep contamination, pumps, sampling and workers in world state. Run a conventional static encounter only if conflict occurs. No environmental Poison, damage or sickness is created by description.
