# Pokémon play, enrichment and exploratory behavior seeds — pass 145

Status: NON-CANON proposals. These are original Ouros candidates derived from the research principles in Pass 145. They do not establish species behavior, PTU mechanics, institutions or regional canon.

## 30 worldbuilding and quest candidates

1. The Ball Nobody Uses. A nursery introduces a new bouncing ball because staff assume it will be popular. Every observed Pokémon chooses older objects or open running space instead. The useful outcome is a better preference record, not a quest to make the ball desirable.

2. The Same Toy, Three Uses. One persistent object is chased by a juvenile, used as a leaning surface by an older Pokémon and ignored by a third. Material Culture keeps one object identity while the play layer records different interactions.

3. Rough Play, Wrong Headline. A short video of two Pokémon wrestling is published as proof of an attack. Longer footage shows repeated voluntary re-engagement, but the classification remains an assessment rather than an automatic truth detector.

4. The Game Stops at Noon. A recurring group activity disappears during the hottest part of summer while morning observations continue. Urban Heat, Diel Activity and Care can all contribute evidence without a procedural `heat = no play` rule.

5. The Snow Slope Returns. A hillside becomes a recreational site for several local Pokémon only during certain snow conditions. Cryosphere owns the snowpack. The play layer owns the observed use.

6. The Former Partner at the Fountain. A released former partner is observed playing in a public fountain with wild Pokémon years later. The event confirms presence and behavior. It does not imply a request to rejoin the old Trainer.

7. The Training Prop Becomes a Toy. A marker used during formal drills is left in a practice yard after the session. A Pokémon later interacts with it without cues or criteria. Chronicle preserves the transition from training prop to recreational object use.

8. The Toy Becomes a Research Question. A Pokémon repeatedly combines two loose objects during recreation. Researchers disagree whether the behavior is object play, problem solving or tool use. Cognition receives the evidence; classification can stay unresolved.

9. Two Species Join Once. During a seasonal gathering, individuals from two species briefly participate in the same chase. No friendship, mutualism or cultural tradition is inferred from one event.

10. The Invitation Is Not Returned. One Pokémon repeatedly approaches another with a familiar play-like signal. The second remains nearby but never joins during the observation window. The record says exactly that and nothing about rejection or relationship quality.

11. The Enrichment Rotation That Worked Too Well. Staff rotate several objects to prevent one yard from becoming monotonous. A previously ordinary rope becomes the preferred option after another object is removed for repair. Choice availability matters as much as the selected object.

12. The Broken Toy With a History. A damaged ball is replaced for daily use but kept because photographs show several generations of nursery Pokémon interacting with it. It eventually becomes a small local archive object.

13. The Adult Returns to the Old Game. A Pokémon documented playing a particular locomotor game as a juvenile stops for years, then participates again as an adult. The record prevents the assumption that play belongs only to juveniles.

14. The Quiet Yard. A facility has a day where no structured play is observed. Appetite, health and other behavior remain normal. Nothing is diagnosed and no emergency quest appears.

15. The Photographer Effect. A popular photography spot develops around a recurring play site. The behavior becomes less frequent during visitor peaks. Tourism and Visual Records can investigate disturbance without assuming the Pokémon are afraid.

16. The Play Site Moves Ten Meters. A wild group shifts a recurring object-play area after a path is rebuilt. The group remains present. A stale observer report incorrectly says the behavior vanished.

17. The Working Pokémon Break. An institutional Pokémon has a regular voluntary recreation period away from its work role. The observed play is not counted as task performance and refusal to work during that period is not disciplinary state.

18. The Competitive Retiree Finds Something New. A competitively retired Pokémon begins using a shallow water feature it never used during its active career. Aging, Care and Play preserve the role transition without treating retirement as decline.

19. The Juvenile After Evolution. A Pokémon that used a particular small object before evolution no longer fits the same setup afterward. The facility redesigns available options rather than interpreting non-use as loss of interest.

20. The Wild Playground After Closing. Urban Pokémon begin using a human public play area late at night. Public Space owns access/schedules; Urban Wildlife owns recurring presence; Play records the activity. The city must decide whether to alter lighting or maintenance without making the site a spawn farm.

21. The Water Trough Game. Several Pokémon repeatedly push floating leaves around a ranch water trough. Grassland and Working Pokémon records show the site has other functions, so recreation cannot block operational access without negotiation.

22. The Object Everyone Thought Was Enrichment. Staff believe a hanging object is used recreationally until review shows interactions occur almost exclusively around feeding time. The classification shifts toward functional behavior without deleting earlier observations.

23. The Game That Became Training. A Trainer notices a spontaneous chase pattern and later designs a formal positioning drill around it. The spontaneous episodes remain play; the later objective-based sessions belong to Training.

24. The Game That Did Not Become Training. A coach suggests turning a favorite activity into a drill. The Trainer decides to keep it recreational. No optimization pressure follows.

25. The Seasonal Ball Route. A lightweight ball repeatedly blows between three yards during a windy season and different Pokémon interact with it at each location. Material Culture tracks the same object; Meteorology explains movement; no one needs to “own” the story.

26. The Shelter Used for Play. A structure installed as shade/shelter becomes a climbing or chase feature during cool weather. Architecture owns the structure. Care reviews safety. The play layer records alternate use.

27. The Missing Participant. One member of a recurring play group stops appearing, while surveys confirm it is still present elsewhere and active. Researchers keep several hypotheses open instead of writing illness, conflict or exclusion into canon.

28. The Familiar Game With a New Partner. A persistent Pokémon performs a recurring play sequence with a different individual years later. The continuity belongs to the behavior and participant history, not to an inferred permanent pair bond.

29. The Enrichment Study Changes Its Mind. A research station initially ranks three options by raw use count. Later analysts realize one option was unavailable during several observation windows. The revised assessment keeps the original dataset and corrects the interpretation.

30. Nothing Happened in the Yard. A routine session records several safe choices, ordinary interaction and no incident. It is compressed into Chronicle. Years later it becomes valuable baseline evidence after the facility changes.

## Long arc: Five Summers at Cedar Yard

Year 1: a small care facility opens an outdoor yard with several simple recreation options. Staff collect only basic notes.

Year 2: different individuals show stable but context-dependent selections. The facility stops treating one “most popular” object as universally preferred.

Year 3: a heat season shifts most activity toward morning and shaded spaces. Care and Urban Heat investigate without assuming a welfare crisis.

Year 4: a new juvenile cohort uses the same structures differently. One older persistent Pokémon rarely joins social play but continues object interaction.

Year 5: the facility renovates the yard using five years of observations. Some old objects are repaired, some retired and one preserved as a local archive item.

The arc succeeds if the institution becomes better at offering choice and recording uncertainty. It does not need a villain, battle or mechanical reward.

## Long arc: The Toy With Four Histories

A durable object begins in a nursery.

It later travels with one Pokémon to a Trainer household, returns to a community facility after that Pokémon is released, and eventually becomes historically interesting because old photographs and logs preserve several unrelated interaction histories.

The object retains one Material Culture identity.

Each use context retains its own provenance.

The arc can connect Nursery, Pokémon Agency, Homes, Public Memory, Visual Records and Museums without claiming the object has magical or mechanical effects.

## Long arc: The Play Atlas

A regional research program records recreational and exploratory behavior across forests, cities, wetlands, farms and care facilities for many years.

Early editions overgeneralize from juveniles and from Pokémon living under human care.

Later editions separate species-level claims, local population patterns, life-stage observations and individual histories more carefully.

Some “play traditions” turn out to be independent responses to the same local affordance. Others become candidates for Social Learning research.

The atlas remains useful precisely because it preserves revised interpretations instead of pretending the first edition was perfect.

## Encounter contract: Nursery Yard Escalation

Narrative premise:

Staff are uncertain whether a rough interaction is still voluntary play. A separate threat or genuine escalation may require a battle.

FULL implementation dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement — BLOCKING when participants withdraw, re-enter or must be separated dynamically;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full turn/round lifecycle — PARTIAL;
- full stateful damage pipeline — PARTIAL if combat begins;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL when exact statuses are involved;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING as a complete family if protected lanes or reactions are needed;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL if authoritative items appear;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for WITHDRAW, REENGAGE, PROTECT or DEESCALATE goals;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

REDUCED implementation:

End recreation in world state. Staff move noncombatants to safety before AutoPTU opens. If a real confrontation remains, use a static arena and only actual combatants. The earlier interaction can remain `UNRESOLVED` in the behavior record.

## Encounter contract: Riverside Object Play Spillover

Narrative premise:

Wild Pokémon repeatedly interact with floating or rolling objects near a public route. Their movement sometimes crosses traffic.

FULL implementation:

The important missing pieces are complete movement, tactical AI for CROSS/WITHDRAW/non-hostile route goals, and adapter/playback. Dynamic water adds the blocking environment/reaction family if it becomes tactical.

REDUCED implementation:

Resolve route use and Pokémon movement before combat. Pause or reroute traffic. If another conflict appears, freeze one shoreline geometry and run a conventional battle. Play objects provide no combat modifier.

## Encounter contract: Enrichment Yard Equipment Failure

Narrative premise:

A recreational structure breaks while Pokémon are present. The continuing story is evacuation, inspection, repair and whether the revised option is voluntarily used later.

FULL implementation:

Requires complete movement for dynamic evacuation, tactical AI for WITHDRAW/REACH_EXIT/PROTECT and adapter/playback. Any falling-object, collapse or zone effect additionally requires the exact environmental mechanic to be validated.

REDUCED implementation:

Close and evacuate the yard through world state. Run any unrelated battle in a safe static area. Architecture and Material Culture handle repairs. The play layer later records reintroduction and interaction history.

## Non-combat scene: Choice Trial

A caretaker or researcher provides multiple safe options for a bounded observation window.

The output is which options were available and what was observed.

There is no success threshold, reward multiplier or mandatory preferred choice.

This scene should compress after repeated routine sessions.

## Hard non-inferences

- play -> XP;
- play -> Levels;
- play -> Tutor Points;
- play -> Loyalty/Friendship;
- play -> healing;
- play -> Injury recovery;
- play -> Status removal;
- play -> Poke Edge;
- repeated toy use -> mechanical Item proficiency;
- object play -> tool use;
- joining another Pokémon -> social learning;
- mixed-species play -> mutualism;
- play partners -> friendship or kinship;
- rough play -> battle;
- no play observed -> illness;
- no play observed -> dislike;
- adult age -> no play;
- juvenile status -> guaranteed play;
- toy placement -> spawn increase;
- Minecraft animation -> authoritative preference;
- camp interaction rewards from Sword/Shield -> PTU rules.
