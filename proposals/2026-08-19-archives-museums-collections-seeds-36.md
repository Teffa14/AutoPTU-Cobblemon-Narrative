# Archives, Museums & Collections Seeds — Pass 36

Status: NON-CANON Ouros candidates. Original material derived from high-level research patterns. No external characters, prose or plots are imported.

## 1. The Wrong Label

A museum specimen has been displayed for years under an obsolete classification. A new field observation suggests the label is wrong. The story is about verifying provenance, finding the original field notes and deciding how the institution publicly corrects itself.

## 2. The Honest Replica

A famous object in a regional hall is a clearly documented reconstruction. A rumor spreads that the museum has been deceiving visitors. Players can trace where the rumor started, compare records and help explain why replicas can still have educational value.

## 3. The Object That Was Never Accessioned

Workers discover an old crate in storage with no accession number. The contents are real, but nobody knows whether they were donated, loaned, deposited for research or simply left behind. The institution cannot responsibly display or dispose of them until provenance is reconstructed.

## 4. Closing Week

A temporary exhibition is ending. Several borrowed objects must be condition-checked, packed and returned through transport routes that are currently unreliable. The challenge is logistics and custody, not theft.

## 5. The Missing Field Notebook

A celebrated expedition is well documented except for one notebook cited repeatedly by later researchers. Nobody knows whether it was lost, retained by a family, misfiled or never deposited. Multiple archives contain indirect references.

## 6. The Quiet Reading Room

Players discover that different visitors keep requesting the same obscure set of records for unrelated reasons: a family-history question, an ecological survey and an old transport dispute. Their overlap reveals a larger historical connection without making any researcher secretly malicious.

## 7. Gallery Under Revision

A museum voluntarily closes one room after new archaeological evidence undermines the old exhibit narrative. Staff disagree over how much uncertainty should appear in the replacement display.

## 8. The Loan Nobody Wants to Move

A culturally important but physically fragile object is scheduled for a major regional event. Curators, conservators, event organizers and transport operators disagree about whether moving it is worth the risk.

## 9. The Cataloger's Winter

A small institution receives a huge donation from a retired researcher. Most of it is mundane but potentially valuable as long-term scientific data. The core problem is backlog, staffing and prioritization rather than finding a single treasure.

## 10. The Empty Case

A display case is empty and visitors immediately assume theft. Records show the object was legitimately removed for conservation, but the public notice failed to propagate after a communications outage. The incident becomes a media/public-trust problem.

## 11. The Returned Trophy

A battle institution returns a historic trophy to the community that originally created it while commissioning a replica for future tournaments. Public memory changes, but tournament records remain unchanged.

## 12. Two Libraries, Two Editions

Two regional libraries hold different editions of the same historical work. The revisions contradict each other. Players can compare marginalia, publication dates and later evidence to understand how the accepted story changed.

## 13. The Damaged Map Cabinet

A water leak damages maps from several decades. Some routes no longer exist; others were real only during specific seasonal or infrastructure states. Restoration becomes a way to reconstruct changing geography.

## 14. Museum Night Survey

Staff notice that wild Pokémon regularly enter the grounds after closing. The goal is to observe why before installing barriers. The answer may involve light, warmth, food, roosting or an older habitat connection.

## 15. The Restricted Coordinates

An archive contains exact locations for a sensitive nesting site. The records are historically important but unrestricted publication could create conservation pressure. Players help design a useful public version while preserving full research access under controlled conditions.

## 16. The Donor's Version

A major donor financed an exhibit and supplied a family history that conflicts with other records. The institution must distinguish donor testimony from verified fact without turning the donor automatically into a villain.

## 17. The Traveling Science Hall

A mobile exhibition visits small settlements that lack permanent museums. Each stop adds local observations and objects to a temporary community display. The traveling institution becomes a way for remote regions to see each other's discoveries.

## 18. The Disputed Restoration

A damaged object can be restored to look complete, stabilized in its current state or reconstructed digitally. Different stakeholders prefer different approaches. None of the options grants a mechanical bonus.

## 19. The Archive of Failed Ideas

A research institute preserves abandoned hypotheses, failed prototypes and null results. A current crisis resembles one of those old failures closely enough that revisiting it becomes useful without proving the old idea correct.

## 20. The Small Museum Becomes Important

A minor local collection accumulates relevance over several arcs because players repeatedly deposit copies of maps, field reports, photographs and recovered contextual data. Its importance emerges from world history rather than a prewritten legendary artifact.

## 21. The Exhibit That Splits the Town

A new exhibit presents two historically supported interpretations of a founding event. Residents disagree over whether presenting uncertainty is responsible scholarship or disrespectful indecision. The conflict stays social/civic unless other world state escalates it.

## 22. The Researcher's Boxes

A deceased NPC researcher left boxes of notes, specimens and correspondence. The archive can preserve validated past facts but cannot invent the researcher's private thoughts or undocumented later decisions.

## 23. The Fossil Without a Site

A real fossil enters a museum with no reliable excavation context. It may still be scientifically useful, but its information value is lower than a comparable specimen with full provenance. The quest is to recover context, not to prove the fossil rare.

## 24. The Visitor Who Knows More

A visitor notices that an exhibit caption is wrong because they have firsthand experience from a prior player-driven event. The institution can request evidence, update the record and credit the contribution, turning Chronicle history into public history.

## 25. The Sealed Case File

An old case is closed, but part of its archive remains restricted because it contains private medical or identity information. Players investigating the public history must find independent evidence instead of receiving every sealed record automatically.

## 26. The Museum in Recovery

After a regional crisis, the institution prioritizes people and Pokémon before collections. Later arcs deal with drying records, documenting losses, borrowing replacements, rebuilding storage and deciding which exhibitions should return first.

## Longer arc A — The Catalogue That Changed the Region

Phase 1: a small misidentification is discovered in a local collection.
Phase 2: related catalog records in several institutions reveal a pattern of older classifications based on incomplete field knowledge.
Phase 3: new expeditions test the revised model.
Phase 4: museums, researchers and public media update different parts of the story at different speeds.
Phase 5: the region eventually gains a better public understanding, but old books and memories remain as historical evidence of how knowledge changed.

No mastermind or forgery is required.

## Longer arc B — The Traveling Collection

Phase 1: several institutions agree to exchange objects and exhibits.
Phase 2: each transfer exposes differences in catalog practice, access policy and public interpretation.
Phase 3: transport, conservation and scheduling problems create Jobs and Social scenes.
Phase 4: one disputed object forces a formal return/stewardship review.
Phase 5: the network survives by adopting better shared provenance standards rather than centralizing every collection.

## Longer arc C — The Library Beneath the Library

Phase 1: renovation exposes an older sealed stack area beneath a current civic library.
Phase 2: most material is ordinary institutional history, but it fills major gaps in settlement chronology.
Phase 3: cross-references connect the holdings to archaeology, family records, old route maps and ecological observations.
Phase 4: players help prioritize conservation and public access while some records remain restricted for valid reasons.
Phase 5: the reopened archive becomes a persistent research hub whose holdings generate future hooks rather than resolving into one final treasure.
