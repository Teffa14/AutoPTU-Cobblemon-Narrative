# Pokémon Agency, Partnership & Release Seeds — Pass 45

Status: NON-CANON Ouros candidates.

These concepts are original structures derived from the research in Pass 45. They must not be promoted to canon without PTU/Caelo mechanical review and continuity review.

## 1. The First Partner Who Does Not Rush

A new Trainer is assigned or introduced to a first partner that cooperates with basic travel but is reluctant to enter formal battles immediately.

The story is about learning routines and limits, not forcing an instant friendship label.

Mechanical obedience must remain unresolved until PTU/Caelo rules support it.

## 2. Orchard Return

A Pokémon released years earlier is repeatedly observed in the same orchard during one season.

The former Trainer can visit, but previous ownership does not automatically grant command authority.

## 3. The Old Ball in the Drawer

A household keeps the old Poké Ball and records of a former partner that has lived independently for years.

The object becomes a family-memory artifact without implying the Pokémon is still owned.

## 4. Temporary Flood Partner

A wild Pokémon familiar with a floodplain repeatedly assists rescue crews during high water.

It cooperates during specific emergencies and returns to its habitat afterward.

## 5. Transfer Day

A legitimate rehoming event is complicated by transport delays, missing paperwork and a Pokémon that has never met the new caretaker before.

The challenge is logistics and continuity, not a guaranteed obedience conflict.

## 6. The Clinic Regular

A wild Pokémon appears at the same clinic after injuries several times over multiple years.

Staff recognize it and prepare appropriately, but treatment does not make the clinic its owner.

## 7. Former Champion, New Routine

A retired competitive Pokémon no longer battles formally and now spends most days at a garden or workshop.

Its public reputation survives even though its daily life has changed.

## 8. The Pokémon Who Stayed Behind

During a relocation, one long-term partner remains at an old settlement because an authored event established a local responsibility or residence.

The Trainer and Pokémon can remain associated despite living apart.

## 9. Sanctuary Handoff

A rescued Pokémon moves from emergency custody to a sanctuary.

The handoff preserves medical, habitat and provenance records without assuming permanent ownership by the rescuers.

## 10. The Familiar Wild Face

A player repeatedly encounters the same identifiable wild Pokémon on one route.

It can remember observable interactions through entity history without becoming a hidden Friendship meter.

## 11. Ranger-Like Local Helper

A settlement has a tradition of working temporarily with wild Pokémon during seasonal maintenance.

The partnership ends after the task unless a separate authored event changes the relationship.

## 12. The New Trainer, Old Methods

A transferred Pokémon has a long history with another Trainer's routines.

The new Trainer must learn what is already documented about training signals and preferences.

The system must not invent disobedience penalties.

## 13. A Poké Ball Without a Pokémon

An archive preserves a ball associated with a famous historic partner, but the Pokémon's later fate is unknown.

The artifact does not prove death, ownership continuity or current location.

## 14. The Reappearing Migration Partner

A former partner leaves to join a migration and is sighted again during later seasons.

Each sighting can update ecology and Chronicle state without automatically restarting the old partnership.

## 15. Institutional Partner Rotation

A ferry service works with several Pokémon over many years.

Staff changes, retirement and different schedules mean no single worker can assume command authority over every institutional Pokémon.

## 16. The Partner Who Knows the Route

A Pokémon has years of observed experience on one mountain trail.

Its route familiarity is narrative history, not a free Navigation bonus unless the mechanical system supports one.

## 17. Research Contact, Not Specimen

Researchers repeatedly observe and interact with one identifiable wild Pokémon.

The individual becomes a long-running research contact without being captured or reduced to a sample source.

## 18. Rehomed but Remembered

A Pokémon transferred to another household still appears in the former household's photographs, club archive and public history.

The new association does not erase old relationships.

## 19. The Voluntary Worksite Visitor

A wild Pokémon begins appearing at a repair yard because of a stable environmental or food-related reason.

Workers may adapt to its presence without declaring it an employee or mascot automatically.

## 20. Release Site Stewardship

A conservation group monitors an area where rehabilitated Pokémon are released.

Each individual can retain a provenance chain without becoming permanently tracked in intrusive detail.

## 21. The Trainer Who Misreads Familiarity

An NPC assumes a frequently encountered wild Pokémon wants to be caught because it approaches them often.

Evidence later shows only that the Pokémon is comfortable using the same resource site.

The story is about correcting an interpretation, not punishing the NPC as malicious.

## 22. Battle Partner for One Day

During a crisis, an institution authorizes a temporary battle partnership between a specialist and a Pokémon normally associated with another team or facility.

Command authority and duration must be explicit.

## 23. The Former Partner's New Collective

A released Pokémon joins a wild collective.

When its former Trainer returns, the collective remains an independent ecological actor.

Historical familiarity cannot automatically bypass group behavior.

## 24. Retirement Ceremony Without Ownership Change

A town honors a Pokémon retiring from public service.

The event changes public memory and schedule, but custody and residence may remain exactly the same.

## 25. Two Caretakers, One Pokémon

A Pokémon routinely lives across two locations under an authored shared-care arrangement.

The system must support multiple care roles without inventing legal ownership rules.

## 26. The Missing Association Record

A famous Pokémon is clearly linked to a historic Trainer in photographs, but the formal record of how the association began has been lost.

Public memory is strong while provenance remains incomplete.

## 27. The Partner Who Refuses One Place

A Pokémon consistently avoids one building after an authored or observed event.

The system records the location-specific behavior and leaves the cause unresolved until evidence exists.

## 28. New Home, Same Trainer

A Trainer moves regions while one partner temporarily remains at a nursery or family residence.

Party absence does not automatically end association.

## 29. The Rescue Pokémon Who Never Joins

A wild Pokémon assists players throughout a rescue arc and then returns to its ordinary territory.

The emotional payoff comes from continuity and recognition, not from mandatory capture.

## 30. A Partnership Recorded Incorrectly

An old newspaper calls a Pokémon the Trainer's 'owned partner', while institutional records only show temporary service together.

The resulting research quest connects media history, archives and agency state.

# Long arc A — The Long Goodbye

A long-standing partner gradually moves from active battle roster to local institutional work, then to retirement.

Possible stages:

1. reduced competitive schedule;
2. new routine at a settlement institution;
3. public misunderstanding that the Pokémon was abandoned;
4. clarification through records and observed visits;
5. eventual retirement event;
6. later callbacks when new Trainers know the Pokémon through local history.

The arc never requires permanent separation if canon does not support it.

# Long arc B — The River Familiar

A wild Pokémon becomes recognizable across several years of river-related stories.

Year one:
- researchers record first sightings.

Year two:
- it assists during a flood incident.

Year three:
- tourists wrongly assume it is a town mascot.

Year four:
- conservation staff discover its home range is larger than believed.

Year five:
- a route project affects one of its regular sites.

The Pokémon remains wild throughout unless an explicit later event changes that.

# Long arc C — Three Trainers, One History

A Pokémon has three major human associations across its life:

- an original Trainer;
- an institutional caretaker during a long recovery or service period;
- a later active Trainer.

The campaign uses records, meetings and public memory to show that each relationship is real without ranking them as 'true owner' or 'best friend' unless canon actually establishes that.
