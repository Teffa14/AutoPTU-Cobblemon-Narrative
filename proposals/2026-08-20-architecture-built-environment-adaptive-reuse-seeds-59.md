# Ouros Architecture & Built Environment Seeds — Pass 59

Status: NON-CANON PROPOSALS.

These are original Ouros candidates derived from high-level research patterns. They do not copy source characters, cities, plots or distinctive buildings.

## 1. The Market in the Old Depot

A freight depot lost most rail traffic years ago. Vendors now use the main hall, but loading bays, service tracks and staff corridors remain visible. A new accessibility project reveals that one sealed corridor still connects to an older district utility route.

## 2. The Street That Kept Its Name

A fire destroyed the original civic hall decades ago. The hall no longer exists, but the street, bus stop and local festival still use its name. A new public-works proposal creates conflict over whether to preserve the foundation or build directly over it.

## 3. Three Versions of the Same Bridge

The current bridge is the third structure at the same crossing. Historical maps, photographs and public memory mix details from all three versions. A survey must determine which foundation belongs to which period before repairs begin.

## 4. The Roof Habitat

A warehouse district gradually gains green roofs. Wild Pokémon begin using several rooftops as resting sites. Residents like the change, but one planned rooftop venue overlaps with the most frequently used habitat area.

## 5. The Station Without Trains

A station closed after a route realignment. The waiting room became a clinic annex while platforms became a pedestrian market. A reopening proposal would restore transport but displace several later uses.

## 6. The House Inside the Factory

A former industrial building was divided into workshops, housing and a club space over many years. No single floor plan describes the current structure. An emergency drill exposes several doors that exist only on obsolete plans.

## 7. The Courtyard Everyone Uses

A privately managed block contains a courtyard that residents have treated as a public shortcut for decades. Minecraft access and social permission no longer match cleanly. Ouros must preserve the distinction rather than deciding ownership from foot traffic.

## 8. The Uneven Arcade

A covered shopping street was rebuilt in stages after several storms. Some sections have modern drainage and step-free access; others preserve older floor levels. A tourism surge exposes the practical differences.

## 9. The Bellhouse Conversion

A small historic signal building no longer serves transport. Local groups propose a reading room, field station or small museum. Each option preserves different physical features and creates different service dependencies.

## 10. The Warehouse That Became a Nesting Site

A long-abandoned warehouse has become a seasonal nesting location. A developer proposes reuse rather than demolition, but no one yet knows whether construction timing can coexist with the seasonal occupation.

## 11. The False Ancient Wall

A wall is popularly described as the oldest surviving structure in town. Material analysis suggests much of it was rebuilt later using salvaged stone. The wall remains culturally important even if the popular history is wrong.

## 12. The New Road Through an Old Block

A settlement wants a direct transport route through an irregular historic district. The shorter route would improve service access but remove several courtyards and narrow passages that currently support local businesses and wildlife movement.

## 13. The Building Everyone Draws Wrong

Postcards show a famous landmark with a symmetrical façade. Survey measurements reveal decades of small additions made it asymmetrical. The idealized image becomes a research topic rather than proof that the current building was altered illegally.

## 14. Temporary Shelter, Permanent Neighborhood

Emergency housing built after a crisis remains in use years later. Residents have added gardens, workshops and small services. A planned replacement now has to account for the fact that temporary structures became a real neighborhood.

## 15. The Empty Upper Floors

A busy market occupies the ground floor of an older commercial building while upper levels remain unused. Opening them could create housing or club space, but surveys reveal obsolete service systems and uncertain access routes.

## 16. The Underpass Garden

A neglected underpass is gradually converted into a shaded public garden. Pokémon begin using the cooler corridor. A future transport expansion must decide whether to preserve the garden, relocate it or redesign the route.

## 17. The Stair Street

A hillside district grew around a steep pedestrian route. A new accessible connection is proposed without erasing the historic stair street. The project becomes about adding a parallel route rather than replacing the old one.

## 18. The Shopfronts Behind the Facade

A row of shops appears uniform from the street, but each unit contains fragments from different older structures. Renovation reveals walls, floor levels and service routes that do not align.

## 19. The Ruin That Became a Plaza

A damaged institutional building was stabilized but never rebuilt. Residents gradually use the open interior as a plaza. A future generation debates whether reconstruction would restore history or destroy the place's newer identity.

## 20. The Ferry Warehouse

A harbor warehouse was converted into passenger facilities when ferry traffic increased. Cargo infrastructure remains underneath public circulation. A service failure exposes the older logistics network and forces temporary rerouting.

## 21. The Clinic in the Old Hotel

A former hotel became a clinic during a long recovery period. Guest rooms became treatment rooms; a ballroom became shared services. The building still contains hospitality-era spaces that no longer match the clinic's needs.

## 22. The Alley That Became a Main Route

A narrow service lane becomes heavily used after a new transit stop opens nearby. Businesses adapt entrances toward it, but emergency access and crowd flow were never designed for the new role.

## 23. The District With Two Centers

A settlement expanded around a new station while the old civic center remained active. Both districts retain services and public spaces. Quest generation should treat the city as polycentric rather than funnel every event into one hub.

## 24. The Foundation Under the Gym

Routine renovation exposes a foundation older than the current Gym. The discovery does not prove the prior structure's purpose. Battle schedules, archaeological access and public interest must coexist while evidence is collected.

## 25. The School After the School

An academy moved to a larger campus. Its old building became apartments, a small workshop and a community room. Alumni still refer to the building by its school name, creating confusion in historical records and directions.

## 26. The Lighted Waterfront

A waterfront redevelopment improves safety and activity at night. Researchers later observe a change in nocturnal Pokémon behavior. The lighting project becomes a cross-system investigation rather than an automatic conservation failure.

## 27. The Reconstructed Landmark

A famous tower was rebuilt after severe damage. The public treats it as the same landmark. Archives preserve fragments of the original. Ouros records continuity of identity while preserving that the current physical version is newer.

## 28. The Invisible District Boundary

Residents use one neighborhood name while official maps use another boundary. Businesses, delivery routes and public memory follow different versions. No single polygon becomes truth without an authored institutional definition.

## 29. The Building That Outlived Its Institution

An organization dissolved decades ago, but its headquarters survives and now hosts several unrelated tenants. Old insignia, storage rooms and archives create story hooks without implying the organization secretly still exists.

## 30. The Street Widening That Never Finished

A widening project was completed on three blocks and abandoned on the fourth after conditions changed. The partial intervention creates unusual frontage, leftover land and mismatched utilities that continue shaping local activity.

# Longer arcs

## Arc A — Five Lives of the Foundry

An old foundry passes through industrial use, closure, emergency shelter, workshop collective and mixed public use over many years. Each phase retains some physical features and creates new claims about what deserves preservation. Players encounter the same structure across multiple careers and generations, eventually understanding the building through its accumulated layers rather than one hidden secret.

## Arc B — The City That Grew Sideways

A settlement originally grows along a river. A later station creates a second center, a university creates a third cluster and protected habitat prevents direct infill between them. Transport, services, housing and social life gradually create a polycentric city. The arc can span many small projects without requiring a villain or a single master plan.

## Arc C — After the Great Fire

A historic district suffers major damage. Recovery begins with safety and temporary uses, then becomes a debate over reconstruction, adaptive reuse, memorial preservation, housing needs and ecological opportunities created by vacant parcels. Several years later, the district visibly contains old fragments, rebuilt structures and new buildings instead of reverting to its previous snapshot.
