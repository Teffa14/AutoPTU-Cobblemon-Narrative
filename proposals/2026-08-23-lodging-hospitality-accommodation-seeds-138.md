# Ouros Lodging, Hospitality & Accommodation Seeds — Pass 138

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Every item requires continuity, originality, PTU/Caelo and implementation review before canon promotion.

These candidates use the Pass 138 lodging layer. They are designed to produce durable Chronicle without turning every overnight stay into a quest.

## 1. The Room That Is Confirmed but Not Ready

A visiting Trainer has a valid reservation during a festival, but the assigned room remains under maintenance after a plumbing failure. The useful scene is operational: front desk, housekeeping and the guest all have correct but incomplete information.

Possible outcomes include a room swap, delayed check-in, another property or voluntary cancellation. No fraud is assumed.

## 2. The Last Bed Before North Pass

A small mountain lodge has one allocatable sleeping space left before a weather closure. Several travelers have legitimate reasons to request it.

The decision should depend on explicit policy, timing and player choice. The generator must not invent a hidden moral ranking.

## 3. The Hotel With Empty Rooms and No Vacancy

Several rooms are physically empty but held for an incoming emergency-response team. Walk-in guests see empty windows and assume staff are lying.

The conflict is caused by allocation state, not deception.

## 4. Three Inns, Three Kinds of Full

One property has every room occupied. One has rooms but no staff to turn them over. One has rooms ready but no units matching an accessibility request.

A regional event exposes that “no vacancy” can have different causes.

## 5. The Returning Research Team

The same field researchers return to one inn every wet season for five years. Staff know their booking pattern and equipment-storage needs, but this does not imply friendship or privileged access.

Their changing dates become a useful indirect record of environmental change.

## 6. The Guestbook Has a Missing Year

A century-old lodge preserves guestbooks except for one season. The gap may reflect renovation, lost records, a change in operator or deliberate removal.

The missing volume is an archival question, not proof of conspiracy.

## 7. The Bungalow That Changed Function

A resort cottage becomes staff housing, then research quarters, then guest accommodation again after twenty years.

The same `unit_id` or supersession chain preserves physical and operational history across uses.

## 8. The Tournament Overflow Town

A major battle circuit event fills lodging in the host city. A nearby town receives competitors, families, media crews and vendors who commute in by rail each day.

Demography records temporary presence; Rail records commuter pressure; Lodging records stays.

## 9. The No-Show Who Arrived Elsewhere

A guest is marked no-show at one inn, but later evidence shows they arrived safely at another property after a route diversion.

The original no-show record remains historically correct.

## 10. The Room Key Still Works

A guest checks out, but an old physical key or token still opens a side door because the lock was never updated.

Physical access failure does not restore reservation rights or create permission.

## 11. The Pokémon Companion Needs Another Space

A Trainer’s reserved room is suitable for the human guest but not for the companion Pokémon’s validated physical needs. The property offers a courtyard, pool annex or other alternative if available.

The story is about facility fit, not species exclusion as a universal rule.

## 12. The Companion Refuses the Assigned Space

A Pokémon repeatedly withdraws from an accommodation area despite the room being operationally suitable. The handler chooses another arrangement.

The venue records the change without inventing a diagnosis or mechanical Status.

## 13. The Inn That Became a Shelter for Three Nights

A flood evacuation temporarily converts a commercial inn into emergency accommodation. Existing guests are moved, some voluntarily to nearby towns.

After the crisis, ordinary operations resume, but the conversion remains part of property history.

## 14. The Empty Lobby During the Biggest Festival

Every room is full, yet the lobby is unusually quiet because event schedules keep guests away all day.

Loaded NPC count must not be used as occupancy truth.

## 15. The Room With Two Historical Numbers

A renovation renumbers corridors. An old diary refers to Room 12; the current building has a different Room 12.

Architecture + Lodging revision history resolves whether both references point to the same physical unit.

## 16. The Lost Item Was Never Lost

Housekeeping reports an object left behind. The guest says it was intentionally given to another person who had already collected it.

Material Culture and custody resolve the chain. No theft is inferred.

## 17. The Lost Item Was in the Wrong Room Record

An object found during room turnover is associated with the wrong prior stay because two rooms swapped assignments at check-in.

The correction updates the finding context without rewriting the item’s physical history.

## 18. The Old Inn Has the Best Route Knowledge

A roadside inn has decades of public route notices, closure boards and guest questions. Its staff are useful local observers but not omniscient guides.

Cartography and Travel decide route truth.

## 19. The Quiet-Floor Experiment

A lodging property dedicates one floor or building to lower-noise stays during migration season and tracks whether complaints or wildlife disturbance change.

No mechanical stealth or sleep bonus is created.

## 20. The Breakfast Is Full, the Hotel Is Not

A venue’s breakfast room reaches capacity because locals and day visitors also use it. Hotel occupancy is moderate.

Food venue capacity and room occupancy remain separate.

## 21. The Worker Bunkhouse Outlives the Project

Temporary construction housing remains after a major project ends. It later becomes seasonal-worker housing or a low-cost hostel.

Architecture owns adaptive reuse; Lodging owns the stay history.

## 22. The Same Guest, Different Name Record

A returning guest changed their public name or the property previously stored an alias. Identity/Record Linkage determines whether the stays belong to one actor.

Staff recognition alone is insufficient evidence.

## 23. The Lodge at the Migration Stopover

A small lodge exists beside a recurring Pokémon stopover. Tourism grows gradually around the seasonal event.

One year the migration shifts. The lodge remains operational, but demand changes.

## 24. The Hotel That Learned Not to Advertise a Pokémon

A venue becomes famous after a wild Pokémon is repeatedly seen nearby. Crowds disturb the site. Years later the operator removes the Pokémon from advertising and works with conservation staff.

The wild individual was never hotel property.

## 25. The Accessible Room Became Storage

An old operational shortcut gradually turned an accessible guest room into overflow storage between busy weeks. A new reservation exposes the problem.

The meaningful response is operational correction, not a permanent character judgment.

## 26. The Last Ferry, No Room

Travelers reach an island after the return ferry is cancelled and discover lodging is full.

Possible alternatives include public emergency space, authorized camping, a later ferry or sleeping aboard a vessel only if Maritime permits it. The generator cannot conjure capacity.

## 27. The Dormitory Allocation Dispute

A research institute has enough beds overall but not enough rooms for several teams’ equipment, privacy and accessibility constraints.

The conflict is allocation and facility fit, not simple bed count.

## 28. The Property With No Front Desk at Night

A rural guesthouse uses scheduled arrival windows rather than 24-hour staffing. A late train causes multiple guests to arrive after the operator leaves.

Rail and Lodging together produce the problem; neither state is incorrect.

## 29. The Former Partner Returns to the Courtyard

A released former partner is observed years later in the courtyard of a lodge once used by the Trainer.

The observation can be emotionally meaningful without asserting motive, ownership, recognition or desire to rejoin.

## 30. Nothing Happened at Room 7

Room 7 has five years of ordinary stays, cleaning, minor repairs and no notable incident.

That history matters because it gives the world baseline and prevents every persistent room from becoming a mystery box.

# Long arc A — Five Seasons at Meridian Junction Hotel

Year 1: A new rail service increases overnight demand.

Year 2: A tournament creates the first full-capacity weekend and pushes overflow into nearby guesthouses.

Year 3: An accessibility retrofit removes several rooms from inventory temporarily but improves future matching.

Year 4: A storm closes rail access, turning the property into short-term accommodation for stranded travelers rather than event guests.

Year 5: A new hostel opens nearby. The hotel is no longer always full, but returning guests, staff history and old event photographs make it a recognized local institution.

The arc measures success through accumulated history and adaptation, not endless growth.

# Long arc B — One Room, Six Uses

A single physical room begins as an ordinary guest room. It later becomes temporary medical overflow during a crisis, staff quarters during renovation, accessible accommodation after retrofit, a research-team room for several seasons and eventually a small heritage interpretation room when the property changes use.

Each phase keeps its own authority. Care does not make it permanently clinical. Lodging does not own the museum phase. Architecture preserves physical revisions.

# Long arc C — The Lodging Road

A historic travel corridor once relies on small inns spaced one day apart. Rail then reduces demand. Some inns close; one becomes a café, one worker housing, one stays open for hikers, and another later becomes important again when a new research station opens.

The arc connects Travel, Rail, Demography, Architecture, Food, Markets and Public Memory while allowing quiet years with no quest.

Primarily non-combat.
