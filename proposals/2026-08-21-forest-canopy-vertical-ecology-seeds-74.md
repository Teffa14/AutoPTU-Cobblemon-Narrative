# Forest canopy & vertical ecology seeds — pass 74

Status: NON-CANON CANDIDATES. Original Ouros material derived from high-level research patterns. No external plot, prose or character is transplanted.

## 1. The Missing Crown Route

A route used by arboreal Pokémon disappears from survey records after one old canopy tree falls. Ground encounters look normal. Repeated upper-canopy observations decline.

Possible causes remain open: lost branch connectivity, changed food availability, storm displacement, another route replacing it or observer bias.

## 2. The Tree Everyone Counts Twice

Two research teams catalogue what they believe are two giant trees on opposite sides of a ravine. Historical maps suggest the ravine changed course and the records may refer to the same landmark tree from different viewpoints.

## 3. Hollow Number Seven

A cavity used repeatedly over several years becomes a reference point for wildlife surveys. One season it remains empty while neighboring cavities remain active.

The absence is evidence, not a conclusion.

## 4. The Rope Bridge Generation

A public canopy bridge has been rebuilt three times. Older residents still use names based on the first alignment, while current maps show the third.

A route-history quest can reconstruct why each version changed.

## 5. The Gap That Worked

A storm creates a canopy gap. Several years later, regeneration succeeds unusually well. The interesting question becomes why this gap recovered better than nearby ones.

## 6. The Gap That Did Not

A second opening of similar size fills with a different vegetation structure. The two sites become a long-running comparative research project.

## 7. The Cavity Maker

Fresh tree cavities appear along a short corridor. Pikipek are one plausible explanation, but old damage, decay and another species remain alternatives until observed.

## 8. Upper Forest, Empty Floor

A player reports that a species has vanished because none appear on the trail. Canopy observations show normal activity above.

This becomes a lesson in vertical sampling rather than a false extinction event.

## 9. The Fallen Landmark

A famous old tree falls naturally. Its story does not end.

The same entity becomes:
- blocked route;
- new canopy gap;
- deadwood habitat;
- public-memory event;
- material-culture question;
- multi-year decomposition object.

## 10. The Nest Nobody Owns

A nest sits beside a maintained research platform. Players can observe it, but physical proximity gives neither custody nor ownership.

## 11. The Midstory Bloom

A flowering plant becomes abundant only in the middle layer after a canopy disturbance. Pollinator observations shift vertically without the regional population necessarily changing.

## 12. Branchline Survey

Cartographers attempt to map an arboreal route used by Pokémon. Several connections are temporary because branches flex, break or become inaccessible after weather events.

The resulting map has a validity date.

## 13. The Quiet Crown

The forest soundscape remains active at ground level but loses a familiar upper-canopy call during one seasonal window.

Soundscape data opens an ecological investigation without identifying a cause.

## 14. Canopy Market Day

A settlement with maintained elevated walkways hosts a seasonal market above ground level. Increased visitors create pressure on bridges, waste systems and nearby habitat.

This uses Tourism, Workplaces, Infrastructure and Conservation together.

## 15. The Emergency Descent

A canopy clinic or research outpost loses one access route. The challenge is moving people and supplies through remaining infrastructure before weather worsens.

Reduced implementation resolves evacuation outside AutoPTU.

## 16. The Old Platform

An abandoned research platform becomes habitat. Reopening it for a new study creates a legitimate conflict between scientific access and current occupancy.

## 17. The Orchard Edge Ladder

A farm installs elevated access for harvest and maintenance. Wild Pokémon begin using the structure as a route.

No one intended to create habitat, but the world adapts.

## 18. The Bridge Below the Nest

A busy route runs directly under a recurring nesting site. Most years there is no issue. One year disturbance rises because festival traffic and construction overlap.

## 19. The Canopy Archive

A decades-long collection of maps, photographs and field notes records the same grove at different heights. A historical interpretation changes when an overlooked upper-canopy photograph is catalogued correctly.

## 20. The Broken Crown After Fire

A burn mosaic leaves one large tree alive but badly damaged. It remains an important vertical refuge while lower vegetation regenerates.

The tree may survive, decline or fall later. The system must not decide early.

## 21. The City Under the Trees

A ground-level neighborhood grows beneath a long-established canopy district. New lighting, delivery routes and emergency access gradually change the ecological relationship between the two layers.

## 22. The Fruit Route

A Passimian subgroup is repeatedly observed moving between two fruiting zones. The route changes after one food source fails.

Do not generalize the observed social structure to unrelated species.

## 23. The Hollow Clinic Call

A wild Pokémon repeatedly appears near a cavity with an injured or unwell individual inside. Players initially assume aggression or territorial behavior. The care layer must resolve health facts separately.

## 24. The Treehouse Without a Town

An abandoned elevated structure is discovered deep in a forest. It may have been a research post, hunting platform, lookout, temporary camp or something else.

Architecture, archives and archaeology each provide evidence without instantly resolving its purpose.

## 25. The Storm Census

After a major wind event, researchers survey which crowns, cavities and bridges remain. The immediate count later becomes baseline evidence for years of regeneration.

## 26. The Cavity Conflict That Isn't

Two species use the same persistent tree at different times of day. Observers initially report competition, but repeated observation shows temporal separation.

## 27. The Laddered Grove

A conservation group installs minimal observation access to reduce trampling at ground level. Years later the structure itself requires maintenance and changes visitor flow.

## 28. The Last Emergent

One very tall tree remains above an otherwise even-aged forest. It becomes a landmark, acoustic reference, roosting site and weather observation point.

Its eventual loss would affect several systems at once.

## 29. The Crown Seen From Three Towns

Three settlements use the same distant emergent tree as a navigational landmark but call it by different names and attach different histories to it.

The cartography and public-memory layers can preserve all three traditions.

## 30. The Forest Above the Trail

A heavily traveled route passes through a forest whose most active ecological layer is above the players. Ground encounters remain sparse while upper-layer observation, calls and falling fruit reveal a much busier world.

The goal is to make the forest feel inhabited without spawning mobs every few meters.

# Longer arcs

## Arc A — Five Years of One Tree

Year 1: a landmark tree is surveyed and its cavities recorded.

Year 2: a storm damages one crown and shifts an arboreal route.

Year 3: a cavity gains repeated occupancy while a canopy gap below changes understory light.

Year 4: the tree becomes a local conservation question because a bridge project needs clearance.

Year 5+: the tree either survives under a revised design, falls naturally or is removed after an authored decision. Its provenance continues into deadwood, material culture, memorials or habitat state.

No single ending is predetermined.

## Arc B — The Two Forests in One Place

A region's ground surveys and canopy surveys appear to describe two different ecosystems despite sharing the same footprint.

Across several seasons, players and institutions build a vertical atlas combining:
- flora;
- soundscape;
- light;
- cavity use;
- arboreal collectives;
- nesting;
- canopy routes;
- disturbance history.

The arc ends with a better model of the forest, not necessarily a villain or boss.

## Arc C — The Elevated District

A settlement gradually develops maintained canopy infrastructure.

Phase 1: a small research platform.
Phase 2: public access and a bridge.
Phase 3: service demand, maintenance staff and emergency rules.
Phase 4: shops or housing proposals.
Phase 5: conservation and ecological consequences become visible.

Players can influence whether the district remains limited, expands, relocates or adopts a different design.

# Encounter contracts

## Canopy Bridge Failure

Narrative premise: a maintained elevated connection is damaged while nearby wild Pokémon continue using natural crowns.

Full version:
- multiple elevations;
- explicit safe/unsafe edges;
- reach-exit/protect objectives;
- legal alternate movement modes;
- possible fall/forced-movement consequences only after exact rules are verified;
- objective-aware AI;
- semantic Minecraft playback.

Reduced version:
- resolve route damage and evacuation in overworld;
- choose one stable platform or clearing;
- start a conventional static AutoPTU battle only if combat occurs;
- no falling, branch collapse or vertical cover mechanics.

## Hollow Tree Care Call

Narrative premise: activity around a persistent cavity leads to a health/investigation scene.

Full version:
- layered access;
- patient/nest protection;
- non-KO withdrawal behavior;
- vertical traversal where legal.

Reduced version:
- reach cavity through overworld exploration;
- keep patient/nest outside the grid;
- if defenders attack, use a single static arena and ordinary legal battle resolution.

## Gap Survey After Storm

Narrative premise: a newly opened canopy gap changes observation conditions and route access.

Full version:
- multiple observation elevations;
- explicit movement between layers;
- changed sightlines only through a future visibility model;
- noncombat objectives.

Reduced version:
- survey and sampling remain overworld actions;
- battle, if any, occurs in a frozen clearing;
- no invented concealment, falling-branch damage, Rough Terrain or buffs.
