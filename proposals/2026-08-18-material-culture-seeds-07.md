# Ouros Material Culture & Crafting Seeds — Pass 07

Status: NON-CANON proposals. Original Ouros candidates derived from structural research. Locations, institutions, professions, materials and exact mechanics remain placeholders until canon and PTU/AutoPTU review.

## Seed CA — The Maker's Mark

A respected local craftsperson places a recognizable mark on commissioned equipment.

Years later, an older item carrying the same mark appears far from the workshop.

The mark can reveal:
- where the object was probably made;
- the approximate period of the artisan's career;
- a trade route or former customer connection;
- whether the workshop changed hands;
- why a collector or institution recognizes it.

The mark is narrative provenance, not an item bonus. Exact craftable objects remain governed by PTU/Caelo.

## Seed CB — The Berry Cooperative

Several growers and food-oriented specialists share access to an important local crop. A weather or ecological change reduces one ingredient for the season.

The resulting problem can create several lanes:
- researchers determine whether the decline is temporary;
- explorers look for another legal source;
- merchants arrange imports;
- growers debate rationing and seed preservation;
- players help protect a remaining harvest;
- an artisan tests substitutes.

The system should not automatically create famine or severe price inflation. The shortage exists to create understandable choices and relationships.

## Seed CC — The Workshop Without Parts

A specialist is present and willing to work, but a disrupted supply route has removed one required material or tool component.

Possible solutions:
- restore the route;
- locate an alternate supplier;
- negotiate temporary access to another workshop;
- recover a delayed shipment;
- wait for the route to reopen;
- use another approved service with different tradeoffs.

This turns service availability into world state rather than an arbitrary shop cooldown.

## Seed CD — The Traveling Craft Fair

A recurring public event brings artisans, repair specialists, cooks, researchers, merchants and collectors from several regions into one settlement.

Players can participate through:
- commissions;
- demonstrations;
- social scenes;
- temporary jobs;
- contests or exhibitions where mechanically appropriate;
- sourcing requests;
- professional networking;
- historical-item appraisal;
- investigation of a disputed object.

After the fair, some relationships and suppliers persist even though most temporary stalls disappear.

## Seed CE — The Fossil's Journey

A fossil or excavation find has a documented chain from field discovery to transport, storage, research and eventual display.

A dispute later emerges because one link in that history is incomplete.

Possible questions:
- Was the specimen labeled correctly?
- Did two containers get exchanged?
- Does the original site still exist?
- Who actually found it?
- Was an institution promised eventual return or display?

The scenario connects excavation, evidence custody, public memory and research without assuming ownership law that Ouros has not yet authored.

## Seed CF — Repair Before Replacement

A personally meaningful piece of equipment is damaged during an adventure.

Instead of automatically deleting or replacing it, the world can offer a repair path through an appropriate specialist when the governing rules permit repair.

Narrative outputs may include:
- a visible scar or replacement component;
- a new relationship with the repairer;
- provenance recording the event that caused the damage;
- a later callback when someone recognizes the object.

No repair grants extra statistics unless a real PTU/Caelo rule says so.

## Seed CG — The Apprentice's First Commission

An apprentice receives their first important independent order while their mentor deliberately stays in the background.

Players might become involved because:
- the requested material is difficult to source;
- the client changes requirements;
- the apprentice lacks confidence;
- the workshop has another emergency order;
- one component arrives damaged;
- a professional rival offers unsolicited help.

Success or failure changes the NPC's career and relationships, not a hidden crafting-level meter.

## Seed CH — Two Ways to Supply a Town

A large distributor offers reliable, inexpensive access to common supplies. Local specialists worry that dependence on one supplier will weaken regional workshops and traditional skills.

Both sides provide genuine value.

Players may encounter:
- residents who prefer lower cost and consistency;
- artisans who depend on local clients;
- emergency responders who need reliable volume;
- a conservation group concerned about increased extraction;
- a distributor willing to contract local producers rather than replace them.

Possible outcomes include coexistence, specialization, partnership, market dominance or a later supply vulnerability.

## Seed CI — The Material That Shouldn't Be Here

Players discover a material batch in a location far outside its known regional supply.

The anomaly can become evidence of several different things:
- an old trade route;
- legitimate import;
- a traveling artisan;
- reused salvage;
- geological or ecological change;
- smuggling only if separately authored evidence supports it;
- incorrect assumptions about the material's known range.

The object begins an investigation because provenance is surprising, not because possession proves wrongdoing.

## Seed CJ — Market After the Storm

A storm closes the easiest trade route into a settlement for a short period.

Visible effects appear gradually:
- one merchant limits specialty stock;
- another begins using a substitute;
- transport work becomes more valuable;
- local producers receive more customers;
- a visiting trader chooses a longer route;
- rumors exaggerate the severity of the shortage.

Restoring access resolves much of the pressure, while some new supplier relationships may remain.

## Seed CK — The Artisan Who Remembers

A recurring workshop NPC keeps concise memory of meaningful customer interactions.

They may remember:
- the first major commission they made for a player;
- who supplied a rare material;
- an item they repaired after a famous battle;
- a debt or favor;
- whether the player credited them publicly;
- a difficult order they refused.

Later content can emerge from this relationship without requiring the artisan to become a major quest giver every time the player visits.

## Seed CL — Salvage Is Not Empty Loot

A ruined industrial site or abandoned facility contains potentially useful salvage.

Different actors care about the same materials:
- a settlement needs repair components;
- researchers want machinery left intact;
- an artisan wants specific reusable pieces;
- a conservation group worries that removal will disturb a habitat;
- a faction wants exclusive access.

The system should not assume that anything in a dungeon belongs to the first player who clicks it. Exact rights and permissions require future Ouros canon.

## Seed CM — The Working Memorial

A tool or item used during an important historical rescue is still functional years later.

An institution wants to display it. Another group argues it should remain in active service because replacing it would be wasteful or symbolically wrong.

The conflict can involve:
- public memory;
- item provenance;
- institutional tradition;
- the wishes of its current custodian;
- whether a replica or exhibit record would satisfy everyone.

No historical importance changes the item's mechanical stats.

## Seed CN — Pokémon at Work

A workshop, farm, courier service or field team relies on one or more Pokémon whose actual governed capabilities make them useful to the work.

When one persistent Pokémon becomes unavailable because of injury, migration, training or another story state, the humans must adapt.

Possible consequences:
- temporary slower service;
- another Pokémon with a different legal capability changes the workflow;
- a player helps with transport or gathering;
- an apprentice learns a method that uses less specialized assistance.

The story treats the Pokémon as a participant with history rather than a machine-like production modifier.

## Seed CO — The Recipe That Travels

A useful technique becomes known in a new region through an actual chain of people and institutions.

Possible transmission:
- a visiting specialist teaches a local apprentice;
- an academy shares approved material;
- a retired artisan donates a manual;
- two workshops exchange methods after cooperating during a crisis;
- a player introduces specialists who would otherwise never meet.

Recipe provenance creates social history. The narrative layer cannot grant the recipe unless the actual PTU/Caelo prerequisites remain satisfied.

## Seed CP — The Festival Commission

A major public event creates temporary demand for decorations, food, repairs, equipment preparation and commemorative objects.

Different players may interact through:
- collecting ordinary supplies;
- escorting a shipment;
- helping a specialist meet deadlines;
- negotiating between an organizer and local producers;
- documenting a traditional technique;
- resolving a mistaken delivery.

After the event, an artisan may gain new clients or the settlement may decide to retain a temporary workshop.

## Seed CQ — The Suspicious Batch

Several customers report that supposedly identical goods from one shipment behave or look inconsistent.

Possible authored truths include:
- accidental mixing of two batches;
- emergency substitution that was poorly communicated;
- storage damage;
- a supplier using cheaper material;
- deliberate counterfeit goods;
- customers misunderstanding a cosmetic variant.

The case begins with quality/provenance evidence. It should not pre-label a merchant as criminal.

## Seed CR — The Workshop That Outgrew the Street

A once-small specialist becomes regionally successful after several player-linked events.

Growth creates second-order effects:
- more apprentices and customers;
- delivery traffic;
- demand for storage;
- higher material consumption;
- a nearby business benefits from visitors;
- residents complain about noise or congestion;
- the workshop considers relocating or opening a second site.

Success becomes future world state rather than an ending screen.

## Seed CS — The Shared Tool Library

A settlement creates a communal pool of specialized equipment that residents can borrow instead of everyone owning the same tools.

The system can create social stories around:
- maintenance responsibility;
- training new users;
- scheduling during an emergency;
- a missing tool with an innocent explanation;
- an institution offering to sponsor expansion;
- whether expensive specialist equipment should remain public or controlled by experts.

Exact usable tools and mechanical access remain governed by PTU/Caelo.

## Seed CT — The Last Traditional Maker

A regional technique is becoming rare because its final active specialist has no successor.

The problem does not require the technique to be mechanically superior.

Players can become involved through:
- documenting the process;
- finding an interested apprentice;
- helping the maker obtain sustainable materials;
- arranging an exhibit or workshop;
- deciding whether the tradition naturally ends rather than forcing preservation.

This supports material culture without treating every old tradition as sacred or automatically better.

## New implementation and canon questions

- Which PTU/Caelo crafting systems and homebrew recipes will Ouros actually retain?
- Does current AutoPTU model individual item instances or only item types/stacks?
- Which items can be legally repaired, modified or crafted in the current engine?
- Which Pokémon resource-producing capabilities are implemented and queryable?
- How should Minecraft containers and dropped items retain an Ouros provenance ID?
- What threshold determines when an item becomes a persistent named instance?
- Will shops use fixed PTU/Caelo prices, an authored regional price table or a later limited economy model?
- How should supply routes update when their Minecraft regions are unloaded?
- Which professions/artisan roles belong in Ouros canon?
- What ownership and salvage rules, if any, will different regions establish?
- How much crafting can occur in Minecraft directly versus through AutoPTU/UI services?
- Which cosmetic item variants can be safely represented without affecting battle legality?
