# The Spring That Went Cold — Pass 328

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Date: 2026-09-07

## Premise

A long-used hydrothermal feature loses much of its visible heat or flow. During the same period, a nearby crack or old vent begins producing stronger steam or warm runoff. A local authority restricts part of the site while several groups argue about whether the change is temporary, caused by maintenance, caused by a natural shift, or evidence of a larger problem.

The premise does not require a villain. The mystery comes from several correct observations being interpreted at different scales.

## Persistent location structure

The location should use stable feature IDs across visits:

- `UPPER_SPRING`: the historically important outlet.
- `MINERAL_TERRACE`: deposits preserving earlier flow paths.
- `SERVICE_WALK`: maintained access used by staff and visitors.
- `LOWER_RUNOFF`: a channel or habitat influenced by the outlet.
- `OLD_VENT`: a previously minor or dormant surface feature.
- `MONITOR_POINT`: a place where dated observations are recorded.
- `DEPENDENT_SITE`: a greenhouse, bathhouse, research station, workshop, cultural facility, or other use that depends on the feature. Exact use remains unresolved.

No listed feature becomes canon merely by appearing in this proposal.

## Initial contradiction

Three reports can all be accurate:

A maintenance crew reports that the spring basin and service works are unobstructed.

A monitoring team records lower output at the historically important outlet.

A resident or worker reports stronger steam or warm runoff from another part of the site.

The investigation asks which claims are supported, what each claim actually covers, and which decisions can be justified before the subsurface cause is known.

## Exploration loop

First visit: establish the feature map, obtain current observations, identify who received which reports, and preserve timestamps.

Archive or institutional leg: compare earlier monitoring records, maintenance work orders, closures, photographs or sketches, and public notices. Old mineral deposits can be evidence that water once followed another route without explaining why it moved.

Second field leg: inspect multiple persistent features rather than treating the entire thermal field as one state. Look for changes since the first visit and record the new observations separately.

Decision leg: recommend a feature-scoped response. Options can include monitoring, keeping one path closed, reopening a stable sector, changing maintenance access, protecting a newly affected habitat, or delaying a high-cost intervention until stronger evidence exists.

Revisit: show the consequences and whether later observations strengthen or weaken the original interpretation.

## Open explanations

The scenario should keep several explanations viable until evidence rules them out:

- mineral deposition or other obstruction altered a surface path;
- underground plumbing shifted naturally;
- groundwater recharge changed;
- a small seismic or geological event changed flow;
- maintenance unintentionally changed a local outlet;
- engineered extraction or diversion changed pressure or supply;
- Pokémon activity affected a feature in a way that is specifically evidenced rather than assumed from species flavor;
- more than one small cause occurred close together.

No explanation is canon-approved in this file.

## NPC and faction candidates

A site steward knows access rules, prior closures, and operational pressure but does not automatically know subsurface truth.

A field researcher knows observations and uncertainty but may lack historical work-order context.

A maintenance lead knows what was repaired and where but may have received only a narrow monitoring summary.

A dependent-site operator has legitimate economic, cultural, or service needs and may advocate reopening without being dishonest.

A conservation or habitat observer may care about runoff, microbial mats, nesting, feeding, or another local ecological use without having authority over public access.

An archive custodian can recover earlier records but does not gain knowledge of present physical conditions unless someone reports them.

These roles can become named NPCs only after region and institution canon are chosen.

## Knowledge and consequence design

The same institution can hold incompatible beliefs because staff members received different evidence.

A public notice records what was communicated, not hidden world truth.

A closure should point to the feature, evidence set, authority, date, recipients, and review condition that produced it.

A later correction creates a new decision lineage. It does not erase the earlier decision or make every NPC retroactively aware of the correction.

Consequences can include diverted foot traffic, delayed work, altered local income, new monitoring jobs, a protected habitat sector, public trust changes, or a follow-up expedition.

## Reduced encounter version

The complete narrative premise can run without dynamic hydrothermal combat mechanics.

Use authored world states such as:

- `SPRING_OUTPUT_REDUCED`
- `VENT_ACTIVITY_OBSERVED`
- `MINERAL_TRACE_HISTORICAL`
- `SERVICE_WALK_OPEN`
- `FEATURE_RESTRICTED`
- `THERMAL_FEATURE_MONITORING`
- `OBSERVATION_STALE_AFTER_CHANGE`

These are narrative/world-state descriptors, not PTU statuses.

If combat occurs, use a static tactical map and only ordinary verified targeting, movement, calculations, initiative, and legal-action infrastructure. Unsafe thermal sectors can simply be outside the legal encounter area or represented by pre-authored blocked geometry. No heat damage, steam condition, vent pulse, healing, slide, forced movement, gas rule, or environmental reaction is implied.

## Intended mechanically rich version

A future full version could support steam that changes visibility, timed vents, unstable or slippery sectors, rescue/interception near dangerous ground, shifting hazard boundaries, feature-specific weather/zone interactions, or relevant Moves/Abilities/Items/Trainer Features.

Those behaviors must remain gated by the capability families recorded in the Pass 328 design contract and readiness snapshot. The Minecraft adapter may render the phenomenon but cannot implement missing PTU rules.
