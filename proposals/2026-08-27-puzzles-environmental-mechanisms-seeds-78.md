# Ouros Puzzle & Environmental Mechanism Seeds — Pass 78

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON.

Nothing in this file establishes Ouros geography, factions, institutions, ancient cultures, technology, Pokémon associations or PTU mechanics. Names are working labels only.

These candidates use `design/puzzles-environmental-mechanisms-persistent-state-extension.md` and the provenance in `research/2026-08-27-puzzles-environmental-mechanisms-scan-78.md`.

## Design intent

The common goal is to make puzzle solving alter a persistent place. Inputs have perceptible feedback. Wrong attempts do not create accidental campaign softlocks. Bypasses leave state. Old solutions can become outdated after real repairs or reconfiguration. Pokémon-assisted approaches require exact authoritative capability evidence.

## The Door Opened Somewhere Else

Premise:

A local control accepts an input and visibly registers it, but the passage it changes is in another part of the site.

The challenge is not repeatedly guessing whether the control worked. The player receives a strong local confirmation such as a deep mechanical sound, a pressure change, an indicator shift or a mapped status light, then has to infer where the output lies.

Persistent outputs can include:

- one remote passage changes state;
- Cartography can append the newly verified connection;
- later visitors can find the route already open;
- Maintenance can eventually discover wear caused by repeated operation.

Canon questions:

Who built or operates the mechanism, and what technology/architecture makes sense for the location?

## Three Chambers, Any Order

Premise:

Three independent rooms each contribute one state to a central mechanism. No room requires another to be completed first.

Each module has its own observation grammar and gives a distinct central feedback cue when solved. The final system activates only when all required modules are confirmed.

Why it fits Ouros:

- partial progress persists across visits;
- players can split exploration priorities;
- one inaccessible room does not erase work elsewhere;
- a later world event can reconfigure one module without resetting all three;
- the site can support different route orders for different parties.

No combat is required.

## The Emergency Reset Is Part of the Place

Premise:

A complex mechanism has a clearly marked or discoverable master reset because its original operators needed a safe recovery method.

Using it restores a known baseline but may also close optional routes or reset a temporary convenience state.

Story value:

The reset teaches that the place had ordinary users, procedures and maintenance practices. It also prevents trial-and-error from becoming a softlock.

Possible later callback:

The reset control itself becomes damaged, forcing a Maintenance handoff rather than a harder version of the same puzzle.

## The Bypass Leaves a Trace

Premise:

Players can operate a sealed access system correctly, use an authorized override, repair around it or physically breach a weak secondary barrier if the world and capabilities support that approach.

The routes converge narratively but produce different persistent site states.

Examples of consequences:

- proper operation preserves the mechanism;
- an override creates an access log;
- repair adds a new component/provenance record;
- a destructive bypass creates visible damage and a future work order;
- a temporary workaround may fail later for a causal reason.

The system never marks every method as the same generic `SOLVED=true` result.

## The Puzzle Everyone Learned Wrong

Premise:

A publicly circulated guide describes a mechanism accurately as it existed years earlier. A later repair or retrofit changed one dependency.

Players who follow the old instructions get partial, intelligible results rather than an arbitrary failure. They can compare:

- current physical response;
- old diagrams;
- repair records;
- wear patterns;
- a newer local observation.

The actual mystery is historical configuration drift.

No source is treated as lying merely because it is stale.

## The Mechanism That Became Habitat

Premise:

A dormant water-control, ventilation or moving-gate system has been inactive long enough for Pokémon to use part of it as habitat.

Restarting it may restore access or service but change the current ecological arrangement.

The decision therefore crosses systems:

- Puzzle layer establishes what the machinery can do;
- Ecology establishes current occupation and observed behavior;
- Maintenance/Technology establishes whether operation is safe;
- Conservation may own any protection decision;
- Travel owns resulting route access.

The puzzle itself does not decide that restoration or preservation is morally correct.

## The Side Room Is the Instruction Manual

Premise:

An optional room contains diagrams, test controls, maintenance markings or an older small-scale version of the main mechanism.

Players can still experiment without it, but inspecting the side room makes the causal grammar much clearer.

This creates a reward for exploration that is knowledge rather than loot.

A later revisit can make the side room valuable again if the mechanism is reconfigured.

## The Indicator Is Broken, the Mechanism Works

Premise:

The main mechanism responds correctly, but its normal confirmation light, bell or display has failed.

This candidate deliberately separates a real feedback failure from a badly designed puzzle. Other observable evidence exists:

- vibration in a conduit;
- a changed soundscape;
- a distant pressure gauge;
- visible movement through a window;
- a map/status board that updates independently.

Players can solve the immediate access problem and separately create a Maintenance record for the failed indicator.

## One Input, Two Legitimate Outcomes

Premise:

A routing mechanism can direct limited water, power, counterweight force or access toward one of two branches. Both are valid operating states.

The challenge is a world-state choice rather than discovering the author's single answer.

Potential tradeoff:

- branch A opens a shorter exploration route;
- branch B restores a service or protects another zone;
- later actions may allow reconfiguration, but time or access state makes the current choice meaningful.

Exact utility/technology details require canon approval.

## Five Switches, Four Actual Changes

Type: noncombat investigation puzzle.

Premise:

Five controls are documented. Current observation shows only four distinct outputs.

Possible explanations remain open until evidence resolves them:

- one switch was disconnected during a repair;
- two controls now operate the same output;
- one output is remote and unnoticed;
- a control is decorative/redundant;
- records combine two historical configurations;
- the current mechanism is damaged.

The solution is provenance plus observation, not an automatic sabotage reveal.

## The Wrong Success State

Premise:

Players successfully open the route they need, but the mechanism's finite configuration closes a secondary path.

The game communicates both changes immediately.

This supports a puzzle with tradeoffs rather than binary success/failure. The secondary closure may matter to:

- another expedition;
- a return route;
- maintenance access;
- wildlife movement;
- a later public route.

Players can make a knowledgeable choice because consequences are visible before leaving the mechanism behind.

## A Mechanism Accumulates Repairs

Type: long-term arc.

First visit:

The party encounters the mechanism in its old configuration and learns its basic operating grammar.

Second visit:

A previous bypass or high-use period has left visible wear. A temporary fix changes one interaction.

Third visit:

A proper repair or retrofit occurs. Old public instructions become partially stale.

Fourth visit:

New users operate the updated system. The party's earlier observations remain historically correct but no longer fully describe current behavior.

Later callback:

A new problem can be solved faster because the party and local operators possess transition history, repair provenance and a known reset path.

The arc creates institutional/site memory without a hidden “puzzle level.”

## Rotating Platform Interruption

Type: mechanically rich encounter.

Narrative premise:

A conflict begins while a large traversal mechanism is in use. The intended final version allows platform orientation to change tactical paths during the encounter.

Full version:

- platform states change legal paths and possibly LoS;
- controls can be contested;
- timing occurs through authoritative battle lifecycle;
- if rotation physically relocates actors, landing/collision/forced-movement rules apply;
- autonomous opponents understand relevant objectives only when tactical AI supports them;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon renders AutoPTU-owned state and never resolves it.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement if relocation occurs;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- full stateful damage pipeline;
- status lifecycle when selected combatants require it;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions for dynamic battlefield topology/effects;
- move-specific behavior;
- abilities;
- items;
- Trainer Features/perks as used;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced version:

Operate the mechanism before combat. Its chosen orientation selects one reviewed static arena. The platform does not move until AutoPTU finishes. The story still centers on reaching/using the mechanism, while unsupported dynamic rules are removed.

## Gatehouse Override Under Pressure

Type: mechanically rich encounter.

Narrative premise:

A route-control station must be operated while hostile or territorial combatants contest the area.

Full version:

- participants may need to REACH/ACTIVATE/CLEAR access to controls;
- gates change routes and LoS during the encounter;
- interception or forced movement can matter if source/runtime support exists;
- objective-aware AI can defend, withdraw from or contest controls;
- all tactical state is owned by AutoPTU.

Primary dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS;
- base movement legality;
- core calculations;
- action economy/initiative;
- full turn/round lifecycle;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions;
- complete movement if interception/forced relocation is used;
- move-specific behavior, abilities, items and Trainer Features/perks as selected content requires;
- AI legal-action infrastructure;
- AI tactical policy;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback support.

Reduced version:

The controls are resolved before combat or at explicit world-state checkpoints between separate ordinary battles. Each gate configuration maps to a static arena. No Minecraft gate animation changes AutoPTU legality mid-resolution.

## Central Mechanism Verification

Type: noncombat scene.

Premise:

Several modules appear solved, yet the central system has not transitioned to its expected operating state.

The party checks:

- module state records;
- remote output indicators;
- physical connections;
- maintenance revisions;
- previous bypasses;
- whether one module was solved under an obsolete configuration.

Possible outcome:

The answer can be a disconnected output, incomplete verification, stale documentation or a real fault. The scene hands off to Maintenance when the evidence establishes a technical problem.
