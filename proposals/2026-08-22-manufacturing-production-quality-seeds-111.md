# Pass 111 Proposals — Manufacturing, Production Runs & Quality State

Status: NON-CANON candidates. Every mechanic remains subject to PTU/Caelo and implementation validation.

These concepts assume the Pass 111 manufacturing layer and the pre-existing Material Culture, Technology, Workplaces, Supply Chains, Waste, Cases, Retail and Infrastructure layers.

## 30 worldbuilding and adventure candidates

### 1. The Lot That Passed Twice

A production lot receives two apparently valid inspection records from different points in the process. The values disagree because the second check was performed after an approved rework step, but the public rumor becomes “the factory changes its test results.” The useful story is reconstructing chronology before accusing anyone of falsification.

### 2. The Wrong Revision on the Line

A line runs a valid old process revision after a software/document synchronization failure. The output is placed on hold while staff determine whether the revision difference matters for this specific product. The incident can connect Technology, Digital Systems, Workplaces and Supply Chains without requiring sabotage.

### 3. The Good Part From a Bad Run

A production run is interrupted by an unrelated equipment fault after some components have already completed their required steps. The institution has to determine which outputs belong to the affected window. “Run failed” cannot automatically make every item bad.

### 4. The Deviation With No Defect

An operator records that a step took longer than the process definition expected. Later observations show no confirmed output problem. The deviation remains part of history instead of being erased because the product was released.

### 5. The Defect With No Recorded Deviation

A downstream workshop discovers that a component does not fit its documented specification, but the manufacturing record shows no process excursion. The investigation must consider measurement, source material, process data, documentation and downstream handling before choosing a cause.

### 6. First Run After Maintenance

A major line returns from preventive maintenance. The first production run becomes a high-attention observation window, with technicians, operators and a research team all interested for different reasons. A normal result can be narratively meaningful because it confirms recovery.

### 7. Automated Line, Human Judgment

A highly automated facility can run with few operators, but a borderline output still requires an institutional decision. The story focuses on who has the authority and information to place a hold, not on whether automation is “good” or “bad.”

### 8. The Batch That Waited for Release

A finished lot sits in storage for days because one supporting record is missing. Supply Chains shows physical stock but zero available stock. A local shortage grows even though visitors can literally see boxes through the warehouse window.

### 9. The Recall That Wasn't a Scandal

A credible field signal causes a manufacturer to recall a narrow production window before the root cause is known. Media attention assumes wrongdoing. The eventual cause may be mundane, serious, external or remain partly unresolved; responsible caution should not be written as confession.

### 10. Three Factories, One Specification

Three sites produce nominally interchangeable components. Small differences in equipment, staffing and process history produce different investigation clues when a downstream problem appears. The correct conclusion may involve one site, several sites or none.

### 11. The Old Process Makes Better Stories

Residents romanticize an older artisan process after a product becomes mass-produced. Historical records show the old workshop had its own limitations and irregularities. The conflict is cultural memory versus operational need, not “craft good, factory bad.”

### 12. The Pilot Run Everyone Remembers

A factory still displays one surviving object from its first pilot run. Years later, archives reveal that the pilot was technically incomplete but socially important because it proved the region could make the product locally. The object becomes a museum candidate without gaining mechanical powers.

### 13. The Perfect Yield That Was a Data Error

A run appears to have produced more finished units than its input genealogy can explain. The tempting explanation is theft or counterfeiting. The real problem might be duplicated posting, merged lots, stale data or another mundane reconciliation failure.

### 14. The Pokémon Helper Opts Out

A Pokémon that has long participated voluntarily in moving light materials stops reporting to the production area. The site adapts operations first. Investigation of the reason remains open and must not procedurally invent fear, illness, mistreatment or disloyalty.

### 15. The Scrap Stream Became a Resource

A material once treated as routine scrap becomes useful to a workshop or restoration project. Waste and Material Culture create a new provenance path. The factory does not retroactively become “zero waste,” and old disposal history remains real.

### 16. The Rework Lot With Two Histories

A reworked lot contains outputs from two parent batches that were legitimately merged under an authored process. Years later, one parent becomes relevant to a recall investigation. The genealogy matters more than the current item label.

### 17. The Part That Fits but Is Not Approved

A replacement component physically fits an old machine but belongs to an unverified revision. Technicians can demonstrate compatibility claims without the system silently treating “it fits” as “it is authorized and safe for use.”

### 18. The Factory Museum Line

A retired production line remains intact as an educational exhibit. A shortage later raises the question of whether it could produce parts again. Technology owns whether the equipment works; Manufacturing owns whether a valid process still exists; Credentials/Workplaces own who can operate it.

### 19. The Factory That Makes No Finished Product

A regional plant produces only refined material or intermediate components. Most residents underestimate its importance until an outage affects several unrelated industries. The story teaches supply dependency without turning the site into a consumer shop.

### 20. The Temporary Line That Became Permanent

An emergency production cell created after a crisis continues operating years later because it proved useful. Its improvised layout, staffing arrangements and records were never designed for permanent operation. Public Works and Workplaces must decide whether to formalize, relocate or retire it.

### 21. The Night Shift Finds the Drift First

Night workers notice a gradual change in sound, vibration or output appearance before instruments show a clear failure. Their observation enters evidence without automatically becoming the correct diagnosis. Soundscapes and Technology can corroborate later.

### 22. The Spare Tool From Another Region

A borrowed production tool solves an immediate outage, but its setup differs from local practice. The first output is held while staff document what actually changed. Interregional recognition applies to technical practice without inventing universal certification law.

### 23. The Batch Born During a Blackout

A production run survives a short power interruption because backup systems worked, but one data logger has a gap. The physical product and the production record now have different uncertainties. The incident can connect Energy, Digital Systems and Quality without applying “blackout = ruined.”

### 24. The Retired Jig Used Once More

An old fixture is temporarily returned to service for a legacy component. Its existence is not the issue; the unanswered question is whether the current process still defines how it should be used. Archives become operationally relevant.

### 25. The Process Change After the Fire

A facility rebuilds after a fire and changes layout, ventilation and production sequence. Years later, people compare outputs before and after the rebuild. The comparison must distinguish process revision from nostalgia and from environmental recovery.

### 26. The Production Run Interrupted by Migration

A seasonal wildlife movement crosses a service road required by a factory. The facility can pause, reroute or change schedule rather than treating the Pokémon as enemies. Conservation, Road Ecology and Manufacturing share the consequence.

### 27. The Factory Garden Buffer

A landscaped buffer originally created for noise or dust gradually develops ecological value. Expansion plans now affect both production capacity and a habitat that did not exist when the factory opened. Industrial and conservation goals can both be legitimate.

### 28. Same Product, Different Route

Two output lots of the same item type reach the same market through different distribution networks. One later develops a condition issue caused after production. Manufacturing history helps prove that the production run was shared while downstream handling was not.

### 29. The Lot With the Misprinted Label

A packaging error prints the wrong revision/date code while internal genealogy remains intact. The visible label is evidence, not the whole identity of the lot. Recall scope depends on what actually happened, not on the most alarming reading of the package.

### 30. The Thousandth Unit Ceremony

A factory marks a production milestone with a small public event. The “thousandth unit” is historically important only if counting records actually support the claim. Even if the number later proves off by one, the ceremony itself remains a real public event.

## Long arc A — Five Revisions at Meridian Works

Year 1: Meridian Works operates a mature process with stable local staff and one long-serving production line.

Year 2: a supply change forces a limited process revision. The new input is mechanically equivalent only where authoritative rules say so; otherwise it remains a specification question.

Year 3: maintenance and a utility upgrade alter the physical line. A pilot run produces several deviations but no confirmed defects.

Year 4: an external field report triggers a narrow recall. Investigation shows that only one revision window is plausibly affected.

Year 5: the site adopts a new process, but keeps the old line and records as training/history material.

The arc leaves a visible production chronology in the settlement without requiring one villain or one definitive “best” version.

## Long arc B — The Lot Genealogy

A component lot is manufactured in one settlement, refined/assembled into a larger item in another, stored through a seasonal transport closure and finally distributed to several institutions.

Years later a failure signal appears at one destination. Players and NPCs reconstruct parent lots, process versions, transport histories, maintenance and downstream use. The final conclusion may narrow the issue to one child branch while preserving all unaffected items.

The arc is designed to exercise Manufacturing, Material Culture, Supply Chains, Rail/Maritime, Technology, Cases and Archives through one persistent chain of objects.

## Long arc C — From Workshop to Factory

A respected local workshop gains demand beyond what individual commissions can support.

First it creates a repeatable pilot process. Then it trains new staff, adds shared tooling, reserves supply, builds a small line and eventually becomes a regional manufacturer.

At each stage players can influence questions such as:

- which parts remain hand-finished;
- how much production stays local;
- whether the old workshop remains open;
- what environmental buffers or logistics improvements accompany expansion;
- how apprentices become operators or specialists;
- how provenance remains visible after volume increases.

The outcome can be a thriving factory, a deliberately limited cooperative, a split artisan/industrial model or another authored arrangement. Scale alone does not decide success.

## Encounter contract 1 — Assembly Line Emergency Stop

FULL version:

An anomaly on an active production line coincides with unexpected Pokémon presence. Workers need evacuation, a safe-stop point must be reached and the Pokémon may need a retreat route.

Primary capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS: VERIFIED
- base movement legality: VERIFIED
- complete movement including push/pull/knockback/interception/forced movement: BLOCKING for moving workers, moving carriers, displacement or corridor interception
- core calculations: VERIFIED
- action economy/initiative: VERIFIED
- full turn/round lifecycle: PARTIAL
- full stateful damage pipeline: PARTIAL
- status lifecycle: PARTIAL
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING if machinery, heat, steam, conveyors or exclusion zones gain tactical effects
- move-specific behavior: PARTIAL
- abilities: PARTIAL
- items: PARTIAL
- Trainer Features/perks: PARTIAL
- AI legal-action infrastructure: VERIFIED
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for `EVACUATE`, `REACH_SHUTOFF`, `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_TECHNICIAN`
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING

REDUCED version:

World state stops the line and evacuates workers first. The server freezes a safe static factory-floor arena. AutoPTU resolves only combatants who remain. Production diagnosis and disposition occur afterward.

## Encounter contract 2 — Quality Hold Warehouse Transfer

FULL version:

A held lot must be isolated or moved while a separate conflict threatens the transfer corridor. Nobody may infer that the lot is defective merely because it is on hold.

Primary capability dependencies:

- verified ordinary targeting/calculations/action economy
- complete movement/interception: BLOCKING for cargo escort/intercept
- lifecycle/damage/status/move/ability/item/Feature families: PARTIAL where used
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for `PROTECT_CARGO`, `INTERCEPT`, `CLEAR_ROUTE`
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING only if warehouse conditions become mechanical

REDUCED version:

Secure the lot under custody outside the grid. Run a static chokepoint encounter. Resume the transfer afterward. Production records and inspections, never battle victory, determine release or further hold.

## Encounter contract 3 — Rework Cell Disturbance

FULL version:

A known rework operation is interrupted by a machine problem or wildlife incursion. Staff need to leave while work-in-progress keeps its exact lot identity and genealogy.

Primary capability dependencies:

- targeting/base movement/core/action economy at current VERIFIED scope
- complete movement: BLOCKING for worker/WIP movement and interception
- lifecycle/damage/status/move/ability/item/Feature families: PARTIAL where used
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions: BLOCKING if active machinery/process areas become mechanics
- AI tactical policy: BLOCKING for `WITHDRAW`, `PROTECT_WORKER`, `REACH_EXIT`
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback: BLOCKING

REDUCED version:

Pause rework. Move staff and WIP to a server-authoritative safe state. Run a conventional static encounter if needed. Continue the same rework record afterward; combat does not create a fresh output lot.

## Mechanics guardrail

Nothing in these proposals authorizes:

- manufacturing DCs;
- item quality bonuses;
- capture bonuses/penalties based on manufacturing provenance;
- factory hazards;
- conveyor forced movement;
- furnace/fire damage;
- chemical status effects;
- machine HP;
- automatic Pokémon labor bonuses;
- random defect generation;
- player-created recipes;
- unvalidated repair/crafting outputs.

All such mechanics remain blocked until PTU/Caelo and implementation evidence support the exact behavior.
