# Pass 176 Proposals — Pokémon Spatial Ecology, Home Ranges, Site Fidelity & Territoriality

Status: PROPOSED / NON-CANON
Nothing in this file overwrites established Ouros lore.

## 30 worldbuilding candidates

### 1. The Hill Used Every Winter but Owned by Nobody

A persistent wild individual returns to the same hillside each cold season. Local residents call it "its hill," while Land Tenure shows several human use rights crossing the same space. The story is about coexistence between behavioral attachment and legal access, not choosing which boundary is "real."

### 2. Three Pokémon, One Orchard Edge

Telemetry and field signs show three known individuals repeatedly using the same orchard boundary. They are rarely observed together. The overlap could be shared food, different active windows, observation bias or a changing landscape.

### 3. The Scent Marks Outlive the Marker

Repeated marks once supported a defended-area hypothesis. The known individual disappears from observation, but old chemical/physical evidence persists. Researchers must distinguish current territoriality from historical evidence.

### 4. The Range That Shifted After the Bridge

A new bridge changes human traffic and habitat connectivity. A known Pokémon begins appearing on the opposite bank. One year is insufficient to determine whether this is an excursion, an expanded range or a permanent shift.

### 5. The Familiar Individual on the Wrong Side of the River

A photographed individual appears outside every previous range assessment. The observation is authentic. The explanation remains open: rare excursion, seasonal resource, river crossing, displacement, bad prior coverage or changing routine.

### 6. The Empty Core Area

A location used intensively for years suddenly receives no detections. The broader range is still active. The disappearance can become a question without declaring abandonment or death.

### 7. The Park That Became a Shortcut

An urban park was once a core-use site. After a redevelopment project, the same population still crosses it but spends little time there. The place remains inside the wider spatial footprint while changing ecological function.

### 8. One Excursion, Big Rumor

A famous Pokémon appears far beyond its known range for one afternoon. Media coverage immediately announces a "new territory." The scientific record keeps a single excursion event until more evidence exists.

### 9. The Old Range Map Is Still Correct for Its Year

A museum displays a research map from twenty years earlier. Tourists treat it as current. The map remains historically accurate while the present spatial-use revision differs.

### 10. The Boundary Nobody Observed

Two clusters of detections appear to leave a clean gap. The gap aligns exactly with an area that researchers could not access. The apparent boundary may be biological or methodological.

### 11. The Pair That Patrols Together

Two persistent Pokémon are repeatedly observed moving through the same defended area. They may share a spatial unit without the system inferring pair bond, parentage, breeding, Pack Mon or shared initiative.

### 12. The Call Carries Farther Than the Patrol

Acoustic monitoring detects territorial calls well outside the subject's most frequently observed movement area. Soundscape reach and physical use remain distinct.

### 13. The Patrol Without a Fight

A known territorial individual regularly approaches an access point, displays and returns to its core area when hikers back away. Years can pass without combat because the behavior is predictable and the route is managed well.

### 14. The Tolerated Neighbor

One neighboring Pokémon repeatedly enters an area where other conspecifics are chased away. The exception becomes a social/ecological question rather than proof of friendship or collective membership.

### 15. The Newcomer Who Is Never Challenged

A recently observed individual uses part of a defended area but receives no visible response. Possible explanations include different timing, tolerated status, incomplete observation or a territoriality hypothesis that was too broad.

### 16. The Mountain After the Fire

A territorial mountain Pokémon remains associated with the same landscape after wildfire but changes its core-use areas while vegetation recovers. Environmental restoration, spatial behavior and any authored stewardship behavior can evolve together.

### 17. The Territory Crosses Four Parcels

A single defended ecological area overlaps public forest, a research reserve, a farm and a trail easement. No institution gets automatic authority over the whole behavior footprint.

### 18. The Waterpoint Only Matters in August

Several local ranges overlap at one spring for a short dry-season window. Increased co-occurrence does not prove a new collective or permanent territorial collapse.

### 19. The Night Boundary

Day observations show extensive overlap. Night observations show repeated exclusion around a roost approach. Territoriality may be context-specific rather than a permanent all-day property.

### 20. The Former Partner Establishes a Range Nearby

A released former partner is later documented repeatedly using a valley near the old Trainer's town. The same identity persists. The location does not restore custody, ownership or relationship state.

### 21. The Route Through Someone Else's Core Area

A migration corridor intersects a local resident's high-use space for one week each year. Migration and territoriality interact without one authority replacing the other.

### 22. The Camera Traps Made the Range Smaller

A new camera network is concentrated near roads and produces a tighter apparent home range than the older telemetry study. The correction is methodological, not ecological.

### 23. The Range Expanded When the Orchard Closed

A food source disappears and a known Pokémon starts using more scattered locations. The wider footprint could reflect resource search, but causation remains a hypothesis until supported.

### 24. The Territory Became a Plaza

A long-used urban site becomes a formal public plaza. Architecture and Public Space change, while an old spatial association remains visible through recurring visits and public memory.

### 25. The Defended Tree Falls

A storm destroys a repeatedly defended tree. The Pokémon may relocate the core-use area, continue defending the surrounding site, disappear temporarily or adopt another resource. None is predetermined.

### 26. The Boundary Atlas Has Confidence Bands

A research institute publishes a public atlas showing fuzzy zones and evidence dates instead of crisp lines. This becomes a recognizable visual language for Ouros ecology.

### 27. The Territory Story Was Too Simple

Decades of local lore describe one dominant individual controlling an entire valley. Historical records later show multiple overlapping users and selective defense around only two resources. The folklore remains culturally important while the ecological model becomes more precise.

### 28. The Old Patrol Road

A recurring patrol route persists after a road closes to vehicles. Over years the same alignment becomes habitat, trail, research transect and public story.

### 29. The Individual Returns After Three Years

A known Pokémon disappears from observations for several seasons and later returns to a former site. Chronicle records the gap honestly; it does not fabricate where the Pokémon was during the missing years.

### 30. Nothing Happened at Cedar Ridge

A full year of monitoring finds essentially the same range, core-use sites and overlap pattern as before. The quiet year becomes valuable baseline evidence when a later change occurs.

## Longer arcs

### Five Range Maps of Alder Valley

Year 1 establishes a coarse map from field observations. Year 2 adds telemetry and reveals a larger use footprint. Year 3 a bridge opens and one individual begins making excursions. Year 4 the original core area loses a major resource. Year 5 a revised assessment supports a partial range shift while showing that another individual's range barely changed.

The arc teaches players that maps are dated scientific products rather than invisible ecological walls.

### The Individual Who Used Three Cities

A persistent wild Pokémon is first known from an industrial fringe, later uses a redeveloped river corridor and eventually becomes a recurring presence near a third settlement. Photography, Telemetry, Urban Wildlife, Public Memory and spatial assessments all tell different parts of the same life history. The Pokémon never becomes a mascot automatically and does not need to be captured for the arc to matter.

### The Overlap Years

Two local populations increasingly overlap after habitat restoration reconnects a landscape. Early observers expect conflict. Some sites show avoidance, some simultaneous use and one resource shows genuine defense. Over several years the world develops a mixed picture rather than a single winner/loser narrative.

## Encounter contracts

### Territorial Display at Shared Waterpoint — FULL

Premise: during a seasonal dry window, several Pokémon use the same waterpoint. A persistent territorial individual begins displaying near the approach while another group and field team are present.

World state before battle:

- Freshwater owns water availability;
- spatial ecology owns current overlap/territorial evidence;
- Conservation/Research Ethics owns field-team activity;
- Wild Collectives owns group identity;
- no actor is automatically hostile.

Intended full tactical version:

- non-hostile actors can withdraw or cross toward safe exits;
- the territorial individual can display, hold position, withdraw or chase according to authored tactical policy;
- researchers can evacuate;
- water access remains a semantic objective without granting a battle bonus;
- combat begins only if a real confrontation occurs.

Capability dependencies:

- targeting/footprints/range/LoS — VERIFIED;
- base movement legality — VERIFIED;
- complete movement including interception/forced movement — BLOCKING for dynamic withdrawal/chase/crossing;
- core calculations — VERIFIED;
- action economy/initiative — VERIFIED;
- full lifecycle — PARTIAL if advanced timing is used;
- full stateful damage — PARTIAL if combat occurs;
- status lifecycle — PARTIAL if exact Status effects occur;
- terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions — BLOCKING if water/mud/protected areas have tactical effects;
- move-specific behavior — PARTIAL when exact Moves matter;
- abilities — PARTIAL;
- items — PARTIAL;
- Trainer Features/perks — PARTIAL;
- AI legal-action infrastructure — VERIFIED;
- AI tactical policy — BLOCKING for display/withdrawal/non-KO objectives;
- Minecraft/Cobblemon/Craftics adapter/playback — BLOCKING.

Reduced version:

Close the field session and move researchers away through world state. Resolve background Pokémon movement outside the grid. If a subset still enters genuine combat, freeze a dry static arena adjacent to the waterpoint with no water/mud/territory mechanics. After battle, update only the observed behavior and interruption record. Victory does not establish territorial ownership or remove the subject from the range.

### Range Survey at New Wildlife Crossing — FULL

Premise: a new wildlife crossing has opened and researchers are testing whether known local individuals have changed their ordinary space use.

Intended full version:

- persistent subjects may cross, approach and retreat through a dynamic corridor;
- researchers move between observation points;
- traffic or other actors may create changing route constraints;
- AI can pursue `CROSS`, `WITHDRAW`, `REACH_OBSERVATION_POINT` or `AVOID_CONFLICT` rather than pure KO logic.

Capability dependencies:

Complete movement, AI tactical policy and adapter/playback are BLOCKING. Environmental family is BLOCKING if traffic, barriers or crossing zones become tactical. Basic targeting, base movement, calculations, action economy and legal-action infrastructure remain VERIFIED; combat-specific lifecycle/damage/status/move/ability/item/Feature families remain PARTIAL when invoked.

Reduced version:

Perform crossing observations entirely in world state. If an unrelated confrontation occurs, pause traffic and wildlife movement, isolate a legal static arena and run only actual combatants. The spatial assessment consumes the pre/post observation record, not the battle winner.

### Familiar Individual Beyond Historic Range — FULL

Premise: a well-documented persistent Pokémon is observed outside every previous range revision near a newly changed landscape feature.

Intended full version:

- players may follow at a respectful distance;
- the subject can withdraw, reach cover or continue along a route;
- other actors may intercept the party or subject for independent reasons;
- the objective is observation/identification rather than KO.

Capability dependencies:

Complete movement is BLOCKING for pursuit/withdrawal/interception. AI tactical policy is BLOCKING for non-hostile path objectives. Adapter/playback is BLOCKING. Terrain/weather/hazards/zones/reactions is BLOCKING only if the changed landscape has tactical effects. Identity and spatial interpretation remain outside AutoPTU.

Reduced version:

Confirm identity using Photography/Telemetry/Field Signs outside combat. Resolve the Pokémon's movement as world state. If another threat produces battle, place only those combatants in a static legal arena. The distant sighting remains an `EXCURSION_EVENT` unless later evidence supports a range revision.

### Range Reconciliation Workshop — NON-COMBAT

Three institutions have different maps because they used different observation methods and date ranges. The correct outcome may be several compatible products with different scopes. Science/Metrology/Telemetry/spatial ecology handle the review. No battle is required.

## Canon questions intentionally left open

- Which Ouros species/populations have authored territorial behavior?
- Which persistent wild individuals already have long observation histories at campaign start?
- What precision of home-range information is public?
- Which ranges are sensitive because they reveal nests, dens or release sites?
- Which institutions can publish reviewed assessments?
- How frequently can spatial state progress offline?
- How do player-built roads, farms, lighting, waterways or settlements influence a range without becoming direct spawn exploits?
- Which official species-lore behaviors apply locally and which are absent or modified by Ouros ecology?
- What PTU/Caelo rules, if any, govern territorial encounters beyond ordinary battle behavior?

Until canon review answers these questions, every candidate in Pass 176 remains proposed.
